Generation and inspection method

Construction

  • Loaded the supplied 256×144 RGB source with PIL and preserved it byte-for-byte as frame 00.
  • Built one locked clean plate from the source using localized OpenCV inpainting only under the original ball, grabber, pile, and contact shadow. Pixels outside those repair masks retain the photographic source background.
  • Recomputed every dynamic element for frames 01–19 in 2D code: Bezier-like grabber jaws, analytically shaded ball ellipses, a noisy implicit sand silhouette, granular lobes, impact crater/rim, ejected grains, soft table/pile/ball shadows, and foreground sand occlusion.
  • Used a hand-authored vertical trajectory with fall acceleration, impact squash, rebound, second contact, damped wobble, and a three-frame still hold. Fixed random seeds make all texture and grain placement deterministic.
  • Saved through the required local artifact_io.save(frames, HERE) call, then generated transition_check.png as an additional motion/continuity audit sheet.

Inspection modalities actually used

  1. Visual image inspection with the image-view tool: viewed first_frame.png at original resolution before coding.
  2. Numeric image inspection with PIL/NumPy: checked dimensions, dtype, representative RGB samples, and broad green/blue/dark color-mask bounds in the source.
  3. Visual image inspection with the image-view tool: viewed the generated inspection.png storyboard and transition_check.png at original resolution after the first render.
  4. Numeric frame/tensor inspection with PIL/NumPy: measured per-transition changed-pixel counts, mean absolute RGB differences, and maxima; asserted frame 00 equality; decoded and compared every PNG against every uint32 tensor plane.
  5. Visual image inspection with the image-view tool: viewed the regenerated transition_check.png after the revision. No browser/video playback inspection was claimed or used.

Revision made after inspection

The first visual/numeric review showed minor procedural texture shimmer during the intended final hold. I froze the ball's orientation/microtexture phase and the settled sand-rim random seed from frame 17 onward, regenerated all artifacts, and inspected the revised transition sheet. Frames 17, 18, and 19 are now pixel-identical.

Verification results

  • Frame stack: uint8 (20, 144, 256, 3).
  • Packed tensor: uint32 (144, 256, 20) encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • All 20 PNG files match their corresponding packed tensor planes exactly.
  • The last two transitions contain zero changed pixels, confirming the final still hold.

Visible limitations

  • Frame 00 is the exact photographic input, while later dynamic actors are procedural; the sand granules, ball surface, and jaw rendering therefore become slightly cleaner and more graphic at the first transition.
  • Removing the source actors requires localized inpainting. The repaired wall/table regions are soft and plausible at this small resolution, but they cannot reconstruct hidden photographic detail.
  • The ball is described as a tennis ball, but the source has no clearly resolved seam or fuzz. The animation preserves its smooth blue appearance with subtle synthetic surface flecks rather than inventing a strong seam.
  • The grabber's support mechanism is cropped out of the source. Only the visible jaws are animated, with their hinge remaining off-screen.
