METHOD

Inspection modality actually used

  • Visual: direct image-tool reading of first_frame.png, of 4×–8× nearest-neighbour crop enlargements with coordinate grids (ball/grabber and sand regions), and of my own generated evidence: ball-vs-source comparison strips, the all-20-frames contact sheet, transition_check.png, zoomed fall/impact/rest crops, and inspection.png.
  • Numeric: RGB sampling on pixel rows/columns, HSV-like threshold masks and connected-component stats for ball/sand/grabber bounds, quadratic wall-model residuals, per-frame difference maps against the static plate, mask bounding boxes, mean/percentile colour stats, and tensor/PNG equality checks.
  • No external images, reference videos, networks, learned weights, physics engines, 3D scenes, or meshes were used.

What was built

generate.py produces 20 × 256×144 RGB frames at 4 fps and saves them through artifact_io.save (plus transition_check.png).

  1. Static plate. Frame 0 is kept byte-identical. For later frames a background plate is built once: the ball and its motion-blur halo are detected as deviations from a quadratic RGB fit of the plain wall (plus a teal-tint test), grabber pixels are spatially protected, and the hole is feather-filled with the wall model plus real micro-texture sampled only from neighbouring pixels that are themselves verified clean wall. Wall, table, sand pile and the blue/black grabber are otherwise untouched and stay pixel-static (verified: 0 delta in far wall/table regions; frames 10–19 are byte-identical).
  2. Ball (fully recomputed per frame, 4× supersampled). Ellipsoid disk with analytic front-face normal, Lambert+wrap shading from an upper-front-right key light matched to frame 0 (shadow teal ~(15,78,108), lit ~(47,120,153)), lower-hemisphere blue-teal occlusion, grazing darkening, low-amplitude felt mottle/grain, two projected tennis-seam arcs (thin lit streak + faint groove, attenuated at the limb), and a soft irregular felt silhouette with sparse fuzz hairs. Spin rotates the seams while falling. Motion blur is temporal: 6–12 premultiplied sub-samples across a ~0.12 s shutter. Impact squash (1.10×/0.88) and a smaller re-contact squash are included.
  3. Physics (2D, screen scale). y = 38.5 + 15t + 27.5t² for the fall (contact at frame 5, v≈84 px/s), one small ~4 px rebound on frames 6–7, re-seating on frame 9, rest from frame 10 at center y=101.
  4. Sand. Largest connected green component defines the pile with a soft alpha. Impact creates an elliptical crater via a radial remap (inward texture compression + rim push), cavity darkening (strongest under the ball), and a lit granular front rim; amplitude evolves 0.75 (impact) → 1.0 (while the ball is airborne) → 0.85 retained. A soft shadow on the sand tightens/darkens as the ball approaches; a thin contact shadow is drawn at impact/rest. Eighteen small deterministic grains eject from the contact ring on ballistic arcs and disappear by frame 9. The embedded ball gets an irregular granular sand crescent wrapping its lower sides (texture sampled from the photographed pile, patchy clod highlights).

Revisions made after visual inspection

  1. First inpainting attempts left ball-coloured ghosts (Telea bleed / incorrect mask that deleted ball core while protecting grabber colours); fixed with a spatial grabber-protection mask, full-interior fill, feathered wall-model fill, and validated clean-wall texture sources.
  2. Ball sprite initially leaked a faint alpha rectangle (alpha baseline bug), was too bright/cyan with strong yin-yang seams, then too dark/mottled; retuned palette, occlusion and seam strength/position over three iterations to match the frame-0 ball (core mean tracked against the source).
  3. Crater was initially too subtle and the embedded lip had a straight, uniform band; strengthened cavity/rim contrast and rebuilt the lip as an irregular crescent (thin at front-centre, wrapping the sides, broken clod highlights).
  4. Impact pose was too flat/high; reduced squash, pressed the ball into the pile, replaced a broad dark gap with a thin contact shadow, and moved ejecta launch points to the contact ring (grains now composite behind the ball instead of onto its face).

Visible limitations

  • The synthetic ball matches frame 0's colours and softness but is not pixel-identical to the photographed one (frame 0 itself is the original); seam placement is an analytic approximation, and per-frame felt mottle is procedural, so close freeze-frame comparison with frame 0 shows small texture differences even though frame 1 is visually continuous.
  • Motion blur is a sub-sampled streak at a long effective interval; very fast frames (3–4) are necessarily streak-heavy at 4 fps.
  • Crater deformation is a 2D remap/shading model of a single photographed pile (no 3D height field); displaced clods are limited to ejecta grains and the front lip, and the grabber performs no opening motion (it simply releases).
  • Sand interaction is only visible at the impact zone; the rest of the pile correctly stays static, and rest frames 10–19 are intentionally identical.
