Generation and inspection method

Initial visual observation

The only image supplied to the visual model was the original first_frame.png attached to the user's request. That observation established the tabletop, gray wall, suspended grabber, blue ball, sand mound, camera direction, and soft illumination. OBSERVATION.md was written before generate.py, with image-space bounds, support/contact, uncertainties, and all twenty planned frame events.

The original PNG was additionally opened with PIL and converted to NumPy arrays for numeric color samples, color masks, object bounds, and smooth color fitting. Those original-input measurements refined the upper-right ball highlight and the sand color/contrast estimate. This is distinct from the later inspection of generated outputs.

Two-dimensional synthesis

generate.py uses deterministic Python, NumPy, PIL, SciPy array filters, and OpenCV raster operations. All files and work remain in the current workspace. There was no network access, other agent, external model, learned weight, physics engine, mesh, 3D scene, surface-normal calculation, or material renderer.

Frame 00 is copied exactly from the input. The stationary photographic wall, wooden table, grabber, and unaffected outer sand are retained. A tight elliptical mask and OpenCV image inpainting restore the wall hidden by the initial ball. The tool remains fixed while the ball separates from its tips. A small strip of newly exposed table uses nearby static table pixels for background repair.

The ball is recalculated for every subsequent frame from a tilted ellipse, image-space color polynomials, smooth edge shading, curved blue seams, and procedural fine fuzz. Its color coefficients come from a least-squares fit to initial-input colors; moving ball pixels are never an extracted image patch. Position follows a short analytical gravity drop, followed by strongly damped penetration into the sand. Release begins at 0.08 s; modeled first contact is approximately 0.281 s. At 4 fps the samples show descent at 0.25 s and the post-contact state at 0.50 s. Shape compression and rotation decay on impact.

The moving central sand surface is synthesized using persistent multiscale noise and hundreds of small angular cohesive fragments. Its boundary changes with local crest compression; elliptical rim and cavity functions add contact relief. A ragged front lip covers part of the ball's lower edge. Soft shadow fields express the falling ball and sand contact, and eleven small nearby fragments follow bounded image-space flight/settling paths. The outer mound remains photographic. Four-times raster supersampling antialiases generated geometry; the static photographic base uses nearest-neighbor expansion so downsampling preserves its pixels exactly.

Later numeric inspection and revisions

No generated PNG, contact sheet, transition image, or player was opened with an image Read/View tool or supplied to the visual model. Generated PNGs were opened only with PIL for array comparison and numeric analysis. Inspection used Python, NumPy, OpenCV connected-component masks, shell text, and file consistency checks. Therefore this report makes no claim of visually reviewing the generated continuation.

The first generation passed the tensor, PNG, player, first-frame, stationary region, ball silhouette, and settling checks. Further numeric measurements found that the new sand had less local grain contrast than the input. The procedural grain contrast was increased by a factor of 1.55, and explicit preservation was tightened around the tool boundary, including one nearby background pixel changed by inpainting. A remaining blue pixel in the queried cleared-wall rectangle was identified numerically as an unchanged original grabber pixel at (148, 23), rather than a residual ball pixel. These were numeric-driven revisions; no visual output inspection occurred.

After regeneration, verify.py checks:

  • Exactly twenty 256 × 144 RGB PNGs and a uint32 (144, 256, 20) tensor.
  • All PNG pixels equal their tensor slices encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • Frame 00 equals the initial input pixel for pixel.
  • All twenty standalone-player frames reconstruct exactly from its packets.
  • Checked stationary table/wall/tool regions remain pixel-identical.
  • A coherent blue connected component descends and then remains in contact.
  • Frames 07–19 are identical after residual motion finishes.
  • The two required inspection/evidence PNGs are valid image files.

Results are saved in verification.json; analytical poses and change counts are in motion_metrics.json. inspection.png, transition_check.png, and change_map.png are saved evidence only and were not viewed by the agent. Run python generate.py and python verify.py to reproduce the artifacts. artifact_io.save(frames, HERE) exports the required frame stack, packed tensor, inspection sheet, and a standalone five-second player at constant 4 fps.

Expected visible limitations

These limitations follow from the method and numeric checks, rather than viewing the outputs. The ball and locally rebuilt sand have procedural detail that cannot reproduce every photographic fiber or sand ridge. The concealed wall is an inpainted approximation. Perspective, sand cohesion, penetration depth, and seam rotation are inferred from a single frame, without calibrated physical dimensions. The grabber stays in the supplied pose as the ball drops; its small opening motion is not separately resolved. At 4 fps, the fastest part of the collision occurs between samples, and the settled final pose lasts for most of the clip. Numeric checks establish consistency and localization, but cannot establish the full perceptual realism of the generated video.
