Generation method and inspection record

The inspection modality was exclusively programmatic numeric image access. PIL decoded the RGB arrays; NumPy supplied channel samples, regional averages, coordinate maps, frame differences, and exact equality checks; OpenCV supplied color-threshold components, masks, and local background reconstruction. Coordinate maps were emitted as text characters. No image Read/View tool was used, no generated or source image was supplied to a visual model, and no visual inspection is claimed. The saved inspection and transition PNGs are evidence for later display.

generate.py implements a deterministic two-dimensional animation. It retains the fixed photographic wall, tabletop, remote sand, and upper grabber support. It reconstructs wall pixels hidden by the original ball and moving grabber tips using local RGB inpainting. The two lower tool arms are regenerated as curved outlines and blue interior ribbons, with a smooth opening around fixed upper attachments.

Each moving ball is calculated anew from an antialiased elliptical silhouette, analytic two-dimensional color fields, seeded mathematical fiber texture, and rotated curved seam fields. The source ball is not cropped, moved, or resized. The rendered ball uses four-times supersampling and a small focus blur. The main falling sample has a short exposure blur that is much smaller than its travel between samples. Frame 00 and the initial hold at frame 01 retain the source pixels exactly.

Release occurs at 0.63 seconds. A constant downward acceleration of 265 pixels per second squared places contact at approximately 1.10 seconds. A strongly damped contact curve produces about 11.6 pixels of penetration and negligible rebound. The center settles near (135.8, 80.7). The local sand develops a dark indentation, solid grainy side ridges, and an irregular front lip. Foreground sand hides the ball's lowest edge. A few freshly generated small ellipses describe nearby cohesive crumbs following short ballistic arcs. Distant grains and the pile's base stay fixed. Added cast-shadow strength is calibrated to zero while the ball is initially held, then increases during the fall.

Revisions made after numerical checks:

  • Strengthened and widened the pale curved seams and adjusted their vertical extent. The regenerated held ball's core RGB mean changed from approximately (32.24, 106.73, 138.21) to (38.14, 106.60, 137.06), close to the source mean of (38.62, 107.18, 136.36).
  • Darkened the lower ball shading to better match sampled source rows. The core RGB mean absolute error decreased from approximately (8.74, 4.92, 5.15) to (6.79, 4.56, 4.87).
  • Removed an added shadow discontinuity during the initial hold by subtracting the held-position shadow baseline.
  • Replaced soft side-ridge coverage with solid irregular elliptical silhouettes and grain.
  • Consolidated grain rendering into bounded supersampled patches, making regeneration faster without moving a source texture patch.

Limitations relevant to the displayed result: the seams, ball fuzz, articulated lower arms, and new sand grain approximate the source photograph numerically and may look more regular than photographic detail. Local inpainting can leave slight smoothness in the cleared gray wall. Four fps samples the short fall and impact sparsely; the first contact is between exported samples. The physical scale and kinetic sand properties are unspecified, so the shallow indentation and damping are plausible choices rather than measured material behavior. These limitations were assessed from the method and pixel statistics, without visually viewing the output.

The output is exactly 20 frames of 256 by 144 RGB at four fps, for five seconds. artifact_io.save(frames, HERE) saves the PNG sequence, inspection sheet, standalone player, and pixels.npz. The tensor is uint32 (144, 256, 20) packed as 0xRRGGBB. All twenty decoded PNGs are checked against that tensor, with zero channel mismatches. Frame 00 is checked for pixel identity to first_frame.png. audit.json records the motion states, transition bounds and differences, file consistency, and exact preservation of pixels outside the complete dynamic region. transition_check.png records six before/after/difference triplets.
