Generation method and actual inspections

Output

Twenty 256×144 RGB frames at 4 fps, occupying five seconds. Frame 00 is the decoded source image without changes. generate.py imports the local artifact_io helper and calls artifact_io.save with a uint8 array of shape (20,144,256,3). The saved pixels.npz contains the uint32 tensor pixels of shape (144,256,20), with each RGB pixel packed as 0xRRGGBB. index.html is the standalone pixel-animation player.

Two-dimensional construction

The wall, table, and unaffected part of the sand pile retain the source photograph. A local cubic color field fitted to nearby uncovered wall pixels fills the region revealed when the ball and jaws move. No moving source-object patch is cropped, translated, or scaled.

The upper grabber attachment stays fixed. Opening jaws use explicit 2D Bézier outlines, black borders, blue faces, and a thin highlight. The ball is regenerated from its 2D silhouette each frame, with diffuse analytic shading, two projected curved seams, deterministic multiscale surface texture, optical edge softness, and short exposure blur while falling. Position follows a gravity-like quadratic fall, followed by plastic indentation and very small damped recovery. The ball's deformation, orientation, support shadow, and sand occlusion are recomputed.

The impact area is generated with deterministic green material noise and 290 irregular shaded clumps whose positions shift outward under compression. A dark depression, raised elliptical collar, short radial fissures, and a rough foreground lip describe the local sand deformation. Five small cohesive clumps move a few pixels and settle. Stationary sand beyond the impact region retains its original silhouette and photographic texture. Unaffected pixels outside the explicitly bounded active regions are restored exactly from the source. The final pose and material stop changing after frame 07.

Only local Python, NumPy, OpenCV, PIL, and the provided output helper were used. There were no network requests, outside assets, AI generation models, learned weights, physics engines, or 3D scene representations.

Inspection modalities actually used

  1. Visual input: read the source image attached in the user message.
  2. Image-tool visual inspection: supplied the original first_frame.png and .tmp/first_frame_enlarged.png (4× enlargement) through view_image before coding.
  3. First generated-output visual inspection: supplied transition_check.png and .tmp/final_enlarged.png through view_image. The transition sheet showed frames 00, 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, and 09. These inspections revealed an overly sharp ball, prominent seams, a straight contact cut, and some blue contamination in the reconstructed wall.
  4. Final generated-output visual inspection: after one revision, supplied the regenerated transition_check.png and .tmp/final_enlarged.png through view_image again. Confirmed the softened ball, less prominent seams, irregular curved foreground contact, and cleaner uncovered wall. These are the final saved image versions.
  5. Numeric inspection: used PIL/NumPy for dimensions, selected source colors, dtype and tensor shape, exact source-frame identity, every saved frame PNG versus its packed tensor slice, unchanged background samples, and the identical final held frames. The generator also prints changed-pixel counts per frame transition.

There were six view_image calls in total, in addition to the attached visual input. No video playback was viewed. inspection.png was generated as an additional contact sheet but was not itself supplied through an image tool. Only the listed sampled output frames were visually inspected; all twenty were numerically verified.

Revisions

One regeneration followed the first output inspection. The revision replaced edge-based wall inpainting with a local polynomial color fit, softened the ball outline and surface, reduced and repositioned seam highlights, increased sand-clump relief and collar shading, changed the foreground lip from a nearly straight cut to an irregular curved boundary, and restored exact stationary pixels outside the active regions. The initially generated and revised outputs were both checked for PNG/tensor consistency. Final outputs replace the draft.

Visible limitations

The procedural ball is smoother and more regular than the source's photographic ball, so a small appearance change is visible at the first transition. The regenerated local sand is an approximate 2D interpretation of a compacted rim and granular clumps; its fine texture is simpler than the original photographic sand. At 4 fps, the brief free fall and first impact are represented by only a few frames. The response of the sand and the unseen wall illumination are plausible choices from a single image, rather than measured quantities.
