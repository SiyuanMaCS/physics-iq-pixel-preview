ball-in-sand · 生成方法

Blue tennis ball released from the blue-black grabber into the pile of green kinetic sand. Everything is local Python pixel math (numpy + Pillow); no external models, engines, or other cases were used. frames/00.png is a byte copy of first_frame.png.

Background restoration

The ball moves vertically, so the wall/table it vacates must be rebuilt:

  • A 46×37 inpaint rectangle (x100–136, y43–88) is modelled row by row. Wall rows interpolate clean frame-0 wall colour between median anchor strips at x94–98 and x138–144; wood rows interpolate between x82–90 and x142–158 and add a same-row grain residual sampled from the clean table. The wall/wood edge per column comes from a wood-colour threshold on frame 0.
  • Verified numerically: restored wall in the fully vacated strip (rows 43–48 at frame 1, rows 45–55 at frame 2) differs from the row-anchor model by ≤~1 intensity level. The grabber (y0–42) and table outside the pile are pixel-identical to frame 0 on every generated frame.

Ball sprite and motion

  • One RGBA sprite (32×29) is extracted from frame 0: coverage is an analytic ellipse (centre (118,58.5), radii 14.8×12.8) with a 0.25-unit anti-aliased edge; RGB is the unpremultiplied frame-0 colour over the modelled background. Frame 0 contains a baked dark motion-blur fringe on the ball, so dark sprite pixels are partially lifted toward the core colour RGB(30,110,142) to avoid a near-black crescent at contact.
  • Centre trajectory (fixed x=118): 58.5, 61.6, 68.0, 77.6, 84.2, 81.5, 83.0, then 83.0 — constant-acceleration fall (~52 px/s²), a small damped rebound off the sand, and rest embedded in the crater.
  • Falling frames (1–3) add four faint alpha-weighted ghost stamps below the ball as forward motion blur; impact frame 4 squashes the sprite vertically ×0.88 (×1.07 horizontal), frame 5 relaxes to ×0.97/×1.02.
  • A soft elliptical contact/approach shadow is darkened onto the sand surface.

Sand deformation

  • The frame-0 pile is converted per column to a top/bottom height field (green-chroma threshold, x74–164).
  • Each frame applies a column shift delta(x): Gaussian dent (σ=10.5 px, depth 0.92·penetration) flanked by a narrow raised rim ring at |u|≈15 px (≤2.3 px rise). Columns are vertically resampled from the original pile texture, with the shift attenuated to zero over 34 px toward the fixed pile base; the exposed crater hollow is painted self-shadowed green sand, and raised crests reuse shaded crest colour. Kinetic sand holds the shape, so frames 7–19 are static.
  • Twelve 1–2 px green grains follow analytic ballistic arcs (g=3.8 px/frame²) on frames 4–6, launched from the rim ring.

Outputs

generate.py → pixels.npz (144×256×20 0xRRGGBB uint32 tensor), frames/00.png–19.png, inspection.png, transition_check.png, and index.html (via the supplied artifact_io.save).

Verification (numeric only; no image viewing tool used)

  • All 20 PNGs unpack to exactly the uint32 tensor values.
  • frames/00.png == first_frame.png byte-for-byte.
  • Ball strong-blue centroid moves monotonically downward through frame 4, rebounds slightly frame 5, and is identical frames 7→19 (cx stays 118±0.8).
  • Dent-zone sand top drops from y89 (frame 0) to y95–97 at impact and holds.
  • Grabber region and right-side table unchanged across all frames.
  • Frames 8–19 pixel-identical (settled rest pose).
