2. Clean table background

For every frame except frame 00 the balls/shadows are composited onto one static clean-wood plate:

  • per row, hole pixels are filled with that row's wood tone measured from a clean strip (x = 44..72, left of all objects and shadows): the strip's Gaussian low-frequency mean gives the level, and its high-frequency residual (grain, mean ≈ 0) is tiled across the run. Wood tone is constant along x in a row (horizontal grain), verified numerically, so row-wise filling reproduces the surrounding wood without seams;
  • regions outside the holes are never rewritten (verified: pipe, couch, floor and untouched table pixels are exactly identical to frame 00 in all frames).

3. Ball sprites

Each ball is cut from frame 0 as a 23×23 patch with an analytic anti-aliased disk alpha centered at the measured (sub-pixel) center. The RGB inside the alpha is the original photographed felt, including the seam highlight.

4. Contact shadow

From the isolated grey ball, the multiplicative ratio frame0_wood / clean_wood is sampled where its shadow ellipse is visible (ratio clipped to [0.55, 1]), stored in ball-centered coordinates, the occluded-behind-disk cells filled with cv2.inpaint (NS), then Gaussian smoothed.
