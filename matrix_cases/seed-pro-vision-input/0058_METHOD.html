Physics interpretation

The grabber opens (prongs rotate outward about their off-frame pivots) and releases the duck; the duck free-falls vertically, still upright, into the open box, crossing the rim band and coming to rest on the box floor hidden behind the front face. At 4 fps a true 1 g fall (~0.25 s for ≈70 px at this scale) would occupy one frame interval, so gravity is deliberately scaled down (constant acceleration g ≈ 1.3 px/frame², fall starts between frames 1 and 2) so the release, fall and entry are legible across the 5 s clip. Motion blur uses a ~180° shutter (half-interval exposure) along the vertical path. Inside the unlit opening the duck is strongly darkened (factor ≈0.4→0.15 through the 4 px band) and pixels at/under the front rim (y ≥ 87) are occluded by the front face. A very faint warm-dark patch remains in the depth of the opening after landing (within the interior's existing value range) to hint at the duck resting inside.

Method outline (see METHOD.md after implementation)

Static background = frame 0 with duck/prong holes diffusion-inpainted from the surrounding wall (one fixed background reused every frame so there is no inter-frame flicker). Duck and prongs are extracted once from frame 0 as soft-alpha RGBA sprites by color unblending against the local wall color. Frames 1–19 composite rotated prongs and the duck sprite with analytic constant-acceleration vertical motion, half-shutter multi-stamp motion blur, and the box opening darkening/occlusion model.

METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0058_attempt_01

What happens

The suspended orange-black grabber opens its two inward-angled prongs and releases the yellow rubber duck. The duck free-falls vertically, still upright, into the open dark-green fabric box; it crosses the dark opening band, is occluded by the front face, and comes to rest hidden inside the box. The clip is 20 frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s); frame 0 is the exact input first_frame.png.

Pipeline (all local Python; numpy + Pillow only; see generate.py)

  1. Measurement. Colours and geometry of the wall, table, box, opening band, duck, beak and prongs were measured from first_frame.png with numpy masks (recorded in OBSERVATION.md). Key values: duck core ≈ (153,121,3), beak ≈ (150,88,3), prong orange ≈ (136,50,10) / black ≈ (17,13,10), front face ≈ (31,29,15), interior band y≈83–87 (values 15–35), solid front face from y≈87.

  2. Wall estimate. The three sprite rectangles (duck x117–142, y13–36; left prong x121–129; right prong x133–141, both y0–12) are filled with a 350-iteration Jacobi (4-neighbour mean) diffusion from the true wall boundary, giving the local wall colour behind every object pixel.

  3. Soft-alpha sprite extraction. For each pixel inside a sprite rectangle, the blend alpha along each candidate object-colour vector (duck/beak or prong orange/black) is solved by projection against the local wall colour (a = clamp((I−W)·(C−W)/|C−W|²)); the best vector is kept, low-alpha noise is thresholded (duck 0.10, prongs 0.12), and intrinsic colour is reconstructed as W+(I−W)/a. Alpha and premultiplied colour are then Gaussian-blurred (σ≈0.7 px) to match the soft, slightly defocused look of the source. Sprites keep float RGB 0–255 with alpha 0–255.

  4. Cleaned static background. The union of sprite masks (alpha > 10), dilated by 2 px without edge wrapping, is diffusion-inpainted (500 iterations). Filled wall values agree with same-row wall references to within ~1–3 levels. This single fixed background is used for frames 1–19, so exposed pixels never flicker.

  5. Grabber release. Each prong sprite is placed on a full-frame layer, rotated (bilinear, premultiplied alpha, transparent fill) about its off-frame pivot — left (125.3, 0), right (136.8, 0). Opening schedule: frame 1 = 7°, frames 2–19 = 13° outward, moving the tips ~2–3 px apart and slightly upward.

  6. Duck motion. Constant acceleration from rest starting at frame t=1: vertical offset p = 0.5·g·(f−1)² with g = 1.3 px/frame², no horizontal motion and no rotation (a released object with no torque stays upright). Positions are sub-pixel and resampled bilinearly from the sprite (zero-alpha padded ring + transparent transform fill, so there is no edge-clamp fringe or ghost line).

  7. Motion blur. Per frame, 1–9 stamps are spread over the central 56% of the 0.25 s interval (≈180° shutter); premultiplied colours and exposure coverages are accumulated (simple coverage averaging), which yields a vertical blur whose length follows the velocity (up to ≈14 px/interval near the rim).

  8. Box entry. Screen-row lighting: full brightness above y=83; inside the 4 px opening band the duck is darkened by factors 0.42/0.30/0.22/0.16 (rows 83–86); at y ≥ 86.6 both coverage and colour are zeroed — the front face occludes it. Consequently the full-colour duck reaches the rim on frame 11, only a thin dark blur remains in the opening on frame 12, and it is fully inside on frame 13.

  9. Resting hint. From frame 13 a very faint warm-dark Gaussian blob (σ 4×0.8 px, strength 0.18 then 0.35, hard support ±10.5/±2.4 px, colour (24,19,8)) sits deep in the opening, suggesting the duck resting in the dim interior; its peak stays inside the interior's natural value range (≈18 vs surrounding 15–22). Frames 15–19 are pixel-identical.

  10. Output. artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, shape 144×256×20), frames/00.png…19.png, inspection.png and the standalone index.html delta player. transition_check.png (written by generate.py) magnifies the box-opening crop (x108–151, y70–91) for frames 9–13 above the ×4-amplified consecutive-frame difference rows.

Honest simplifications

  • Gravity is slowed. At this image scale true 1 g sends the duck through the ~70 px fall in one 0.25 s frame interval, making the event invisible at 4 fps. g = 1.3 px/frame² spreads release→entry over frames 1–12 (≈2.8 s) for legibility; all other motion (constant acceleration, upright body, exposure blur) is physical.
  • The hidden landing (≈frame 13.8) and the faint post-landing interior hint are inferred/simplified rather than simulated inside the box; no bounce is shown because the front face fully occludes it.
  • The prongs rotate rigidly about fixed off-frame pivots; the tool's upper body (above frame 0) is never visible and does not move.
  • No external assets, engines, models or image tools were used; the only inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. Outputs were checked numerically (tensor/PNG equality, frame-00 byte identity, changed-pixel footprints, colour/centroid trajectories, static-region invariance); no image viewing tool was used on generated frames.
