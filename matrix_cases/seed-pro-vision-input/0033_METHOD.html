METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0033

What the clip shows

Locked-off single shot, 20 frames, 256×144 @ 4 fps (5 s). A blue and a yellow tennis ball roll out of the left and right pipes along the same line at equal speed, touch at frame 6 (t = 1.50 s), and rebound elastically (equal masses ⇒ velocities exchanged) back toward their pipes at the same speed. Frame 00 is byte-for-byte the supplied first_frame.png.

Everything was produced with local Python pixel operations (numpy + PIL); no external models, engines, references or image-viewing tools. Verification was numeric only (masks, centroids, differences, ascii maps).

Measurements (see OBSERVATION.md for details)

  • blue centre (75.98, 49.88), R = 7.5 px; yellow centre (149.73, 58.50), R = 6.5 px (colour-mask centroids and bbox radii).
  • travel axis u = (0.9932, 0.1162); initial gap 74.26 px.
  • equal head-on speed v = (74.26 − 14.0)/2 / 6 frames = 5.02 px/frame (20.1 px/s), so contact (gap = Rb + Ry = 14.0) lands exactly on frame 6; positions per frame are analytic and tabulated in OBSERVATION.md.

Background plate

The two ball disks and their inward-pointing soft dark blobs were removed from frame 0 inside two ROIs: for each row the background is a linear ramp between the medians of clean flank columns on both sides of the ROI, plus detrended wood grain copied from a clean table snippet (x 170–213, per-row linear trend removed and wrapped into the ROI). Blob pixels were selected with a darkness ratio > 0.08 against the ramp, outside the disk, and only on the inward side (projection along u past R + 0.5). A 2 px feather blends the fill into untouched pixels. Outside the ROIs the plate equals frame 0 exactly, so both pipes, the couch strip and all grain stay untouched in every frame (verified: max pipe change 0–1 intensity level).

Ball appearance model

For each ball a 21×21 / 23×23 RGBA sprite was taken from frame 0 against the plate:

  • alpha: radial coverage profile built from the max-channel |frame − plate| delta, medians in 0.25 px radial bins, smoothed, forced to 1 in the core and 0 beyond R + 1; sampled bilinearly at subpixel positions.
  • decontamination: ball RGB = (F0 − (1−α)·plate)/α.
  • shading: per-channel least-squares sphere field a + b·nx + c·ny + d·nz + e·(1−nz²) (ambient + Lambert key light + limb-darkening term) fitted on the disk interior.
  • surface modulation: measured RGB / shading-field ratio (contains the blue curved seam streak and the fuzzy tennis texture), clamped and feathered to 1 at the limb.

Rendering at each frame: fixed screen-space shading field at the new subpixel centre multiplied by the modulation sampled on the sphere after a 3-D roll by φ = s/R about the in-table axis perpendicular to travel (Rodrigues rotation of surface normals; unknown back hemisphere mirrored). φ accumulates to +4.0 rad at contact and reverses sign afterwards (angular rate ±0.67 rad/frame), consistent with pure rolling through the elastic rebound. Frame-0 pose reconstruction error is mean |Δ| ≈ 3.8 (blue) / 5.1 (yellow) within the disks.

Shadows

A soft multiply-darkening stamp (1 − lum_F0/lum_plate, capped at 0.5) was extracted in a ~45 px window around each frame-0 ball, zeroed inside the disk and on the outward hemisphere, and reduced to the single connected blob anchored inward of the ball (isolated table-noise specks discarded) before [1,2,3,2,1]/9 smoothing. Stamps translate rigidly with the ball centres and are composited before the balls, so near contact (frame 6) the balls correctly cover overlapping shadows; at the 1 px seam the yellow ball's edge alpha blends over the blue edge (olive antialias line).

Frame composition and I/O

For t = 1..19: plate copy → both shadow stamps → blue sphere → yellow sphere (bilinear coverage). Frame 0 is the original image verbatim. artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor (144×256×20), 20 PNGs, inspection.png and the standalone index.html. A script asserts every PNG equals its tensor slice and frame 00 equals first_frame.png. transition_check.png is a code-built contact sheet (frames 0/3/5/6/7/12/16/19, amplified consecutive-frame differences, and the analytic trajectory overlay).

Numeric verification results

  • 20/20 PNGs match the tensor exactly; frame 00 == first_frame.png.
  • Colour-mask centroids match analytic positions within 0.7 px for every frame; contact gap at frame 6 = 14.00 px; blue/yellow mask overlap = 0 px.
  • Rebound symmetry: frames t and 12−t are byte-identical for t = 1..5 (same positions and rolling arc).
  • Roll: changing arc 0 → +5 px at fixed centre changes disk pixels by mean 5.7 (blue) / 4.1 (yellow); angular-rate sign flips at contact.
  • Pipes identical in all frames; every changed pixel vs the plate lies inside a ball/shadow footprint (0 stray px/frame).
  • End positions (t = 19): blue (41.1, 45.8), yellow (184.6, 62.6) — both fully on the table, still heading toward their pipes.

Honest limitations

  • The inward dark blobs in frame 0 are treated as shadows rigidly attached to each ball (their measured position suggests this); they move with the balls rather than staying fixed on the table.
  • The sphere model uses an affine + limb-darkening lighting fit and a mirrored back hemisphere; frame-0 disk reconstruction is not pixel-perfect (p95 error ~18–21 levels), so frame 12 (same pose rendered by the model) differs from frame 0 inside the disks by ~1–3 levels on average while all geometry matches.
