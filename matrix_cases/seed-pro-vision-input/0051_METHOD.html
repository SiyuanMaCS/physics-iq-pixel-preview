METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0051

Continuation of first_frame.png: the wooden stick on the black rotating platform sweeps clockwise (viewed from above), knocks the first domino (B4, already tilted 12° in the input), and the cascade runs through the 3-tile near row (B4 → W3 → B3). B3 falls into the gap and lands short, so the 4-tile far row (B1 W1 B2 W2) never moves. 20 frames, 256×144, 4 fps.

Everything was produced with local Python (numpy + PIL) pixel operations in generate.py. No image viewing/reading tool was used on any generated frame; all quality checks were numeric (pixel masks, color statistics, coordinate measurements), as required. Frame 00 is the untouched input image, asserted pixel-identical after writing.

1. Camera and tile model (analytic weak perspective)

Measured from frame 0 (numbers and segmentation scripts in .tmp/, summary in OBSERVATION.md):

  • chain axis f̂ = (0.971, 0.241) (+f toward camera/platform);
  • tile width axis û = (0.919, −0.394); vertical maps straight up;
  • height/width HR = 2.60, thickness/width TH = 0.28;
  • per-tile local scale s (projected width): B3 7.62, W3 8.90, B4 9.10;
  • base centers P: B3 (133.5, 77.5), W3 (148.0, 81.0), B4 (155.7, 84.6).

A tile at tip angle θ maps local face coords (u ∈ [−½,½], v ∈ [0, HR]) to

screen = P + u·s·û + v·(−sinθ·s·f̂ + (0, −cosθ·s)).

Each tile is drawn as three parallelograms in painter order: broad face, +f thickness side, top cap. Quads are rasterized per frame with analytic 2×2 subpixel coverage (draw_parallel).

Face texture. Face pixels are sampled from the tile's own upright face in frame 0, bilinearly warped through the inverse of the model above: the falling tile literally takes its real pixels with it. A per-pixel validity map (frame-0 pixels inside the upright face matching the tile color: dark for black tiles, blue-white for white) blends real texture with a sampled flat fallback color — black face (23.5, 23, 20), white a top→bottom gradient (162,169,195)→(157,163,189) — wherever the warp reads a fringe/occluded source. Deterministic per-pixel hash jitter (~1–2 levels) keeps the fallback from looking posterized. Side/top quads use flat sampled colors (side (24,23,25) / (146,154,178); top (45,48,52) / (163,177,206) with a rim-light gradient) plus small hash noise. A tight elliptic contact shadow (α ≤ 0.40·exp(−3.2 r²)) is multiplied into the ground under each moving tile, centered slightly toward its pivot.

2. Background plate

A single static plate is built once:

  1. Remove the three near-row tiles. The removal footprint is the union of the same three quads the renderer draws at each tile's frame-0 pose (B4 at 12°), dilated by 2 px — so the hole always contains every pixel a redraw can touch. (An earlier convex-hull rasterizer silently produced an empty mask because of polygon winding; the union-of-quads version is winding-consistent and is verified numerically.)
  2. Remove the stick and the green tape (segment/quad masks, tape dilated 2 px) and a small dark contact-shadow patch below B4.
  3. Fill holes on the wooden table by nearest clean source pixel using an 8-neighbour chamfer distance transform (weights 3 orthogonally, 4 diagonally, forward/back sweeps), copying the source color. Sources are restricted to warm wood-colored pixels below the horizon line, and rod tan / tape green pixels are excluded, so wall, tile rims and fringe can never be copied into a hole. Holes over the platform silhouette are filled separately from clean platform pixels.
  4. Feather a 1 px seam band with a 3×3 average, and add deterministic per-pixel grain (σ = 3) over the filled table holes so the nearest-pixel fill keeps the grain amplitude of the surrounding wood (measured dy/dx neighbor differences match frame-0 wood statistics).

The far row, platform body, wall and all other pixels stay exactly as in frame 0.

3. Physics

Each falling tile is integrated independently as an undamped inverted pendulum from its base:

θ̈ = K·sinθ,  K = 6 rad/s²,  RK-style Euler with dt = 10⁻⁴ s,

clamped at θ = 89.6° (lying). Initial conditions:

  • B4 starts at θ₀ = 12°, ω₀ = 0.35 rad/s at t = 0 (it is mid-knock in the input);
  • W3 starts at t = 0.10 s with ω₀ = 0.60 (pushed by B4);
  • B3 starts the instant W3 reaches the measured 18° contact angle (t ≈ 0.54 s, found by dense integration), with ω₀ = 0.50.

Resulting angles are printed by the script (table also appended to OBSERVATION.md): B4 flat by f04, W3 flat by f05, B3 flat by f07; all static thereafter. In the final frames the nearer fallen tiles visibly overlap the farther ones (B4 on W3 on B3), which is the correct stacking for a chain falling toward −f; painter order handles the occlusion.

Gap outcome (measured, not assumed). W2→B3 pitch is 4.27 w while B3's maximum reach is HR + TH/2 ≈ 2.74 w, so it must land ~1.1 w short. In the final frames the flat B3's leftmost dark pixel is x = 111 and W2's rightmost white pixel is x = 107: an untouched ~4 px corridor. A numeric check confirms zero dark domino-colored pixels in that corridor band in every frame f07–f19.

Stick. The stick rotates about the platform axis PIVOT = (223, 55) in the same ground basis used for tiles (plan scale 13.5), at constant motor speed ψ̇ = 10°/s, ψ = 0°→47.5°. Its frame-0 endpoint (149.5, 65.0) fixes the initial plan coordinates; increasing ψ rotates it from the û direction toward +f̂, which at this pose initially sweeps the tip up-left (continuing to push the 12°-leaning B4) and then along the circular arc up and right — the numerically tracked rod bearing advances monotonically every frame. It is rendered as a distance-field capsule (edge (131,119,89), core (185,173,134), offset highlight (201,191,154)), with the green tape (81,141,83) drawn as a 7.6×2.9 quad at fraction 0.159 from the pivot, rotating with it.

Static-tile guarantee. A tile whose integrated angle is exactly 0 and whose frame-0 pose is upright (B3 at f01–f02) is not re-rendered at all: the frame-0 pixels inside its removal hull are copied back verbatim after the plate is built. Numeric verification: at f01 zero pixels differ inside B3's face or hull; at f02 the face is still pixel-identical and the only hull differences (33 px) are W3's white top cap touching B3's 2-px dilated side fringe at the instant of contact (16°, just before the scripted 18° transfer) — B3 itself remains untouched until f03.
