METHOD — case_0020

What happens

The black pipe on the left points at a cardboard ramp propped on a blue block. A yellow tennis ball rolls straight out of the pipe onto the tabletop (frames 01–07), meets the ramp and climbs it (08–12), stops at the apex near x ≈ 180, y ≈ 41 (12), rolls back down (13–16), exits onto the table moving left (17) and slows to a near stop around x ≈ 129 (18–19). The camera, table, pipe, ramp and block never move.

How it was made

Only local pixel operations were used: numpy/PIL/scipy/OpenCV on first_frame.png. No external models, engines, references or image-viewing tools — all scene analysis was numeric (color thresholds, connected components, per-row edge scans). Steps, in order:

  1. Measure (OBSERVATION.md): ball centroid (86.3, 51.5), radius ≈ 9.3 px, felt color; cast-shadow offset (+13, 0); ramp near-edge line tip (154, 76) → (245, 40), i.e. direction (0.931, −0.365), about 21° uphill; pipe axis y ≈ 51; blue block x 209–255, y 40–89.
  2. Background reconstruction. A removal mask covers the original ball, its f0 cast and contact shadows, and the faint felt fringe (per-channel thresholds on the yellow-green felt score and on darkness relative to a per-row linear table model, plus a small dilation). Inside the mask, the table is reconstructed with OpenCV Telea inpainting (radius 3), which propagates the surrounding wood gradients and the static pipe cast-shadow continuously. Telea output is smooth, so band-pass wood grain sampled from a clean foreground patch (y 96–123) is added back inside the mask with a feathered envelope.
  3. Sprite. The ball RGB is cut from frame 00 (27×27 crop) with a geometric disc alpha: opaque inside r = 9.3, a 1.6 px anti-aliased rim. It is stored premultiplied and placed each frame with a single bilinear sub-pixel translation, so there is no double-resampling blur and no black fringe. The real ball shows no discernible markings at this resolution (uniform felt with a fixed diffuse highlight), so the sprite is not texture-rotated; rolling is conveyed by the physically shaped path.
  4. Shadow. One anisotropic Gaussian darkening per frame, offset down-light (light comes from the left in the source): (+11.5, +1) with σ (7.5, 3.8) on the table and (+9, +3.5) with σ (6.5, 3.5) on the cardboard; the two are blended at the contact and exit frames.
  5. Path. Hand-tuned keyframe centres (table in OBSERVATION.md) following constant-speed pipe exit (~9.1 px/frame), gravity-shaped deceleration up the 21° slope (steps 8.5 → 8 → 7 → 5 → 3), reversal at the apex, accelerating descent and frictional slow-down on the table. Centre y follows the measured ramp line.
  6. Outputs via artifact_io.save: pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor), 20 PNGs, inspection.png, index.html; transition_check.png is written by generate.py. Frame 00 is then overwritten with the original first_frame.png, so it is byte-identical to the provided input.
