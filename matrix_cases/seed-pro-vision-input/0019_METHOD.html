Later frames were inspected exclusively through code: masks, centroids, colour statistics, per-frame diffs and ASCII maps printed from numeric arrays.

Pipeline (generate.py)

  1. Measurements. Geometry and colours were measured numerically from first_frame.png (see OBSERVATION.md): ball centroid (103.7, 72.3), pipe mouth (11, 113), ramp quadrilateral with hinge edge (119, 28)→(182, 66), ball palette, shadow lobes and key-light direction.
  2. Clean background plate. Only the frame-0 ball/fringe and its two measured soft shadow lobes are removed with cv2.inpaint (Telea, radius 5). The inpainted zone receives synthesised horizontal wood-grain noise (filtered Gaussian noise, σ 3.2–4.0 per channel) blended by a Gaussian feather of the inpaint mask, because Telea alone leaves the table grain over-smoothed. All other pixels are untouched.
  3. Ball motion. A single straight screen-space track along d = (0.911, −0.414) (pipe→ball→ramp ray). Constant 43.5 px/s on the table to the hinge crossing at t = 1.25 s; constant −12.0 px/s² deceleration on the incline afterwards. The ball ends at (225.6, 16.5) with speed ≈1.5 px/s, fully inside the ramp quad and clear of the blue block (verified numerically).
  4. Ball stamp (analytic). A fuzzy disc: empirical radial palette measured from the frame-0 ball (core (178,227,66) → mid (164,206,63) → rim (144,179,60)), a screen-fixed soft bluish highlight on the right limb, darkening on the left limb, and a desaturated seam crescent at the lower-right rim that rotates with φ = arc/R (clockwise roll). The rim carries deterministic hash-based fuzz; a soft green defringe halo surrounds it. A short box-shutter smear (six faint trailing discs, ~3.3 px at full speed) approximates the motion blur already visible in frame 00 without softening the crisp disc. Parameters were calibrated by minimising the colour error of a stamp placed at the frame-0 position (final mean |ΔRGB| ≈ 19 over the ball footprint).
  5. Shadows (analytic, multiply blend). On the table: two Gaussian lobes fitted to the measured frame-0 shadow (lower-left tail, axes 18×7 at 170°, amplitude 0.27; compact upper lobe, 8×6, amplitude 0.16). On the incline: a compact 6.6×3 lobe just up-left of the ball. The two shadow models crossfade over frames 04→06 around the hinge contact.
  6. Static-scenery protection. A mask of the blue block (and the dark top-right corner, 1 px dilated) is restored from the untouched plate after compositing, so the ball can never repaint scenery it never reaches.
  7. Outputs. Frame 00 is a verbatim copy of first_frame.png. All frames go through the provided artifact_io.save (uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, PNGs, inspection sheet, player). A separate transition_check.png is written: trajectory overlay on frame 00, ×3 zooms of frames 04/05/06 at the hinge, and amplified per-pixel diff heat-maps for the 03→07 transitions.

Verification performed

  • frames/00.png is byte-exact to first_frame.png; all 20 PNGs decode to exactly the packed uint32 tensor in pixels.npz.
  • Ball centroid per frame follows the planned arc (constant-speed table segment, monotonic deceleration on the ramp); every ball pixel from frame 06 onward lies inside the measured ramp quadrilateral.
  • The blue block, couch, pipe and dark corner are pixel-identical to frame 00 in every generated frame.
  • Frame-to-frame changed-pixel counts stay localised to the ball/shadow track and shrink with speed (≈1090 changed px at full speed → ≈210 at the near-stop), consistent with a locked-off camera and one moving object.
  • Rendered shadow at the frame-0 position matches the measured frame-0 shadow within ~2–4 % luminance ratio at lobe cores after calibration.

Honest limitations

  • The background behind the moving ball is a single static plate; the ball never moves back over ground it previously occupied, so no temporal inpainting artifacts arise.
  • Surface contact at the hinge is geometric (straight screen track through the measured crossing point) rather than a full 3-D rigid-body simulation; it is numerically consistent with the 2-D image geometry.
  • The ball sprite is analytic, not a copy of the frame-0 ball; residual per-pixel colour error against the real sprite remains (mean ≈19/255 in |ΔRGB| over the footprint), concentrated in the fuzzy rim and seam.
