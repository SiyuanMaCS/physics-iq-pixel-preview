METHOD.md

METHOD

Fully local 2-D pixel synthesis. No external models, engines or reference material; everything derives from first_frame.png (256×144).

1. Scene measurement (numpy/PIL/scipy, no visual inspection tools)

  • Dominant-colour segmentation + connected components located the black platform, the eight dominoes and the rubber duck; per-column/per-row luminance profiles gave each domino's base centre and upright face box (see OBSERVATION.md).
  • White domino faces are lavender-tinted under the scene lighting (≈(165,152,170)); black faces ≈ (15,10,9). The exposed stick segment is tan (172,148,124). Fitting its visible segment against the black holder gave pivot (190,27), initial angle 52°, radial extent 10.5–17.5 px.
  • The screen-space fall direction (left, slightly toward the viewer) is u = (−0.98, +0.19); the duck's topple pivot was set at (130,85).

2. Background plate

A mask of all objects that ever move (8 enlarged domino polygons with base-shadow ellipses, the dilated duck component with belly shadow, and the stick segment) is removed from frame 0 with OpenCV Telea inpainting (radius 4 px). Telea was chosen over nearest-pixel fill because the upper dominoes overlap the platform's dark front edge; it blends that edge correctly. The large duck hole receives extra deterministic, vertically streaked Gaussian grain (seeded, σ≈4, blurred on a 1×7 kernel) to match the table's front-to-back grain. The resulting plate was checked numerically (zone means vs. a ring of real table pixels; no residual saturated yellow pixels).

3. Moving-object assets

  • Dominoes: the exact upright face strip of each domino is cut from frame 0. A binary alpha mask is built from the face colour rule (lum/neutral test for black faces; lavender test for white faces), closed, hole-filled, restricted to the component crossing the strip centre, dilated 1 px and feathered (Gaussian σ 0.6) — so background wood inside each cut box never warps with the slab.
  • Duck: the largest warm connected component (yellow body, orange bill, dark eye) in an ROI, holes filled, dilated 3 px and feathered, cut as an RGBA sprite (53×58 px).

4. Per-frame animation

Tilt keyframes (generate.py:KF) implement the event chain: stick 52°→98° (clockwise on screen), D8 knocked at 0.5 s, then D7, D6, D5; D5 reaches the duck at ≈1.25 s; the duck tips 8°→90° between 1.5 s and 3.0 s; D4 starts moving as the duck's back reaches it (duck at 55°), and the cascade continues D4→D3→D2→D1, with everything at rest by 4.5 s.

Each tipping slab is a rigid rotation about its bottom edge projected along u (slight width contraction and 0.10·sinθ darkening while the face turns from the key light), rendered as: (a) a soft elliptical contact shadow at the base plus a fall-direction cast streak, (b) a dark thickness cap quad, (c) the perspective-warped textured face. The duck is its sprite rotated rigidly about (130,85) with a translating soft shadow. The stick is a dark inner segment plus a tan outer segment.

Only footprints of objects moving in that frame are replaced by the background plate; static objects retain their exact frame-0 pixels and are restored after drawing, so a mover never erases or double-draws an object that is still upright. Shadows are composited in a first far-to-near pass, bodies in a second.

5. Verification (numeric only)

  • frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png.
  • pixels.npz packs 0xRRGGBB into a (144,256,20) uint32 tensor; verified equal to the 20 saved PNGs.
  • The base64 delta stream embedded in index.html was independently re-decoded and compared frame-by-frame against the PNGs — exact match.
  • Per-frame changed-pixel counts/bboxes stay localised to the active part of the chain (140 px for the first stick-only frame; frames 18–19 identical once everything is at rest).
  • Yellow duck pixels track continuously from x≈123–165 (upright) to x≈81–129 (lying toward D4) with constant mass; no yellow residue remains in the cleared original position.
  • Intermediate geometry was checked with ASCII luminance maps and colour dumps (no image Read/View tools were used).

Known limitations

The result is a stylised 2.5-D composite rather than a true physics simulation: tilt timings are hand-tuned keyframes, the duck is a rigid sprite rotation (no body deformation), and the inpainted table inside the duck hole is synthetic grain rather than recovered real texture.
