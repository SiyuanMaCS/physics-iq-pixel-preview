METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0027

How the 20 frames were actually made. Everything is local Python pixel math (numpy / scipy.optimize / PIL) on first_frame.png; no external models, engines, network, or other cases were used. All generation is deterministic (fixed seeds). Frame 00 is a byte-for-byte copy of first_frame.png; frames 01–19 are produced by re-compositing a fitted analytic ball, its shadow, and a faint reflection over an inpainted static plate.

Outputs: generate.py, pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB, shape (144,256,20)), frames/00.png–19.png (256×144 RGB, 4 fps / 5 s), inspection.png (key-frame sheet written by artifact_io.save), transition_check.png (8 frames spanning the wood→glass crossing), index.html (self-contained delta player).

1. Geometry measured from frame 0

  • Ball silhouette: centre c0 = (81.51, 57.44), radius RAD = 9.69 px.
  • Table top right edge x = 146.0; camera elevation above the table plane CAM_ELEV = 0.32 rad.
  • The ball rolls on a straight level screen line (cy = 57.44 constant) with mild deceleration: cx(t) = 81.51 + 8.10·t − 0.025·t(t−1), t = 0..19 (89.61 at frame 1 → 226.86 at frame 19; ≈ 8.1→7.2 px/step).
  • Rolling angle φ = (cx − 81.51)/RAD about the view-projected depth axis ROLL_AXIS = (0, cos 0.32, −sin 0.32), sign chosen so the top surface moves rightward; total ≈ 15 rad ≈ 2.4 turns.

2. Shading model fitted to the frame-0 ball

View-space surface normals of an orthographic sphere are n = (u, v, sqrt(1−u²−v²)), u=(x−cx)/RAD, v=(y−cy)/RAD. Intensity:

I(n) = (amb + dif·max(0,l·n)^γ) · (1 − ao·max(0,n_y)²) + rim·(1−n_z)³·max(0,l·n)

(ambient + Lambertian with gamma, bottom contact ambient occlusion, grazing fuzz rim). Eight parameters (lx,ly,lz,amb,dif,γ,ao,rim) and a per-channel felt albedo A are fitted by scipy.least_squares on plain-felt pixels (u²+v²<0.72, angularly away from the seam curve). Residuals use a Tukey biweight (1.48·MAD scale) so the seam/fuzz outliers do not pull the fit; 97 multi-starts, ranked by plain-felt RMSE with feasibility guards. Shading and seam orientation are refitted alternately twice. Deterministic result:

p = (−0.277, −0.123, 0.680, 0.617, 0.567, 1.387, 0.305, 0.182), A = (32.7, 87.1, 163.2).

3. Tennis-seam orientation

The seam is the standard tennis curve q(t)=(cos t, sin t cos t, sin t sin t). A rotation matrix Rsm aligns it to the visible seam: pixels whose shading-normalised colour deviates > 10% are weighted by |deviation|, and the rotation vector is fitted (48 multi-starts) to put the curve through them, minimising angular chord distance × weight. Result:

Rsm = [[0.820,−0.475,0.319],[0.401,0.875,0.273],[−0.409,−0.095,0.908]].

4. Sphere texture (empirical, completed)

A 120×240 equirectangular modulation texture F (colour relative to A·I), cell-centred, y-polar (lat=asin(y), lon=atan2(z,x)):

  1. Every frame-0 ball pixel is back-projected to a sphere direction; the observed modulation img/(A·I) is bilinearly splatted into the texels with a coverage weight that is full well inside the disc and fades out by n_z=0.05–0.35 and ρ=0.84–0.96.
  2. The back hemisphere is filled using the seam's 2-fold symmetry (x,y,z)→(x,−y,−z), which maps unseen surface onto observed surface while preserving the seam.
  3. Any texels still uncovered use a procedural fallback: a light seam band (gaussian centre, σ=0.085 rad) plus a dark groove at angular distance ≈ 0.17 rad, coloured from the observed seam, plus two-octave trilinear value noise per channel. The fallback is multiplicatively normalised so its area-weighted per-channel mean equals the empirical-front mean (the ball cannot brighten/darken when rolling), then blended by coverage, clipped, and given a σ≈1.1-cell cos(lat)-weighted gaussian smooth.

Sampling is periodic bilinear in longitude (convention matches the build grid exactly — a class of sign/offset bugs was fixed during development).

5. Grazing-rim correction map

The analytic intensity misses the felt/fuzz scattering tint at grazing angles. Because camera and light are fixed, this residual is pose-independent in view space: observed − A·F·I is binned in (ρ, azimuth) over the dense limb (6 ρ bins over 0.74–1.02 × 30 azimuth bins, ρ 0.72–0.97 trusted), each bin taking a robust mean after a joint-channel 3-MAD outlier reject, forward-filled in ρ and periodic-interpolated in azimuth, periodically gaussian-smoothed (σ≈1.4 bins azimuth, [1,2,1]/4 in ρ), tapered to zero by ρ≈0.80–0.94, and clipped to ±30 levels. It is bilinearly added inside the disc only.

6. Fuzz fringe and frame-0 joint fit

The silhouette extends to ρ = 1.272 (FRINGE_OUTER). Coverage ramps linearly over ρ=0.99–1.272; inside the limb the dense colour eases from ρ=0.92 toward a fitted fuzz colour with strength 0.778: FRINGE_COL = (112.6, 79.4, 106.9). Fringe parameters and the shadow parameters below were jointly tuned against frame 0 (numerical minimisation of per-pixel error in .tmp/fit_ring2.py), after the three shading/texture convention bugs were fixed.

7. Static plate (wood inpaint)

The static scene is first_frame.png with the frame-0 ball, fuzz fringe (disk r = 1.24·RAD) and its contact shadow removed:

  • Wood pixels are classified by colour/range thresholds; a 2D polynomial surface (terms up to x·y², 8 coefficients) is least-squares fitted to them, giving the smooth wood colour field.
  • Grain = img − base (matches the measured horizontal streak structure); hole pixels (rows 43–77) are filled with base + grain copied from the same row by mirroring across the left/right hole boundaries (weighted by distance to the far edge), with a far-left shift fallback. The hole mask is dilated by 1 px so nothing of the original fringe remains.
  • Every pixel outside the hole stays byte-identical to the input. The glass, wall, leg and stool regions are never repainted.

8. Shadow and reflection

The frame-0 contact shadow is two multiplicative gaussians on the background, jointly fitted to frame 0 (offsets from the ball centre):

  • long soft cast shadow: (dx,dy)=(21.31, 3.04), σ=(7.78, 2.41), a=0.335
  • broad contact crescent: (dx,dy)=(4.85, 7.17), σ=(6.08, 2.64), a=0.568

For each frame they translate with the ball; strength is ×0.80 on pixels outboard of the table edge (x>147), approximating light passing around the transparent sheet. Once the ball is over the glass (cx>150) a very faint elliptical blue reflection (α up to 0.055, σ=(7, 2.6), centred 1 px left and 9.5 px below the ball, brightness-adapted) is composited below it.

9. Per-frame compositing

For frames 1–19, in a bbox around the ball:

  1. shadow multiply, then faint reflection, on the float plate;
  2. 5×5 supersampled sphere samples: fringe-extended normals are inverse-rolled (n₀ = n·Rᵀ about ROLL_AXIS by −φ), mapped into the texture frame (s = n₀·Rsm), textured (sample_texture), shaded, rim-corrected in view space, and fringe-colour mixed;
  3. samples are coverage-averaged and alpha-composited over the background;
  4. bytes are written with round-to-nearest (floor(out+0.5)), and shadow/reflection contributions that cannot move any channel by ≥1 rounded level are snapped off exactly. This quantisation guarantee, plus exact identity for zero-coverage samples, means every pixel outside the true ball/shadow/reflection footprint is bit-identical across all frames.

Frame 0 is appended directly from the decoded input. The packed tensor and delta player are written by the supplied artifact_io.save.

The crossing geometry (glass edges, dark reflection band) is left exactly as captured, and the ball passes over it without explicit refraction.
