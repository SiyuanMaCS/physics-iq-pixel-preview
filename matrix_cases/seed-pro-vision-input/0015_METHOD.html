ball-in-basket · 生成方法

All pixels were produced by local Python (numpy, PIL, scipy.ndimage) analytic rendering and pixel operations. No external models, engines, references or network access were used. Frame 00 is the untouched first_frame.png.

1. Measurements from frame 0 (see OBSERVATION.md)

  • Basketball disc fitted from limb edges: centre (108.9, 39.4), radius 28.2 px (median radius residual ≈ 0.5 px). Features: vertical seam groove at sphere coordinate a ≈ −0.07; two near-white specular window streaks at (a,b) ≈ (−0.72, +0.06) and (+0.25, −0.02); base orange ≈ (190, 73, 2).
  • Crate front face x56–153, y59–133: solid rim band (y59–66), handle slot (≈x87–113, y66–75), diamond lattice (y76–130) whose enclosed holes show the background, solid base. The rear rim edge slopes in perspective (y ≈ 58.5 − 0.056·(x−76)).
  • Dim orange visible deep in the handle slot shows the ball tip is already at the crate mouth at release.

2. Static background reconstruction (clean)

Because the camera is locked off, every later frame is a fixed background plus the moving ball. The background is reconstructed from frame 0 itself:

  1. Wall — every pixel flagged as ball/halo (fitted disc dilated by 7 px, plus warm-pixel mask) above y57 is replaced by a per-row least-squares linear-in-x fit through the remaining wall-like anchor columns (lum>112, neutral colour), so the soft horizontal wall gradient is preserved. Dark ceiling band and dark plastic pixels are excluded from anchors and left untouched. Verified: repaired wall pixels agree with same-row neighbourhood medians to ≲11/255 (p95).
  2. Rim band — ball/warm pixels over the crate lip (y55–66, x82–152, split along the measured slanted rear edge) are repainted from solid dark plastic sampled directly below each pixel (front face lum ≈ 5–10), restoring an intact rim.
  3. Handle cavity — orange seen through the slot is replaced by vertically interpolated cavity shadow from the dark rows above/below, with a final sweep catching residual warm pixels.
  4. Plastic network / openings — thresholding the reconstructed panel (lum < 22) and 8-connected flood filling from the top/bottom rim bands yields the exact plastic-bar mask; its enclosed complement gives the diamond holes. The handle aperture is an ellipse (rx 12.8, ry 4.1 centred (100, 70.3)) traced from the frame-0 handle map. Pixels outside the opening masks can never change in later frames (verified numerically).

3. Ball rendering

The ball is an analytic sphere. For each output position inside the disc, sphere coordinates a=(x−cx)/r, b=(y−cy)/r, n_z=√(1−a²−b²) are computed and the surface colour comes from a 6-term model

C(a,b) = c0 + c1·n_z + c2·b + c3·b² + c4·a + c5·a²

fitted per channel (ridge regression, λ=8) to ≈1700 clean disc pixels of frame 0 after masking the seam and specular streaks (fit MAE ≈ 2.4/2.4/3.7 per channel). Added on top:

  • the dark seam groove (Gaussian in a, fading toward the lower hemisphere);
  • the two white specular streaks (2-D Gaussians blended toward (254,242,233));
  • 1 px anti-aliased limb coverage.

Below the rim the ball is only painted through the measured opening masks, composited over the background with the coverage alpha, and dimmed to match the frame-0 evidence of the shadowed interior: ≈×(0.60, 0.46, 0.40) through the handle (slightly lighter toward the slot top) and ≈×(0.44, 0.32, 0.26) through the diamond lattice. The opaque plastic mask always composites over the ball.

4. Physics and exposure

  • Release from rest at t=0; gravity g = 530 px/s² (diameter 56 px ≈ 0.24 m ⇒ ≈ 9.81 m/s²); no horizontal motion.
  • Piecewise parabolic phases with instantaneous impacts on the crate floor (ball-centre rest height cy = 98, ball bottom ≈ y126), restitution e = 0.55: impacts at t ≈ 0.47, 0.99, 1.27, 1.43, 1.52 s, then settled; a ≤8 % vertical squash/widen is applied in the 55 ms around each contact.
  • Each 4 fps output frame integrates 96 substep composites over a centred 0.10 s exposure, producing the vertical motion smear (fast fall around the first impact, shrinking blur on the smaller bounces). Frames 8–19 are the static settled scene.

5. Verification performed (numeric only)

  • frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png.
  • Every PNG matches the packed 0xRRGGBB uint32 tensor in pixels.npz (shape 144×256×20).
  • Plastic/background pixels outside the handle/lattice opening masks never change vs the reconstructed background in frames 1–19; frames 8–19 are pixel-identical.
  • Orange visible inside the crate on each frame lies within the ball disc swept over that frame's exposure; the ball clears the rim correctly and re-emerges above it only at the first bounce apex.
  • inspection.png and transition_check.png generated; index.html is the standalone delta player from artifact_io.save.

Honest limitations

  • The lower third of the crate front is nearly black in frame 0, so holes there cannot be distinguished from plastic; the settled ball is therefore visible only through the handle and the upper diamond lattice (its lower half stays hidden in the dark interior), which is consistent with the source evidence.
  • Ball shading is a fitted smooth analytic model, not ray tracing; interior dimming factors are estimated from the small frame-0 slot sample.
