METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0022 (vision input)

What happens on screen

The grey tennis ball, already visible mid-table in frame 0, rolls left → right across the light wood coffee table at constant depth and constant speed (4.2 px/frame ≈ 18 px/s), its soft contact shadow moving with it. It reaches the table's diagonal right end at frame 19, beginning to overhang the edge (the sphere occludes the dark dresser behind it; the shadow is clipped to the tabletop). Nothing else moves: couch, pipe, dresser, camera all locked. Frame 00 is byte-identical to first_frame.png.

Honest method statement

No rendering engine, AI model or external asset is used. Everything is local Python pixel math (NumPy + Pillow) on first_frame.png, per GENERATION_RULES.md.

  1. Wood background model. For each table row y = 58–99 a line is fit to each RGB channel from two clean wood windows (x 55–125 and x 185–206, which avoid ball, shadow, pipe and dresser). This gives a smooth per-row estimate of the unoccluded wood, including its real lighting gradient.
  2. Ball extraction. The ball is cool/bluish (B−R up to +35) while wood is warm-neutral. An alpha matte is the maximum of (a) chroma evidence (B−R) − expectedWoodB−R and (b) a geometric anti-aliased disk centred on the measured centre (162.0, 80.0), radius ≈ 8.6 px — the geometric term guarantees an opaque sphere interior (chroma alone went transparent at the lit top). Gaussian 0.8 px feather matches the frame's soft focus.
  3. Shadow extraction. From the background model, per-pixel darkening s = (L_bg − L_obs)/(0.3·L_bg) is taken inside a measured ellipse (centre ≈ (155, 83.5), axes ≈ 24 × 7.6, elongated toward the light-opposite/left side) and excluded under the ball. Per-channel gain obs/bg is stored so the shadow keeps its observed warm tint (R ≈ 0.90 / G ≈ 0.85 / B ≈ 0.80 at mid strength).
  4. Clean table inpaint. One clean background frame is built: over the object+shadow support (≈ x 133–179, y 72–90) a same-row wood strip from 46 px to the left is cloned in, with a per-row linear brightness correction between the two seam columns and a feathered (≈2 px) blend. Measured seam jumps ≈ 1.1–1.4 levels vs 0.7–0.9 normal wood; no visible seam and no cool ghost in the vacated area.
  5. Layer unmixing. The observed pixels are decomposed into a shadowed-wood layer wood·mult (per-channel multiplier map) and a solid ball colour layer solid (unmixed through the alpha against shadowed wood), clamped to valid ranges.
  6. Animation. For frames 1–19 both layers are shifted horizontally by 4.2·t px using bilinear interpolation from the original layers (no cumulative re-warp, so no progressive softening). The ball is alpha-composited over the shadowed clean table. The shadow gain is applied as a zero-baseline field and masked with a 2 px-feathered wood mask along the fitted table-end edge x = 207 + 2.4(y − 65), so the shadow never darkens the dresser past the edge while the ball itself legitimately occludes it on overhang.
  7. Output. artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor pixels.npz, frames/00–19.png, inspection.png and the delta-encoded standalone index.html. transition_check.png (20 tracked ball crops with per-frame |Δ| heatmaps) is written by generate.py.

Numeric verification results (all from local code; no later-frame image viewing)

  • Every frames/NN.png equals its pixels.npz slice packed as 0xRRGGBB (0 mismatches × 20).
  • frames/00.png is exactly first_frame.png; the index.html first+delta stream reconstructs all 20 frames exactly.
  • Ball centre follows x = 162.0 + 4.2·t within ≤ 0.4 px and stays at y = 80 ± 0.6 px (disk-constrained cool-chroma centroids); per-frame displacement 3.4–5.0 px, mean ≈ 4.2.
  • Ball core colour is translation-invariant (e.g. odd-phase frames identical to frame-0 core ≈ (108, 124, 149)); contact-shadow absolute luma stays 93–96 at every frame.
  • Couch, pipe, left table and bottom strip are byte-identical to frame 0 in all 20 frames.
  • Changed-pixel envelope per transition hugs the ball (y 72–89, x window moving 4.2 px/step, ≈ 230–270 px); no stray flicker.
  • Vacated region at frame 19 matches neighbouring wood (mean RGB (145.3, 145.0, 141.3) vs (145.1, 142.3, 137.8), grain-level std).
  • Dresser pixels beyond the table end are untouched except for the exact ball silhouette where the sphere overhangs the edge at frames 17–19 (physically correct occlusion); the shadow never crosses the edge.

Known approximations

  • The sphere carries no surface markings/felt fuzz (none are resolvable in the 256×144 source); rolling is conveyed purely by translation at constant depth.
  • The inpainted strip reuses same-row wood grain shifted by 46 px with only a brightness ramp — at this resolution and grain amplitude (±3–5 levels) seams are below the source's own grain.
  • Shadow tint is the measured single sample-average warm tint rather than a full light transport model.
