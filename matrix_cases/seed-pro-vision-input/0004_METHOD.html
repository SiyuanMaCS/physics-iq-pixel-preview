ball-behind-rotating-paper · 生成方法

Task: continue first_frame.png for 20 frames (256×144, 4 fps, 5 s) showing the blue-black grabber lowering an orange tennis ball behind a clockwise- rotating folded cardstock tent, then releasing it on the black platform. Frame 00 is a byte-exact copy of the input. Everything below was produced with local Python (numpy + Pillow) pixel operations and analytic geometry; no external models, engines, masks, or network access were used. Later frames were checked only by local numeric code (.tmp/verify.py and ASCII/statistic helpers), never by an image viewing tool.

1. Reconstructing the scene from frame 0

Measurements (channel statistics, colour-classifier masks, ASCII density maps) and a non-linear least-squares pinhole fit of the card/platform features give the camera and rigid geometry (fit RMSE 2.69 px):

  • pinhole camera, unit view distance, elevation β=1.1568 rad, focal f=480.9 px, principal point (146.75, 90.16); per-pixel unit rays are built from the same model for every frame (locked-off shot);
  • platform: cylinder of radius R=0.0896, height h=0.20, top at Y=0, axis through the origin;
  • cardstock tent: ridge base P=(0.0067,0,−0.0134), panel half-width L=0.1305, fold half-depth d=0.0848, ridge height hr=0.1531, initial yaw θ0=0.0512 rad. The two panels are four triangles between ridge endpoints and the front/back hem endpoints. Yaw only changes with time: θ(t)=θ0−(115°)·t/19 (clockwise in image space ⇒ θ decreases).

These constants are stored in .tmp/calib.npy and are the single source of geometry for all later steps.

2. Static clean plate

Frames 1–19 start from a "plate" with the frame-0 card removed:

  1. Rasterise the t0 card triangles (2× supersample) and dilate the mask (MaxFilter 5/7); union it with neutral card-like pixels (luminance 115–200, low inter-channel spread) inside the dilated neighbourhood. The grabber silhouette mask is always excluded.
  2. Pixels inside the analytically projected top disk are filled with near uniform (6,5,3) + σ≈0.8 grain, plus a soft squared catch-light band near the fitted far/near rim ellipses (verified: 4721 filled px, mean (5.55,4.55,2.54)).
  3. Pixels above the disk (the ridge strip against the wall) are filled from a degree-3 polynomial wall model per channel (fitted on classified wall pixels, σ≈5 residual) + σ≈3.2 grain.

The wood table, cylinder front face, and grabber are untouched frame-0 pixels.

3. Cardstock rendering

For each frame the four panel triangles are intersected with a 512×288 grid of the same camera rays. Triangle membership uses exact affine coordinates in the skew triangle basis: with edge vectors e1,e2 (not orthogonal in general), the hit point is converted via the inverse Gram matrix aa=(a22·h1−a12·h2)/det, bb=(a11·h2−a12·h1)/det; an earlier orthogonal projection here incorrectly covered the whole frame. Nearest ray-hit wins a per-subpixel z-buffer and selects front/back texture coordinates (linear across each triangle).

Texture: the front panel is bilinearly unwrapped from frame 0 at θ0 onto a 320×220 (along-ridge × ridge-to-hem) grid; samples covered by the tool shaft or outside the frame are rebuilt from the per-row median (paper rows have σ<2 in frame 0) plus a mild horizontal gradient, and σ≈1.1 grain is added. The back texture is the same paper tinted ×(0.965,0.96,0.97)+small warm offset, since frame 0 shows the back panel only as an edge-on sliver.

Shading: each panel's analytic unit normal (which rotates rigidly with yaw) is lit by a fixed room light with a constant per-panel term 0.42 + 0.60·max(n·L,0) + 0.06·max(−n·L,0), divided by the same term evaluated for the front normal at θ0, so the re-textured card matches frame 0 exactly at t0 (front factor 1.0; measured f01 vs f00 card patch means 176.6 vs 177.7, quantiles within ~1) and darkens plausibly as it turns. Factors are clipped to [0.40,1.12]. The shading is deliberately not view-ray dependent: the light field is baked into the paper texture, so only the panel/light angle changes.

The 2× colour layer is alpha-premultiplied and bilinearly downsampled to 256×144; coverage cov gives soft edges, while the depth handed to the ball is the block-minimum z of each 2×2 subpixel group (conservative occlusion).

4. Tennis ball

The ball is a world sphere, radius 0.0335, hanging on one fixed vertical line X=−0.0325, Z=−0.0725 (under the back panel at θ0; the resting contact point is 0.0795 from the platform axis vs R=0.0896 — just inside the back rim). Its centre descends in height:

Y = [0.30, .250, .220, .195, .170, .148, .125, .103, .083, .066, .052, .043, .037, .0345] for f00–f13, then 0.0335 (= radius, resting on the disk) for f14–f19.

Each covered pixel ray is intersected analytically with the sphere; the hit is drawn only if its ray distance is smaller than both the card block-min z and the platform z-buffer (top disk plane within the radius plus the cylinder side). Shading: Lambert against the same room light with ambient 0.52 and a 0.14 wrap term over orange base (223,122,27), an orange rim-light (1−(d·n)²)·26, a deterministic per-pixel fuzz jitter, and two pale sinusoidal great-curve seams. The sphere-vs-panel separation along the whole path stays ≥6.7 mm (minimum at f07), so occlusion is always a true behind-the-card pass, never interpenetration. Geometric visible pixels run 252→1045 while held, 57–117 during the f09–f13 hide, and climb 62→410 through f19.

5. Grabber, prongs, compositing order

Per frame: plate → sphere layer (z-tested) → card premultiplied over with coverage → prongs → grabber.

  • Two small dark prongs flank the ball from f01 while it is held, then retract upward (9/20 px, alpha 1.0→0.6→0.25) across f10–f12 and disappear.
  • The grabber itself never changes appearance: its frame-0 pixels under a hand-fitted foreground silhouette are pasted verbatim onto f01–f13. After release it lifts vertically: the same RGBA frame-0 layer is alpha-composited at offsets {f14:8, f15:14, f16:20, f17:26, f18:30, f19:30} px upward; the vacated footprint shows only plate/card content.
  • f00 is written directly from the input PNG bytes.

6. Outputs and verification

generate.py writes, via artifact_io.py: pixels.npz (the 144×256×20 uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor), frames/00.png–19.png, an 8-keyframe inspection.png, a self-contained delta-coded index.html player, and transition_check.png (key frames plus ×4 amplified consecutive-frame differences). METHOD.md (this file) and OBSERVATION.md accompany them.

.tmp/verify.py checks (final run: OVERALL PASS): tensor shape/dtype; every PNG equals its tensor uint32; frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png; grabber pixels are fixed f01–f13 and the lifted layer is exactly the shifted frame-0 tool f14–f19 with the old footprint vacated; orange-classifier counts follow enter → held-large (479–569 px f03–05) → near-full hide (0–5 px f09–13) → monotonic reveal (24→184 f14–19, ≥8× the deepest hide); final orange bbox at the back rim; no orange outside the ball corridor; ≥6 mm sphere/card clearance; one fixed descent world line (dx=dz=0); resting contact inside the disk; 115° CW card sweep; geometric z-buffer visibility windows; inter-frame changed-pixel counts bounded with no frozen middle frames and a moderate lift transition; near-black disk fill.

Known cosmetic residuals, also measured rather than viewed: at f01 the sphere centre still projects above the frame (v=−10.2), so only a grazing sliver of orange reaches the top edge; the turned-away back panel stays quite dark in the last frames; a handful of edge pixels near the card rim differ subtly from frame 0 through coverage blending while the uncovered plate pixels themselves are constant.
