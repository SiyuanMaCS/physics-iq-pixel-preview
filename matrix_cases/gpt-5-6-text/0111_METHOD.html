OBSERVATION.md

Source-frame observations

  • Numeric inspection confirms an RGB uint8 image at 256 x 144. The camera is a fixed, close tabletop view.
  • The upper band is a cool gray/lilac background (common colors around RGB 164/162/173 to 172/170/181). Most of the lower scene is warm brown wood (large clusters near 117/87/59 and 128/94/57). A shallow, slightly rising boundary crosses about y=6 at x=85 and y=29 at x=252.
  • The clear water glass is centered near x=124. Its observable highlight/edge envelope is approximately x=96..151, y=38..119. Strong vertical glass edges occur around x=100..105 and x=144..149; its curved base is around y=102..118. The water/refraction band is near y=58..63.
  • A diagonal match enters from the upper left. Its narrow light wooden shaft follows approximately x-y=74..79 from the top edge to the ignition area. The shaft terminates near x=123, y=45.
  • The small flame is centered around x=123, y=39. Its hottest pixels are x=122..124, y=36..40 (up to roughly RGB 253/233/122), with orange outer pixels around x=118..128, y=35..45. The charred dark head occupies about x=122..130, y=46..53 (the darkest local pixels are roughly RGB 25/19/19).
  • The strongest relevant edge energy is concentrated in the match/flame and glass region x=96..150, y=32..118. The remainder of the shot should stay locked and unchanged apart from faint flame illumination, water ripples, bubbles, and smoke.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  1. Frame 00 is the untouched input exactly.
  2. Frames 01-03: the match begins a smooth down-and-slightly-right lowering motion; the live flame flickers subtly while retaining the source appearance.
  3. Frames 04-07: the match head approaches and passes the glass/water opening. The flame compresses and leans slightly; a weak warm reflection follows on the glass.
  4. Frames 08-10: the burning head reaches the water surface. A small contact ripple forms; the flame rapidly contracts from a yellow-orange tongue to a tiny ember.
  5. Frames 11-13: the visible flame is extinguished. The dark match head continues just below the water line, with two or three small bubbles and expanding elliptical ripples.
  6. Frames 14-16: lowering slows to a stop. The bubbles rise and fade; a thin pale smoke wisp above the contact point curls upward and becomes more transparent.
  7. Frames 17-19: the match remains held in the water. Ripples settle, the ember is fully dark, and the last smoke dissipates. The camera, glass, table, framing, and lighting remain fixed throughout.

Planned synthesis

Use only deterministic local Python/Pillow/OpenCV/Numpy operations. Build a clean plate for the narrow match/flame mask with local texture-aware inpainting, translate the extracted match layer along the measured diagonal, preserve the portion optically behind the glass with a mild water tint/refraction, procedurally render compact flame/ember masks, and add low-amplitude analytic ellipses, bubbles, smoke, and warm light. No image-viewing tool or external model is used.

METHOD.md

Method

The continuation was made entirely with deterministic local pixel operations in generate.py. I did not use an image Read/View tool, an external model, a renderer, another case, or network content. All source understanding came from local Pillow/Numpy/OpenCV code reporting numeric RGB values, connected-component bounds, gradients, and line geometry.

Source measurement

The source was measured as RGB uint8, 256 x 144. Numeric color clustering and edge/line analysis located:

  • the diagonal match shaft at approximately x - y = 74..79;
  • the flame around (123, 39) and dark head around (126, 49);
  • the glass envelope around x=96..151, y=38..119;
  • the water/refraction band around y=59..63.

These measurements and the frame-by-frame action design were recorded in OBSERVATION.md before generate.py was written.

Pixel synthesis

Frame 00 is loaded directly from first_frame.png without processing. For frames 01-19:

  1. Soft analytic masks isolate the narrow diagonal shaft and match head. A slightly expanded mask also covers the original flame.
  2. OpenCV Telea inpainting produces a local clean plate only under that moving object. A Gaussian edge feather blends the filled pixels back into the untouched source.
  3. The original match pixels are moved as a premultiplied-alpha layer with cubic subpixel translation. A hand-tuned easing curve lowers it from 0 to 22 pixels and shifts it about 1.6 pixels right.
  4. Pixels crossing the measured water line are cooled, attenuated, and softly refracted. Bright neutral front-glass highlights from the source are composited back over them.
  5. The live flame is rendered as three nested, anti-aliased analytic lobes. Its contour has deterministic frame-to-frame flicker. It compresses at water contact during frames 08-10 and is absent from frame 11 onward. A small ember fades through frame 12.
  6. Perspective ellipses create small water ripples. Deterministic rising bubbles, a thin fading smoke wisp, and a very localized warm glow complete the extinguishing event.
  7. The camera/background is never transformed. All changed pixels remain inside the measured action area; numeric auditing found no changes outside x=65..160, y=0..125.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNG frames, pixels.npz, the inspection sheet, and the standalone player. generate.py separately writes transition_check.png, whose panels pair selected frames with 4x-amplified adjacent-frame difference maps and changed-pixel counts.

After saving, the script:

  • asserts frames[0] is byte-identical to first_frame.png;
  • reloads every PNG and compares it byte-for-byte with the generated RGB array;
  • repacks every pixel as uint32 0xRRGGBB, transposes it to (144, 256, 20), and compares it exactly with pixels.npz;
  • asserts all frames are RGB 256 x 144 and the tensor has dtype uint32.

Run python generate.py from this directory to reproduce and re-verify every generated artifact.
