+def make_ball_alpha(source: np.ndarray) -> tuple[np.ndarray, tuple[float, float]]:
+    """Derive an opaque interior and color-aware antialiased silhouette."""
+    rgb = source.astype(np.int16)
+    red, green, blue = rgb[..., 0], rgb[..., 1], rgb[..., 2]
+    yy, xx = np.indices((H, W))
+    search = (((xx - 132.0) / 31.0) ** 2 + ((yy - 35.0) / 30.0) ** 2) < 1.25
+    chroma_core = (red - green > 10) & (red - blue > 13) & search & (yy < 63)
+    component = largest_component(chroma_core)
+
+    # The dark channel lines on the ball are real foreground.  The convex hull
+    # includes them while the outer two-pixel ring retains chroma-derived alpha.
+    ys, xs = np.where(component)
+    points = np.column_stack([xs, ys]).astype(np.int32)
+    hull = cv2.convexHull(points)
+    solid = np.zeros((H, W), np.uint8)
+    cv2.fillConvexPoly(solid, hull, 255)
+    solid = solid > 0
+    inner = ndimage.binary_erosion(solid, iterations=2)
+    outer = ndimage.binary_dilation(solid, iterations=2)
+    chroma = np.minimum(red - green, red - blue).astype(np.float32)
+    edge = np.clip((chroma - 1.5) / 11.0, 0.0, 1.0)
+    alpha = np.where(inner, 1.0, np.where(outer, edge, 0.0)).astype(np.float32)
+    alpha = cv2.GaussianBlur(alpha, (0, 0), 0.42)
+    alpha[alpha < 0.008] = 0.0
+
+    # Weighted center remains stable even though the lowest edge meets the rim.
+    total = float(alpha.sum())
+    center = (float((alpha * xx).sum() / total), float((alpha * yy).sum() / total))
+    return alpha, center

OBSERVATION.md

PixelWorld-Text observation and event plan

Inspection method

first_frame.png was inspected only by local Python decoding to an RGB NumPy array. Measurements below come from numeric RGB/luminance thresholds, connected components, row/column runs, and sampled scanlines. No image viewing or image-read tool was used.

Scene measurements

  • Source is RGB, 256 x 144. The camera is locked and the whole image remains static except for the basketball and its contact shadow.
  • The background is a neutral gray wall with a mild left-to-right and top-to-bottom exposure gradient.
  • The orange basketball is the dominant red/orange connected component in the upper center. Its visible bounds are about x=106..158, y=10..58, with center near (132, 36) and nominal radius about 25 px. The bottom already meets/overlaps the crate's rear/top rim.
  • The black plastic crate spans about x=97..174, y=56..121. Numerically, its top/rear rim starts around y=56; the very dark interior is mostly y=58..97; its sides and opaque front body continue through about y=121. Interior lattice highlights occur around y=64..90.
  • The wooden tabletop begins behind the crate at about y=98 and fills the lower frame. Its warm brown grain and perspective edges must remain fixed.
  • The ball is aligned over the crate opening, so the intended action is a vertical gravity drop into the crate. The crate front must occlude the lower part of the ball after entry.

Physical/motion design

The ball begins at rest and accelerates downward. Its center follows a hand-tuned gravity arc from approximately y=36 to a squashed interior contact near y=99 (placing its deformed bottom near the crate bottom at y=120). Because it is inflatable, it briefly squashes vertically at the first contact, rebounds with strongly damped speed, then settles inside. A soft localized contact shadow deepens below it. The ball rotates slightly during the drop; the source's seams and texture therefore move coherently with the sphere rather than being repainted.

  2. Add a subtle moving contact shadow within the already-dark crate opening.
  3. Affine-warp the extracted source basketball (translation, tiny rotation, contact squash) with a feathered alpha matte.
  4. Reapply an analytically selected crate foreground/front layer to enforce entry occlusion.
  5. Keep frame 00 exactly equal to first_frame.png, without reconstruction or recompositing.

METHOD.md

Generation method

Actual method used

The sequence is generated entirely by generate.py from the local RGB pixels in first_frame.png. No external images, masks, models, engines, or network resources are used.

  1. The source PNG is decoded to a uint8 RGB array of shape (144, 256, 3).
  2. The basketball is isolated numerically inside a restricted upper-center search region. A red/orange chroma test finds the largest connected component; its convex hull preserves the dark seam markings; a color-aware two-pixel boundary and 0.42 px Gaussian produce the antialiased alpha matte.
  3. A static clean plate is constructed only under the initial ball:
    • Above y=54, a quadratic RGB surface is least-squares fitted to nearby unoccluded wall pixels. This preserves the measured wall exposure gradient without introducing external texture.
    • At and below y=55, each hidden row is interpolated between visible left/right fragments of the crate's rear rim and opening.
    • The replacement boundary is feathered by 0.7 px.
  4. For frames 01–19, the original premultiplied ball pixels and alpha are affine-warped. The center follows the explicit vertical trajectory [38, 44, 54, 68, 86, 99, 88, 79, 75, 81, 96, 88, 84, 87, 95, 90, 91, 94, 94] after the exact source frame. Rotation increases gradually from 0.7 to 13.6 degrees so the source seams move coherently.
  5. At the three contacts, anisotropic scaling gives the inflatable ball a short vertical squash and corresponding lateral bulge. The first/main contact uses scale (1.10, 0.82); later impacts are smaller.
  6. A soft analytic elliptical shadow is applied only inside the measured crate opening, with strength driven by the ball's vertical proximity.
  7. The original crate-front pixels are composited back over the ball from about y=96 downward inside x=97..173. This makes the ball enter the container, hides the lower sphere during rebounds, and prevents resampling leakage below the crate.
  8. Frame 00 bypasses all processing and is a direct array copy of first_frame.png.

Artifacts

  • pixels.npz stores pixels as (144, 256, 20) uint32, packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png are the decoded RGB frames.
  • inspection.png is the required eight-frame overview produced by artifact_io.py.
  • transition_check.png contains six selected before/after pairs plus an amplified max-channel delta panel for numeric transition inspection.
  • index.html is the standalone 4 fps player generated from the packed tensor deltas.

Verification performed

generate.py reopens every saved PNG, repacks it to uint32 0xRRGGBB, and asserts exact equality with the corresponding pixels.npz plane. It separately asserts that frame 00 equals the decoded source array exactly. The final run reported nonzero, bounded changes for all 19 transitions, with every changed pixel confined to the central ball/crate motion region.

All scene inspection and revision decisions were based on numeric RGB arrays, connected components, luminance scanlines, changed-pixel bounds, and gradient statistics. No visual image-view tool was used, so no claim of visual review is made.
