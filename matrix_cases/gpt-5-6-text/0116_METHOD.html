OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • first_frame.png decodes as one RGB image with shape (144, 256, 3) and uint8 channel ranges R 2..222, G 0..189, B 0..194.
  • The shot is already 256×144. Spatial statistics indicate a fixed interior setup: a broad neutral gray field over the upper/left area, a warm brown lower area, a bright neutral vertical region around x=84..116, and a very dark strip near the right edge (x≈240..255). These areas must remain locked.
  • Strong warm/orange segmentation (R-G>35, R-B>50) gives the main ball image at x=116..134, y=85..101 (273 strong-orange pixels; centroid about (124.9,92.6)) and the smaller mirror image at x=210..224, y=71..82 (150 pixels; centroid about (217.0,76.1)). Including their dark rims, the practical moving footprints are approximately x=113..138, y=81..104 and x=207..228, y=67..85.
  • The first ball has an approximately 19×17-pixel orange core; the reflection is approximately 15×12 pixels and is higher in the frame. This scale/height difference is a useful depth cue and will be preserved.
  • Orange suspension traces occur at x=120..123, y=0..46 for the nearer setup and around x=219..222, y=0..43 for the reflected setup. Dark, low-saturation connector/magnet structure continues below those traces toward the dark upper rim of each ball.
  • Exact frame 00 is a hard constraint: it will be copied directly from the decoded source without alteration.

Motion and compositing plan

  • Camera, mirror, room, lighting, and all fixed surfaces remain perfectly static for all 20 frames.
  • Build a clean plate only underneath the two moving ball/connector footprints using local row/column interpolation from surrounding source pixels; no external imagery is used.
  • Extract the two ball appearances from frame 00 with softly feathered elliptical alpha masks so their original shading and warm highlights remain intact. Extract the narrow connector/magnet regions separately with tapered masks.
  • Lower the physical ball and its mirror image together. Preserve the reflected ball's smaller apparent size and its initial 16-pixel vertical offset. Keep the upper cord anchors fixed and lengthen the visible suspension paths down toward the translated connector/ball assemblies.
  • Use a slow, nearly constant descent totaling about 26 pixels over 4.75 seconds, with integer vertical offsets by frame:

METHOD.md

Method

What was done

The animation is a deterministic local-pixel continuation of first_frame.png. I did not use an image viewing tool, network resource, external model, reference case, mask, or renderer. Scene understanding came from local Python decoding plus numeric RGB/channel statistics, threshold components, bounding boxes, row/column samples, and centroid checks.

  1. The source was decoded as RGB uint8 and measured at exactly 256×144.
  2. Strong warm-pixel components located the nearer ball at approximately (125,93) and its smaller mirror image at approximately (217,77). Narrow orange traces near x=121 and x=221 identified their suspension paths.
  3. A localized clean plate was made with OpenCV Telea inpainting only under measured cord, connector/magnet, and ball footprints. The inpainted result was blended back through the observed foreground mattes, so neighboring fixed background is retained rather than broadly replaced.
  4. The two original ball appearances were extracted with softly feathered ellipse mattes. Narrow low-saturation connector/magnet detail was selected by its RGB residual from the clean plate inside tapered corridors. This retains the source highlights and shading instead of redrawing the balls.
  5. For frames 01–19, the extracted assemblies were translated downward by the integer offset schedule in OBSERVATION.md, ending 26 pixels lower. Both receive the same displacement, while the reflection keeps its original smaller scale and higher location.
  6. The original cord pixels were resampled vertically from their fixed top anchors to the translated assemblies. This lengthens the suspension paths without moving the camera or inventing a new cord texture.
  7. Frame 00 bypasses all processing and is the decoded source copied unchanged.

Timing and scene behavior

  • 20 frames at 4 fps, covering 5 seconds.
  • Single locked-off shot; no cuts, zooms, pans, lighting animation, labels, or unrelated motion.
  • The only intended motion is the slow 26-pixel lowering of the ball/magnet assembly and its synchronized mirror image.

Artifact and integrity checks

generate.py calls the supplied artifact_io.save helper to create pixels.npz, the 20 PNG frames, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. It separately creates transition_check.png with three before/after/difference triplets.

After saving, the generator verifies:

  • array shape (20,144,256,3) and dtype uint8;
  • frame 00 pixel equality with first_frame.png;
  • every decoded frames/00.png–frames/19.png equals its in-memory RGB frame;
  • pixels.npz contains uint32 shape (144,256,20);
  • every tensor entry exactly matches the corresponding packed 0xRRGGBB PNG pixel.

The final numeric audit confirmed that the strong-orange centroids move by the requested offsets while retaining their initial horizontal positions, sizes, and relative height. No claim of visual-tool review is made.
