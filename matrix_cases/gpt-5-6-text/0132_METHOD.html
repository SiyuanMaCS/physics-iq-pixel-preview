OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation

Only local numeric RGB inspection was used. The source is an RGB image of shape 144 x 256; no image viewer or image-read tool was used.

Measured layout

  • The upper background is a cool blue-gray wall/backdrop. The wooden tabletop occupies most of the frame below it.
  • The clear acrylic sheet lies across the central tabletop. It is mostly visible through subtle reflected edges and does not need to move.
  • The main red-paint component contains 81 strongly red pixels, with bounding box (x=131..143, y=50..57) and an approximate center at (137, 54). A few isolated red-brown table pixels elsewhere are not part of the paint.
  • The black platform is the large dark connected component at (x=215..255, y=52..104) (about 1,400 near-neutral dark pixels).
  • A green circular hub on the platform measures approximately (x=223..245, y=43..65), centered near (234, 54).
  • The attached brush/bright arm is a thin, nearly neutral reflective component running from about (143, 53) to (224, 44). PCA of its 199-pixel bright component gives a center of (185.16, 48.63), a long-axis direction of (-0.9945, 0.1051), and a transverse half-width of about 1.8 pixels. Its left tip touches the right side of the red dollop in frame 00.
  • The shot is locked off; the table, acrylic, paint mass, platform, hub, background, lighting, and framing remain fixed.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00: exact source image. The brush tip is at the paint dollop.
  • Frames 01-03: clockwise motion begins immediately around the measured green hub. The distal brush tip passes through/takes paint from the dollop's right edge. A short wet red contact mark begins at the measured contact point.
  • Frames 04-08: the brush rotates steadily clockwise and clearly separates from the original dollop. The tip carries a small red coating; the curved wet trace lengthens along the physically swept tip path on the acrylic.
  • Frames 09-14: continued steady clockwise rotation. The brush is visibly past the paint, while the base and hub remain fixed. The smear remains on the acrylic and becomes slightly thinner away from the dollop.
  • Frames 15-19: the brush continues to roughly 22 degrees clockwise from its starting pose, ending clearly past the dollop. The paint trail and original dollop remain, with a modest amount visibly transferred to the brush tip.

Motion is continuous with an approximately constant angular increment per frame, appropriate to a real-time scientific demonstration.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic, source-derived 2D pixel construction. I did not use an image viewer, image-reading UI, external model, external engine, network resource, mask, or another case.

  1. I loaded first_frame.png locally as an RGB NumPy array and inspected numeric channel statistics, thresholded connected components, scanlines, and a PCA of the thin reflective brush component.
  2. Measurements placed the paint at (131..143, 50..57), the fixed green hub at roughly (234,54), and the brush along the fitted line y = 68.085 - 0.10508*x, from x=142 through x=226.
  3. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the decoded RGB-pixel level from the source. For subsequent frames, only the narrow original brush footprint is reconstructed with constrained OpenCV Telea inpainting.
  4. The original brush pixels and their antialiased alpha are affine-rotated clockwise about the measured hub. The angle advances linearly from 0 to 22 degrees over frames 00–19. A subtle transformed contact shadow follows it.
  5. A small red load is composited onto the distal tip after contact. A tapered, irregular wet-paint trace follows the mathematically rotated tip path, joining the original dollop and remaining fixed on the acrylic. The measured green hub is composited last so the attachment retains the source occlusion.
  6. artifact_io.save writes the uint32 packed tensor, PNG sequence, inspection contact sheet, and standalone player. A separate transition sheet pairs four representative output frames with amplified adjacent/interval RGB deltas.

Validation checks the exact frame count, shape, dtype, exact frame-00 identity, nonempty adjacent motion, packed-tensor/PNG equivalence, and required artifact dimensions.
