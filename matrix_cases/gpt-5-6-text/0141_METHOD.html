OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • first_frame.png is RGB, 256 x 144, with no alpha channel. Channel means are approximately (92.3, 88.6, 89.0) and medians are (84, 82, 84), so the scene is dim and mostly neutral.
  • The camera is treated as completely locked. The broad dark background occupies the upper and side regions; the warmer, brighter support/cutting-board region becomes most prominent toward the lower right.
  • A pale, blue-white folded form is centered in the frame. Numeric bright-component analysis places its main footprint at approximately x=102..149, y=38..109. Its brightest narrow upper ridge is near x=123..131, y=44..61; the wider lower folds extend toward x=108..151, y=72..106.
  • A second broad bright component at x=191..238, y=108..143, plus a diagonal sequence of highlights farther up, is consistent with highlights/reflections from the glass cutting board rather than a second moving subject.
  • There are no distinctly orange pixels in frame 00 under the test R > 1.25G, G > 45, B < 100. This makes frame 00 a useful exact pre-flare reference. The burn is therefore localized at the folded paper's bright upper ridge around (128, 52) and develops immediately after the exact source frame.
  • All measurements were made with local Pillow/NumPy RGB arrays, threshold masks, row runs, and connected-component statistics. No image viewing or image-reading UI/tool was used.

METHOD.md

Method

Generation

generate.py reads first_frame.png locally with Pillow and operates on its RGB array with NumPy/SciPy. It creates 20 frames at 256 x 144; frame 00 is returned as a direct array copy before any effects are evaluated.

The continuation is a locked-off procedural composite:

  1. A pale-pixel-gated elliptical scorch field gradually darkens a small area around the measured upper paper ridge (128, 52). Two spatial sine terms roughen its boundary so the mark does not look geometrically perfect.
  2. A compact Gaussian light field adds a restrained, time-varying warm cast around the ignition point. Smooth spatial gates guarantee that this cannot alter distant parts of the frame.
  3. White smoke is modeled as a deterministic sequence of anisotropic Gaussian puffs. Each puff has a continuous age, buoyant upward motion, leftward drift, expansion, fade-in/fade-out, and two analytic turbulence waves. A narrow continuous field joins the newest puff to the flame. A soft gray fringe followed by a brighter neutral body gives the smoke volume.
  4. The flame is built from nested tapered masks: a red-orange outer envelope, orange/yellow middle, and pale yellow core. Height and lean are continuous sums of sine waves. A smaller secondary tongue intermittently appears after the fire is established.
  5. All float composites are rounded and clipped to uint8 RGB. No camera transform, global exposure change, or framewide noise is used. Outside the local x=84..171, y=0..100 effects area, later frames remain byte-identical to frame 00.

The smoke opacity was revised after numeric inspection showed that the initial render did not reach a clearly pale/white value against the dark background. The final render has roughly 500 neutral smoke pixels above the paper in established-fire frames, while remote pixels remain unchanged.

Output and checks

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 packed 0xRRGGBB tensor, all 20 PNGs, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, containing four before/after pairs and amplified absolute-difference panels.

The generator then verifies:

  • array shape is exactly (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype is uint8;
  • frame 00 equals the decoded source frame exactly;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and equals the packed frame tensor exactly;
  • each decoded PNG equals its corresponding in-memory frame exactly;
  • every consecutive pair changes, with changes confined to the intended fire/smoke area.

python generate.py
