+def antialiased_layer(lines, ellipses, blur=0.0):
+    """Create RGB/alpha glass or smoke marks at 3x resolution."""
+    scale = 3
+    rgba = np.zeros((H * scale, W * scale, 4), np.uint8)
+    for points, color, width in lines:
+        pts = np.rint(np.asarray(points) * scale).astype(np.int32)
+        cv2.polylines(
+            rgba, [pts], False, color, max(1, int(round(width * scale))), cv2.LINE_AA
+        )
+    for bounds, color, width in ellipses:
+        x0, y0, x1, y1 = bounds
+        center = (int(round((x0 + x1) * scale / 2)), int(round((y0 + y1) * scale / 2)))
+        axes = (max(1, int(round((x1 - x0) * scale / 2))), max(1, int(round((y1 - y0) * scale / 2))))
+        cv2.ellipse(
+            rgba, center, axes, 0, 0, 360, color,
+            max(1, int(round(width * scale))), cv2.LINE_AA
+        )

OBSERVATION.md

PixelWorld-Text observation and plan

Numeric observations from first_frame.png

  • The input is RGB uint8, 256 x 144. Its full channel ranges are R 41-254, G 5-252, B 0-195.
  • The camera is locked. The background is a cool gray field, brightest in the upper half (typical block means about RGB 145-175-185), and the setup occupies the lower/central portion.
  • A broad bowl/glass outline is measured from about (72,45) down to (84,105) on the left and from (212,43) down to (202,97) on the right. The bowl and red liquid continue to the bottom edge. The principal red connected region is (82,53) through (203,143).
  • The narrower transparent jar is centered over the setup. Its strongest initial wall/rim evidence is at x about 118-122 and x about 189-191 from the top to y about 44, with a horizontal lower lip/reflection around y 43-46. The useful jar center is x about 155.
  • The lit match bundle is centered within roughly x 133-169. Warm/red flame pixels occupy the upper part of that bundle; darker match/stem structure continues toward the liquid. Strong measured local edges include a vertical segment near x 136, y 74-97 and short horizontal/diagonal structures between x 133-175, y 44-100.
  • The final jar mouth should stop around y 99-102, coincident with the dark/red liquid surface. A downward displacement near 55 pixels therefore covers the bundle without changing framing.
  • No image viewing tool is used. These measurements come from RGB thresholds, connected components, Canny/Hough geometry, pixel samples, and block-class maps printed by local Python.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  1. Frame 00: exact source frame, byte-for-byte pixel equality after RGB decoding.
  2. Frames 01-02: jar begins moving down very slightly; flame remains lively.
  3. Frames 03-06: steady downward motion; the glass rim passes the upper flame while transparent wall highlights/refraction move with it.
  4. Frames 07-10: jar surrounds most of the burning bundle; flame flicker becomes smaller as oxygen is depleted.
  5. Frames 11-13: eased final descent; jar mouth reaches the red liquid around y 99-102.
  6. Frames 14-16: flame rapidly contracts, reddens, and extinguishes; a faint gray smoke wisp forms inside the jar.
  7. Frames 17-19: jar stays seated; smoke rises subtly and red liquid climbs a few pixels inside the jar, then the scene settles.

The animation will retain the photographic source as the base. A targeted inpainted clean plate removes only the initial narrow jar highlights, and those translucent glass cues are translated/reconstructed over the unchanged setup. Flame attenuation, smoke, and the small internal liquid rise are local pixel composites rather than a scene redraw.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is generated locally by generate.py from first_frame.png. No external assets, models, image viewers, masks, engines, or network resources are used.

Scene analysis

I inspected the source only as numeric RGB data. The measurements in OBSERVATION.md came from channel statistics, quantized color/block maps, red/warm color masks, connected components, Canny edges, Hough line segments, and individual pixel samples. This identified the stationary bowl, the red liquid, the central match/flame area, and the narrower glass jar geometry.

Animation construction

  1. Frame 00 is a direct RGB array copy of first_frame.png.
  2. A small mask covers only the measured original jar walls and lower lip. OpenCV Telea inpainting estimates the clean background under those narrow translucent highlights; a Gaussian feather avoids a hard mask boundary.
  3. The real source-to-clean-plate residual is translated downward on an eased 55-pixel path over frames 01-13. This reuses photographic highlight texture instead of moving an opaque rectangular crop.
  4. The moving jar is reinforced with low-opacity, antialiased cool highlights, dark edge refraction, shallow elliptical top/bottom rims, and a roughly 3% transparent body tint. The bowl and matches remain source pixels beneath the glass.
  5. The measured warm central flame region gets small deterministic exposure flicker while the jar descends. After the jar mouth reaches the liquid, its warm pixels blend toward a dim spent-match tone and a final ember contracts.
  6. Late frames add two very faint blurred gray smoke paths inside the jar. A translucent red elliptical surface rises five pixels inside the seated jar to suggest pressure change in the classic covered-flame demonstration.
  7. All composites are calculated in floating-point RGB and rounded/clipped once per output frame.

Outputs and checks

artifact_io.save writes 20 RGB PNGs, pixels.npz, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. generate.py also creates transition_check.png from selected output frames and 5x amplified adjacent-frame absolute differences.

The generator asserts all of the following on every run:

  • shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype uint8;
  • exact equality of frame 00 with the decoded input PNG;
  • 20 saved PNGs exactly equal to the in-memory frames;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and exactly matches the packed 0xRRGGBB values from every PNG.

No claim of visual review is made; final review was numeric (change masks and bounding boxes, regional RGB means, edge positions, frame hashes, and adjacent-frame delta statistics).
