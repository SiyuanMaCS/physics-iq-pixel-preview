ball-ramp · 生成方法

I used only local numeric RGB processing of first_frame.png; I did not use an image viewer, image-reading tool, external model, external engine, network resource, mask, or reference image.

The source frame is loaded as an 8-bit RGB array. Numeric HSV connected-component measurements isolate the supplied yellow-green tennis ball near (102,72), and numeric Hough-line measurements establish the cardboard ramp direction. Frame 00 is copied directly from the source without modification.

For frames 01–19, the original ball and its existing shadow are removed once with a small, local OpenCV TELEA inpaint restricted to an ellipse around the ball. Low-amplitude high-frequency residuals sampled from nearby cardboard are feathered into the center so the reconstructed patch does not become unnaturally smooth. The rest of the source frame is untouched.

The photographed ball is reused as a softly matted sprite rather than redrawn. Its path is analytic: s(t) = 18t - 1.9t^2 pixels along the measured ramp unit vector (0.874,-0.486), sampled at 4 fps and clamped at rest. This gives constant opposing acceleration, representing gravity slowing a ball that has rolled out from the left-side pipe and uphill onto the ramp. Sprite rotation is -s/r radians for measured radius r=10 pixels, coupling rotation to traveled distance without slip. Bicubic subpixel warping avoids integer-position jitter. A soft multiplicative broad shadow and tighter contact shadow move with the ball, following the source frame's left/lower shadow direction.

artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, RGB PNG sequence, inspection contact sheet, and standalone player. transition_check.png contains amplified consecutive-frame differences with changed-pixel counts and a path panel. The generator finally reloads every PNG and pixels.npz and asserts exact equality with the generated RGB tensor, including exact equality of frame 00 with first_frame.png.
