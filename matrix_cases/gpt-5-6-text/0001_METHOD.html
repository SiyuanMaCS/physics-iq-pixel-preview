ball-and-block-fall · 生成方法

Actual generation method

The animation is generated entirely by local Python pixel operations in generate.py; no external images, models, engines, masks, cases, or network resources are used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frames 00 and 01 use those source pixels directly, and frame 00 is explicitly reassigned from the source before saving.
  2. A clean plate is made with OpenCV Telea inpainting. The inpaint mask is limited to the measured brown-ball disk and the measured upright orange-block rectangle. Blue-dominant pixels are excluded from the ball mask so the blue gripper jaws remain visible after release.
  3. The ball sprite is extracted from the source with a feathered circular alpha mask. Blue gripper-tip pixels inside the crop are suppressed. The orange block is extracted with a narrow, softly feathered rectangular alpha mask.
  4. Motion is represented by 20 sampled poses at 4 fps:
    • The ball accelerates vertically, squashes at its first pillow contact, rebounds, makes a smaller second contact, and settles.
    • The rigid block falls a shorter distance, then rotates through a damped tip and rocks into a nearly horizontal rest.
  5. Pillow response is produced by local OpenCV remapping of the existing pillow texture with a small downward Gaussian displacement. Contact amplitudes are largest at impact and damp afterward.
  6. Analytic elliptical shadows are composited before the objects. They narrow and darken with decreasing object-to-pillow clearance. Both deformation and shadow fields are numerically clipped outside their local support so distant source pixels remain unchanged.
  7. Pillow texture, table, lighting, camera, and gripper positions otherwise remain locked to the source. The final three frames are identical, representing complete mechanical settling rather than ongoing drift.
  8. artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone delta-compressed HTML player. generate.py separately creates transition_check.png with selected frames and 5x-amplified consecutive-frame absolute differences.

Verification performed

  • All 20 output frames are RGB uint8 arrays of shape 144 x 256 x 3.
  • frames/00.png is pixel-for-pixel identical to first_frame.png (maximum channel error 0).
  • Repacking all PNG pixels as 0xRRGGBB and transposing to 144 x 256 x 20 exactly matches the pixels array in pixels.npz.
  • The standalone player contains 20 frames' worth of initial and delta packets.
  • Consecutive-frame changed-pixel counts and bounding boxes were measured. All post-release edits are localized to the two object paths and their pillow-contact neighborhoods; there is no full-frame camera or lighting motion.
  • Object-color masks were tracked numerically through the sequence to confirm the fall, rebound/tip, and final stable poses.
  • The generated contact-sheet files were not visually reviewed with an image viewing tool; inspection in this run was numeric and code-based, per the task constraint.
