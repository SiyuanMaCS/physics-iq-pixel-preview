ball-ramp · 生成方法

This continuation was made entirely with deterministic local RGB pixel operations in generate.py. No image viewing tool, network source, external model, external engine, mask, or reference image was used.

Scene measurement

I inspected first_frame.png numerically with Pillow, NumPy, and OpenCV: RGB statistics and samples, HSV-connected components, edge detection, and probabilistic Hough lines. Those measurements located the tennis ball near (63,45), the ramp top edge near (121,75) to (205,55), and the blue support at x=196..226, y=50..85. The pre-code measurements and event plan are recorded in OBSERVATION.md.

Pixel generation

  1. Frame 00 is a direct RGB array copy of first_frame.png with no modification.
  2. A clean static plate is created for later frames by masking a radius-11.5-pixel disk around the original ball and using OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. The rest of the photographed frame is untouched.
  3. A 25 x 25 patch centered on the original ball supplies the actual source ball pixels. A measured circular alpha silhouette with a narrow smooth boundary converts it to a premultiplied sprite.
  4. The ball follows a hand-measured physical trajectory: down-right from the pipe, contact at the ramp's low end, decelerating uphill travel, a subpixel turnaround, and left/down rollback. Sprite rotation is cumulative distance divided by the measured 9-pixel radius, and reverses during rollback.
  5. Starting at frame 05, a restrained blurred ellipse darkens the few ramp pixels immediately below the ball to represent contact shadow. It is aligned to the measured ramp angle of about -13.4 degrees.
  6. All rendering is done in RGB/float arrays and rounded once to uint8. Output frames are assembled into a (20, 144, 256, 3) tensor.

Outputs and checks

  • artifact_io.save packs RGB as uint32 values in exact 0xRRGGBB order and saves pixels.npz with shape (144, 256, 20).
  • The same tensor produces frames/00.png through frames/19.png, inspection.png, and the self-contained index.html player.
  • transition_check.png contains all 20 frames in order, with per-transition changed-pixel counts and red bounds around the changing region.
  • The generator decodes all 20 saved PNGs and compares them pixel-for-pixel to the in-memory tensor. It separately compares the NPZ tensor against independently packed RGB values.
  • Frame 00 is pixel-identical and, with the current encoder, file-identical to first_frame.png; both have SHA-256 9b22838f5cc7ee1d573e7ff2203120523ab163d93381e6081dd196271475cd2f.
  • Consecutive changed-pixel counts are compact and nonzero: 602, 537, 559, 551, 666, 675, 640, 601, 575, 518, 500, 484, 456, 330, 445, 482, 500, 560, 589.

The inspection sheets were generated for deliverable completeness, but they were not opened with an image viewing tool; assessment and revision used numeric RGB inspection only, as requested.

Reproduction

Run:

python3 generate.py

The script deterministically regenerates and verifies every generated artifact.
