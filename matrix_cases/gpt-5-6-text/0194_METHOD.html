OBSERVATION.md

PixelWorld observation and event plan

Numeric scene observations

  • Source: first_frame.png, decoded as RGB uint8, 256×144. RGB byte SHA-256 is 726f27dcd769a7ba8cb471d9a8826cfa1ce21909c3097254a20bcaeba645e464.
  • The shot is fixed and front-facing. The upper roughly 65 rows are a gray wall/backdrop; two rumpled gray pillows fill most of rows 64–123; a dark brown surface/floor occupies the bottom.
  • The 30-lb kettlebell is the connected near-black object at approximately x=72–116, y=0–61. Its body occupies about x=80–113, y=29–61, while its arched handle rises into the top border. Its lower center is x≈96.
  • The green paper is the connected saturated-green component at x=168–200, y=22–63 (1,210 strongly green pixels), centered at x≈185. A narrow near-black holder/edge runs from the top border to y≈26 at x≈183–190.
  • Pillow material beneath the kettlebell is light gray, with a visible top/highlight around y=66–79 near x=95 and folds continuing to a dark lower seam around y=108–123. Pillow material beneath the paper starts near y=64–70 around x=185 and remains medium gray down to the same lower seam.
  • The two object bottoms are already close to their respective pillow tops, so the placement travel should be modest. A final downward shift near 11 pixels puts the paper onto the right pillow. The heavy kettlebell should continue another few pixels while producing a broad indentation, unlike the paper.
  • Illumination is soft, neutral, and brighter from upper left. Motion must preserve the exact wall/pillow texture outside the local interaction zones.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is generated entirely from first_frame.png with local Python RGB operations. No external images, masks, models, engines, network resources, or image viewing tools were used.

Scene extraction

generate.py decodes the opening image as a 256×144 RGB uint8 array. It derives two source sprites:

  • The kettlebell matte is the 8-connected low-luminance component seeded inside the bell. It is bounded to x=67–121 and y=0–61 so nearby dark pillow folds cannot join the moving object. A subpixel Gaussian feather preserves the photographed edge.
  • The paper matte is the 8-connected high-green-chroma component, combined with the narrow dark overhead holder directly attached to it. This matte receives the same small feather.

The original silhouettes are dilated and reconstructed with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. This creates only the gray pixels revealed behind the descending objects. The photographed RGB inside each object matte is retained, rather than redrawing either object.

Motion and interaction

Both sprites follow the same eased integer descent through frame 08, reaching 11 pixels of travel simultaneously. The paper then remains at 11 pixels. The kettlebell continues to 15 pixels while the left pillow compresses, rebounds by 1 pixel, and settles.

The pillow response is an analytic backward RGB warp. A fourth-power horizontal falloff centers the deformation at x=96, while vertical smoothstep envelopes move the pillow top most and pin the lower seam. The maximum texture displacement is 8 pixels. Source-textured, soft elliptical shadows are applied behind the two objects at contact; the paper shadow stays shallow, and the kettlebell shadow broadens with compression. Thin source-colored overhead connections are extended from the top border during descent so neither held object develops a clipped floating edge.

Everything outside the local interaction region is copied unchanged. Frames 16–19 are an identical stable end hold.

Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, pixels.npz, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, whose upper row contains pre-contact/contact/maximum-compression/final keyframes and whose lower row contains 4× amplified numeric RGB deltas from each preceding frame.

The generator verifies all of the following before it exits:

  • shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and RGB uint8 generation;
  • frame 00 is pixel-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every saved PNG is pixel-identical to its in-memory frame;
  • pixels.npz is uint32, shape (144, 256, 20), packed as 0xRRGGBB, and exactly matches all PNG RGB values.
