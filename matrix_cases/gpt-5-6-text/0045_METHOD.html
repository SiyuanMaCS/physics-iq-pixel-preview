OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Numeric scene observations

Inspection was performed only by local RGB-array analysis of first_frame.png; no image viewing tool was used.

  • Source is exactly 256 x 144 RGB. The camera is locked.
  • The upper field is a cool light gray surface/backdrop. The lower field is a warm brown wooden tabletop. The irregular transition is around y=40-55 and is partly hidden by the apparatus.
  • Saturated green-paper pixels occupy x=50-179, y=38-92 (3,094 pixels under the broad green threshold). Their centroid is approximately (111.5, 63.6).
  • The paper is diagonally oriented. Its measured outer envelope is approximately the polygon (75,52) -> (153,38) -> (179,72) -> (78,93) -> (50,92). Dark olive folds/shadows inside this envelope are also paper, so raw green thresholding alone is not a complete matte.
  • The left blue-black grabber enters from upper-left, with its dark body principally around x=23-81, y=26-58 and blue jaw near x=54-81, y=38-56. Its contact corner is about (76,53).
  • The right blue-black grabber enters from lower-right, principally around x=180-255, y=55-88, with blue jaw near x=183-222, y=61-84. Its contact corner is about (179,72).
  • The pulling axis is therefore roughly (105,19): left/up versus right/down. A physically plausible cross-tear runs roughly perpendicular to this axis, jagging from the upper paper edge near (127,43), through (121,64), to the lower edge near (112,85).
  • The paper has strong existing texture: bright green areas around RGB (22,154,31), deeper folds around (12,97,8), and olive shaded regions around (81,73,28). These observed pixels should be retained rather than replacing the sheet with a flat fill.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations (numpy, OpenCV, and Pillow). No network resources, external models, external engines, masks, other cases, or image viewing tools were used.

Scene analysis

first_frame.png was read as a numeric RGB array. I measured color-threshold bounding boxes, centroids, row/column runs, connected components, edge lines, and sampled RGB values. Those measurements are recorded in OBSERVATION.md. In particular, the visible paper is strongly concave and folded, so the implementation retains its measured silhouette instead of replacing it with a flat polygon.

Pixel construction

  1. The paper matte comes from a restricted green-chroma key inside the measured paper envelope, followed by a small close and a one-pixel expansion for antialiased edge pixels.
  2. Each blue-black grabber matte comes from dark/blue RGB seeds restricted to its measured diagonal corridor. Small morphological closing joins highlights enclosed by the dark body; connected-component filtering rejects isolated noise.
  3. A clean plate is reconstructed only beneath the paper and grabbers with OpenCV Telea inpainting. Fine source grain is added back only within this removal matte. Every background pixel outside that area stays bit-for-bit locked to the source.
  4. The observed paper texture is retained. Tool-colored pixels at the two contact regions are locally inpainted in the paper texture because the grabbers are later composited as separate layers.
  5. A measured jagged tear path runs from approximately (127,42) through the narrow center to (112,87). Frames 03-10 develop a dark stress crease into a widening slit, with pale green fibrous rims. From frame 11 onward, this path partitions the real paper matte into two irregular pieces.
  6. Both tools pull along the normalized measured axis (0.984, 0.178). After the final fibers fail, each paper piece uses the same translation as its gripping tool plus a small opposing rotation about the contact point. Motion follows an accelerating-then-decelerating displacement schedule, ending at about 12.7 pixels of travel per side.
  7. Soft cast shadows, edge interpolation, and small torn-fiber colors keep the layered motion integrated with the real tabletop. Frame 00 is explicitly replaced with the untouched source array after rendering.

Output and checks

artifact_io.save packs every RGB pixel as uint32 0xRRGGBB in a (144, 256, 20) tensor, writes the 20 PNG frames, creates the inspection contact sheet, and embeds delta-coded pixels into the standalone HTML player. transition_check.png pairs selected later frames with 5x-amplified absolute consecutive-frame differences for numeric transition inspection.

Validation compares every decoded PNG to its matching packed tensor plane and checks frame 00 byte/pixel equality with first_frame.png. Review was numeric only; no claim of visual-tool review is made.
