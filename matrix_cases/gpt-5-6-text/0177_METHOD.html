OBSERVATION.md

Source observations and event plan

Numeric source inspection

The source was inspected only with local numeric RGB arrays, row/column samples, color statistics, and textual edge/saturation maps. No image Read/View tool was used.

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB (uint8). Camera and framing remain locked.
  • Background: a warm, neutral wall in the upper half and a brown tabletop in the lower half. The broad wall/table transition is around y=64--91 because of perspective and soft focus.
  • Teapot rotation axis: approximately x=133. The dark knob is around x=128--137, y=9--19; the yellow lid/shoulder is around x=116--148, y=20--34.
  • Main vessel silhouette: about x=102--163, y=32--99. It widens gradually from the upper shoulder and narrows slightly near the bottom. Its conspicuous painted regions are blue on the viewer-left, red on the viewer-right, with yellow/olive accents above and below. Those color boundaries provide strong rotation cues.
  • Platform: a black, short cylindrical plinth around x=100--163, y=99--124. It stays completely stationary; only the teapot above it rotates.
  • Lighting: soft ambient illumination, with localized specular highlights. The background and all shadows remain stable.
  • Measured source channel means are approximately R=129.5, G=110.1, B=97.7. The frame contains only the described setup.

Motion model

The teapot rotates clockwise about its vertical axis through 38 degrees over the 19 intervals (8 degrees/second). Rotation is synthesized as a cylindrical texture reprojection of the extracted vessel, with slow horizontal advection of its painted surface, cosine foreshortening, moving analytic side shading, and a small geometry change at the silhouette. The knob and lid follow the same axis. The platform, table, camera, and background stay fixed. Motion is linear in physical angle and therefore uniform in real time.

METHOD.md

Method

I used only local Python numeric pixel operations on first_frame.png; I did not use an image Read/View tool, any external model, an external engine, a network resource, or another case.

The teapot outline was measured from RGB row samples plus textual edge and saturation traces. I separated that outline from the black platform at y=100, then reconstructed only the narrow background area hidden by the source vessel using robust left/right row colors. The reconstruction is used when rotation uncovers a few silhouette-edge pixels; the original platform, table, and background remain fixed.

For each generated frame I advance a physical yaw angle by 2 degrees (8 degrees per second). The source vessel is treated as a set of horizontal cylindrical cross-sections. Each destination pixel is mapped through longitude with source_x = center + radius * sin(asin(q) + angle), which advects the recorded blue, red, and yellow paint and the recorded highlights around the vertical axis. I add only mild analytic side shading and cosine silhouette evolution. The knob, lid, and body use the same angle. This produces a steady clockwise turn while the black platform stays stationary.

Frame 00 is copied directly from the source. The other 19 frames are rendered at 256 x 144 RGB. artifact_io.save writes PNG frames, the packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. The generator then decodes every PNG, re-packs all RGB values as 0xRRGGBB, compares them with pixels.npz, and asserts exact source equality for frame 00. transition_check.png is a labeled 5x-gain absolute-difference strip for four adjacent frame pairs.

No visual review is claimed; validation was numeric and programmatic.
