light-on-mug · 生成方法

The animation was made entirely with local Python numeric RGB operations (numpy, Pillow, and small scipy.ndimage filters). The source was not opened with an image viewing tool. Scene measurements came from RGB thresholds, connected components, row/column luminance summaries, and coordinate probes.

first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB array and used as the locked background/lighting plate. Frame 00 is kept pixel-identical in the tensor and is also copied byte-for-byte to frames/00.png after artifact export.

For frames 01–19, the mug handle is rendered as a supersampled elliptical ceramic ring. Its horizontal projection, position, and foreshortening are driven by one analytic angular phase over 20 frames. The phase proceeds rear → right → front → left → rear, which is clockwise when viewed on the platform from above. The measured source mug silhouette is restored over the ring to give correct rear occlusion. A small ochre surface mark is visible only on the front half of the mug and traverses right-to-left, reinforcing the same rotation.

The handle alpha also generates a shifted, Gaussian-softened cast-shadow lobe, so the moving projection affects the wall/surface shadow coherently. The large existing source shadow remains anchored. A low-opacity warm dash follows a foreshortened ellipse on the black platform, making the platform's clockwise rotation legible while leaving its geometry fixed.

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, RGB PNG sequence, contact sheet, and standalone HTML player. transition_check.png shows selected generated frames over 5× amplified adjacent-frame RGB differences. The generator then decodes the tensor and compares it against every in-memory frame and every PNG, checks source equality, dimensions, modes, dtypes, and nonzero motion in all 19 transitions.
