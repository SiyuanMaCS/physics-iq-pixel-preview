cut-paper · 生成方法

The sequence is generated locally by generate.py with NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. No image viewer, image-read tool, external model, external engine, network resource, other case, or subagent was used.

Pixel construction

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB array. Frame 00 is a direct array copy and is therefore byte-exact.
  2. The green paper is isolated with channel-difference thresholds, a small morphological close, and the largest filled external contour. Blue-black tool masks are isolated inside measured left/right regions using blue chroma and dark-value thresholds connected to the frame edges.
  3. Newly exposed tabletop is reconstructed only under a dilated object mask. OpenCV Telea inpainting supplies broad color continuity, and subdued texture sampled from unobstructed lower table rows restores real source grain after per-row robust color matching. Unaffected pixels remain from the locked source frame.
  4. The paper keeps its photographed RGB texture. A measured jagged path follows the existing crease from the top paper edge to the silhouette's narrow neck. Before failure, the intact paper undergoes a small horizontal tension warp while a dark crack and sparse bright green fiber rim progressively grow along that path.
  5. After failure, the original paper alpha is partitioned into two coherent fragments. The lower fold is assigned to the left fragment and the upper-right fold to the right fragment. Each fragment receives a small rigid rotation and outward translation; the matching grabber uses the same transform so its jaw remains attached. Sparse one-pixel edge losses and fiber tint prevent a mechanically smooth cut.
  6. Soft shadows are derived analytically from transformed alpha masks. The pull reaches a small overshoot, rebounds, and damps during the final frames. The camera/background never pans, zooms, or changes exposure.

Timing

The output contains 20 RGB frames at 256 x 144. Playback is fixed at 4 fps for 5 seconds. Frames 01--09 build tension and propagate the crack, frame 10 is the failure transition, frames 11--16 widen the gap, and frames 17--19 show a small damped rebound.

Validation

generate.py verifies all of the following after writing:

  • array shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and uint8 RGB type;
  • exact equality of frame 00 and first_frame.png;
  • all 20 PNG files exactly match the generated RGB arrays;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), type uint32, and every value exactly matches packed 0xRRGGBB pixels.

inspection.png is the required eight-frame contact sheet. transition_check.png contains source/taut/final checkpoints and 4x amplified absolute-difference panels for the opening, tear, and settling transitions. These files were generated for downstream inspection; no claim of visual review is made. Numeric checks additionally confirmed two substantial green connected components after tearing, zero changed pixels below row 104 between the first and final frame, and motion confined to the original action region.
