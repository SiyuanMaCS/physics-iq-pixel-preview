OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation of first_frame.png

The source was inspected only as RGB arrays and numeric summaries; it was not opened with an image viewing tool.

Frame and scene measurements

  • Source: 256×144 RGB, uint8. The camera is locked off.
  • Background/wall: cool gray, approximately rows 0–61. Corner RGB values are [129,136,142], [137,146,153] at the top and wooden-table colors at the bottom corners.
  • Table: begins around y=62–64 and occupies the lower portion. Dominant quantized colors are warm browns around [128,96,64], [128,96,48], and [112,96,64].
  • Black balloon: principal dark connected component at x=55–131, y=20–96 (4,175 pixels below luminance 50). It is roughly 76 px wide and 77 px tall, with its lower-right boundary tapering through x=114 at y=90 and x=106 at y=93.
  • Black platform: second dark component at x=172–228, y=68–101 (784 pixels below luminance 50). Its upper surface starts near x=185–202 at y=68 and widens toward its base.
  • Match/rotating arm: a thin, pale wooden diagonal runs from approximately (106,93) to (195,75), with one-pixel highlights. Numerically traced peak-luminance points include (120,90), (139,86), (163,81), (182,77), and (193,75). The right end is attached near the platform pivot (196,76); the left/head end is at the balloon's lower-right boundary.
  • The image contains no other large foreground components. All animation will preserve the camera, table, wall, and platform registration.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00: exact source image.
  • Frames 01–04: match begins a smooth clockwise rotation about the platform pivot; the lit head travels upward along the balloon's right edge. Flame flicker is small and temporally coherent; a faint warm reflection follows it.
  • Frames 05–07: the flame presses against the balloon. The balloon edge dimples locally, then stretches sharply; rotation eases as contact resistance rises.
  • Frame 08: rupture onset at the heated contact point, with a tiny bright rim and the first black rubber slivers separating.
  • Frames 09–11: rapid collapse/pop. The inflated silhouette retracts toward its neck while several small rubber fragments move ballistically outward. The previously occluded wall/table is reconstructed from local texture and gradients.
  • Frames 12–15: balloon is gone except for falling black scraps; the match completes a small additional clockwise arc and its flame settles.
  • Frames 16–19: fragments descend/bounce on the tabletop and come close to rest. The platform remains fixed; the flame continues subtle natural flicker.

Temporal motion uses eased angular rotation, subpixel anti-aliased compositing, short flame flicker, and gravity-like fragment trajectories. No camera movement or newly introduced scene object is planned.

METHOD.md

Method

Source inspection

first_frame.png was loaded locally with Pillow as a uint8 RGB array. I did not use an image Read/View tool. Scene understanding came from numeric RGB samples, quantized color counts, luminance/saturation summaries, connected-component bounds, threshold runs by row, and coordinate traces of the thin diagonal highlight.

Generation

generate.py performs the complete deterministic render:

  1. It reads the 256×144 source and preserves it byte-for-byte as frame 00.
  2. The balloon is segmented as the largest low-luminance component inside its measured ROI. The low threshold prevents dark wood grain from merging with the silhouette; contour filling restores the balloon's internal highlights to the mask.
  3. The original pale diagonal match is removed for frames 01–19 with a narrow local Telea inpaint. The black platform and camera-fixed source remain unchanged.
  4. Background hidden by the balloon is reconstructed in two regimes. The smooth gray wall uses a fitted spatial illumination polynomial from unoccluded wall pixels. The tabletop uses local Telea propagation of surrounding wood colors and grain. A soft y-dependent blend joins these at the table boundary, and a subpixel mask edge prevents a hard seam.
  5. The match is redrawn as a rigid shaded wooden arm rotating clockwise around the measured pivot (195,75). Its motion follows smoothstep easing over an 18-degree sweep. A charred head, ember, blurred orange glow, and two-tone upward flame are rendered at 4× resolution and Lanczos-downsampled. Two fixed-frequency sine terms produce deterministic flame flicker rather than random frame noise.
  6. Frames 01–07 preserve the inflated balloon while the flame warms its surface. Frame 08 cuts a jagged, localized rupture through the silhouette. From frame 09 onward the reconstructed background replaces the balloon.
  7. Ten small folded-rubber fragments follow deterministic ballistic paths with gravity, rotation, glints, and tabletop shadows. The match continues its small clockwise sweep after rupture.
  8. artifact_io.save packs RGB into uint32 0xRRGGBB, transposes it to the required (144,256,20) tensor, saves all 20 RGB PNGs, builds inspection.png, and embeds the animation in the standalone index.html player. transition_check.png contains enhanced absolute-difference maps for transitions 00→01, 07→08, 08→09, and 18→19.

Verification

  • 20 frames, each RGB 256×144, at the required 4 fps / 5 seconds.
  • frames/00.png is exactly equal to first_frame.png at every RGB channel.
  • pixels.npz has uint32 shape (144,256,20).
  • Every packed PNG pixel was compared against the corresponding 0xRRGGBB tensor entry for exact equality.
  • Temporal difference bounds remain localized to the match, flame, balloon/fragment region, and the reconstructed pixels exposed by the pop; the platform and camera do not move.

No external assets, masks, models, engines, network calls, or subagents were used.
