OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and event plan

All observations below come from local Pillow/NumPy RGB inspection of first_frame.png; no image viewer or image-read UI was used.

Frame measurements

  • Source is RGB, 256 x 144, with 8,269 distinct RGB triplets. Channel means are approximately (118.1, 104.6, 92.6).
  • The camera is locked. Large, stable vertical structure edges occur near x=118-129 and x=188-202. A warm brown surface occupies the lower portion and rises toward the left; the upper/left regions are mostly neutral gray. The far-right region is very dark.
  • The prominent real orange/yellow ball is centered near (120.5, 72.5). Its strongly warm pixels span roughly x=111-129, y=64-82; the useful cutout extent is x=109-132, y=55-84 so the darker attachment immediately above it is retained.
  • The smaller/dimmer mirror image is centered near (187.2, 57.0). Its strongly warm pixels span roughly x=181-193, y=52-61; the useful cutout extent is x=178-198, y=42-66 so its dark attachment is retained.
  • The real cord highlight is near x=118 and the reflected cord is near x=189. Both continue upward out of frame. Their in-frame upper portions remain fixed while the lower ends lengthen as the assemblies descend.
  • The reflection is smaller and dimmer in the source, so it should retain its existing scale, brightness, and softness rather than being synthesized as a second identical ball.
  • Only the two hanging assemblies and the newly exposed/extended cord segments should change. Background, mirror, supporting structure, table/surface, exposure, and framing remain frozen.

METHOD.md

Method

I analyzed first_frame.png locally as numeric RGB data with Pillow and NumPy. I did not use an image viewer, image-read tool, external model, network resource, or another case.

The animation is a source-pixel cutout/compositing continuation:

  1. Two tight, hand-measured masks isolate the large real ball plus its dark magnetic attachment and the smaller/dimmer reflected assembly. The masks deliberately retain the source reflection's own scale, blur, shading, and color.
  2. OpenCV's local Telea pixel inpainting makes a clean plate only beneath those two starting footprints. Nothing outside those footprints is reconstructed or moved.
  3. The masked source pixels are premultiplied, translated vertically with bilinear subpixel sampling, and composited back over the clean plate. The real assembly moves 26 pixels; the reflection moves 22 pixels to preserve its smaller apparent scale.
  4. Motion follows cubic smoothstep timing over frames 00-19, producing a slow start, controlled descent, and soft stop without swing, bounce, camera movement, or lighting change.
  5. Newly exposed cord lengths are antialiased one-pixel segments. Their orange/brown colors are numerically sampled from the corresponding real and reflected cords in the first frame, then slightly subdued for the local shadow.
  6. Frame 00 is assigned directly from the decoded source array and is therefore pixel-identical to first_frame.png. artifact_io.py writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, PNG sequence, inspection contact sheet, and standalone player. A separate transition_check.png shows representative rendered frames and amplified consecutive-frame differences.
