OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

Numeric RGB observations

  • Source: first_frame.png, inspected only with local Python/Pillow/NumPy numeric RGB operations and terminal text maps. No image viewer or image-read tool was used.
  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB pixels. The camera is locked off.
  • Background: cool gray wall/backdrop over approximately y=0..85. The wooden tabletop occupies approximately y=86..143, with warm brown grain and a perspective brightness gradient.
  • Real 30 lb kettlebell: dark, matte silhouette and highlights spanning approximately x=75..142, y=22..92. Its lower-right contact contour is around x=137..142, y=85..92.
  • Mirror/reflection: the reflected kettlebell occupies approximately x=145..201, y=19..83. A dark vertical mirror/frame boundary is visible near the far-right edge. These regions must remain static.
  • Tennis ball: yellow-green pixels form one connected component at x=133..168, y=123..143; the ball is clipped by the lower image edge. Its inferred full-circle center is about (151,145) and its inferred radius is 21–22 px. Observed ball colors run from olive edge tones near (140,160,55) to highlights near (178,224,61). A pale curved seam is present across the visible upper hemisphere.
  • Perspective: the ball is much closer to the camera than the kettlebell. As it travels toward the kettlebell, its apparent radius should reduce from about 21.5 px to about 8 px.
  • Motion target: the ball approaches almost vertically in image space, drifting slightly left from x=151 to x=147; final contact is against the kettlebell's lower-right edge near (139,90).

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00: exact source frame, including the ball clipped at the lower edge.
  • Frames 01–03: the ball begins a slow roll upward/away from camera; its lower half becomes visible as it enters the frame. Preserve a continuous start without camera or background motion.
  • Frames 04–07: continued slow roll along the tabletop toward the kettlebell, with modest perspective shrink and visible seam rotation.
  • Frames 08–11: the ball crosses the central foreground/midground, continuing slightly left while shrinking; a soft contact shadow remains attached to the table.
  • Frames 12–15: approach the kettlebell's lower-right edge. Motion remains smooth and the kettlebell and reflection remain completely immobile.
  • Frame 16: near-contact approach, ball center around (147.3,97) and radius about 8.5 px.
  • Frame 17: first contact at the kettlebell's lower-right base, ball center near (147,94.5) and radius about 8 px.
  • Frame 18: very slight tennis-ball compression at impact; the 30 lb kettlebell does not move.
  • Frame 19: ball settles round against the kettlebell, with no rebound or camera movement.

Construction notes

  • Create a clean tabletop plate for frames after frame 00 by locally inpainting only the original ball footprint.
  • Reconstruct a full tennis-ball sprite from measured source colors, the visible source pixels, analytic spherical shading, fine deterministic felt texture, and a curved seam. Rotate its surface appearance while translating it to show rolling.
  • Composite a small soft shadow before the ball. Keep all pixels outside the original-ball cleanup and moving ball/shadow regions identical to the source.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is generated entirely by local deterministic Python pixel operations in generate.py. No external models, engines, network resources, masks, other cases, or subagents were used. first_frame.png was inspected only as numeric RGB arrays, RGB/HSV-style threshold statistics, connected components, row/column summaries, and terminal text maps. No image viewing tool was used, so no claim of visual review is made.

Scene reconstruction

  1. Pillow loads the 256 x 144 RGB source and NumPy measures object bounds and colors.
  2. Frame 00 is assigned directly from the source uint8 array without any resampling or rewriting.
  3. For frames 01–19, the clipped original tennis-ball footprint is removed with OpenCV Telea inpainting. The mask is restricted to the measured ball circle below y=121. A restrained high-frequency residual sampled from unobstructed wood 48 pixels to the left is feathered into the mask interior so the filled area retains tabletop grain.
  4. A complete 52 x 52 RGBA tennis-ball sprite is reconstructed from:
    • the actual visible source-ball pixels in the upper hemisphere,
    • measured yellow-green source colors,
    • analytic spherical normals and upper-left/front lighting,
    • deterministic fine felt variation, and
    • a pale curved tennis seam.
  5. The sprite is bicubically rotated to make the felt and seam visibly roll, area-resampled smaller with perspective, and alpha-composited along the measured path. A soft analytic tabletop shadow is composited first.
  6. The ball center travels from the inferred source position near (151,145) to (147,94.5). Its apparent radius decreases from about 21.7 pixels to about 8.1 pixels. Frame 17 is first contact; frame 18 uses a subtle 8.65 x 7.45 pixel squash; frame 19 settles nearly round. The heavy kettlebell, its reflection, mirror, table, background, and camera remain fixed.

Output and checks

  • artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNG frames, packed uint32 tensor, inspection contact sheet, and standalone HTML player.
  • transition_check.png contains selected next-frame images and enhanced absolute-difference maps for numeric/transition auditing.
  • The generator asserts the exact shape (20,144,256,3) and uint8 type.
  • Frame 00 is asserted byte-for-byte equal to the RGB pixels of first_frame.png.
  • Every frames/00.png–frames/19.png file is decoded and asserted equal to its generated RGB array.
  • Every generated frame is packed as 0xRRGGBB; the result is asserted exactly equal to the stored uint32 pixels.npz tensor of shape (144,256,20).
  • Consecutive-frame changes remain localized to the ball, shadow, and original-ball cleanup corridor; the measured changed-pixel bounding boxes never extend beyond x=128..175.
