+def blend_mask(frame, color, mask, opacity=1.0):
+    """Alpha-blend a solid RGB color through a float mask in [0, 1]."""
+    a = np.clip(mask.astype(np.float32) * opacity, 0.0, 1.0)[..., None]
+    out = frame.astype(np.float32) * (1.0 - a) + np.asarray(color, np.float32) * a
+    return np.clip(out + 0.5, 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)

+def composite_warp(frame, texture, mask, center, dx, dy, angle, scale=1.0):
+    """Rotate/translate one masked source layer and composite it over frame."""
+    matrix = cv2.getRotationMatrix2D(center, angle, scale)
+    matrix[0, 2] += dx
+    matrix[1, 2] += dy
+    rgb = cv2.warpAffine(
+        texture,
+        matrix,
+        (W, H),
+        flags=cv2.INTER_LINEAR,
+        borderMode=cv2.BORDER_CONSTANT,
+        borderValue=(0, 0, 0),
+    )
+    alpha = cv2.warpAffine(
+        mask.astype(np.float32),
+        matrix,

+        borderValue=0,

OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and animation plan

Source measurements

  • The source is an RGB PNG of exactly 256 x 144 pixels. Pixel inspection was done with local Pillow/NumPy code only; the image was not opened in an image viewer.
  • The frame is a locked camera view. Its upper field is a cool, blue-gray projection-screen/background gradient. Mean source RGB is approximately (96.45, 103.26, 119.19).
  • A strong foreground boundary runs diagonally from about (240, 80) through (128, 101) to (16, 120). The neutral, textured region below it is the concrete/ceramic support surface. At the mug's horizontal center the impact height is therefore about y=100.
  • The yellow mug is the only coherent yellow component: 141 strongly yellow pixels, bounding box (118,47) through (133,62), centroid (125.1,56.9). Including shaded rim, handle, body, and base, the useful source patch is (113,44) through (136,70).
  • The mug is about 18 px wide by 23 px high. Its lower edge is near y=69, leaving roughly 30 px of clearance above the hard surface.
  • The mug silhouette has a thin oval rim around y=46..53, a body down to y=66, and a small shaded handle bulge on the left around x=113..119, y=54..65.
  • The grabber is blue-black/cool neutral and overlaps the source mug region. Its continuation will remain suspended near the original mug height while its two short jaws separate laterally during release.
  • The background around the hanging object varies smoothly enough to reconstruct the newly exposed area by row-wise interpolation between the untouched pixels at x=107..111 and x=138..142, while retaining deterministic fine sensor noise.
  • Source corners, useful for checking that the camera remains fixed, are (89,99,124), (180,168,141), (47,47,47), and (85,85,85) clockwise from upper left.

METHOD.md

Method

The continuation was generated entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations. No image viewer, image-reading UI tool, external model, network source, physics engine, mask, or neighboring case was used.

Scene extraction

first_frame.png was decoded to its numeric RGB array with Pillow. NumPy statistics and printed RGB classifications located the coherent yellow component and the diagonal hard-surface boundary. The mug mask is recovered inside a measured central region using red/green/blue channel differences, connected-component selection from a seed inside the mug, small morphological closing, and a geometry constraint matching the measured oval rim, body, base, and handle. The resulting mask has 354 pixels and bounds (114,46) to (134,67).

The compact mug footprint is dilated by two pixels and reconstructed with OpenCV Telea inpainting. Every pixel outside that footprint is then restored from the source, so the camera, screen, hard support, illumination, and texture remain locked. The cool blue-black grabber jaws are analytic polygons with a subtle cool edge highlight; they separate symmetrically and remain suspended at the release point.

Motion and breakage

The intact mug uses the actual source RGB texture and mask. It is translated by [0, 0, 0, 1, 4, 9, 16, 24, 32] pixels through frame 08 and rotates gradually to 7 degrees. A small blurred ellipse on the measured contact surface supplies the tightening cast shadow.

At contact, the mug mask is partitioned into seven Voronoi ceramic pieces. Each piece retains the corresponding source texture. The pieces receive individual horizontal, vertical, and angular velocities; a deterministic integrator applies downward acceleration, collision against the measured sloped surface y = 122 - 0.17x, damped rebound, friction, and angular damping. Five tiny chips use the same outward/downward event. A brief low-opacity warm-gray dust puff expands and fades over six frames. The final output sample is held so that the broken ceramic is settled rather than perpetually moving.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, the (144,256,20) uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone HTML player. transition_check.png contains all 19 amplified adjacent-frame differences with changed-pixel counts. The generator then reloads pixels.npz and every PNG and asserts exact equality with the in-memory RGB frames. It separately asserts that frame 00 is byte-for-byte pixel-equal to first_frame.png and that no transition exhibits whole-frame/camera motion.
