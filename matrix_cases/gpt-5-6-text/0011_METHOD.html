ball-hits-nothing · 生成方法

The animation is produced entirely by generate.py from the local first_frame.png. I did not use an image viewer, image-reading UI, outside reference, network resource, learned model, mask, or external rendering engine.

Scene analysis

I loaded the source with Pillow as a numeric RGB array. Dimensions, regional RGB means, quantized-color counts, connected components of warm/dark color masks, row samples, and small RGB patches located the scene elements. Those measurements identified the moving orange tennis ball at about (54.6, 51.2), the smaller stationary red ball at about (109.4, 35.8), and the dark left-side pipe/opening. The measurements and frame-by-frame plan are recorded in OBSERVATION.md.

Generation

  1. Frame 00 is a direct array copy of first_frame.png; it is never redrawn.
  2. For frames 01–19, only the original ball and compact shadow footprint is reconstructed. A local OpenCV TELEA inpaint uses immediate boundary pixels from the gently varying tabletop. Pixels outside that explicit elliptical mask are restored byte-for-byte from the source, so the locked camera and all stationary objects remain fixed.
  3. The tennis ball center advances linearly from x=54.6 to x=253.0 at fixed y=51.25. This gives constant straight motion at the requested constant 4 fps and finishes at the right boundary.
  4. The ball surface is derived from bilinear samples of the actual source ball. Surface coordinates rotate by traveled-distance/radius (rolling without slipping). Source lighting is divided out approximately and a fixed analytic spherical light from upper left is applied at the destination. A restrained, rotating low-opacity curved seam adds a readable roll cue at the ball's small pixel size.
  5. A compact analytic Gaussian contact shadow moves beneath the ball. Soft coverage at the sphere edge and subpixel centers avoid hard one-pixel stepping.
  6. All computations are clipped and rounded into 8-bit RGB. artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. transition_check.png contains selected destination frames and 4x-amplified absolute RGB differences.

Verification

After saving, generate.py reopens all 20 PNGs and checks exact equality with the in-memory RGB frames. It checks that frame 00 is exactly equal to the source. It also reconstructs uint32 0xRRGGBB values and checks exact equality with the (144, 256, 20) tensor stored in pixels.npz.
