ball-rolls-on-glass · 生成方法

The sequence is generated locally and deterministically by generate.py. No network service, external model, external engine, other case, mask, or score is used. I did not use an image Read/View tool and did not visually review the result. Source understanding and revision used only local numeric RGB arrays, color thresholds, sampled values, gradients, line coordinates, connected components, hashes, and per-frame pixel-difference statistics.

Construction

  1. The script verifies the exact SHA-256 and 256 x 144 RGB dimensions of first_frame.png.
  2. Frame 00 is the unmodified source RGB array. In this run, frames/00.png is also byte-for-byte identical to first_frame.png (both SHA-256 values are 227cdec2a1f3a0e60b3c606dcaee3df19750b42fcf30b0528c888aecfdf6a415).
  3. A small elliptical mask, centered on the measured original ball and extended slightly over its contact shadow, is locally reconstructed with OpenCV Telea inpainting. The resulting clean plate is used only for frames 01-19. The wall, table, glass, exposure, and camera remain fixed.
  4. The source ball is isolated from its 31 x 31 RGB crop using its measured blue-minus-red chroma and a soft 11.4-pixel circular boundary. A normalized Gaussian decomposition separates smooth screen-fixed illumination from high-frequency fuzzy/color texture.
  5. The ball follows the 20 measured/planned center positions in OBSERVATION.md. Distance traveled along that polyline is divided by the measured radius to obtain physical roll angle. The texture residual rotates clockwise by that angle while the smooth illumination remains tied to the locked camera/light. Subpixel affine transforms and alpha compositing place the ball.
  6. A small soft tangent shadow follows the ball. As the center crosses onto the numerically measured glass quadrilateral, that shadow becomes lighter and a very faint, vertically compressed blue reflection is clipped to the pane. No other part of the scene is animated.
  7. The final frames carry the ball progressively through the right edge. Frame 18 retains a narrow trailing sliver and frame 19 completes the exit, preserving motion through the last transition.

Output and checks

artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, the (144, 256, 20) uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, containing selected transition frames and 4x-amplified absolute-difference panels.

The generator then performs these assertions:

  • array shape is exactly (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype is uint8;
  • frame 00 is pixel-identical to the source;
  • every saved PNG decodes pixel-identically to its in-memory frame;
  • every packed uint32 in pixels.npz matches the corresponding PNG RGB value;
  • all 19 adjacent transitions contain motion.

The final adjacent changed-pixel counts are:

[824, 704, 706, 726, 796, 849, 905, 924, 924, 926, 931, 949, 951, 948, 951, 951, 639, 269, 33]

Strong-blue connected-component centroids progress monotonically from (81.5,57.4) in frame 00 to (243.4,85.2) in frame 16, then clip at the right edge in frames 17-18 and disappear in frame 19. This numerically confirms the intended continuous table-to-glass traversal and exit.
