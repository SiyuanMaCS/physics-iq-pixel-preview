ball-in-sand · 生成方法

The animation is generated entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as the sole scene image. Frame 00 is copied directly from that RGB array with no compositing or re-encoding step before it enters the output tensor.
  2. Numeric RGB thresholds and measured geometry separate the round held ball from the blue-black grabber. The original ball RGB texture is retained inside a softly feathered analytic ellipse. The grabber is retained as two source-derived alpha layers.
  3. Pixels newly exposed by the release are reconstructed from unobscured gray-wall strips on both sides of the assembly, interpolated independently for each image row. This keeps the wall's original illumination gradient and subtle grain.
  4. The two grabber jaws open using opposite horizontal shears pivoted at the top border. The ball follows a hand-authored gravity/contact trajectory: acceleration into the sand, squash at impact, a low damped rebound, second contact, short rightward settling, and rest.
  5. The ball texture is translated and briefly rescaled during impacts. The two fastest falling frames receive a small vertical exposure blur.
  6. Contact shadows are analytic blurred ellipses. Sand shoulders, the shallow foreground cradle, and sparse ballistic grains use colors sampled exclusively from green-dominant pixels in the original sand pile. No new scene object or external asset is introduced.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally writes a six-row previous/current/amplified-difference transition sheet and asserts exact agreement among the in-memory frames, every PNG, and pixels.npz.

No image viewing tool, network resource, external model, reference case, mask, score, external engine, or subagent was used. Scene interpretation and revision relied on local numeric RGB statistics and array-derived measurements.
