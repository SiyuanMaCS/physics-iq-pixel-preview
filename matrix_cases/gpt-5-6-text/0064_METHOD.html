fill-glass-red-drink · 生成方法

The sequence is generated entirely with local Python, Pillow, and NumPy pixel operations. No image-view tool, external model, network source, external engine, or mask was used.

generate.py decodes first_frame.png as a 256×144 RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is a direct array copy. The source already contains an active narrow red stream at x≈160–165 and a partly filled receiving glass below it, so the animation continues that observed action rather than adding a new spout or changing the camera.

For frames 01–19, the script:

  1. Builds a supersampled antialiased mask for the rising liquid band inside the measured glass interior.
  2. Applies a locally luminance-aware translucent red tint, which keeps the woven basket texture visible through the clear glass as refraction/texture.
  3. Recolors the existing stream corridor with small analytic width and center variations and a moving specular highlight.
  4. Adds a curved, gently rippling meniscus and a compact impact highlight at the stream contact point.
  5. Leaves every pixel outside these masks equal to frame 00, maintaining the locked-off shot.

The fill surface rises smoothly from about y=96 to y=82 over 19 intervals. Motion is deterministic and generated at the required constant 4 fps.

The output tensor in pixels.npz is stored under key pixels, has shape (20, 144, 256), dtype uint32, and encodes each pixel as 0xRRGGBB. After saving the PNGs, the script decodes all 20 files and asserts exact equality with this tensor. inspection.png is a 5×4 contact sheet at native frame resolution; transition_check.png contains keyframes and 5× amplified absolute differences from frame 00.
