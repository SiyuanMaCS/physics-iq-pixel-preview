OBSERVATION.md

PixelWorld observation and event plan

Source measurements (numeric inspection only)

  • Source: first_frame.png, RGB, 256 x 144 pixels.
  • Camera: fixed 16:9 composition. No camera motion will be introduced.
  • The upper scene is a cool neutral gray backdrop/mirror arrangement; the lower scene transitions to a warm brown support surface.
  • A narrow dark/neutral vertical mirror-frame strip occupies approximately x=124..137. A nearly black static field begins around x=215 in the upper portion of the frame.
  • Foreground teapot: approximately x=76..121, y=51..102. Its silhouette is bulbous, with a small lid/top around y=51..62 and a broad body around y=63..99. Numerically sampled colors show a yellow lid/highlight, deep blue body regions, and red decoration.
  • Foreground black platform: approximately x=80..132, y=104..123. It remains stationary.
  • Mirror image: approximately x=167..211, y=44..79, with its reflected dark base around y=80..95. It is smaller and slightly higher than the foreground object because of the mirror perspective.
  • The source contains strong matching blue/red/yellow color groups in both the foreground object and its mirror image. The reflected silhouette also exposes a leftward red projection, consistent with the opposite/mirrored view.
  • The source luminance spans about 0.5..250.4, with the darkest connected static region at x=215..255, y=0..106.
  • Animation anchor points: foreground (99, 101), reflection (191, 79). The object bottoms stay registered to these anchors.

Motion design

The teapot completes one steady clockwise revolution in five seconds. The animation uses a vertical-axis turn, not an in-plane spin: horizontal texture phase, apparent width, shear, highlight position, and silhouette asymmetry evolve together. The mirror uses the same physical phase with horizontally reversed screen-space cues. The platform, room, mirror frame, and camera stay fixed. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the source.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

The sequence is generated locally with Python, NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. No image viewer, image-read UI, external model, network source, reference case, mask, or external rendering engine was used.

  1. first_frame.png is decoded as a 256 x 144 RGB array.
  2. The scene is measured through numeric RGB/chroma/luminance summaries, scanline ranges, connected components, and text-only coarse maps. Those measurements are recorded in OBSERVATION.md.
  3. Two soft polygon masks isolate only the visible foreground teapot and its smaller mirror image. The black foreground platform and reflected base are deliberately excluded so they remain fixed.
  4. A static plate is constructed by locally inpainting the two masked silhouettes with OpenCV Telea inpainting. Pixels outside the two compact working regions are never changed.
  5. Each teapot image is rendered as a vertical-axis turn. For every destination scanline, the renderer measures the source silhouette center and radius, maps screen x to a cylindrical surface angle, advances that angle by a constant 18 degrees per frame, and resamples the source texture bilinearly. Apparent width and a small height-dependent shear describe asymmetric parts turning in depth. This is a yaw/turntable motion, not an in-plane image rotation.
  6. Because only one source view exists, the unseen rear is conservatively synthesized from a horizontally mirrored, slightly darker and less saturated copy of the observed enamel texture. Front/rear sampling is joined with a smooth limb transition. A fixed screen-space curvature light preserves the locked studio illumination.
  7. The reflected layer uses the same physical phase but reverses the horizontal screen-space mapping and shear. It is composited before the foreground object. The result is synchronized physical rotation with the expected mirrored visual direction.
  8. Frame 00 bypasses all synthesis and is copied directly from first_frame.png. Frames 01–19 use phases 18–342 degrees. Camera, background, mirror frame, table, and both platform/base regions stay fixed.

Output construction

  • artifact_io.save writes the canonical pixels.npz tensor as uint32 0xRRGGBB with shape (144, 256, 20).
  • It also writes frames/00.png through frames/19.png, the eight-pose inspection.png, and the standalone delta-encoded index.html player.
  • generate.py writes transition_check.png as a labeled contact sheet of all 19 consecutive absolute differences, amplified four times over a dim registration image.

Verification

The generator reopens all 20 PNGs and asserts:

  • every frame is RGB and exactly 256 x 144;
  • the sequence has exactly 20 frames;
  • each decoded PNG, packed as uint32 0xRRGGBB, exactly equals the corresponding plane of pixels.npz;
  • frames/00.png exactly equals the decoded RGB pixels of first_frame.png.

Additional numeric temporal checks were performed without visually opening any image. Across transitions 00→01 through 18→19, full-frame mean absolute RGB change stays in a narrow 2.52–2.89 range. Changed pixels stay inside x=74..214, y=42..104, covering only the foreground/reflection work areas. Platform-region mean values remain stable to rounding throughout the sequence.
