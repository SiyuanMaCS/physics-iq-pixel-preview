METHOD.md

Method

I used only local numeric inspection and local pixel processing. I did not use an image Read/View tool, external model, network service, or rendering engine.

first_frame.png was decoded with PIL and measured with NumPy/SciPy-style numeric operations: RGB/luminance statistics, threshold masks, connected-component bounds, scan-line samples, and Sobel edge coordinates. Those measurements are recorded in OBSERVATION.md.

The animation is generated by generate.py as follows:

  1. Frame 00 is copied directly from the decoded source RGB array.
  2. Three source-derived polygon masks isolate the near/right domino group. A narrow polygon isolates the source arm. OpenCV Telea inpainting reconstructs only the small portions of wood/table and platform that those moving elements uncover.
  3. Each domino is a masked RGB cutout from the source. It is rotated in 2D about its measured bottom contact point with bilinear alpha resampling. The right black piece falls first, followed by the light piece and then the left black piece. Smoothstep timing avoids discontinuous angular velocity, and small one-frame settling offsets soften the stops.
  4. The wooden arm is drawn analytically as an anti-aliased, shaded rod with a highlight and short grain strokes. It revolves about the measured platform pivot from a leftward bearing toward the upper-left, which is clockwise on the visible clock face. A fixed dark/green hub covers the joint.
  5. Soft local shadows are transformed with the moving domino silhouettes; the camera, platform, distant domino group, and all other source pixels remain locked.
  6. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, all 20 PNG frames, the inspection sheet, and the standalone player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png and verifies every saved PNG and the packed tensor against the in-memory RGB frames.

The final timing is 20 frames at 4 fps (5 seconds). The final domino stops at the measured gap, so the farther group remains standing.
