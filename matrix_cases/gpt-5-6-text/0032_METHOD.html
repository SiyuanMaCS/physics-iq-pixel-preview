+def radial_alpha(size: int = 25, core: float = 7.35, edge: float = 9.45) -> np.ndarray:
+    """A compact, antialiased circular matte matching the measured ball silhouettes."""
+    c = (size - 1) / 2.0
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:size, :size]
+    d = np.hypot(xx - c, yy - c)
+    alpha = np.ones_like(d, dtype=np.float32)
+    alpha[d >= edge] = 0.0
+    feather = (d > core) & (d < edge)
+    t = (d[feather] - core) / (edge - core)
+    alpha[feather] = 0.5 + 0.5 * np.cos(np.pi * t)
+    return alpha
+
+
+def make_clean_plate(source: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
+    """Remove only the two initial balls and their tight contact shadows."""
+    mask = np.zeros((H, W), np.uint8)
+    for cx, cy in (BLUE_CENTER, YELLOW_CENTER):
+        # Circle covers the colored body; ellipse also covers its small footprint shadow.
+        cv2.circle(mask, (round(cx), round(cy)), 12, 255, -1, lineType=cv2.LINE_AA)
+        cv2.ellipse(mask, (round(cx), round(cy + 7)), (13, 5), 0, 0, 360, 255, -1,
+                    lineType=cv2.LINE_AA)
+    # TELEA uses only neighboring pixels and retains the stationary tabletop gradient/texture.
+    plate = cv2.inpaint(source, mask, 4.0, cv2.INPAINT_TELEA)
+    return plate

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Numeric observations from first_frame.png

  • Source is RGB, 256 x 144 pixels. The camera is locked and the table occupies the full image; the action happens in a narrow band around y=48..66.
  • Illumination is subdued and neutral. Median RGB rises from approximately (71, 66, 11) in the top 24 rows to approximately (134, 134, 130) in the bottom 24 rows. This establishes the tabletop's existing vertical tone/lighting gradient and must remain fixed.
  • The dark pipe structures enter at the extreme left and right edges. Their geometry and all background texture remain stationary.
  • The blue ball is the dominant connected blue component: 209 strongly blue pixels, chromatic bounding box x=85..101, y=49..64, center approximately (93.0, 56.9), visible radius about 9 px.
  • The yellow ball is the dominant connected yellow component: 216 strongly yellow pixels, chromatic bounding box x=170..186, y=48..63, center approximately (177.9, 55.8), visible radius about 9 px.
  • The centers begin 84.9 px apart. The slight 1.1 px vertical offset will be retained so the collision does not look mathematically sterile, while both centers follow the same tabletop-depth track.
  • Both objects need identical speed magnitude. For equal masses in a head-on elastic collision, their velocities exchange: visually they rebound with equal and opposite speed.

The listed x coordinates are ball centers; interpolation is subpixel and both balls always have equal speed magnitude. The blue/yellow y coordinates remain near 56.9/55.8. Rotation angle is tied to traveled distance with radius 9 px so there is no sliding.

Rendering constraints

  • Reconstruct only the two small source-ball/shadow footprints to obtain a clean plate; never alter the pipes, camera, crop, or tabletop outside those footprints.
  • Move source-derived ball appearance with feathered circular alpha, add compact contact shadows at the new positions, and rotate surface texture according to arc length.
  • Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at pixel level from the decoded RGB source.
  • Required diagnostics will show a contact sheet and adjacent-frame pixel differences without relying on image-view tooling.

METHOD.md

Method

What was generated

This is a locked-camera, single-shot 5-second sequence at 4 fps. Two source-derived tennis balls travel toward one another at the same 5-pixel-per-frame speed, make a compressed head-on contact on frame 07, and rebound with equal and opposite velocities. The pipes, crop, lighting, and table remain stationary.

Construction

  1. I inspected first_frame.png only by local code and numeric RGB measurements. I did not use an image Read/View tool. The measured colored components have centers near (93.0, 56.9) and (177.9, 55.8), with approximately 9-pixel radii.
  2. A clean plate was made by masking only the two ball bodies and their compact footprint shadows. OpenCV's local Telea inpainting reconstructed those small patches from neighboring tabletop pixels. This preserves the original background everywhere else.
  3. Each ball was extracted from the source with a feathered circular alpha matte. Edge RGB was decontaminated against the clean plate, while the ball's photographed shading and small-scale surface variation remained source-derived.
  4. The ball detail was bicubically rotated. Angle is computed from arc length (angle = distance / radius), with opposite signs for opposite motion, so translation and rolling remain coupled rather than sliding.
  5. Soft analytic contact shadows were multiplied into the clean plate underneath the new positions. The yellow ball, which is about 1.1 pixels farther up-table, is composited before the blue ball.
  6. On contact frame 07 only, both sprites are horizontally compressed to 88% and vertically expanded to 106%. This provides a brief readable tennis-ball impact without moving the camera or introducing unrelated effects.
  7. Equal-mass elastic collision behavior is represented by exchanging the translational velocities after contact. Both speed magnitudes remain 5 pixels per frame.

Output encoding and checks

  • frames/00.png is a byte-for-byte copy of first_frame.png; its decoded RGB is also asserted equal to the source array.
  • frames/01.png through frames/19.png are RGB PNGs at 256 x 144.
  • pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 tensor with the workspace-standard shape (144, 256, 20) (height, width, time). Each value is packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • The generator decodes that tensor back to RGB and compares every pixel against every PNG. It also verifies dtype, dimensions, frame count, and exact frame-00 equality. The latest verification report is stored in .tmp/verification.json.
  • inspection.png is a labeled 5 x 4 contact sheet of all frames.
  • transition_check.png contains key frames and 4x-amplified absolute differences for selected adjacent transitions, including both sides of contact.
  • index.html is the supplied serializer's standalone delta-packed player. It needs no external frame fetch and provides play, replay, loop, and frame-scrub controls at 250 ms per frame.

Reproduction

Run:

python3 generate.py

The process is deterministic and uses only local NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and the supplied source frame.
