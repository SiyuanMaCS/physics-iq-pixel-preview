OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation

The input was inspected only through local RGB arrays, threshold statistics, row spans, and coarse text maps. No image viewing tool was used.

Measured composition

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB. Camera is locked and the image is already softly shaded/photographic.
  • Background: low-saturation gray wall over the upper portion. The wooden tabletop begins at about y=82 (with the apparent boundary partly hidden by the objects) and fills the bottom of frame.
  • Kettlebell: very dark neutral object centered near x=141. The handle is cropped by the top frame boundary. Its two visible handle legs are approximately x=101..119 and x=159..177 at the top, merging into the body around y=20..27. The main body spans about x=104..172 at y=27, x=112..171 through its broad middle, and tapers to x=137..150 near y=80. A small cool-colored marking occurs around x=149..160, y=53..59.
  • Cup: bright, low-saturation styrofoam form under the bell, approximately x=126..164 and y=82..101. The bottom is fixed at the tabletop around y=101/102. Its shaded lower portion is about RGB 180/170/164 while the lit upper body reaches RGB 240..253.
  • Lighting: wall values are chiefly neutral RGB 110..165; table values are warm brown, commonly around RGB 90..125 / 65..90 / 35..60. The kettlebell is chiefly RGB 14..32 with soft gray edge highlights.
  • The 30 lb mass supplies about 133 N of static load. A thin disposable foam cup at this scale should buckle quickly rather than support the bell for the full shot.

METHOD.md

Method

I used only first_frame.png, prompt.txt, local Python code, and numeric RGB inspection. No image Read/View tool, network resource, external model, mask, reference, or rendering engine was used.

Construction

  1. I measured the wall/table split and the per-row RGB spans of the dark kettlebell and bright neutral cup. These measurements define source masks rather than a redrawn object.
  2. The kettlebell mask is the union of two handle-leg polygons and a row-traced body polygon, softened by a subpixel Gaussian edge. The original kettlebell RGB pixels, including the small colored marking, are translated downward intact.
  3. Because the original handle is cropped by the top edge, moving it down would otherwise expose an artificial straight cutoff. I extended the two handle arms into 20 rows of negative source coordinates by copying measured top-row arm texture and curving each arm gently inward.
  4. The cup mask combines a measured polygon with brightness/neutrality separation from the brown tabletop. Its lower edge remains fixed at y=102. A reverse-map warp compresses the original cup texture vertically, widens it modestly, and applies a small alternating lateral displacement to depict sidewall buckling.
  5. Pixels exposed behind the moving objects are reconstructed locally, one masked run at a time, by interpolating the actual RGB values immediately to the left and right on the same row. A deterministic low-amplitude texture term avoids unnaturally flat fill. The rest of the source frame is copied exactly.
  6. A restrained analytic contact shadow is applied at the cup/table contact. The cup is composited first and the kettlebell second, maintaining the original depth order.
  7. Compression follows [1.00, 0.93, 0.72, 0.43, 0.24, 0.18, 0.23, 0.20, 0.22, 0.21, ...]: rapid collapse, one small overshoot/rebound, then a stable crushed equilibrium. Kettlebell descent is coupled to lost cup height.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, 20 PNG frames, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, whose lower panels show 4x-amplified frame-to-frame RGB deltas. The script verifies:

  • shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and uint8 RGB frames;
  • exact byte-for-byte equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png;
  • pixel equality of every saved PNG with the in-memory frames;
  • exact equality of pixels.npz with the packed 0xRRGGBB frames;
  • no changes outside the bounded interaction region;
  • nontrivial final-state motion and a populated standalone index.html.

No claim of visual review is made; validation was numeric and structural.
