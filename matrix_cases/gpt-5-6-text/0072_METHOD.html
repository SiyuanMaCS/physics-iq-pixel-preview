Pixel construction

  • Frame 00 is a direct copy of the decoded source RGB array.
  • Later frames retain the complete source frame as the fixed background and alter only measured apparatus/glass regions.
  • The juice stream follows a quadratic Bézier curve from the nozzle toward the changing liquid surface. Flow width, reach, one-pixel sheen, nozzle bead, breakup, and final droplets are drawn as 4× supersampled analytic masks and reduced with Lanczos antialiasing.
  • The receiving liquid is clipped to a perspective-tapered glass polygon. It rises from approximately y=119 to y=105. A coral tint is alpha-composited over the original refracted pixels rather than replacing them.
  • Mixing uses a deterministic descending plume with spatial sinusoidal modulation. The source Canny edges are partially restored after tinting so rim/refraction detail remains crisp.
  • Surface ellipses, an impact depression, small splashlets, and deterministic bubble paths provide continuous fluid response. A restrained moving band within the measured reservoir interior indicates transferred volume.
  • Flow attacks smoothly, remains steady, then tapers into a short thread and detached drops. The last frame retains only settling liquid and a faint residual disturbance.

Packaging and checks

artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, the standalone player, inspection contact sheet, and a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor packed as 0xRRGGBB. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png, pairing selected frames with 4× amplified consecutive-frame differences.

The generator then decodes every saved PNG, repacks every pixel to uint32, and asserts exact equality with the corresponding pixels.npz plane. It also asserts that frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
