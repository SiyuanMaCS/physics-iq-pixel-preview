+def ellipse_mask(center, radii, feather=2.0):
+    """Return an anti-aliased elliptical mask over the full frame."""
+    cx, cy = center
+    rx, ry = radii
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:H, :W].astype(np.float32)
+    distance = np.sqrt(((xx - cx) / rx) ** 2 + ((yy - cy) / ry) ** 2)
+    edge = max(feather / min(rx, ry), 1e-4)
+    return np.clip((1.0 + edge - distance) / (2.0 * edge), 0.0, 1.0)

+    mask = np.zeros((H, W), np.uint8)

OBSERVATION.md

Numeric source observations and event plan

Source measurements

  • first_frame.png is RGB, 256 x 144. Pixel ranges are R 0–146, G 0–145, B 0–151, with mean RGB approximately (100.6, 97.4, 88.5).
  • The shot is fixed. The pale, low-contrast tabletop fills most of the frame from roughly y=30 downward. Its texture and illumination vary gently, so later frames should retain the source pixels wherever objects are absent.
  • A dark pipe enters from the left/upper-left. Its tabletop-side silhouette reaches roughly x=20–30 around y=40–55; the line from that side to the brown ball is shallow and points right with a small upward component.
  • The opposite pipe enters from the right. Its dark region occupies approximately x=239–255, y=47–76, with an outlet near (239,62) and an axis pointing left with a slight downward component.
  • A small brown tennis ball is already visible at about (133,45). Its strongly differing core spans about x=125–140, y=38–52, so its displayed radius is approximately 8 px. Its unobstructed continuation is a shallow right/up line, approximately (1.0, -0.09), which clears the vertically offset right pipe.
  • A larger grey tennis ball is already visible at about (57,77). Its object extent is approximately x=43–75, y=66–87, or about 14 x 11 px in radius. The line from the right outlet to the ball points left and slightly down, close to (-1.0, 0.09).
  • The two paths are visibly offset and do not intersect. Their unequal apparent sizes are consistent with the grey ball being nearer the camera on the lower part of the table.
  • All measurements above came from numeric RGB samples, threshold/component statistics, and printed pixel classifications; no image viewing tool was used.

20-frame event plan (4 fps)

  • Frame 00: exact, byte-for-byte pixel reproduction of the supplied first frame; both balls are already in flight/rolling away from their pipes.
  • Frames 01–03: both balls advance rapidly and linearly. The brown ball moves right/up at about 12 px per frame; the grey ball moves left/down at about 15 px per frame. Texture orientation changes to communicate rolling, and a short direction-aligned exposure trail communicates speed at the low frame rate.
  • Frames 04–05: the grey ball crosses the left boundary and disappears naturally. The brown ball continues along its upper straight track.
  • Frames 06–09: only the brown ball remains visible, rolling rapidly toward the right boundary with a subtle perspective increase in radius.
  • Frames 10–11: the brown ball crosses the right boundary and disappears.
  • Frames 12–19: the locked-off scientific setup remains at rest after both balls have left the field of view. No camera movement, new objects, cuts, or looping occurs.

The implementation will first reconstruct a clean tabletop under the two frame-00 balls with local texture-aware inpainting. Later balls will be translated cutouts derived from the source balls, with rotating internal texture, soft contact shadows, slight perspective scaling, and modest analytic motion blur. Pipes and all untouched scene pixels will remain sourced directly from frame 00.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

generate.py performs all generation locally with NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV pixel operations.

  1. It loads first_frame.png as a 256 x 144 RGB array and places that unmodified array at frame 00.
  2. It marks only the two measured ball footprints and reconstructs the tabletop beneath them using OpenCV's local TELEA inpainting. A very small amount of nearby source high-frequency texture is restored inside those footprints so the settled plate is not unnaturally flat.
  3. For each ball, the script subtracts the reconstructed plate from frame 00 inside a feathered ellipse. This produces a source-derived ball residual instead of inventing a differently styled object.
  4. That residual is separated into low-frequency shape/shading and high-frequency texture. Stable shading is translated and perspective-scaled without rotation; the fine texture is rotated by traveled distance/radius to communicate rolling while keeping the apparent light direction stable.
  5. The brown ball follows the measured upper corridor at (12.0, -1.1) pixels/frame. The grey ball follows the lower corridor at (-15.0, 1.35) pixels/frame. Both use constant speed, straight tracks, slight depth-appropriate scale change, a soft contact shadow, and a short direction-aligned exposure blur.
  6. Objects are clipped naturally at the image boundary. Once both balls leave, frames remain the reconstructed locked-off plate; there is no camera motion, cut, repeated launch, or loop in the generated frame sequence.
  7. artifact_io.save packs the 20 RGB frames as uint32 0xRRGGBB values in pixels.npz, writes the numbered PNGs, creates inspection.png, and embeds the delta-coded pixels into the standalone index.html player. The generator separately writes transition_check.png, containing all 20 frames in chronological order.

Verification performed

  • Array shape and type: exactly (20, 144, 256, 3) and uint8.
  • Frame 00: pixel-for-pixel equal to the decoded first_frame.png RGB array.
  • Packed tensor: exactly (144, 256, 20), uint32, and equal to a fresh 0xRRGGBB packing of the generated frame array.
  • PNG sequence: all 20 decoded PNG arrays equal their corresponding generated arrays.
  • Motion-region audit: the two frame-to-plate components advance monotonically on distinct upper and lower tracks. The brown component clears the right pipe above it; the grey component exits at the lower-left. Pixels outside the measured motion corridor remain unchanged.
  • Settled tail: after the balls leave, the remaining frames are identical, as expected for a locked-off setup.

All source interpretation and verification used local code and numeric RGB/array inspection. No image Read/View tool was used, so no claim of visual review is made.
