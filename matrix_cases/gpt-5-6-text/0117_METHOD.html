+def ellipse_alpha(cx: float, cy: float, rx: float, ry: float, feather=0.8):

+    d = np.sqrt(((xx - cx) / rx) ** 2 + ((yy - cy) / ry) ** 2)
+    # Convert the normalized edge distance to approximately pixel-sized feather.
+    edge_scale = min(rx, ry) / max(feather, 0.05)
+    return np.clip((1.0 - d) * edge_scale + 0.5, 0.0, 1.0)

+    """Translate a 2-D or RGB array vertically without wraparound."""

+        np.float32([[1, 0, 0], [0, 1, dy]]),
+        (W, H),
+        flags=interpolation,
+        borderMode=cv2.BORDER_CONSTANT,
+        borderValue=0,
+    )

OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation and event plan

Inspection constraints and source

  • Source: first_frame.png only, inspected with local Python/Pillow/OpenCV numeric RGB, HSV, edge, connected-component, scan-line, and circle measurements.
  • No image Read/View tool and no external reference were used.
  • Source format is RGB uint8, 256 x 144.
  • Global RGB mean is approximately (115.83, 105.67, 97.90); the upper scene is predominantly low-saturation gray, while the lower region is warm brown.

Measured scene layout

  • The camera is treated as locked. All walls, mirror edges, and lower surfaces stay pixel-stationary.
  • The primary orange cord occupies about x=109..113 from the top edge through y=68. It is slightly irregular rather than a perfectly straight synthetic line.
  • The dark magnet/coupling is approximately x=104..123, y=69..73.
  • The directly seen orange tennis ball has a detected center near (114, 81) and an apparent elliptical radius of roughly 10 x 9 pixels. Its colored extent is approximately x=104..124, y=73..90.
  • A second, mirror-produced ball image is clipped by the dark right mirror/frame edge. The visible reflected warm-colored lobe occupies about x=220..229, y=60..78; circle fitting places the implied center around (232, 69) with radius about 9 pixels.
  • A strong fixed dark boundary begins around x=231..233 in the upper/right scene. It must not translate with the reflected ball.
  • The lower/background boundary is diagonal on the left and changes into warm brown surfaces beginning around y=85..100 across the center/right. Moving-object removal therefore needs local background reconstruction rather than a flat fill.

Motion design

  • Motion is deliberately slow and continuous: a total downward travel of 19 pixels over frames 00..19 (one pixel per frame, 4 pixels/second).
  • Frame 00 is the exact unmodified source.
  • The ball and magnet move together. The cord above them remains anchored at the top and is extended downward using measured cord colors/texture, representing cord being paid out.
  • The visible mirror ball follows the same 19-pixel downward displacement. It remains clipped at the stationary mirror/frame edge.
  • No camera motion, new object, lighting change, bounce, rotation, or lateral drift is introduced.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

This sequence was made entirely with local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. No image-view tool, external asset, external model, web reference, mask, or rendering engine was used.

  1. Pillow loaded the source as a 256 x 144 RGB uint8 array.
  2. Numeric RGB/HSV summaries, scan lines, connected components, Canny/Hough measurements, and circle fits located the direct ball, the narrow connector/magnet, the orange cord, the clipped mirror image, and fixed mirror boundaries.
  3. The direct ball mask is an antialiased measured ellipse centered near (114, 81). A measured polygon covers its dark coupling. The irregular connector mask is derived from its RGB residual against a row-wise interpolated estimate of the gray background, constrained to the measured connector corridor.
  4. The reflected ball is an antialiased ellipse centered beyond the visible right mirror/frame boundary near (231.7, 69.2). It is clipped at x=230, so the stationary dark frame is never translated.
  5. OpenCV Telea inpainting reconstructs only the small areas hidden by the assembly and reflected ball in frame 00. The rest of the source stays unchanged.
  6. In frames 01..19, the photographed lower connector, coupling, ball, and reflected ball pixels are translated downward by exactly one additional pixel per frame. The top cord remains anchored and a 1-pixel orange core with faint antialiased sides is extended to the descending connector, using a palette measured from the source cord.
  7. Layers are alpha-composited over the stationary reconstruction. The fixed right mirror/frame pixels are then reasserted to preserve occlusion and camera lock.
  8. artifact_io.py packs each RGB frame to uint32 0xRRGGBB, saves pixels.npz, exports the 20 PNGs and inspection.png, and embeds the lossless frame deltas in index.html.

Motion and timing

  • 20 frames, 256 x 144 RGB, 4 fps, 5-second playback container.
  • Frame 00 is an exact pixel copy of first_frame.png.
  • Frames 01..19 descend 1..19 pixels respectively. There is no horizontal motion or camera motion.
  • The mirror image receives the same vertical displacement as the direct ball.

Verification

generate.py verifies all of the following on every run:

  • in-memory tensor shape is (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype is uint8;
  • frame 00 exactly equals the source RGB array;
  • pixels.npz is uint32, has shape (144, 256, 20), and exactly equals the packed in-memory tensor;
  • every decoded frames/00.png through frames/19.png exactly equals its source frame in memory;
  • every transition changes pixels and the changes remain within the measured local motion region;
  • a numeric transition sheet shows selected previous/current pairs and amplified RGB differences.

No claim of visual review is made. inspection.png and transition_check.png were generated as required artifacts, while assessment in this run used numeric pixel checks only.
