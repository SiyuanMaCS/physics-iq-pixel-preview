+def ellipse_alpha(center, radii, feather=1.25):
+    """Return a feathered elliptical alpha matte in source-frame coordinates."""
+    cx, cy = center
+    rx, ry = radii
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[0:H, 0:W]
+    d = np.sqrt(((xx - cx) / rx) ** 2 + ((yy - cy) / ry) ** 2)
+    # Full opacity over most of the ball, then a narrow antialiased boundary.
+    alpha = np.clip((1.0 + feather / min(rx, ry) - d) * min(rx, ry) / feather, 0, 1)
+    return alpha.astype(np.float32)

OBSERVATION.md

PixelWorld-Text observation and event plan

Numeric observations from first_frame.png

  • The source is RGB, 256 x 144 pixels. All inspection was performed with local Python/Pillow/NumPy numeric channel analysis; no image read/view tool was used.
  • The camera is locked. The table/background occupies the whole frame and becomes brighter toward the lower-right. Mean RGB rises from about (59,49,6) on row 0 to roughly (136,137,134) around row 120. This fixed shading and the visible pipes must not move.
  • A large dark pipe/background component reaches from the upper/left edge through approximately x=0..122, y=0..51. A second dark pipe component is attached to the right edge around x=232..255, y=19..82. These are treated as static scene geometry.
  • The blue tennis ball is the saturated blue connected component at x=68..84, y=43..57, approximately center (76,50), with a 17 x 15 pixel visible footprint.
  • The yellow tennis ball is the saturated yellow-green connected component at x=139..158, y=50..66, approximately center (149,58), with a 20 x 17 pixel visible footprint.
  • The center-to-center direction from blue to yellow is (73,8), so the intended line of travel is a shallow down-right axis. The source centers are about 73.4 px apart. The combined apparent collision radius is about 18 px.
  • Ball motion will be equal and opposite along that measured axis at 4 px per frame per ball. This produces head-on contact near the midpoint (112.5,54) and, for equal masses, an elastic exchange/reversal of velocities. The balls remain at the same speed before and after the collision.

Intended construction

Frames 01-19 will use a ball-free background made by tightly masked local inpainting of the two source ball footprints. Source-derived, feathered ball sprites will then be translated along the measured axis, rotated to show rolling, supplied with small moving contact shadows, and briefly deformed at impact. Frame 00 will bypass all processing and remain byte-for-byte equal to the decoded source RGB pixels.

METHOD.md

Method

I generated the continuation with deterministic local Python pixel operations. I did not use an image viewing tool, external model, external engine, network resource, reference case, mask, or score.

first_frame.png was decoded as RGB with Pillow and measured numerically with NumPy channel tests and connected components. Those measurements located the blue ball near (76,50), the yellow ball near (149,58), and established the center-to-center motion axis (73,8).

Frame 00 is the unmodified decoded source array. For frames 01-19, OpenCV's local Telea inpainting fills tightly enlarged masks over the two original ball footprints to produce one fixed background. This background never moves, so the camera, tabletop, and pipes remain locked. Feathered elliptical alpha mattes extract the source ball pixels. Premultiplied source RGB and alpha are transformed with cubic affine resampling to translate and rotate each ball. Small blurred ellipses darken the table beneath the moving contact points to supply moving shadows.

Both centers advance exactly 4 pixels per frame in opposite directions along the measured axis. At central contact, each ball receives an 8% one-frame compression along the collision axis and matching transverse expansion. For the equal-mass head-on response, both velocities reverse and retain equal magnitude. Source texture rotation also reverses with the translational direction, giving a rolling rather than sliding cue.

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, PNG sequence, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png and verifies: source equality for frame 00, every PNG against the in-memory RGB frame, and every packed PNG against its pixels.npz tensor slice.
