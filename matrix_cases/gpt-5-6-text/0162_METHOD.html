OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, inspected only with local Pillow/OpenCV/NumPy numeric RGB operations and text output. No image viewing tool was used.
  • Raster: 256 x 144 RGB. The four corner RGB values are (77,56,37), (184,157,146), (115,83,45), and (143,87,38).
  • The background is a warm, softly varying wall above a reddish-brown table. The table/setup transition has its strongest horizontal gradients around rows 94-101.
  • The Newton's-cradle frame occupies approximately x=47..164. Detected structural lines include the left support from about (47,27) to (58,111), the right support around x=143..156, and upper rails around y=8..25.
  • The blue-black grabber is the coherent blue component at approximately x=7..61, y=61..85, with dark handle pixels extending to the left frame edge.
  • A broad Hough candidate occurred near (69.5,77.5), but direct RGB samples there are brown background. The tool-tip geometry and compact neutral highlight instead localize the held-ball footprint near (60,84), with a rendered sphere radius of about 10 px. It lies between the grabber jaws and the resting chain.
  • The hanging chain and overlapping metal balls occupy roughly x=78..141, y=86..128. Their perspective-projected contact direction slopes down about 0.21 px per x-pixel.
  • The cradle's dark base/frame continues through about x=52..164, y=114..143 and must remain stationary.
  • Lighting is diffuse from the upper left. Moving balls retain a bright upper-left metallic highlight and darker lower-right shading.

Motion design

The animation uses a fixed camera and 4 fps sampling. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte in pixel content. A clean plate is synthesized locally from neighboring RGB patches for only the initially lifted ball/grabber region; the cradle, table, and background stay locked. Analytically shaded chrome balls follow pendulum arcs, while the collision phase transfers velocity to the opposite ball in the familiar Newton's-cradle pattern.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is generated entirely with local Python pixel operations (NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV). No external model, external engine, reference image, mask, network resource, or image viewing tool is used.

first_frame.png is loaded as RGB and placed unchanged at frame 00. Numeric RGB thresholds isolate the blue-black grabber. Soft circular masks identify the initially raised ball and the rightmost resting ball. OpenCV's local Telea inpainting fills only those temporary foreground footprints from neighboring source pixels; a measured section of the rear upright is reconnected with two narrow lines where the tool had hidden it.

The grabber is alpha-composited from its source pixels while translating out of frame. The two moving balls are shaded analytic chrome spheres with a diffuse upper-left key, a narrow specular glint, a cool upper reflection, a warm table reflection, and an antialiased dark rim. Suspension cables are drawn behind each sphere. Their centers follow circular pendulum arcs. At contact, the moving endpoint changes from the left ball to the right ball, then back again with a smaller amplitude, modeling the familiar momentum transfer and damping of a five-ball Newton's cradle.

The camera, cradle frame, four nonmoving balls, base, table, and background are all locked. The event is sampled at 4 fps for 20 frames. artifact_io.save packs RGB pixels as a (144,256,20) uint32 tensor in 0xRRGGBB order, writes the PNG frames and inspection sheet, and embeds the exact frame stream in the standalone player.

transition_check.png is a programmatic diagnostic sheet: each pair shows a later transition frame beside a three-times-amplified absolute-difference heatmap. Verification reloads every PNG and the NPZ, reconstructs RGB from the packed tensor, requires exact equality, and requires the frame-00 PNG file hash to equal the source PNG file hash. Because no image-view tool was used, no visual-review claim is made.
