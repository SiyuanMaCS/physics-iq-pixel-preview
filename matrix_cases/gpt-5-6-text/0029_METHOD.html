ball-train · 生成方法

I treated first_frame.png only as a local numeric RGB array; I did not use an image Read/View tool or an external model.

The source geometry was measured from color and edge statistics. Frame 00 is the decoded source RGB array with no edits. For later frames, I reconstructed the small table area beneath the original balls by interpolating clean RGB samples along each affected scan line, adding back a small amount of source table grain, and feathering the replacement over the three measured ball/shadow regions.

Each ball uses a 25×25 source-derived texture. The unobstructed gray ball comes directly from its measured source crop. Because the brown and orange balls overlap in frame 00, each hidden side is completed by mirroring pixels from that ball's visible side. Circular analytic alpha, mild fixed-direction sphere shading, a restrained curved felt seam, and a compact blurred contact shadow preserve the source lighting while allowing clean translation and rotation. Texture/seam rotation is proportional to horizontal travel.

Motion follows a manually parameterized one-dimensional damped collision. The gray ball moves right at 4 px/frame until contact at frame 07. The gray ball then rebounds by 3 px, the brown ball shifts 4 px, and the already-touching orange ball receives most of the transmitted impulse, rolling from x=164 to x=201 with decreasing frame-to-frame increments. A very small one-frame horizontal squash marks each compression stage. The table, pipe, lighting, and camera remain fixed.

transition_check.png contains numeric-rendered collision frames and amplified per-pixel transition deltas. It is a generated diagnostic, not a claim of visual review. generate.py finally verifies frame-00 equality, every PNG against the in-memory RGB frames, and pixels.npz against the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor.
