OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Numeric observations

  • Source is RGB, 256 x 144. The camera is locked and the full lower field is a warm brown wood surface.
  • The green paper occupies approximately x=69..208, y=0..95. Its outer silhouette has extrema near (69,47), (118,20), (208,0), and (111,95).
  • The paper is not a plain convex sheet in projection. Numeric chroma scanlines show two separated green intervals above y=34, forming a V-shaped starter notch. The notch converges around (136,32) and the remaining joined section narrows toward (111,95). This supplies a physically plausible tear origin.
  • The left blue-black grabber occupies about x=0..75, y=26..58 and contacts the left paper corner around (69,47). The right grabber occupies about x=183..255, y=0..32 and contacts the opposite upper-right region around (193,24).
  • A pale blue band at the very top-left is separate from the compact grabber silhouette and is treated as static background.
  • The paper pixels are strongly chromatic green (typical core near RGB (15,180,42)); the tools contain saturated blue and near-black pixels; the table is dominated by RGB values around (120,105,78).
  • Motion must remain a single, real-time, fixed-frame scientific demonstration. No camera movement, cuts, text overlays, extra objects, or artificial effects will be introduced.

Intended construction

The exact first frame will be retained. Subsequent frames will use local RGB segmentation, table inpainting, soft alpha mattes, and analytic piecewise warps. The two paper regions will be displaced on opposite sides of a deterministic jagged seam, with displacement tapering to the current tear front until separation is complete. Extracted blue-black grabber pixels will translate with their corresponding held corner. Torn edges and a few short fibers will be drawn from measured paper/table colors, then every output will be verified against the packed 0xRRGGBB tensor.

METHOD.md

Method

Summary

I generated the continuation entirely with local deterministic RGB operations. I did not use an image viewer, image-read tool, external model, external engine, network source, reference case, mask, or subagent. Scene understanding and review were based on array statistics, thresholded RGB scanlines, connected components, contour coordinates, bounding boxes, centroids, and per-transition pixel measurements.

Construction

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is copied from that array without modification.
  2. The green paper is identified from its unique green-minus-red/blue chroma. A connected-component filter rejects unrelated pixels while preserving the measured V-shaped notch. A one-pixel expansion and soft matte retain antialiased borders.
  3. The missing paper texture under the clamps is locally reconstructed by extending the nearest measured green-paper RGB pixel, followed by a small blur. This produces no invented object; it only completes the occluded sheet surface needed when a clamp moves.
  4. The blue-black clamps are selected by blue chroma and near-black luminance within the two measured clamp regions. Their alpha edges are softly filtered, but their RGB comes directly from frame 00.
  5. Pixels initially covered by the moving paper and clamps are filled with OpenCV Telea inpainting from the surrounding tabletop. Every pixel outside that removal region remains exactly the source RGB value.
  6. The tear follows the measured diagonal from approximately (136,32) to (111,95). Two low-amplitude sine terms add deterministic paper-scale irregularity. From frames 04-14, the tear front advances down this line with smoothstep timing. The still-joined region below the front is overlaid in its original registration to form a narrowing bridge.
  7. The left half and clamp move left/down; the right half and clamp move right/up. Both use local affine translation plus less than about one degree of rotation around their measured contacts. Displacement is gentle during propagation and increases after separation to represent recoil.
  8. Soft shadows, pale green fracture faces, and a few one-pixel antialiased fibers at the live crack tip are analytic overlays derived from the paper geometry. They remain attached to the tear and do not become particles.
  9. The camera, background outside the working area, exposure, resolution, and frame cadence remain fixed.

Timing

  • 20 frames, 256 x 144 RGB, 4 fps, 5 seconds.
  • Frames 01-03: tension and notch opening.
  • Frames 04-13: progressive tearing.
  • Frame 14: complete separation into two connected green components.
  • Frames 15-19: outward recoil and settling.

Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png and then verifies:

  • frame array shape is (20,144,256,3) and dtype is uint8;
  • frame 00 exactly equals first_frame.png;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, and stores 0xRRGGBB values;
  • every saved PNG repacks exactly to its corresponding tensor plane;
  • every adjacent pair contains motion.

The second numeric review confirmed that the sheet changes from one green connected component to two at frame 14, the two centroids separate afterward, and all changed pixels remain above row 108. transition_check.png contains amplified true-RGB absolute differences for six representative adjacent-frame pairs; it is diagnostic rather than a rendered animation frame.
