OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation and event plan

Inspection method

I inspected first_frame.png only as a 256×144 RGB array. I used numeric RGB/HSV summaries, luminance and Sobel/Canny edge measurements, connected components, Hough line coordinates, and printed text maps. I did not open or view the image with an image-reading or image-viewing tool.

Scene measurements

  • Source: 256×144 RGB PNG. Corner RGB values are (157,155,168), (2,0,1), (105,77,52), and (127,102,79) from top-left clockwise.
  • The upper-left field is predominantly neutral gray around RGB (165,163,171). The upper-right region from about x=215 to the right edge is nearly black until about y=85. The lower field is warm brown/red and starts on an irregular diagonal between about y=48 and y=96.
  • A compact dark assembly is measured at x=134..159, y=29..61. Its darkest connected portions at a luminance threshold of 25 occupy x=135..157, y=38..59. This is treated as the beverage-dispenser outlet, with a flow origin near (151,59).
  • A short glass-rim edge is measured near x=140..178, y=68..82; a detected rim segment runs from approximately (143,74) to (167,72), and the right rim/side appears around x=176..179. The receiving-glass interior is conservatively modeled as the trapezoid from about (140,77) and (176,73) down to (145,138) and (175,140).
  • The initial juice is represented by the existing warm pixels inside the glass. Edges and highlights from the source frame are retained; animation changes are restricted to a soft interior mask and the short air gap from the outlet to the rim.
  • The outlet-to-rim gap is only about 15–20 pixels, so the water stream should be narrow (roughly 2–4 pixels), translucent, slightly irregular, and brightest on one edge. Splash features should remain tiny.

Twenty-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00: untouched source frame, exact pixel identity.
  • Frame 01: the valve has just released; a short clear droplet forms below the outlet.
  • Frame 02: the droplet elongates downward through roughly half the gap.
  • Frame 03: the first narrow water filament reaches the glass rim/juice surface.
  • Frame 04: continuous flow establishes; a small bright impact crown appears.
  • Frame 05: steady translucent stream, first concentric surface ripple.
  • Frame 06: stream shifts subtly left; a few pale entrained bubbles appear below impact.
  • Frame 07: stream returns toward center and the grapefruit juice begins a barely perceptible rise.
  • Frame 08: steady pour; ripple widens and the upper juice is slightly diluted/lighter.
  • Frame 09: stream narrows momentarily while the impact sparkle moves right.
  • Frame 10: full flow returns with a second small ripple cycle.
  • Frame 11: surface rises another fraction; bubbles descend and drift outward.
  • Frame 12: stream bends subtly right, maintaining a continuous connection.
  • Frame 13: a tiny splash bead lifts beside the impact point.
  • Frame 14: bead falls back; the top layer becomes a little paler from mixing.
  • Frame 15: steady stream and subdued ripple; liquid level continues its slow rise.
  • Frame 16: a small leftward stream wobble and fresh micro-bubbles.
  • Frame 17: the stream straightens; surface nears its final level below the rim.
  • Frame 18: flow remains established with a last bright impact glint.
  • Frame 19: stable final pour, slightly raised and diluted juice, no camera motion.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is generated locally from first_frame.png with deterministic NumPy and Pillow pixel operations. No external model, network service, renderer, reference image, or image-viewing tool is used.

Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the RGB-array level from the source. Frames 01–19 keep the locked source frame as their background. The motion is restricted to the measured outlet-to-glass region:

  1. A four-times-resolution RGBA layer draws a narrow, slightly time-varying translucent water filament from the measured dispenser mouth near (151,59) toward the measured glass surface. The layer includes a dim refractive edge, a narrow specular edge, and a rounded leading bead in the first two frames. It is Lanczos-downsampled for subpixel antialiasing before compositing.
  2. A soft trapezoidal mask follows the measured glass interior. Over the pour, the existing warm source pixels are blended very lightly toward a paler grapefruit color, strongest near the surface, to suggest dilution while retaining the photograph's texture and glass edges.
  3. The liquid surface rises by about 4.6 pixels over the sequence. Thin elliptical arcs, a moving glint, occasional tiny splash beads, and three coherent bubble paths show impact and mixing without disturbing the fixed camera or surrounding scene.
  4. artifact_io.save packs every RGB frame into the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, writes the 20 PNG frames, creates the inspection contact sheet, and embeds the same packed pixels into the standalone HTML player.

transition_check.png contains four before/after frame pairs with 8× amplified absolute RGB differences. It is intended for localized-transition inspection; it is generated numerically and was not visually reviewed with an image tool.
