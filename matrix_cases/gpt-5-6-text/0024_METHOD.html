ball-rolls-off · 生成方法

I generated the continuation entirely with local Python pixel operations. I did not use an image viewer, an image read tool, an external model, a renderer, a network service, or another case.

The source frame was inspected numerically with RGB statistics, scanlines, connected color components, and local pixel tables. Those measurements located the ball at approximately (109, 76) with a radius of 7–8 pixels and identified its small contact shadow below it.

generate.py performs four operations:

  1. It copies first_frame.png exactly into frame 00.
  2. It builds a static plate by applying OpenCV Telea inpainting only to a compact mask around the original ball and contact shadow. All other source pixels are retained unchanged.
  3. It derives a soft circular matte and screen-space lighting/color model from the original ball. In frames 01–19 this source-matched ball is composited at measured positions moving from x=116 to x=211, immediately before the numerically detected dark right boundary. A compact Gaussian contact shadow moves underneath it.
  4. It adds a subtle two-branch curved tennis seam. The seam rotates clockwise by horizontal_distance / ball_radius, while the lighting remains fixed in screen space, which conveys rolling without rotating the highlight.

The camera, pipe, table, background, exposure, and every pixel outside the declared motion corridor remain static. The trajectory has mild friction-like deceleration and a one-pixel downward perspective drift. The ball does not bounce or leave the frame.

The script writes all 20 PNGs and the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor through artifact_io.save, creates inspection.png, creates a separate transition_check.png with representative frames and amplified consecutive deltas, and regenerates the standalone index.html player.

Finally, the script reopens every PNG and pixels.npz, reconstructs their values, and asserts exact equality. It also asserts that frame 00 exactly equals the source and that all pixels outside the motion corridor are identical to the source across frames.
