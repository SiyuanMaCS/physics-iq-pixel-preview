OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Numeric scene measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, RGB, 256×144. All measurements below came from local RGB arrays, gradients, connected edge components, and scan-line statistics; no image viewer or image-reading tool was used.
  • The locked frame separates into a cool neutral upper background and a warm wood-toned lower field. The strongest broad horizontal change is around y=64–66.
  • The grapefruit-juice dispenser occupies roughly x=80–190 above and around the horizon. Its juice-bearing glass body is predominantly warm red/brown; a dark central fixture is concentrated around x=118–136, y=35–64.
  • The receiving glass is centered under that fixture. Repeated near-vertical edge responses occur around x=107–109 and x=148–150 down to about y=128, with a curved/broken base response near y=134. The usable interior is therefore conservatively bounded by x=110–147 and y=75–132.
  • Bright rim/specular samples occur near the top of that interior (including a near-white sample around x=115, y=76). To preserve these source cues, generated liquid is composited beneath a restored highlight/edge layer rather than painted across the whole glass.
  • The lower interior becomes unusually smooth below about y=106, consistent with the existing water volume seen through the glass. The animated mixture begins there and rises toward y≈88, leaving headroom below the rim.
  • The pour axis is approximately x=128–130. The nozzle-to-rim gap is short, while the falling jet remains visible through the glass above the rising surface.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

I loaded first_frame.png locally with Pillow as an RGB uint8 array. I did not use an image Read/View tool. Scene placement was inferred from numeric RGB statistics, luminance/color scan lines, gradients, Canny connected components, and repeated edge coordinates. Those measurements and the planned motion are recorded in OBSERVATION.md.

generate.py keeps the camera and every source pixel fixed, then generates frames 01–19 by compositing only within the measured receiving-glass/pour region:

  • an anti-aliased analytic grapefruit-juice jet follows a narrow, slightly time-varying centerline from the dispenser outlet;
  • startup and shutdown are explicitly staged as a bead, short thread, continuous stream, taper, and stopped flow;
  • the liquid is a softly masked, slightly tapered volume inside the photographed glass, with a level rising from about y=107 to y=88;
  • the underlying source texture is color-graded rather than replaced, preserving refraction and wood texture visible through the water/glass;
  • a curling Gaussian plume, phase-changing meniscus/ripples, impact highlight, and two small rising bubble lenses provide localized mixing motion;
  • source sidewalls, bright rim pixels, and specular highlights are blended back over the liquid composite to retain the original glass structure.

The procedure is deterministic and uses only NumPy/Pillow pixel operations. It does not call external models, engines, networks, or other cases.

Artifact construction

artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, packs each pixel as uint32 0xRRGGBB into a tensor shaped 144×256×20 in pixels.npz, creates inspection.png, and embeds delta-compressed frames into the standalone index.html player.

transition_check.png is generated from eight consecutive-frame comparisons. Each tile is a magnified absolute RGB-difference heat map labeled with changed-pixel count and mean channel delta. It is a numeric diagnostic, not a claim of visual review.

Verification

After saving, generate.py reloads every PNG, repacks its RGB channels to uint32, and asserts exact equality with the corresponding pixels.npz slice. It also asserts that frame 00 is exactly equal to first_frame.png. Changes are confined to the central pour/glass region; the rest of the locked shot remains byte-identical to frame 00.
