Actual generation method

I used only local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png.

  1. Loaded the 256×144 RGB source with Pillow and measured the orange component with numeric RGB thresholds and per-row run lengths.
  2. Fitted the visible basketball to a circle centered at (144.5, 23.5) with a 29.5 px radius. The photographed RGB texture—including its highlights and dark seam markings—was sampled into a local RGBA sprite. Analytic one-pixel edge coverage supplies antialiasing. The approximately five-pixel cap clipped by the source's upper boundary was restored by nearest-row texture extrapolation.
  3. Reconstructed the area hidden by the original ball scan-line by scan-line. Each row blends robust median colors sampled immediately to the left and right of a 32 px cleanup circle, with a small amount of nearby high-frequency row texture. This naturally reconstructs neutral wall in the upper rows and the darker crate rim in the bottom rows.
  4. Moved the sprite through a hand-sampled vertical gravity trajectory: accelerating descent, first hidden impact, a damped rebound, second hidden impact, a smaller rebound, and final settling. A subpixel right drift and very small rotation keep the motion organic. Anisotropic squash is applied at the two impact samples, although it is mostly hidden by the crate.
  5. Composited the sprite only above y=53. Below that line, the original crate front remains fully opaque, preserving all photographed plastic edges and slot highlights and making the ball correctly pass behind the crate instead of overpainting it.
  6. Kept the camera, background, right-edge dark structure, crate, tabletop, wood grain, and lighting fixed. Frame 00 is the untouched source array.
  7. Serialized the 20 uint8 RGB frames with artifact_io.save: PNGs, the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. transition_check.png contains labeled before/after pairs and 4× amplified absolute differences for representative transitions.

Verification

generate.py performs these checks after every run:

  • tensor shape is exactly 20×144×256×3 RGB uint8;
  • frame 00 exactly equals first_frame.png;
  • pixels.npz is uint32 with shape 144×256×20;
  • every tensor value equals the corresponding PNG's packed 0xRRGGBB value;
  • all 20 PNG files decode exactly to the generated RGB arrays;
  • specified visible-motion transitions contain changed pixels.

An additional numeric audit confirmed zero changes outside x=110–180, y=0–53 for frames 01–19. No image Read/View tool or visual-review tool was used; scene assessment and revision were based on local numeric RGB inspection only.
