ball-behind-rotating-paper · 生成方法

This sequence was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations. I did not use an image viewer, image-read tool, network resource, external model, external engine, mask, reference case, or subagent. Source inspection consisted of numeric RGB statistics, scanlines, color clustering, edge/contour bounds, and text-only downsampled classifications.

Reconstruction and animation

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is copied directly from that array and bypasses all drawing.
  2. A deterministic static plate is made for generated frames by masking only the measured original cardstock/grabber footprint. OpenCV Telea inpainting provides an initial fill, then horizontally interpolated source-side samples restore the wall/table continuity. Seeded low-amplitude grain prevents a textureless patch.
  3. The cardstock face is extracted from the source image. Dark pixels belonging to the overlapping grabber are locally inpainted. The resulting real source texture is projected into a changing four-corner perspective quad with cv2.getPerspectiveTransform and Lanczos resampling.
  4. Clockwise rotation is represented as yaw of a vertical panel: its projected width narrows according to cosine, near/far vertical edges skew slightly, illumination falls gently toward the far edge, and a thin paper rim becomes visible. The bottom stays aligned to the platform instead of spinning as a flat screen-space rectangle.
  5. The orange tennis ball is analytic local rasterization: a circular coverage field, Lambertian sphere normal, upper-left diffuse/specular light, deterministic nap grain, and two curved seam strokes drawn at 4× resolution. Its hand-keyed center follows a smooth down-right arc.
  6. Layer order is static plate → ball/contact shadow → ball → cardstock → foreground grabber. Consequently, the ball's shrinking orange crescent is genuinely covered by the warped card, rather than being erased by a timed opacity effect. It is fully hidden at placement, then the continued card yaw exposes only a thin side crescent of the settled ball.
  7. The grabber is an antialiased 4× vector raster with a near-black shaft, a dark blue highlight, collar, articulated hook jaws, and rubber pads. It follows the ball to platform height, opens, and retreats upward-left while the ball remains fixed.
  8. Generated changes are feather-limited to the measured central action region. The camera, background, table, and all pixels outside that region remain source-exact.

Timing

The output is 20 frames at a constant 4 fps (5 seconds). The ball is exposed above the card in frames 01–05, progressively crosses behind the left card edge in frames 06–11, reaches the platform behind it in frames 12–13, and is released while the grabber withdraws in frames 14–19. The cardstock rotates throughout and eases into its final yaw.

Verification

generate.py asserts the (20, 144, 256, 3) uint8 shape, exact frame-00 equality, and uniqueness of all frames. After artifact_io.save, the final checks compare every PNG pixel to the uint32 packed 0xRRGGBB tensor stored in pixels.npz. The tensor layout is (144, 256, 20). inspection.png is the required keyframe sheet; transition_check.png contains opening keyframes, occlusion/release/final keyframes, and 8× amplified adjacent-frame RGB differences. These diagnostics were created and checked numerically, not visually reviewed with an image tool.
