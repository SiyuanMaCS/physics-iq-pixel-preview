Numeric scene observation and event plan

Inspection constraints and method

first_frame.png was inspected only by local Python array operations. No image viewer, image-read tool, vision model, external engine, network resource, mask, or other case was used. The RGB array is uint8, shape (144, 256, 3), with 10,570 exact RGB colors. Measurements below come from RGB/luminance statistics, connected components, Canny/Hough geometry, and downsampled numeric color maps.

Scene measurements

  • The source is exactly 256 x 144 RGB.
  • The camera is locked. The upper field is a dark brown/neutral background; the light wooden tabletop occupies the lower field and becomes lighter toward the foreground. The broad table/background transition is near y=70 at left.
  • A dark tube approaches from the left. Its circular mouth is centered near (50, 56), with an apparent outer radius of about 14 px and a usable inner radius of about 9 px. A Hough-circle search consistently selected (50, 56, r=14). Its center is dark (mean luminance about 58 within 5 px), while the asymmetric brighter rim reaches about 80–90 luminance. This is the tube mouth, not a free ball. No distinct free tennis ball is visible in frame 00.
  • The tube/ball contact line is around y=70; a ball center near y=61 with radius 9 px therefore sits naturally on the tabletop.
  • The white/grey lampshade is central and foreground of that travel line. Its measured silhouette is approximately the polygon (123,28) -> (165,36) -> (166,93) -> (149,124) -> (110,112) -> (99,52). Bright neutral connected components cover roughly x=99..165, y=29..124. It must occlude the rolling ball as the ball passes behind it.
  • The shot has subdued, warm illumination. The ball should be grey rather than fluorescent green: mid-grey felt around RGB (116,120,121), a soft upper-left highlight, a darker lower-right falloff, short fuzzy edge fibers, and a pale curved tennis-ball seam.
  • The tabletop texture and all static objects remain bit-identical outside the small ball/shadow footprints and antialiased motion regions.

Physics and visibility plan

The ball radius is 9 px. Its center stays at y=61 (subpixel vertical variation under 0.4 px), so its lower edge tracks the table surface at about y=70. Horizontal motion uses a smooth, slightly decelerating real-time roll. Texture and the curved seam rotate by distance/radius (theta = distance / r) rather than merely sliding. A small soft contact shadow follows the ball. The lamp silhouette is restored after ball compositing, giving physically correct foreground occlusion.

METHOD.md

Generation method

What was generated

The result is a 20-frame, 256 x 144 RGB animation at 4 fps. The camera and all photographed scene content remain locked. A grey tennis ball emerges from the left tube, rolls right along the tabletop, passes behind the foreground lampshade, reappears, and exits the right edge.

Source inspection

The input was inspected only as local numeric RGB arrays. I used array shape and statistics, luminance/color clustering, connected components, Canny edges, and Hough geometry. I did not use an image Read/View tool, vision model, external engine, external reference, network resource, or another case. I did not perform visual review; inspection.png and transition_check.png are generated artifacts for downstream review.

Numeric analysis identified:

  • the tube mouth at approximately (50, 56), outer radius about 14 px;
  • the tabletop contact line near y=70;
  • the foreground lampshade silhouette spanning about x=99..166, y=28..124;
  • the ball travel line at center y≈60.8, with radius 9.2 px.

The detailed observations and 20-frame timing plan are in OBSERVATION.md.

Pixel-generation technique

generate.py uses first_frame.png as the unmodified base for every frame. Frame 00 is a direct array copy of that source.

For frames 01–19, the script adds only:

  1. A small, soft contact shadow that darkens the existing table pixels, retaining the photographed wood texture.
  2. A deterministic 4x-supersampled analytic sphere. Its fixed-world diffuse shading and highlight create volume; several rotating procedural grain bands suggest grey felt; a pale S-shaped curve supplies the tennis-ball seam; and a slightly rough deterministic alpha boundary suggests felt fibers.
  3. Distance-based rotation, theta = -distance / radius, so the seam and felt rotate consistently with rolling distance while illumination remains fixed in screen space.

The measured tube-lip pixels are restored over the ball during its first two emergence frames. The original lampshade pixels are restored through an antialiased polygonal matte after ball compositing, producing partial occlusion on entry and exit and complete occlusion in the middle. No source inpainting or camera transformation is used.

Ball centers by frame are:

hidden, 54, 65, 77, 89, 101, 113, 125, 137, 149, 161, 173, 185, 197, 209, 221, 232, 243, 254, 265
