+def add_contact_shadow(frame: np.ndarray, cx: float, clip_x: float | None) -> None:
+    """Paint a small soft table-contact shadow directly under the sphere."""
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[0:HEIGHT, 0:WIDTH].astype(np.float32)
+    dx = (xx + 0.5 - (cx + 0.8)) / 6.2
+    dy = (yy + 0.5 - (BALL_Y + BALL_RADIUS - 0.2)) / 1.35
+    strength = 0.16 * np.exp(-0.5 * (dx * dx + dy * dy))
+    # While emerging, the tube mouth/lip hides the portion behind its front plane.

+    a = strength[..., None]
+    src[active] = src[active] * (1.0 - a[active]) + shadow_rgb * a[active]

OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation and event plan

Inspection method

first_frame.png was inspected only by local Python/Pillow array reads and numeric RGB analysis. No image viewing tool was used. The source is RGB, 256×144. Its mean RGB is approximately (111.24, 105.17, 95.17) and its channel extrema are (1,0,0) to (181,191,215).

Measured scene layout

  • Locked camera and static scene. The table fills the central and lower field; its sampled colors are muted warm gray/light wood, generally around RGB (118–132, 108–126, 98–118).
  • A bright, cool-white lampshade occupies approximately x=113–166, y=4–43. Pixels in this region commonly have blue-biased highlights above 160 luminance. It is entirely above the intended rolling line, so the ball will pass below it without collision or occlusion ambiguity.
  • A dark tube enters from the left. Its pointed/outlet end resolves as an irregular, near-circular dark patch around x=99–117, y=49–69, centered near (108,59). The surrounding left-side tube/body remains fixed.
  • The outlet contains very dark brown/neutral pixels but no distinct, separately bounded tennis ball. Frame 00 is therefore treated as the moment immediately before release; the first visible ball is introduced in frame 01.
  • The uncluttered lateral rolling corridor is near y=59–67. A 14-pixel-diameter ball centered at y=60 contacts the table near y=67 and remains below the lampshade.

Motion and rendering plan (20 frames, 4 fps)

The background is copied exactly from the source in every frame. Frame 00 is the untouched source. A textured brown tennis ball of radius about 7 px then emerges from the tube and rolls right at constant speed. Its screen-space texture rotates clockwise by translation/radius, while screen-fixed directional shading and a small contact shadow establish rolling rather than sliding. The initial ball is clipped at the outlet plane so it appears to emerge from inside the tube.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

The sequence is generated locally by generate.py with Pillow and NumPy. The source image was not visually opened with an image tool. Scene geometry was inferred from numeric RGB samples, threshold runs, color clusters, and coarse numeric category maps, as recorded in OBSERVATION.md.

The original 256×144 RGB source is copied as the locked background of every frame. Frame 00 is never drawn into and is also copied back to frames/00.png as the literal original PNG, giving both exact pixel equality and byte-for-byte file equality.

Frames 01–18 add a procedurally rendered brown tennis ball:

  • center path: constant-speed horizontal motion from the tube outlet near (107,60) toward and across the right border;
  • radius: 7 pixels;
  • emergence: subpixel samples left of the measured outlet plane are clipped in frames 01–02;
  • shape: analytic projected sphere with 4×4 subpixel coverage per output pixel;
  • material: deterministic brown fine-grain variation and a curved tan tennis seam;
  • rolling: material coordinates rotate clockwise by distance / radius, so texture rotation matches translation;
  • lighting: screen-fixed Lambert shading from the upper-left, independent of material rotation;
  • grounding: a restrained Gaussian contact shadow directly below the ball.

Frame 19 is the unchanged locked background because the ball has fully cleared the right border. No camera motion, background synthesis, external engine, external model, network access, mask, or reference case was used.

Artifact creation

artifact_io.save writes the canonical (144,256,20) uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor to pixels.npz, all 20 PNG frames, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, a sheet containing consecutive before/after pairs at release, early emergence, mid-roll, and exit.

Verification

The generator verifies all of the following on every run:

  1. frame tensor shape is (20,144,256,3) and dtype is uint8;
  2. frame 00 equals the decoded source array exactly;
  3. frames/00.png is byte-for-byte identical to first_frame.png;
  4. pixels.npz has shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, and equals the frame stack packed as 0xRRGGBB and transposed to (H,W,T);
  5. every decoded frames/00.png–frames/19.png is RGB 256×144 and exactly equals its corresponding generated array.

Numeric post-generation inspection also checks changed-pixel bounds, per-frame maximum RGB deltas, and contrast-weighted motion centroids. It found a monotonic horizontal track at approximately y=60, with modifications confined to the local ball/shadow corridor. No claim of visual image-tool review is made.
