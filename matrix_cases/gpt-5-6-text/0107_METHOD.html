OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation and event plan

Inspection method: local Python/Pillow/OpenCV numeric RGB, HSV, connected-component, Canny, and Hough-line measurements only. No image viewer or image-reading tool was used.

Source measurements

  • Source is RGB, 256 x 144. Frame is a locked, slightly muted photographic view.
  • The whiteboard/apparatus occupies approximately x=50..211 for the full frame height. Its left and right uprights are near x=52..55 and x=203..211. Everything outside it remains static.
  • The initial yellow glass marble is the small isolated warm-yellow component at x=172..177, y=24..28, centroid (174.3, 26.2). Its source pixels reach HSV saturation about 175 and it is approximately 6 x 5 pixels in the source.
  • The upper blue ramp has measured long edges from about (196, 27) to (127, 36) and (199, 38) to (127, 46), an inclination of about -7 degrees. The marble touches its upper edge at the upper-right portion.
  • The next yellow/ochre ramp spans about x=72..146, y=45..65 with long-edge inclination about +7 degrees.
  • The middle blue ramp spans about x=131..204, y=64..82 with long-edge inclination about -7 degrees.
  • The lower green ramp spans about x=69..144, y=84..101 with long-edge inclination about +7 degrees.
  • A purple catch/exit piece occupies about x=134..164, y=105..125.
  • The measured arrangement makes a zigzag: the marble traverses each ramp downhill, briefly free-falls through each alternating outside gap, changes horizontal direction on contact, and ends in the lower catch piece.
  • The camera, board, ramps, supports, exposure, and background do not move. Only the marble and its tiny contact shadow change.

Generation constraints

  • Frame 00 will be copied directly from first_frame.png with no decode/re-encode modification in the frame tensor.
  • For frames 01..19, the source marble will be removed once with a small, deterministic, edge-aware local reconstruction mask. The otherwise untouched source becomes the static plate.
  • The moving marble will be rendered analytically at photographic scale with antialiasing, warm-yellow glass shading sampled to match the source's muted range, a moving specular glint, mild transparency/refraction, and a small contact shadow. No external assets or models are used.
  • The final catch-frame marble will be layered behind a copied front-lip patch from the source so the stop reads as a real insertion rather than a floating disc.

METHOD.md

Method

The continuation is a deterministic 2D photographic pixel composite written in generate.py. I inspected first_frame.png only through local numeric RGB/HSV values, connected components, Canny edges, and Hough-line coordinates; I did not use an image viewer, image Read/View tool, external model, external asset, or network resource.

The decoded source image is used as the locked static scene. Frame 00 is assigned directly from that decoded RGB array. For later frames, the original 6 x 5 yellow marble at measured centroid (174.3, 26.2) is removed with a tightly bounded OpenCV Telea inpaint ellipse. No other scene element is regenerated or moved.

The moving marble follows a hand-specified, numerically measured zigzag path over the four alternating ramp lines and into the lower purple catch. Its 6-7 pixel glass body is analytic: 4 x 4 subpixel circle coverage, amber rim, warm yellow body, partial background refraction, a broad upper-left light, a small rotating internal glint, and a soft contact/projection shadow. Short outside-gap frames are treated as free fall; contact frames receive a subtle squash. On frame 19, pixels from the source purple front lip are copied back over the marble so that it is partially occluded inside the catch.

artifact_io.save writes the twenty RGB PNGs, the compressed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, then reconstructs RGB from pixels.npz and asserts exact equality against every PNG and the in-memory frames. It also asserts exact RGB equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png.
