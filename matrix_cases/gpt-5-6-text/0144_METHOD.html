OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation

Inspection method: first_frame.png was loaded locally as an RGB NumPy array. I used channel comparisons, row/column summaries, connected-color extents, and sampled RGB values only. I did not use an image viewing tool.

Measured scene layout

  • Source: 256×144 RGB, fixed camera.
  • Main action is concentrated in the narrow central region, roughly x=92–143.
  • The irregular brown potato occupies approximately x=104–133, y=9–60; its center is near (119, 35), with a tapered lower edge.
  • The glass spans approximately x=92–143. Its mouth/upper rim is near y=72–75 and its rounded base is near y=130–140.
  • The green water-level band is the strong green horizontal feature at y=76–82, across nearly the full glass width.
  • Blue/cyan liquid begins immediately below the marked level, near y=83, and continues through the lower glass. Typical liquid pixels are near RGB (0, 80–110, 95–115).
  • The grabber is aligned with the potato in the central setup and has dark blue/black and pale reflective parts against a muted gray-green background.
  • The lower background/table region becomes brown and dark below roughly y=102–120. Nothing outside the central setup needs to move.

Event plan (20 frames, 4 fps)

METHOD.md

Method

Source analysis

I read first_frame.png locally with Pillow as a 256×144 RGB array. No image Read/View tool, network resource, external reference, mask, score, model, or rendering engine was used. Scene measurements came from RGB channel relations, scanline runs, bounding boxes, sampled colors, and row/column statistics.

The potato's brown skin gives a reliable scanline outline from y=8 through y=58. For each row, the code finds the left and right brown edge and fills between them, which deliberately keeps the pale central potato face/highlight as part of the object. A narrow measured polygon identifies the adjacent dark/reflective grabber shaft.

Frame synthesis

  • Frame 00 and the held frame 01 use the original source pixels. frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png.
  • OpenCV's local Telea inpainting reconstructs only the initially occluded potato/grabber footprint. This clean plate is fixed; the rest of the source frame never moves.
  • The original potato texture and grabber pixels are transformed with affine translation and very small potato rotations. The vertical drop first accelerates and then damps after water entry. The grabber retracts upward.
  • The underwater potato is composited with a depth-dependent cyan absorption layer and reduced red transmission. The source green level band is then laid back in front, retaining the marked-glass appearance.
  • Surface depression, narrow splash tongues, concentric elliptical ripples, a few one-pixel bubbles, and final surface shimmer are drawn analytically with antialiased local primitives. The effects remain inside the central glass setup.
  • The camera, lighting, background, table, and overall frame remain locked throughout.

Timing

The output is 20 frames at 4 fps (5 seconds). It has a short held pose, release and accelerated fall, surface entry around frames 07–09, maximum modest splash around frames 09–10, underwater damping through frames 11–16, and a settled ending through frame 19.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, pixels.npz, the standalone HTML player, and the inspection sheet. transition_check.png contains keyframes plus 3× absolute RGB differences from frame 00.

The generator verifies:

  • shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype uint8;
  • exact source equality for frame 00;
  • tensor shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32;
  • exact equality between every PNG and the corresponding packed 0xRRGGBB tensor slice;
  • an intentional unchanged transition from frame 00 to 01 and nonzero motion thereafter;
  • compact changed-pixel bounds, preventing changes from escaping the central setup.

Review was numeric only; I did not claim visual inspection.
