Source analysis

I inspected first_frame.png only by local Python RGB-array statistics, sampled coordinates, thresholded connected components, row runs, and numeric/ASCII summaries. I did not use an image viewing or image-reading tool. Measurements and the per-frame plan were recorded in OBSERVATION.md before generate.py was written.

Pixel construction

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV for local deterministic pixel operations:

  1. The saturated yellow and cyan-blue pixels are thresholded inside measured local boxes. Per-row extrema are filled to recover the mug and block silhouettes, including their darker internal rim/specular pixels. These masks are lightly feathered for subpixel warping.
  2. A cubic two-dimensional color surface is least-squares fitted to unobstructed illuminated-wall pixels. The fit supplies the hidden wall color underneath the two large source shadows.
  3. The source wall-shadow density is measured as the luminance deficit relative to that fitted wall. Separate broad-mug and narrow-block density maps preserve the real source's uneven interiors and penumbras. The original shadow locations are cleared with a soft fitted-wall blend.
  4. The old mug, block, and the small reflected color directly below them are removed from the platform with local Telea inpainting. No external fill, model, image, or asset is used.
  5. For frames 01–19, the two base-contact points rotate at constant angular velocity through 32 degrees. Rotation occurs in a top-plane coordinate system projected to the image with a 0.36 vertical squash around estimated platform center (155,144), creating a perspective-compressed clockwise elliptical orbit. Objects remain upright under gravity. Projected contact-y controls painter order.
  6. The extracted RGB object pixels are affinely moved to the new contacts. Distance causes a small scale change. The blue cuboid widens slightly with changing azimuth, while the nearly axial mug retains its form. Modest fixed-light gain changes prevent lighting from appearing painted onto the rotating platform.
  7. Soft contact shadows and subdued colored platform reflections remain attached to each moving base. The measured wall-shadow maps translate by a slightly magnified version of each object's displacement, accounting for projection from the fixed low spotlight; their size and density change gently with distance.
  8. Frame 00 bypasses all reconstruction and is copied directly from first_frame.png, guaranteeing exact RGB equality.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in (144,256,20) order, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, containing three before/after transition pairs and 5× amplified absolute RGB differences.

After writing, the script independently reloads pixels.npz and every PNG. It asserts:

  • array shape (20,144,256,3) and RGB uint8 frames;
  • exact frame-00 equality with the supplied PNG;
  • tensor shape (144,256,20) and uint32 dtype;
  • exact equality between every packed tensor pixel and every generated frame;
  • exact equality between every reloaded PNG and its in-memory RGB frame.

No claim of visual review is made; inspection and revision were based on numeric RGB measurements and terminal summaries as required.
