OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • first_frame.png is RGB, 256×144. The camera is locked off.
  • The upper background is a softly graded neutral gray. A warm brown wooden surface begins around y=84–96 (the boundary slopes slightly across the image).
  • A dark, nearly black oval/cylindrical platform occupies approximately x=99–176, y=94–139.
  • The teapot is centered near x=136 and occupies approximately x=92–181, y=20–97.
  • Numerically separated teapot regions are: a dark blue body around x=101–161, y=39–96; an olive/gold lid and knob around x=119–156, y=20–41; a dark/red loop-like handle on the left around x=92–111, y=35–81; and a red spout on the right around x=150–181, y=41–95.
  • The background corners are gray at the top (roughly RGB 160–175) and warm brown at the bottom (roughly RGB 103–106, 75–76, 48–50). The platform is substantially darker than the table.
  • All of these measurements came from local Python RGB arrays, clustering, threshold masks, scan lines, and connected-component/range reports. No image viewing tool was used.

Motion interpretation

The platform and camera remain fixed. The teapot turns clockwise about a vertical axis at a constant slow angular rate. The round central body changes little in silhouette, while its surface shading drifts with the yaw. The left handle and right spout progressively foreshorten toward the center line. For the chosen clockwise direction, the right spout comes slightly toward the camera and downward in the image, while the left handle moves slightly away and upward. Occlusion order changes accordingly: handle behind body, spout in front.

METHOD.md

Method

I generated the sequence entirely from first_frame.png with deterministic local Python pixel operations (numpy, OpenCV, and Pillow). No external images, masks, models, engines, or network resources were used.

The source was inspected numerically through RGB samples, HSV thresholds, clustered-color bounding boxes, scan-line runs, and connected components. A constrained GrabCut segmentation isolates the teapot from the gray backdrop, wooden surface, and black platform. Its foreground seeds are dark or chromatic pixels inside hand-measured polygons covering the lid, body, handle, and spout. The original frame is kept byte-for-byte as frame 00.

For frames 01–19, a clean plate is reconstructed only behind the source teapot with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. The segmented teapot is represented as premultiplied RGB and alpha; its photographed boundary pixels are retained, and bilinear remapping supplies subpixel coverage on moved edges. A continuous inverse warp then simulates a 0°–38° clockwise yaw at a constant 2° per frame. The near-symmetric center changes very little, while pixels farther from the vertical axis receive stronger cosine foreshortening. A small signed vertical displacement makes the right spout move toward/down in the image and the left handle move away/up, supplying depth parallax. A subtle side-dependent tonal adjustment follows the yaw and preserves the existing light direction. The camera, background, tabletop, and black platform remain fixed.

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, RGB PNG frames, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. transition_check.png contains selected frames plus amplified frame-00 and interval difference maps. The generator finally reopens every PNG and verifies exact equality against the in-memory frames and the transposed uint32 tensor.
