+def source_sprite(source: np.ndarray, center: np.ndarray, size: int = 25):
+    """Return a softly matted, premultiplied source-derived ball sprite."""
+    patch = cv2.getRectSubPix(source, (size, size), tuple(float(v) for v in center))
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:size, :size].astype(np.float32)
+    mid = (size - 1) / 2.0
+    radius = np.hypot(xx - mid, yy - mid)
+    # The measured colored components span 18-19 px.  This narrow feather
+    # retains the photographed edge antialiasing while excluding tabletop.
+    alpha = np.clip((10.05 - radius) / 1.05, 0.0, 1.0)
+    alpha = cv2.GaussianBlur(alpha, (0, 0), 0.28)
+    premul = patch.astype(np.float32) * alpha[..., None]
+    return premul, alpha.astype(np.float32)

+def reconstruct_tabletop(source: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
+    """Numerically remove the two source balls and their compact shadows."""
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:H, :W].astype(np.float32)
+    mask = np.zeros((H, W), np.uint8)
+    for center in (BLUE0, YELLOW0):
+        circle = (xx - center[0]) ** 2 + (yy - center[1]) ** 2 <= 12.2**2
+        # Illumination in the source comes mostly from frame-right, so include
+        # the small down-left contact shadow in the cleanup region.
+        shadow = ((xx - (center[0] - 2.0)) / 12.5) ** 2 + (

OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • Inspection method: local Pillow/NumPy RGB reads, threshold masks, per-row spans, and connected components only. No image viewer or image-reading tool was used.
  • Source is RGB uint8, 256 x 144. Channel extrema are (0,0,0) to (205,249,205) and channel means are approximately (111.41,110.33,102.13).
  • The tabletop occupies most of the frame. Its neutral/light samples increase from roughly (90,90,84) in the upper-middle to (145,155,160) toward the lower/right area. Brown, dark edge structures occur mainly along the upper-left diagonal edge and at the upper-right/lower-left corners; these are treated as the two fixed pipe/edge assemblies and are never moved.
  • A blue connected component has 258 strongly blue pixels with bounding box x=111..129, y=63..80, centroid (120.1,71.3). Its apparent diameter is about 19 px.
  • A yellow connected component has 241 yellow pixels with bounding box x=177..194, y=32..49, centroid (185.5,40.5). Its apparent diameter is about 19 px.
  • The center-to-center vector from blue to yellow is (65.4,-30.8), length about 72.3 px. The motion axis therefore uses unit vector approximately (0.905,-0.426). The balls are placed on this same axis and are already outside their edge pipes at frame 00.
  • Both balls will have the same projected center speed, 4.52 px/frame (18.1 px/s), in opposite directions. With an 18.0 px contact separation, first contact occurs at frame 06. Equal-mass, near-elastic head-on collision is represented by exchanged velocities: because the velocities are equal and opposite, each colored ball reverses direction.
  • Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at pixel level from first_frame.png; synthesized cleanup/motion begins only on frame 01.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is a deterministic local pixel synthesis implemented in generate.py.

  1. first_frame.png was read as a 256 x 144 RGB array. Scene measurements came from numeric RGB thresholds, connected-component bounds, row spans, sample values, and array statistics. No image Read/View tool, external model, network source, or external engine was used.
  2. The measured blue and yellow balls are approximately 19 px across, centered at (120.1,71.3) and (185.5,40.5). Soft circular alpha mattes extract each photographed ball as a premultiplied RGB sprite.
  3. A stable tabletop plate is reconstructed once by masking the two original balls plus their small contact-shadow areas, then applying OpenCV's local Telea inpainting with a 3 px radius. This only uses neighboring pixels from the supplied frame. All pipe and edge structures remain source-fixed.
  4. The center axis is the normalized measured vector from blue to yellow. Both ball centers move at exactly 4.5241 px/frame in opposite directions. The speed is chosen so the measured 72.3 px starting separation becomes an 18.0 px contact separation on frame 06.
  5. The impact uses the one-dimensional equal-mass elastic solution. The balls exchange velocities; with equal and opposite incoming speeds, each colored ball reverses direction without a speed mismatch. Frame 06 adds a restrained 5% axial squash and 4% transverse expansion. Fixed-direction soft contact shadows keep the balls grounded.
  6. Rolling is conveyed by inverse-mapped rotation of the source ball texture. Angular displacement is translational displacement divided by the 9 px apparent radius. Angular velocity reverses after the collision together with linear velocity.
  7. Frame 00 is the untouched source array. Frames 01-19 use the stable reconstructed plate and source-derived moving sprites. artifact_io.save writes all PNGs, the 0xRRGGBB uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. transition_check.png contains selected before/after frames and 4x-amplified numeric RGB differences; it was generated diagnostically and was not visually reviewed.
  8. The generator reloads pixels.npz and every saved PNG, repacks the frame arrays to uint32, and asserts exact equality. It also asserts exact array equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png.
