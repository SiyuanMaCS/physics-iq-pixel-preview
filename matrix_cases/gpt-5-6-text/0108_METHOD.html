OBSERVATION.md

Source-frame observations

All observations below come from local RGB-array inspection of first_frame.png; no image viewer or image-read tool was used.

  • Source is already RGB, 256 x 144 pixels. It will be used unchanged for frame 00.
  • The shot is locked off. The useful whiteboard/run area is approximately x=29..166; there are stable dark vertical boundaries near x=28 and x=162..167.
  • The source is dim and warm/neutral overall (mean RGB approximately (111, 109, 108)). The board therefore photographs as gray/brown rather than pure white.
  • Numeric Canny/Hough measurements find the main alternating ramp edges at approximately:
    • upper descending-right edge: (44,11) to (96,25);
    • descending-left edge: (149,29) to (86,36);
    • descending-right edge: (43,43) to (103,53), with a lower edge continuing through (102,63);
    • descending-left edge: (151,64) to (94,68), with its lower edge near y=74..76;
    • descending-right edge: (45,75) to (101,88), with a lower edge through (93,94).
  • A small round/glassy candidate is numerically detectable near (142,20) (roughly radius 4-5), above the high end of the first clearly traversable leftward ramp. A localized yellow-gold patch also occurs around (130,28). These measurements motivate a small translucent amber/yellow marble, about 4.5 px radius, starting near (142,20).
  • The only animated subject will be the marble (plus its tiny contact shadow/reflected highlight). All whiteboard, ramp, and camera pixels remain locked.

METHOD.md

Method

I generated the sequence entirely with local Python pixel operations. I did not use an image viewer, image read/view tool, network resource, external model, mask, reference case, or external rendering engine.

  1. Loaded first_frame.png as a 256 x 144 RGB NumPy array and inspected its numeric RGB statistics, color clusters, connected components, Canny edges, and Hough line segments.
  2. Used the measured alternating line segments to define a 20-sample zig-zag marble trajectory through the photographed magnetic ramp assembly. The path includes supported rolling phases and short gravity-driven transitions at the ramp ends.
  3. Copied the source pixels exactly into frame 00.
  4. For frames 01-19, made one tiny locked background plate by OpenCV TELEA-inpainting the approximately 6-pixel-radius source marble location at (142,20). No other scene region is synthesized or moved.
  5. Rendered a 4.35-pixel-radius translucent amber/yellow glass sphere analytically from per-pixel sphere normals. Its appearance combines warm diffuse shading, a darker glass rim, a fixed upper-left specular reflection, a lower caustic, and a rotating internal amber band. The photographed background remains partly visible through the sphere.
  6. Added a very small soft contact shadow and two low-opacity, velocity-aligned shutter samples. Drop frames use slightly more blur; the scene and camera remain completely static.
  7. Saved all outputs with artifact_io.save, generated transition_check.png from amplified consecutive RGB differences, and then reloaded every PNG and pixels.npz to verify byte-for-byte agreement with the rendered frame tensor.

Numeric validation

  • Frame tensor: 20 x 144 x 256 x 3, uint8, RGB.
  • Packed tensor: 144 x 256 x 20, uint32, values encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png is exactly equal to first_frame.png.
  • Consecutive changed-pixel counts are small and localized (148-239 pixels after the initial replacement), consistent with a fixed camera and one moving marble.
  • 34,802 of 36,864 spatial pixels (94.406%) stay identical to frame 00 for the entire sequence.
  • Validation was numeric; no visual-review claim is made.
