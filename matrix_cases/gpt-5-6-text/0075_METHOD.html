I generated the continuation entirely with local Python (numpy, Pillow, and OpenCV) and numeric inspection of first_frame.png. I did not use an image viewer, an image Read/View tool, a network source, another case, a mask, an external model, or an external rendering engine.

Frame 00 is the original RGB array without modification. For frames 01–19, I formed a clean plate by scanline-interpolating across the measured wall-shadow footprint and locally inpainting the measured blue connected component from adjacent turntable pixels. The wall interpolation is restricted to x=122–142, y=58–103; the object reconstruction is restricted to its lower-center neighborhood.

The platform and block advance by 7.5° clockwise per frame. The block is an analytic shallow cuboid: plan-view corners rotate rigidly, then the turntable's depth axis is compressed by a 0.66 projection factor. Antialiased polygon masks render the top and four short side faces. A fixed cyan spotlight response, narrow highlight, small procedural surface variation, and contact shadow reproduce the blue material's measured range. Existing low-contrast pixels inside a fixed lower ellipse are inverse-warped by the same angle and blended at 16%, moving material texture while retaining mostly fixed specular lighting and a stationary silhouette.

The wall shadow is coupled to that same angle rather than animated independently. Its measured initial center is (132,83), with half-vectors (0,20) and (7,0). The rotating wall projection expands the horizontal component of the long axis, and two antialiased layers create a hard warm-black umbra plus a narrow penumbra. The set, camera, spotlight pool, and surrounding wall remain fixed.

artifact_io.save packs RGB into a (144,256,20) uint32 tensor using 0xRRGGBB, writes the 20 PNGs, creates the inspection sheet, and embeds delta-coded pixels in the standalone player. The generator additionally creates transition_check.png, verifies exact equality of frame 00 to the supplied source, then decodes every saved PNG and compares it with both the in-memory RGB frames and pixels.npz.
