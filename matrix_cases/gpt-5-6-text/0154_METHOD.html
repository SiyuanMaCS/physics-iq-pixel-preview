METHOD.md

Method

This continuation was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations (numpy, Pillow, and OpenCV). No image viewer, image-reading tool, network service, external model, asset, mask, reference, or rendering engine was used.

  1. I inspected the supplied frame through decoded RGB values, quantized-color counts, scanline runs, coarse numeric tile summaries, connected components, and threshold-derived bounding boxes. I did not visually review the source or generated images.
  2. Frame 00 is the supplied first_frame.png exactly. The original PNG is copied byte-for-byte to frames/00.png after artifact serialization.
  3. For later frames, a compact mask around the initial tennis ball and contact shadow is locally inpainted from surrounding tabletop pixels. All source pixels outside that mask remain exact and static.
  4. The ball follows a measured diagonal tabletop path from the lower right toward the kettlebell. Its apparent radius decreases slightly with perspective. An analytic sphere normal provides directional shading; deterministic high-frequency functions add subtle felt texture; a pair of rotating curved seams conveys rolling.
  5. A low, blurred elliptical shadow remains attached to the table beneath the ball. At impact the ball compresses slightly along its direction of travel, recoils by roughly two pixels, and settles. The much heavier kettlebell and every other scene element stay fixed.
  6. artifact_io.py serializes the 20 RGB frames to the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, frame PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone player. transition_check.png contains representative frames plus 4x-amplified adjacent-frame RGB differences.

Validation is numeric: shapes/dtypes, exact first-frame equality, PNG-to-tensor equality, finite frame count, localized inter-frame changes, center trajectory, and required-file presence are checked without claiming visual review.
