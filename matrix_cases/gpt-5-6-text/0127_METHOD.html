OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation

All measurements below were obtained with local Python/Pillow/OpenCV array operations and printed numeric RGB, cluster, edge, and connected-component statistics. No image viewer or image-reading UI was used.

Source measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, RGB uint8, 256 x 144.
  • Global channel mean: RGB (123.72, 106.65, 94.38); channel range RGB (6..229, 5..218, 0..221).
  • Camera: static/locked. The upper field is a softly graded neutral gray backdrop; the lower field is a warm brown wooden surface. The sloped visual boundary occurs around y=42 at the right and y=50--70 toward the left.
  • Dish: approximately x=91..208, y=79..117. Its light-blue/cyan liquid occupies the central ellipse, approximately x=99..196, y=80..114, centered close to (147, 100). The strongest dish/lower-rim edge response lies at y=110..117.
  • Paper towel: the dominant bright low-chroma component local to the apparatus is 1,977 pixels with bbox x=146..179, y=20..97 and centroid (161.2, 60.9). Its silhouette is a narrow, slightly skewed vertical strip: narrower near the caught top and widening slightly toward the bottom.
  • Grabber: a narrow articulated vertical element immediately left of the towel, mainly x=128..145; it enters at the top, includes a small orange-toned region at x=131..144, y=0..11, and continues down beside the towel. The holding/release point is near the towel's left edge.
  • Important depth order: backdrop/wood/dish, then liquid/rim, then the suspended towel and grabber. The towel overlaps the dish in projection before release.

Motion and rendering approach

  • Preserve frame 00 byte-for-pixel after RGB decoding.
  • Derive a softly feathered towel alpha from its measured skewed polygon and source luminance/chroma; reconstruct the newly exposed backdrop/table/dish under that polygon with spatial interpolation and texture-preserving inpainting.
  • Re-render the source towel texture through a fine piecewise-affine mesh. Before contact the mesh translates under gravity with a very small natural tilt. At contact, its lower edge is pinned near the liquid while the upper portion continues, compressing and curling the strip. It then settles as a wider, irregular folded strip on the liquid.
  • Add only restrained effects local to the liquid: progressive cool-blue dampening of the towel after contact, a small contact shadow, and thin elliptical ripples that expand and fade while clipped to the dish/liquid area.
  • Leave the camera, backdrop, table, dish, and tool body fixed. The release is readable from separation of the towel from the holding point; no invented hand or unrelated object is introduced.

METHOD.md

Generation method

Summary

The sequence is generated deterministically from first_frame.png with local Python, NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV pixel operations. No image viewer, image Read/View tool, network resource, external model, mask, reference image, or external engine was used.

Construction

  1. Load the source as a 256 x 144 RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is copied directly, without resampling or recompression in memory. Frame 01 is also an exact establishing hold.
  2. Delineate the paper towel with a measured, softly antialiased polygon covering its irregular bright silhouette near x=146..180, y=19..99. The grabber immediately to its left is excluded.
  3. Build a clean plate only under that silhouette using OpenCV Navier-Stokes inpainting, followed by less than one RGB level of deterministic local texture. All pixels outside the local apparatus region remain sourced directly from frame 00.
  4. Extract the original towel RGB texture and alpha. Reproject it through a 7 x 15 piecewise-affine mesh whose centerline first follows a short gravitational fall and then bends as the lower edge meets the liquid. The mesh remains a single connected component throughout: it compresses vertically, curls, turns, and spreads laterally as a low fold on the water.
  5. Starting at contact, blend a restrained cyan wet tint upward through the source fibers. Retain the original highlights and texture so the result reads as damp paper rather than a flat graphic.
  6. Add a soft blue-gray contact shadow and one or two thin expanding elliptical ripple traces. Ripple pixels are clipped to the measured liquid ellipse and decay to sub-visible differences by the final frames.
  7. Save frames through artifact_io.py, which packs RGB into uint32 values in 0xRRGGBB form and writes tensor order [height, width, time]. Create transition_check.png as 19 adjacent-frame absolute-difference heatmaps.

Timing

  • 20 frames, 256 x 144 RGB.
  • 4 frames per second, 5 seconds total.
  • Frame 00 source hold; frame 01 establishing hold; frames 02--04 detachment/free fall; frames 05--09 contact and collapse; frames 10--13 spreading and dampening; frames 14--19 settled decay.

Validation performed

  • Confirmed frames/00.png decodes exactly equal to first_frame.png (maximum channel difference 0).
  • Confirmed all 20 PNGs decode as RGB uint8 at 256 x 144.
  • Reconstructed every RGB triplet from pixels.npz and confirmed exact equality to all PNG pixels.
  • Checked every adjacent transition numerically for changed-pixel count, mean absolute difference, maximum difference, and motion bounding box.
  • Checked that the warped towel alpha is contiguous at every animated frame and that its final bbox (approximately x=117..177, y=90..112) stays within the dish.
  • Confirmed motion decays smoothly: after the main collapse, significant-change counts diminish to essentially zero by the final two frames.
  • Compiled generate.py and regenerated all artifacts deterministically.

No claim of visual review is made; inspection was performed only through local code and numeric RGB/edge/component analysis, as required.
