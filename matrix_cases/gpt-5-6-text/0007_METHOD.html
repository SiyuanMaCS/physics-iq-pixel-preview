ball-hits-duck · 生成方法

The animation is generated locally and deterministically by generate.py from first_frame.png. No external model, engine, reference image, mask, network service, subagent, or image Read/View tool was used.

Pixel construction

  1. Read the source as an RGB NumPy array and retain it unchanged as frame 00.
  2. Locate the already-visible brown ball from the numeric measurements in OBSERVATION.md. Use a feathered 20×18-ish elliptical alpha mask centered at (84, 52) to preserve its original shading and texture.
  3. Convert the source RGB to HSV and isolate the duck inside a restricted local ROI. The selected connected component combines its saturated yellow body and brown/orange beak; morphological closing and hole filling retain enclosed details such as the eye. A small Gaussian feather antialiases the edge.
  4. Build clean plates only under the two moving objects with OpenCV Telea inpainting over compact, dilated masks. The rest of the source is copied exactly.
  5. Move the original ball texture on a horizontal track. Its texture rotates clockwise in proportion to traveled distance (angle = -distance / radius) and a small analytic Gaussian contact shadow moves beneath it.
  6. At impact, slow and slightly recoil the ball. Rotate the source-derived duck clockwise about its lower support point while translating it right and slightly down. A damped angle sequence creates a small settling bounce before the final hold.
  7. Composite transformed RGB as premultiplied-alpha layers over the appropriate clean plate. The camera, couch, table, and pipe do not move.

Timing

  • 20 frames
  • 256×144 RGB
  • 4 fps
  • 5 seconds total
  • Frame 00 is exactly equal to first_frame.png

Saved diagnostics and verification

  • inspection.png is the standard eight-frame contact sheet produced by artifact_io.py.
  • transition_check.png shows selected approach/contact/settling frames with amplified adjacent-frame RGB differences beneath them.
  • pixels.npz stores packed uint32 0xRRGGBB values with shape (144, 256, 20).
  • The generator reloads every PNG and the NPZ and asserts exact equality to the generated RGB tensor.
  • A numeric fixed-camera audit confirms that all 27,614 pixels per frame outside the broad action corridor x=65–249, y=20–69 remain exactly equal to the source.
  • The final duck remains fully in frame; its final measured chromatic component spans approximately x=218–243, y=27–61.

All assessment described here was numeric/programmatic. No claim of image-tool visual review is made.
