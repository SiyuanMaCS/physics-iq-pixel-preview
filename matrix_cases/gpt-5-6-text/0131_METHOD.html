OBSERVATION.md

Source-frame observations and event plan

The source was inspected only as a 256×144 RGB array (palette counts, thresholded connected components, scan lines, gradients, and sampled RGB values). No image viewing tool was used.

Measured scene

  • Frame size: 256×144 RGB. The camera is fixed and the table/background must remain pixel-stable.
  • The cool blue-gray background occupies approximately y=0..31; the warm wooden tabletop occupies the lower portion.
  • A clear acrylic sheet spans the central tabletop. Its visible upper/edge region is approximately x=72..209, y=35..77, with the transparent sheet continuing farther down through subtle reflections.
  • The red paint is a compact connected component at x=128..142, y=61..67, centered near (134.7,63.6). Its dominant sampled center colors are dark red/burnt red, e.g. (129,42,14) and (152,46,25).
  • The black platform is at the right, approximately x=213..255, y=49..86, centered near (234,69). Its dark mass stays stationary.

The attachment/clamp is near (220,44) and the colored hub region occupies about x=221..243, y=40..56.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely with local numeric RGB operations. I did not use an image Read/View tool, external model, external engine, network resource, mask, score, or another case.

Construction

  1. I loaded first_frame.png as a 256×144 uint8 RGB array and measured color-connected components, scan-line runs, sampled RGB values, and gradients. Those measurements established the red paint bounds, black platform bounds, brush/ferrule line, and attachment point recorded in OBSERVATION.md.
  2. Frame 00 is a direct array copy of first_frame.png; it is not re-encoded before entering the frame tensor.
  3. For frames 01–19, I made a narrow clean plate only under the photographed moving brush/ferrule. A hand-measured line enclosure limits the area; saturated source-red pixels are explicitly protected. OpenCV Telea inpainting fills that narrow strip from immediately neighboring acrylic/table pixels.
  4. I derived the photographed brush alpha from the RGB difference between the source and that local clean plate, constrained it to the measured brush enclosure, retained the low-contrast bristle end, and premultiplied the original RGB by this alpha.
  5. I transformed that source-derived brush layer around the fixed attachment point (220,44). The motion uses a cubic ease-in/ease-out from 0° to -14°; in OpenCV image coordinates that produces the requested clockwise sweep. Cubic resampling, premultiplied-alpha composition, and a restrained offset blur provide subpixel motion and a contact shadow.
  6. During measured contact frames, local analytic masks add wet red paint pickup to several bristle strands and deposit a short curved red trace following the circular tip path. The paint uses dark body color, a small warm highlight, and a darker trailing edge sampled to stay close to the source dollop's RGB range. The mark persists after the brush moves past it.
  7. The table, background, acrylic sheet, black platform, and colored attachment hub are not transformed. All modified pixels lie within the measured interaction region.

Output and verification

  • artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz, frames 00.png–19.png, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player.
  • transition_check.png contains six key interaction crops and amplified consecutive-frame delta crops.
  • The generator reloads pixels.npz and every PNG, reconstructs packed uint32 values as 0xRRGGBB, and asserts exact equality with the generated frame array.
  • It separately asserts that frame 00 exactly equals the source RGB array.
  • Numeric inspection found continuous changes on all 19 transitions and no changed pixels outside the localized brush/paint region (x=110..247, y=25..94).

No claim of visual review is made; review and revision were based on numeric RGB/component/transition inspection only, per the task restriction.
