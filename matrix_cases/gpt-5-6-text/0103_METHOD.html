marble-run-x · 生成方法

I loaded first_frame.png locally as a 256 x 144 RGB uint8 array and inspected it numerically. The scene measurements in OBSERVATION.md came from color statistics, connected components, Canny/Hough line coordinates, and small-block RGB/luminance summaries. I did not use an image Read/View tool and did not claim a visual review.

The continuation is a source-preserving 2-D pixel composite. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from the supplied PNG. For frames 01--19, generate.py starts from that same source image and analytically composites only a compact moving marble region:

  • a soft amber contact shadow whose offset and width depend on contact state and speed;
  • a subpixel-antialiased golden sphere with translucent center, denser rim, lower-right refraction, fixed upper-left specular highlight, and a rotating internal glint;
  • on fast transfers, two very faint intermediate amber samples to represent the short exposure/motion trail expected at 4 fps.

The marble centers follow a hand-measured zig-zag route over the detected upper, middle, long lower, and ochre final ramp regions. Motion is piecewise accelerated along ramps, with gravity-driven transfer arcs at the gaps. The camera/background never moves; pixels outside the marble/shadow/trail footprint are exact source pixels in every generated frame.

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNG frames, the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png with before/current/5x-difference panels. It then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz, verifies exact agreement with the generated RGB frames, checks the exact first-frame requirement, and verifies that all edits remain localized.
