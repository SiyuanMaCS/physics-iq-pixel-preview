OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB, locked camera, low tabletop viewpoint.
  • The wooden tabletop fills the lower image and recedes toward a back edge near y=61 on the left and y=77 on the right. Its grain runs mainly diagonally/rightward.
  • The real black 30 lb kettlebell is centered near (123,34). Its body spans approximately x=104..142, y=18..56; the handle continues to the top edge. Its stable contact/base area is around x=113..135, y=53..57.
  • The mirror/reflected kettlebell is on the right, centered near (200,27), and remains static.
  • The tennis ball initially spans approximately x=68..103, y=86..121, center (85,103), apparent radius about 17 px. Its yellow-green body, highlight, curved pale seam, and soft contact shadow must remain visually recognizable.
  • A dark, out-of-focus foreground form enters from the lower-left corner and remains static.
  • Intended ball route is diagonally away from the camera and to the right, toward the lower-left edge of the real kettlebell. Perspective makes the ball gradually smaller. The collision target is ball center about (110,65) with radius about 11 px, touching the kettlebell at its lower-left contour.

  3. Frame 02: (86,101), seam rotated slightly.
  4. Frame 03: (87,99).
  5. Frame 04: (88,97).
  6. Frame 05: (90,94).
  7. Frame 06: (92,91).
  8. Frame 07: (94,88).

Motion uses smooth ease-in/ease-out rather than equal pixel jumps. The ball texture/seam rotates consistently with distance rolled. A small perspective-scaled shadow stays anchored to the tabletop beneath and slightly to the right of the ball. No new objects, labels, cuts, or camera movement are introduced.

METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was produced entirely with deterministic local pixel operations in generate.py. The only scene input was first_frame.png; no external images, models, engines, masks, or network resources were used.

Pixel construction

  1. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the RGB pixel level from first_frame.png.
  2. The source tennis ball is isolated inside a tight crop with an HSV yellow-green threshold. The connected component containing the measured ball center is selected, morphologically cleaned, and softly feathered to form its alpha matte.
  3. For frames after 00, the original ball and its long down-left cast shadow are removed from the tabletop. Real wood texture is sampled 44 pixels to the right from the same photograph and Poisson-blended into an irregular mask that avoids the black foreground object. This supplies plausible grain while matching the local boundary illumination.
  4. The photographed ball sprite is moved from (85,103) to approximately (109.5,65.8) on a smoothstep trajectory. Its scale changes from 1.00 to 0.68 to match the table perspective. The sprite rotates about 178 degrees clockwise so the photographed curved seam visibly communicates rolling rather than sliding.
  5. A soft, translucent elliptical cast shadow is regenerated beneath every moved ball position. It trails down-left in the same direction as the source shadow and scales with the ball.
  6. At contact, the 30 lb kettlebell remains fixed. Frames 15–17 contain a subpixel-scale damped rebound/settle of the much lighter ball; frames 18–19 are stationary.

Outputs and checks

  • artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and delta-packed standalone HTML player.
  • generate.py reloads pixels.npz and every saved PNG and asserts exact equality with the in-memory RGB frames.
  • It separately asserts that frame 00 exactly equals the supplied first frame.
  • transition_check.png places selected before/after pairs side by side, including initial motion, approach, impact, rebound, and the identical final hold.
  • I visually reviewed both inspection.png and transition_check.png with the available image-view tool. An initial cleanup left a visible remnant of the original shadow; the final version expanded the reconstruction region and replaced that remnant with seamlessly blended photographed tabletop texture.
