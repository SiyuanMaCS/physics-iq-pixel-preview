OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • Source is an RGB 256 x 144 locked-off frame. The tabletop occupies most of the image; a pale gray wall/background meets it along a shallow diagonal near y=41 at the left and y=29 around x=72.
  • The green paper is a bent, tented strip with an overall visible silhouette of approximately x=51..184, y=38..93. Its left low end is near (51, 91), it rises through (104, 44), its highest/right fold is near (153, 38), and its right contact corner is near (184, 69).
  • The left blue-black grabber enters from the upper-left. Its thin shaft begins off-frame near (0, 24), the blue loop is centered near (62, 45), and the jaw contacts the paper around (75, 51).
  • The right blue-black grabber enters from the right. Its blue loop is centered near (207, 72), with the inner jaw/contact around (184, 69); the black body continues beyond x=255.
  • Lighting is soft and comes from the upper-left. The paper casts a soft shadow on the wood, especially beneath the central rise. The camera and background must remain completely static.
  • Intended motion is modest and physically legible: left grabber/paper travel up-left, right grabber/paper travel down-right. The paper first becomes taut, develops a central jagged crack, then separates into two pieces that stay held by the tools.

Pixel-generation plan

  • Derive soft alpha mattes from the source colors for green paper, blue/black grabber parts, and immediate contact regions; do not redraw the photographic foreground from scratch.
  • Produce a static clean plate only under moving foreground pixels using deterministic local inpainting and tabletop texture reconstruction.
  • Warp source pixels with smooth affine/perspective-like mappings. Before breakage, apply a gentle center-out stretch. During tearing, use complementary irregular half masks and progressively open a center crack. After breakage, translate/rotate the source halves with their corresponding grabber sprites.
  • Add restrained source-colored torn-edge fibers, inner-paper darkening, and soft displaced shadows. Keep all other pixels identical to the locked background plate.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual pixel-generation method

This continuation is a deterministic local image-based animation generated by generate.py; it does not use a renderer, external model, network resource, or additional image source.

  1. The source RGB image is read directly from first_frame.png. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from that array.
  2. The green paper matte is extracted in HSV/RGB color space and reduced to its largest connected component. The two grabber mattes are extracted independently with spatial zones plus blue, black, and neutral-metal color tests. Each matte receives a subpixel Gaussian feather.
  3. A static clean plate is made only beneath the moving setup. OpenCV TELEA inpainting reconstructs the wall/table boundary and hidden areas. Real source wood sampled well below the objects lightly restores grain; it is used strongly only where inpainting accidentally borrows low-saturation gray wall color into the tabletop.
  4. Frames 01–10 apply a smooth center-out affine stretch to the intact paper while the photographic grabber sprites translate in opposing diagonal directions. A fixed irregular polyline grows from the lower paper edge toward the upper edge. Its antialiased interior is removed progressively, with restrained dark and pale-green torn rims.
  5. At frame 11 the paper matte becomes two complementary halves divided by a multi-frequency jagged boundary. Frames 11–19 transform each source-textured half and its corresponding grabber together: the left side moves/rotates up-left and the right side moves/rotates down-right. This keeps each paper end aligned to its jaw.
  6. A blurred, displaced copy of the animated paper alpha provides a soft moving contact shadow. Layering order is clean plate, shadow, paper halves, then grabber sprites.
  7. Pixel values are rounded and clipped to RGB uint8. artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, packed tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. The script separately creates transition_check.png with all 20 chronological thumbnails and enlarged tear-stage crops.

Revision and visual inspection

I visually inspected first_frame.png, the generated inspection.png, transition_check.png, and full-resolution frames 07 and 19 with the provided image-view tool. The first render exposed gray wall-colored inpaint through the crack; a second render replaced that erroneous region with actual tabletop texture. A further full-resolution pass reduced an overly regular clone pattern while preserving brown wood in the opened gap. The final reviewed sequence shows a progressive slit, full jagged separation, continued opposing pull, maintained jaw contact, and a locked background.

Verification

The generator asserts all of the following on every run:

  • array shape is (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype is uint8;
  • frame 00 exactly equals the decoded RGB source image;
  • pixels.npz contains a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor packed as 0xRRGGBB;
  • that tensor exactly equals the generated RGB frames after packing/transposition;
  • every saved frames/00.png through frames/19.png decodes exactly to its corresponding generated frame.
