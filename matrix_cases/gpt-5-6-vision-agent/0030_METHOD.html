Pixel generation

The supplied first_frame.png is loaded locally as an RGB array and copied byte-for-byte into frame 00. No generative model, network resource, external reference, mask, or external rendering engine is used.

For frames 01–19, the camera/background is derived from the supplied frame. Three small elliptical masks cover the original balls and their contact shadows. OpenCV's local TELEA inpainting reconstructs those exposed pixels from the immediately surrounding light wood table. All pixels outside this small masked region remain from the original photograph.

The moving balls are analytic 4x-supersampled RGBA sprites. Their appearance combines:

  • spherical diffuse lighting from the upper left;
  • a broad highlight and darker lower/right falloff;
  • deterministic fine sinusoidal variation to suggest tennis-ball fuzz;
  • a clipped curved seam whose phase changes with traveled distance;
  • antialiased silhouettes and soft blurred contact shadows.

The supersampled overlays are reduced with Lanczos filtering and alpha-composited over the static cleaned table. The grey ball advances to the brown ball, loses most of its velocity and rebounds slightly. The brown ball receives the impulse and transfers most of it to the initially touching orange ball. Screen-space motion slopes gently downward to the right to follow the table plane, and per-frame displacement tapers to zero to represent friction.

Timing and output

  • 20 frames
  • 256 x 144 RGB pixels
  • 4 fps
  • 5 seconds
  • Frame 00 exactly equals first_frame.png

artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, the compressed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png to expose the approach/contact/separation frames.

Verification and review

After saving, generate.py reloads every PNG and pixels.npz, repacks the in-memory RGB frames into uint32 0xRRGGBB, and asserts exact equality among all representations. It also asserts exact RGB equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png.

I visually reviewed both inspection.png and transition_check.png with the image viewing tool. The review confirmed the locked camera/background, readable grey-to-brown and brown-to-orange contacts, ball separation, deceleration, and final rest positions.
