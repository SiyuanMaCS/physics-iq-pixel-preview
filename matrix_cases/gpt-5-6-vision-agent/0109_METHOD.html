OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 pixels, landscape, fixed camera.
  • Background: pale gray wall across the upper portion; a warm medium-brown wooden tabletop begins at a diagonal boundary near (0, 58), rises to about (169, 24), and reaches the right edge near (256, 53). A dark out-of-focus region occupies the far upper-right corner.
  • Glass: clear cylindrical tumbler centered near x=139; outer bounds are approximately x=112..166, y=35..130. The top rim is an ellipse centered near (139, 47), about 51 px wide and 19 px high. The waterline is near y=61; the water-filled body continues to the rounded base near y=126.
  • Match: a thin tan-orange wooden shaft runs from off-frame around (27, 0) to its dark, lit head around (148, 59). The initial shaft angle is roughly 26 degrees downward to the right. The head is already at/just inside the mouth of the glass, close to the water surface. A tiny narrow yellow-orange flame is visible around (145, 48..54), partly hidden by glass reflections.
  • Lighting: soft daylight from the upper-left. Shadows are broad and subtle; glass highlights are strongest on the left rim and lower front edge. The source is slightly soft / photographic, so added elements must be antialiased and gently blurred rather than hard-edged.
  • Important occlusion: the portion of the match behind the front wall and water must be refracted, lower contrast, slightly thicker/shifted, and tinted gray-brown. The glass outline and specular highlights should remain in front.

Motion and physics plan (20 frames, 4 fps)

The camera and complete environment remain locked. Only the match, its tiny flame/smoke, and localized water ripples change. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from first_frame.png.

  1. Frame 00 (0.00 s): exact supplied frame; lit match head poised at the water surface.
  2. Frames 01-03 (0.25-0.75 s): shaft rotates downward a few degrees about an off-screen upper-left hand position while translating slightly down/right. The flame remains a small yellow-orange glow and leans subtly up-left.
  3. Frames 04-06 (1.00-1.50 s): the head touches and crosses the waterline near the center of the glass. The visible flame rapidly shrinks, becomes orange-red, and vanishes by frame 06. Small concentric elliptical ripples form on the water surface.
  4. Frames 07-10 (1.75-2.50 s): the extinguished dark head is lowered several pixels beneath the surface. The submerged match portion appears displaced/refracted and muted behind the water/glass. A very small pale smoke wisp rises from the surface while the first ripple expands and fades.
  5. Frames 11-14 (2.75-3.50 s): the match reaches its lowest point and pauses. Residual ripples weaken; the smoke curls slightly left and becomes increasingly transparent.
  6. Frames 15-17 (3.75-4.25 s): the match is lifted a few pixels, still extinguished. A second very subtle water disturbance follows the upward motion; the wet head remains nearly black.
  7. Frames 18-19 (4.50-4.75 s): motion settles with the head near/just below the surface. Smoke has disappeared and only a faint final ripple remains.

Generation approach

Use only local pixel operations. Reconstruct the patch covered by the original match using nearby wall/table/glass pixels and soft interpolation, then composite an antialiased animated shaft/head. Treat the above-water and underwater shaft portions separately, clipping them around the measured waterline. Preserve the original glass by re-compositing selected rim/specular details over the moving match. Draw tiny translucent flame, smoke, and elliptical ripple overlays with supersampling and light blur. No camera motion, zoom, newly introduced objects, or global image changes.

METHOD.md

Method

I generated the sequence entirely with local Python pixel operations using Pillow, NumPy, and OpenCV. No external model, asset, engine, network resource, mask, score, or other case was used.

first_frame.png is loaded as RGB and placed unchanged at frame 00. For frames 01-19, a clean plate is made by masking only the original match shaft, flame, and head and filling that narrow region with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting; the repair boundary is softly feathered into the unchanged source pixels.

The match is then reconstructed as a supersampled antialiased pixel layer. Its measured head follows a hand-authored real-time descent, short pause, and slight lift. The shaft angle changes gradually about an implied off-camera grip. Above the measured water surface it uses warm wood shading; below the surface it is clipped, shifted right, vertically compressed, desaturated, and lowered in opacity to imitate refraction through water and glass. The head changes from ember-brown to dark wet material. A tiny multi-layer teardrop flame shrinks on contact and disappears, after which a faint blurred smoke curl briefly rises.

Water interaction is represented by small perspective-flattened elliptical ripples that expand and fade. Selected rim, meniscus, and side-wall pixels from the cleaned source plate are composited last so the glass remains in front of the moving match. The camera and all background pixels outside the narrow repair/effect regions remain locked.

The final RGB arrays are packed as uint32 0xRRGGBB into pixels.npz through artifact_io.save. The generator reopens every PNG and asserts equality against the in-memory RGB array, asserts tensor shape/type/value equality, and asserts that frame 00 is exactly the supplied source image.
