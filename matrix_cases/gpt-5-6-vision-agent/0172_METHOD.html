OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Locked scene measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, 256 x 144 RGB. The camera is locked off in a single wide shot.
  • Background: two matte gray walls meet near x=151 at the rear corner. Their floor boundary slopes from approximately (0,72) through (151,58) to (255,83). A warm wood tabletop fills the lower part of frame and carries fixed plank seams, grain, lighting, and shadows.
  • Gate: the fixed crossbar is a slightly perspective-skewed cyan/blue block spanning about x=132..169 and y=66..76. Its left red leg spans about x=131..141 and y=73..110; its right yellow leg spans about x=157..167 and y=73..107. These objects and their shadows do not move.
  • Placed block: a slim coral-pink rectangular block is centered near x=155. It runs from about y=27 to y=67 and is already aligned vertically with the center of the crossbar. Its bottom edge is at the crossbar's top surface, so the continuing action begins at contact/placement rather than requiring a large drop.
  • Grabber: a blue-black two-sided tool enters from the upper edge. Its blue left/finger surface occupies roughly x=145..156, while the black right jaw/body occupies roughly x=156..171. The jaws wrap the block near y=25..35. All moving content stays in this narrow central region.
  • Lighting: soft overhead/front illumination, with subdued contact shadows and no exposure or focus change.

Generation intent

The moving block and grabber will be isolated from the source pixels, the occluded gray-wall area will be reconstructed locally from neighboring wall texture and shading, and transformed source-pixel layers will be composited over that fixed clean plate. The structure and table will be preserved from the original frame. Edge masks will be softly antialiased to match the source rendering, while frame 00 remains untouched. During implementation, the planned subpixel settling cue was intentionally replaced by an exact hold in frames 01–04 because the block is already visibly in contact and further descent would falsely penetrate the blue crossbar.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is made entirely from the supplied first_frame.png with local Python pixel operations (numpy, Pillow, and OpenCV). No external images, models, engines, masks, or references are used.

  1. The blue-black grabber is isolated inside its measured central region using material-color tests, followed by connected-component selection anchored to the top image edge. Its blue left jaw and black right jaw are retained as separate source-pixel RGBA layers.
  2. The visible coral pixels are isolated in a narrow block-shaped region. A complete stationary block layer is formed by keeping those original pixels and filling only the small portion hidden by the closed jaws with shading sampled from unobstructed rows of the same block.
  3. A clean wall/table plate is produced by OpenCV Telea inpainting over only the tool and block area. The rest of the supplied frame—including the gate, crossbar, legs, tabletop grain, shadows, and walls—is preserved.
  4. Frames 00–04 hold the exact original image to communicate careful placement and stable contact. Frames 05–14 shift the two source-pixel jaw layers laterally apart and upward with an eased trajectory. The reconstructed coral block does not move after release. Frames 15–19 hold the completed configuration.
  5. Narrow Gaussian alpha feathering and bilinear subpixel translation match the antialiased source edges. There is no camera, lighting, exposure, or background motion.

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, contact sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png and verifies every saved PNG against pixels.npz; it also asserts that frame 00 is exactly equal to the supplied first frame.
