OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Source: first_frame.png, RGB, 256 x 144.
  • Camera: fixed, oblique overhead view of a pale wood tabletop. The image has natural lens softness/noise and broad diagonal wood grain running roughly lower-left to upper-right.
  • Blue ball: visible color/body bounds approximately x=111..129, y=63..80; color centroid (120.1, 71.3); apparent radius about 9 px.
  • Yellow-green ball: visible color/body bounds approximately x=177..194, y=33..49; color centroid (185.5, 40.6); apparent radius about 9 px.
  • The center-to-center line is (65.4, -30.7), length about 72.3 px. This is also the collision axis.
  • Entry fixtures: a dark pipe/nozzle enters from the lower-left border and points toward the blue ball; another dark pipe/nozzle enters near the upper-right border and points toward the yellow ball. Both pipes and the camera remain fixed.
  • Light comes predominantly from upper-right. Each ball has a soft elongated cast shadow displaced about 12-15 px down-left. The yellow ball has a bright upper-left highlight; the blue ball has a dimmer highlight.
  • No new objects or camera motion are introduced.

Physics and 20-frame event plan

Both balls move at the same constant center speed, approximately 4.65 px/frame (18.6 px/s), along the measured center line in opposite directions. They are treated as equal-mass elastic balls: at contact they exchange velocities, which appears as a symmetric rebound. Ball centers never pass through one another.

  • Frame 00 (0.00 s): exact source image; balls at measured initial positions.
  • Frames 01-05 (0.25-1.25 s): symmetric approach at equal speed; fixed background, pipes, lighting, and framing.
  • Frame 06 (1.50 s): near-contact, with the centers about 17 px apart.
  • Frame 07 (1.75 s): impact/contact pose at the midpoint; a very small squash along the collision axis conveys elastic compression without interpenetration.
  • Frames 08-19 (2.00-4.75 s): symmetric elastic rebound. Each colored ball returns along its incoming path at the same speed, moving back toward its own pipe. Shadows remain attached to the tabletop beneath each ball.

Frame timing follows the required player convention: 20 frames shown at 4 fps for a nominal 5-second clip; frame 00 is preserved byte-for-byte.

METHOD.md

Method

The continuation is generated entirely with local Python pixel operations from first_frame.png.

  1. I measured the two ball centroids and used the line joining them as the shared collision axis.
  2. Frame 00 is copied directly from the source array without recompression or modification.
  3. I extracted 25 x 25 RGB cutouts of the photographed balls with softly antialiased circular alpha masks. This retains the source lighting, color variation, and optical softness.
  4. I created a fixed clean tabletop plate by masking each original ball plus its cast shadow. For each covered pixel, the script finds unobstructed samples on both sides along the measured diagonal wood-grain direction and linearly interpolates between them. This continues the actual photographed grain through the two small footprints; all other background and pipe pixels remain from the source.
  5. Both centers advance by the same Euclidean distance each frame in opposite directions. At contact they undergo a symmetric equal-mass elastic rebound, so their velocity magnitudes remain equal and unchanged. The contact frame has a subtle axis-aligned squash/cross-axis expansion.
  6. Each moving ball receives a soft analytic lower-left tabletop shadow matching the measured source-light direction. The original RGB ball cutout is then alpha-composited above it.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, PNG sequence, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. The script additionally creates transition_check.png and re-opens every output PNG to verify exact agreement with pixels.npz.

No external assets, network resources, models, masks, other cases, or rendering engines were used.
