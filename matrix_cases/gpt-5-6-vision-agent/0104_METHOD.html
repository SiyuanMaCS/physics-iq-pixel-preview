OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 pixels, landscape, fixed camera.
  • The white magnetic board fills nearly the full image. Its dark vertical frame/stands lie near x=47–54 and x=200–211; these remain completely static.
  • Lighting is soft and photographic, with a broad grey cast and a bright vertical reflection around x=194–204. The animation must preserve these exact background pixels except immediately around the moving marble.
  • A cropped teal entry/funnel occupies roughly x=61–128, y=0–27. Its visible lower striped lip runs approximately from (87,13) to (128,25).
  • Upper blue magnetic ramp: approximately x=125–199, y=27–48, sloping down toward screen-left. Its usable upper edge runs from about (195,28) to (128,37).
  • Purple transfer/catcher: approximately x=101–131, y=43–67. It forms a bent pocket below the left end of the upper blue ramp.
  • Middle blue magnetic ramp: approximately x=131–202, y=62–85, also sloping down toward screen-left. Its usable upper edge runs from about (197,63) to (134,74).
  • Green ramp: approximately x=68–141, y=84–103, gently sloping down toward screen-right. Its usable top edge runs from about (71,84) to (138,94).
  • Bottom yellow ramp: approximately x=91–160, y=121–134, nearly level with a slight screen-right descent. Its top edge is around y=122–125.
  • The yellow glass marble is treated as a small translucent sphere about 8 px in diameter. It needs a dark amber rim, yellow body, tight pale specular highlight, and subtle contact/drop shadow so it remains realistic at this resolution.

Motion and physics plan (20 frames at 4 fps)

  • Frame 00: exact untouched source image; the marble has not yet been released into view.
  • Frames 01–06: the marble appears at the high, right end of the upper blue ramp and accelerates down the incline toward screen-left, with visible sphere rotation.
  • Frames 07–09: it leaves the upper ramp, falls a short distance, is caught by the purple transfer piece, and is redirected toward screen-right.
  • Frames 10–12: it follows a short ballistic arc to the high, right end of the middle blue ramp.
  • Frames 13–14: it rolls down the middle blue incline toward screen-left.
  • Frames 15–16: retaining leftward momentum, it crosses the staggered gap and lands on the high, left end of the green ramp.
  • Frames 17–18: it accelerates toward screen-right along the green ramp.
  • Frame 19: it falls from the green ramp’s right edge onto the yellow bottom ramp, still within the frame at the end of the five-second shot.

The path is piecewise smooth: ramp segments use accelerating tangential motion, airborne segments use parabolic motion, and contact positions include a small normal offset equal to the marble radius. No camera motion, cuts, board deformation, or added objects are introduced.

METHOD.md

Method

I used the supplied photograph as an immutable background and added only a locally rendered moving marble and its shadow. No external images, models, engines, masks, or references were used.

Motion construction

  • I measured the ramp endpoints directly in first_frame.png and authored a 20-frame, piecewise path through them.
  • On-ramp sections accelerate in the downhill direction. The marble travels from right to left on the two blue ramps, is redirected at the purple piece, then travels left to right on the green ramp and lands on the yellow ramp.
  • The three inter-ramp transfers use short ballistic-looking arcs that preserve the incoming horizontal momentum while adding downward displacement.
  • A cumulative path-length value drives the phase of a small internal inclusion, making the sphere visibly rotate rather than translate as a static disk.

Pixel rendering

  • The marble is rendered analytically at 4x resolution as a 9-pixel-wide amber/yellow glass sphere.
  • Its shading combines an amber rim, yellow transmitted-light center, upper-left white specular reflection, lower-right caustic, and rotating internal inclusion.
  • A small blurred elliptical shadow is tight during ramp contact and lighter/broader during airborne frames.
  • Only the RGBA marble layer is downsampled. It is then alpha-composited onto the original 256 x 144 photograph, preserving all pixels outside the local marble/shadow footprint.
  • Frame 00 is copied from the source with no compositing at all.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. The generator also creates transition_check.png, showing every adjacent transition with a box around changed pixels. It then reloads every PNG and verifies it exactly against the corresponding tensor plane, and verifies frame 00 exactly against first_frame.png.

I visually reviewed both inspection.png and transition_check.png after the final render. The review confirmed a fixed background, localized per-frame changes, a readable yellow glass marble, downhill ramp motion, continuous staggered transfers, and final contact on the yellow ramp.
