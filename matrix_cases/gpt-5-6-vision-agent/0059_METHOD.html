Actual generation method

The animation is a local Python pixel construction based only on first_frame.png and the written prompt.

  1. The source PNG is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is inserted without any modification and is asserted byte-for-byte equal to the source.
  2. A narrow rectangle containing the original held duck, its small contact shadow, and the visible grabber ends is reconstructed from the surrounding wall with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. The rest of the source frame remains untouched.
  3. The duck is extracted from source pixels at (116,13)–(138,36). Its alpha comes from the yellow-versus-gray chroma difference, with a very small blur to retain the original antialiased contour.
  4. The duck is translated along a hand-authored accelerating gravity path. Its total vertical displacements are 0, 0, 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 48, 64, 82 pixels for frames 00–10, with only two pixels of rightward drift and at most four degrees of pitch. Rotation is done in premultiplied-alpha space. Fast samples receive a restrained one-pixel-scale exposure trail and a soft, low-opacity projected shadow.
  5. The grabber is redrawn as two narrow source-color-matched orange/dark fingers. Its anchored arms open over frames 01–03 and remain open and empty thereafter.
  6. When the duck reaches the box, a polygonal source-derived front-face/lip layer is composited above it. This makes the body disappear behind the fabric wall rather than visually passing in front of the box.
  7. At impact, a localized analytic remap applies a maximum 1.8-pixel vertical compression and subpixel lateral sway to the box region. Alternating, exponentially smaller values create a short damped fabric rebound; the surrounding locked camera view is feathered and remains fixed.
  8. artifact_io.save packs the final RGB frames into the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, emits the 20 PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. A separate transition sheet reports changed-pixel counts at the release, fall, entry, impact, and settled stages.

Visual review and revision

I visually reviewed first_frame.png, a nearest-neighbor enlargement of the apparatus, inspection.png, and transition_check.png with the provided image-view tool. The final review confirmed:

  • the framing and background remain locked;
  • the grabber visibly opens before the duck leaves;
  • the fall accelerates rather than moving at constant speed;
  • frame 08 shows first contact with the opening, frame 09 shows partial lip occlusion, and frame 10 hides the duck inside;
  • the impact response is subtle and damped, with no visible box-edge tear;
  • the final frames are stationary with the grabber empty.

No external image, mask, model, engine, or network resource was used.
