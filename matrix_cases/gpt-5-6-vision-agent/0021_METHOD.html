OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Fixed scene measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, RGB, 256 x 144 pixels. The camera is locked off and the image is already the required output size.
  • The pale laminated wooden tabletop fills almost the whole frame. Its dominant grain runs diagonally from upper left toward lower right, with several broad plank seams.
  • A black pipe/nozzle enters from the left edge. Its visible mouth/body occupies approximately x=0..15, y=25..56, and its projected axis points rightward around y=42..44.
  • The yellow-green tennis ball in frame 00 has center approximately (63, 44), radius about 8 pixels, with a soft table contact shadow underneath/right. It is already fully outside the pipe.
  • The cardboard ramp is an inclined quadrilateral whose visible top surface is approximately bounded by (132,31), (232,8), (242,49), and (122,77). Its low/entry edge is the left diagonal from roughly (132,31) to (122,77); this edge crosses the ball's incoming line near (128,44).
  • The cardboard surface rises physically toward image-right. For a straight world-space roll, perspective makes the projected ball path angle slightly upward in the image after entry.
  • A blue rectangular support stands beneath the ramp at approximately x=196..222, y=53..117. The ramp properly occludes its top; the support does not intersect the planned ball path.
  • Illumination is soft and mostly from upper left. The ball is brightest at upper left and darker at lower right. Its contact shadow trails a few pixels down/right on the horizontal table; on the ramp it remains tight to the cardboard surface.
  • The source has mild camera/compression noise. All pixels outside a compact moving-ball/shadow region will remain exactly copied from the fixed source reconstruction.

Motion model

  • Frame rate is 4 fps, so frames are spaced by 0.25 seconds.
  • Frame 00 is preserved exactly. Its ball center is the initial observed state, not a pre-launch hold.
  • On the table the center advances right along y about 44 with mild perspective-adjusted speed. The original ball is removed from the static plate with local texture reconstruction, while frame 00 itself bypasses reconstruction and is copied verbatim.
  • At the ramp entry the center follows a continuous projected path onto the plane. It moves up/right across the inclined cardboard, slows under gravity, and comes nearly to rest on the ramp without adding any unprompted objects or camera motion.
  • The sprite is analytically shaded as a small fuzzy tennis ball, including a restrained curved seam and soft antialiasing. It becomes slightly smaller with distance. Shadows are surface-aware and are clipped to the table or ramp as appropriate.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is generated locally from first_frame.png with NumPy, OpenCV, and Pillow. No external images, models, masks, engines, or network sources are used.

  1. Frame 00 is copied directly from the source without passing through any image operation.
  2. A static photographic plate is made by masking the source ball and its compact contact shadow, then filling only that small area with OpenCV's Telea neighborhood inpainting. Everything else remains the original source pixels.
  3. The real ball's RGB appearance is extracted from the first frame. A soft alpha matte is computed from its yellow-green excess over the neutral tabletop, constrained by the observed round footprint. This retains the source lighting, color, blur, and camera character.
  4. For frames 01–19 the extracted ball is translated, slightly perspective-scaled, and rotated with cubic subpixel resampling. It advances along the pipe axis over the table, crosses the measured left edge of the cardboard, and follows a straight projected path up the plane with progressively smaller displacements to depict gravitational deceleration.
  5. A compact analytic Gaussian contact shadow is composited before the ball. Its offset and aspect change at the table-to-ramp transition, and the ramp shadow is clipped to the measured cardboard polygon.
  6. artifact_io.save packs RGB into the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor and writes the PNG sequence, inspection montage, and standalone player. The generator then unpacks pixels.npz and compares it pixel-for-pixel with every PNG and with the in-memory frames; it separately asserts that frame 00 equals first_frame.png exactly.

inspection.png gives an eight-frame overview. transition_check.png presents enlarged before/after crops and amplified absolute differences for the initial step, ramp approach, ramp crossing, first climb step, and final settling step.
