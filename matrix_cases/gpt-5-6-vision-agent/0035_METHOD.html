block-domino · 生成方法

The animation is generated entirely from first_frame.png with local Python pixel operations (numpy, Pillow, and OpenCV). No external images, masks, models, engines, or references are used.

  1. I measured six visible block silhouettes, their tabletop pivots, the arm pivot, and the platform geometry from the supplied 256 x 144 frame.
  2. A static plate is reconstructed by masking the six blocks and their immediate contact shadows, then applying small-radius local Telea inpainting. Because the original arm crosses the very dark platform, that narrow corridor is rebuilt with a same-surface clone from adjacent platform pixels; this avoids pulling warm tabletop color into the platform. The original platform otherwise remains in place.
  3. Each block is retained as a feathered source-pixel cutout. Once triggered, it is rotated around its measured lower-left tabletop pivot. A staggered angle curve produces the right-to-left cascade, including a small damped overshoot as each block lands.
  4. The arm is redrawn as an antialiased dark wooden stick about the original pivot. Its screen-space polar angle increases from 135 degrees to 178 degrees, which is a clockwise sweep in image coordinates. It is composited behind the blocks.
  5. Soft analytic shadows are projected down/right onto the tabletop and blurred. The broad upper-left lighting, original color texture, fixed camera, and all untouched source pixels are preserved.
  6. Frame 00 is copied directly from the source without processing. The final tensor and every PNG are read back and compared exactly against the generated RGB frame array.

Timing: 20 frames at 4 fps (5 seconds). The arm begins moving immediately, contacts the rightmost purple block, and the blocks fall in order purple, blue, green, pink, red-orange, yellow before settling.
