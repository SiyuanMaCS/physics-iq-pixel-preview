+def bilinear(image, x, y):
+    """Vectorized bilinear RGB sampler."""
+    h, w = image.shape[:2]
+    x = np.clip(x, 0.0, w - 1.001)
+    y = np.clip(y, 0.0, h - 1.001)
+    x0 = np.floor(x).astype(np.int32)
+    y0 = np.floor(y).astype(np.int32)
+    x1 = np.minimum(x0 + 1, w - 1)
+    y1 = np.minimum(y0 + 1, h - 1)
+    fx = (x - x0)[..., None]
+    fy = (y - y0)[..., None]
+    a = image[y0, x0] * (1.0 - fx) + image[y0, x1] * fx
+    b = image[y1, x0] * (1.0 - fx) + image[y1, x1] * fx
+    return a * (1.0 - fy) + b * fy
+

+def smoothstep(lo, hi, value):
+    q = np.clip((value - lo) / (hi - lo), 0.0, 1.0)
+    return q * q * (3.0 - 2.0 * q)

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • The source is a 256×144 RGB image, viewed by a fixed camera in a single continuous shot.
  • A warm, medium-brown wooden tabletop begins around y=37 and fills the lower image. The softly lit pale gray wall fills the upper background.
  • The black motorized display platform is centered near (132, 86). Its top is an ellipse spanning approximately x=91–174 and y=57–96; its cylindrical body extends to about y=116.
  • The yellow-green tennis ball is approximately spherical, centered near (130, 60), with visible bounds about x=109–150 and y=38–82. It rests on the platform top and casts a short, soft contact shadow.
  • The smiley is on the camera-facing hemisphere: two small black eyes around (122, 53) and (136, 55), with a curved black smile across roughly x=120–138, y=62–70. Pale curved tennis-ball seams and fine mottled/fuzzy texture are visible.
  • A thin dark power cable lies behind the ball/platform and exits toward the upper-right. It and every other scene element remain fixed.
  • Lighting is soft and constant, strongest from the upper-left. The ball has brighter upper-left color and a darker lower-right rim.

Motion interpretation

“Rotates slowly clockwise on the black platform” is treated as rotation about the ball’s vertical axis, not a rolling translation. Clockwise rotation carries the front smiley toward screen-right from the camera view. The ball center, silhouette, height, contact point, platform, cable, shadows, tabletop, wall, exposure, and framing stay locked.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00 (t=0.00 s): exact source image; smiley centered toward camera.
  • Frames 01–04 (t=0.25–1.00 s): gentle spin begins; face shifts slightly right while remaining fully readable.
  • Frames 05–08 (t=1.25–2.00 s): steady slow rotation; right eye and smile approach the right half of the sphere.
  • Frames 09–12 (t=2.25–3.00 s): face becomes increasingly foreshortened near the right limb; new yellow felt and seam texture enters from the left.
  • Frames 13–16 (t=3.25–4.00 s): most of the face turns around the right side; only a narrow portion remains visible.
  • Frames 17–19 (t=4.25–4.75 s): rotation continues at the same rate to about 76° total; the front is largely plain felt/seam while the smiley sits at the right edge.

The angular path uses smooth ease-in/ease-out timing to avoid a mechanical first-frame jump while reading as an almost constant, slow physical spin through the middle of the shot.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation uses deterministic local Python/NumPy pixel operations only.

I measured the tennis ball as an image-space sphere centered at approximately (129.55, 59.70) with radii 20.65×21.75 pixels. For each output pixel on that sphere, generate.py computes its visible surface normal, applies the inverse vertical-axis rotation, and bilinearly samples the corresponding material point from first_frame.png. This spherical inverse warp makes the smiley and photographed felt texture translate and foreshorten around the ball instead of sliding as a flat decal.

The source lighting is approximately divided out and the fixed scene lighting is reapplied from the upper-right, so the ball's material markings move while its illumination remains locked to the room. The part of the sphere hidden in frame 00 is continued with deterministic yellow-green felt mottling and a pale curved seam; it is feathered into the photographed hemisphere at the original limb. A soft analytic edge blends the rendered sphere over the untouched source image. The ball's position and silhouette remain fixed; the platform, cable, wall, tabletop, contact shadow, exposure, and camera are copied unchanged from the source.

Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the RGB pixel level from first_frame.png. Frames 01–19 rotate through an eased 76° clockwise turn over 4.75 seconds. The ease only softens start/stop acceleration; the middle of the shot is close to constant angular speed.

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. The generator also creates transition_check.png and then reopens every PNG and pixels.npz to verify exact agreement with the in-memory (20, 144, 256, 3) uint8 RGB sequence and packed 0xRRGGBB values.
