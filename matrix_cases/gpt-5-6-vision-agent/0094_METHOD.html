+def clean_plate(source: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
+    """Remove the original domino and its compact shadow from a local region."""
+    bgr = cv2.cvtColor(source, cv2.COLOR_RGB2BGR)

+    cv2.fillConvexPoly(mask, polygon, 255)
+    repaired = cv2.inpaint(bgr, mask, 3.0, cv2.INPAINT_TELEA)
+    return cv2.cvtColor(repaired, cv2.COLOR_BGR2RGB)

+def projected_corners(theta: float) -> np.ndarray:
+    """Top corners from a rectangle rotated in the (unforeshortened) table plane."""
+    center = np.array([141.75, 43.95], np.float64)
+    length, width = 23.25, 9.55
+    c, s = np.cos(theta), np.sin(theta)
+    along = np.array([c, s])
+    across = np.array([-s, c])
+    plane = np.array([

+         0.5 * length * along - 0.5 * width * across,
+         0.5 * length * along + 0.5 * width * across,
+        -0.5 * length * along + 0.5 * width * across,
+    ])
+    # Approximate camera projection of the horizontal table plane.
+    plane[:, 1] *= 0.58
+    return plane + center

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation

The input is a 256x144 RGB, locked-off tabletop shot. The camera looks obliquely down onto a warm wood-grain table. A matte-black round platform occupies approximately x=130..211, y=25..81, with its visible cylindrical wall extending to about y=83. A small bar magnet/metal assembly sits at x=96..125, y=44..78, pointing toward the platform. Purple tape marks form an angled reference line at x=83..116, y=49..112. A thin dark cable runs from the rear of the platform toward the upper-right edge.

The moving white/lavender plastic domino is centered at approximately (141.7, 46.2). Its visible top is the quadrilateral (128,43), (150,39), (155,45), (134,50), with a roughly 3 px thick front/side wall ending near y=53. It lies on the platform close to the magnet and is initially directed almost left-right (about -12 degrees in image coordinates). Its local soft shadow falls mainly down/right on the black platform. All other objects, lighting, framing, focus, and exposure remain fixed.

Motion model and event plan

The domino rotates clockwise in place about a vertical axis through its center. Motion is continuous in real time and uses a gently eased start and stop so it does not jump between frames. Perspective is modeled by rotating the box footprint in a table-plane coordinate system whose vertical image axis is foreshortened; the top and short vertical walls are then projected back into the camera view. The total turn is 180 degrees, ending in the physically equivalent reversed orientation after one half-turn. A small contact shadow rotates consistently below the box. No camera movement or motion of the magnet/platform/tape/cable occurs.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is generated locally from first_frame.png with deterministic Python pixel operations. Frame 00 is the original RGB image without modification.

For frames 01–19, a small polygon covering only the original domino and its compact shadow is reconstructed with OpenCV's Telea inpainting to form a clean plate. The moving domino is represented as a shallow 3D rectangular box. Its footprint rotates clockwise in an unforeshortened tabletop coordinate system, is projected back through a measured 0.58 vertical perspective scale, and is rasterized at 4x resolution. The visible top and vertical side faces use fixed scene lighting, anti-aliased edges, and a soft rotating contact shadow. The 4x render is area-downsampled to the required 256x144 output. All pixels outside the local domino region come directly from the fixed source/clean plate; there is no camera motion.

The turn follows a cubic smoothstep curve from 0 to 180 degrees over frames 00–19, producing a gentle start and stop at a constant 4 fps. A half-turn is suitable for the visually symmetric unmarked domino while making the clockwise rotation unambiguous in the intermediate frames.

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, the standalone player, inspection sheet, and the uint32 packed 0xRRGGBB tensor. The generator then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz, verifies exact agreement with the in-memory RGB frames, and asserts that frame 00 exactly equals first_frame.png. transition_check.png shows selected frames with localized consecutive-frame difference insets.
