double-cradle · 生成方法

This sequence was generated locally from first_frame.png with deterministic Python, NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV pixel operations. No external images, models, engines, or network resources were used.

Image construction

  1. Frame 00 is the source RGB image copied without modification.
  2. A clean locked-camera plate is reconstructed for later frames. Small masks for the five balls, their immediate reflections, and their fine strings are filled from neighboring source pixels. The grabber's occluded tabletop is reconstructed from a stretched strip of the source wood texture, while the small hidden portion of the black base is rebuilt with perspective polygons and sampled-looking subdued highlights.
  3. The visible chrome ball appearance is sampled from the isolated rightmost source ball with a feathered circular alpha. The same source-derived sprite is composited at the five calculated ball centers.
  4. Suspension threads are redrawn as faint antialiased one-pixel lines from five fixed pivots. Soft dark/cool ellipses below the calculated centers provide moving reflections on the glossy black base.
  5. The grabber is extracted into softly feathered upper and lower jaw layers. During frames 01–03 the layers separate slightly and translate left until they leave the shot.

Motion model

The two released left balls follow a pendulum-like screen-space arc into the three resting balls. At the first central impact, their displacement becomes zero; their momentum is then assigned to the two balls on the right. Successive impacts alternate the moving pair between right and left with decreasing amplitudes. Vertical positions combine pendulum rise with the photographed cradle's diagonal image-plane perspective.

The sampled horizontal offsets by phase are:

  • release: -24, -18, -8, 0 pixels for the left pair;
  • first transfer: +19, +27, +16, 0 pixels for the right pair;
  • second transfer: -16, -24, -14, 0 pixels for the left pair;
  • third transfer: +13, +20, +11, 0 pixels for the right pair;
  • final transfer: -10, -15, -8 pixels for the left pair.

All static scene elements remain locked.

Validation and review

generate.py verifies all of the following after saving:

  • array shape is exactly (20, 144, 256, 3) with uint8 RGB values;
  • frame 00 is pixel-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every saved PNG is pixel-identical to its in-memory frame;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and exact 0xRRGGBB values matching all PNGs.

I visually reviewed inspection.png, transition_check.png, and the reconstructed clean plate. The first review exposed an overlong reconstructed base rim and overly dense suspension wires; the final version shortens the rim to the source cradle footprint and uses one fainter line per ball.
