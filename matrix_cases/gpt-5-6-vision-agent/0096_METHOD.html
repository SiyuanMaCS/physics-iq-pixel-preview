Actual generation approach

This continuation uses only first_frame.png and local pixel operations implemented in generate.py.

  1. The source photograph is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is copied directly from that array without resampling or modification.
  2. For frames 01-19, the small visible footprint of the source domino and its contact shadow is removed once with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. A measured elliptical mask then restores the photographed silver magnet face over the inpainted area, preserving the foreground occlusion and texture.
  3. The moving domino is modeled as a small shaded plastic prism. Its measured screen geometry is 27.2 px long by 7.4 px wide, hinged at approximately (101.2, 47.7). It rotates clockwise through 58 degrees. The angle follows (t / 19)^1.12, which supplies a gentle start while leaving nonzero motion at the last frame.
  4. Each pose is drawn at 4x resolution and downsampled with Lanczos filtering. The rendering contains a pale lavender top gradient, two darker thickness faces, fine edge highlights, and a soft blurred contact shadow. Pixels within the magnet-face ellipse are removed from this moving layer so the domino's root passes behind the stationary magnet.
  5. The resulting RGB frames are passed to the provided artifact_io.save helper to create pixels.npz, all 20 PNG frames, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. transition_check.png separately shows enlarged motion crops and enhanced consecutive-frame differences.

Verification

  • Shape and dtype: exactly 20 frames of 144 x 256 x 3 uint8 RGB.
  • frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • Every one of the 20 frames is unique.
  • Consecutive changes remain localized to approximately x=97..131, y=37..76 around the domino and its shadow; the fixed camera/background does not drift.
  • Every saved PNG was read back and compared pixel-for-pixel with the in-memory frame.
  • pixels.npz was read back and checked as uint32 shape (144, 256, 20), with each value packed as 0xRRGGBB and exactly matching the PNG sequence.

Visual review

I reviewed first_frame.png, inspection.png, and transition_check.png with the available image viewer. The motion reads as a continuous clockwise sweep, the free end remains over the black platform, the shadow remains attached, and the stationary magnet correctly stays in front of the hinged end.
