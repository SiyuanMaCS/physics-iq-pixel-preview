ball-in-basket · 生成方法

This sequence is a local 2D pixel animation derived only from first_frame.png.

Image construction

  1. The orange shell is segmented in RGB space inside a measured ball region. The connected orange component is combined with the measured circular interior so that the original dark seam and white specular highlights remain part of the ball. Pixels belonging to the black crate rim are excluded.
  2. The source ball is stored as a premultiplied-alpha sprite. Because its very top is cropped in frame 00, the missing cap (only a few pixels) is analytically completed from the measured ellipse and extrapolated upper-row colors. This prevents a flat cropped edge after the ball moves downward.
  3. The newly exposed wall/projection-screen area is reconstructed scanline by scanline between real pixels immediately left and right of the original ball. This is appropriate for the smooth locked background and avoids boundary-inpainting artifacts. The small lower patch at the crate mouth is locally inpainted from its surrounding crate pixels.
  4. A foreground occlusion mask is extracted from the original crate using luminance and neutral-color thresholds. The original photographed rim, lattice bars, edge highlights, and shadows are composited over the moving ball. Thus the ball is visible through actual lattice openings but correctly hidden by the plastic structure.

Motion and physics

The ball center follows a hand-integrated damped vertical trajectory sampled at 4 fps: initial gravity-driven descent, first contact near frame 04, a reduced rebound to frame 07, a smaller second impact near frame 10, and short residual oscillations that settle by frames 18–19. Contact frames use modest vertical compression with compensating horizontal expansion. Illumination is reduced progressively as the shell enters the darker crate interior. There is no camera transform and all pixels outside the source ball/reveal region and moving-ball footprint remain derived from the unchanged photograph.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNG frames, the standalone player, the inspection sheet, and pixels.npz. The generator then writes a contact-focused transition_check.png and verifies:

  • array shape is (20, 144, 256, 3) with uint8 RGB channels;
  • frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every PNG is pixel-identical to its generated frame;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and exactly matches packed 0xRRGGBB pixels.

The final inspection and transition sheets were visually reviewed after generation.
