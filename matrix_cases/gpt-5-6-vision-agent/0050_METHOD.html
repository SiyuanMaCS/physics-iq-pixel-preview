dominos-with-space · 生成方法

This sequence was generated locally from first_frame.png; no external images, models, engines, masks, or references were used.

Pixel construction

  1. The 256 x 144 RGB source is loaded unchanged, and frame 00 (also frame 01 for a short initial hold) is a direct array copy.
  2. Small masks describe only the original wooden-arm stroke and the three dominoes nearest the platform. OpenCV Telea inpainting fills those original footprints from neighboring source pixels once the corresponding object begins to move. Only masked pixels are accepted from the repair, so the camera, wall, platform, table, distant row, and almost all wood texture stay source-exact.
  3. The arm is modeled as a 37.2-pixel rigid segment pivoted at (216, 40.2). It rotates clockwise from its observed down-left angle by 29 degrees, with a dark contact shadow, tan/green shaft shading, and a redrawn green hub.
  4. Each moving domino is a shaded perspective quadrilateral rotating leftward about its bottom/table contact edge. The black, white, and black pieces use staggered eased angle curves up to 88 degrees. Narrow highlight/shaded edges and an end cap preserve their slab-like thickness.
  5. Semi-transparent shadows follow the rotating quads and become denser and more compact as each tile reaches the tabletop. Rendering is performed on a 4x RGBA layer and Lanczos-downsampled for antialiased edges before compositing onto the source-derived background.
  6. The three dominoes closest to the arm topple in succession. The final black piece falls into the measured 36-pixel opening but cannot reach the four-piece left row, which remains upright.

Timing

  • 20 frames, 256 x 144 RGB, 4 fps, 5 seconds.
  • Frames 00-01: initial hold.
  • Frames 02-08: clockwise arm sweep and first black domino fall.
  • Frames 07-14: white domino transfer and fall.
  • Frames 11-17: final black domino falls into the gap.
  • Frames 18-19: final rest hold.

Validation and review

generate.py saves all deliverables through artifact_io.save, builds a consecutive-difference contact sheet, and then verifies:

  • frame 00 is array-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every frames/00.png through frames/19.png is RGB and array-identical to the generated frame;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and exactly matches the packed 0xRRGGBB pixels;
  • the generated stack is exactly (20, 144, 256, 3) with dtype uint8.

I visually reviewed first_frame.png, inspection.png, transition_check.png, and the final frame using the provided image viewer. The inspection showed the intended sequential contacts and the final gap stop; the transition sheet confirmed that changes remain confined to the arm/right-row action region and that the start/end holds are stable.
