OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Source is an RGB image at 256 x 144. The camera is locked off and the intended output is 20 frames at 4 fps.
  • A gray wall fills the upper field. The wooden tabletop begins at about y=87, with a shallow perspective edge: approximately (36,87) to (226,87), sloping down toward both side borders.
  • The tennis ball is centered at about (128,103), with a visible extent of x=117..139 and y=91..115. It is yellow-green, has a dark smile/mouth and eyes, and casts a small contact shadow.
  • The suspended clear acrylic box is about 41 px wide. Its top/front rim is near x=106..148, y=47..50; its side edges extend to about y=87. In frame 00 its lower edge is still roughly 4 px above the top of the ball.
  • The dark suspension hardware is centered at x=128. A cord/hook descends from the top border into the black clamp above the box. The prompt identifies the lowering cord as orange; later frames will make the lengthening moving segment subtly orange-brown while retaining the observed dark upper hook/hardware.
  • Lighting is soft and comes from above/front-left. The ball and box stay horizontally centered. No camera motion or unrelated objects should be introduced.

Motion and 20-frame event plan

  • Frame 00 (0.00 s): preserve the supplied image byte-for-pixel after RGB decoding.
  • Frames 01-02 (0.25-0.50 s): the rig takes up tension and begins to descend only slightly.
  • Frames 03-14 (0.75-3.50 s): the box and its top clamp lower smoothly along the vertical centerline. Use a cubic ease-in/ease-out profile to keep real-time motion continuous. The cord visibly lengthens; the table, ball, lighting, and camera remain fixed.
  • Frames 10-14: the transparent box passes around the ball. The ball remains visible through a very light cool acrylic tint; subtle front-edge highlights and a faint glass footprint/contact shadow communicate depth.
  • Frames 15-16 (3.75-4.00 s): the open lower rim reaches the tabletop around y=126 and settles without penetrating the table or moving the ball.
  • Frames 17-19 (4.25-4.75 s): hold the final covered-ball arrangement. A tiny, subpixel-equivalent settling change is allowed at frame 17, then the rig is still.

The future-frame clean plate will be reconstructed locally from neighboring pixels only in the narrow original rig region. The clear box, hardware, shadows, and transparent refraction/tint will then be rendered with local Pillow/NumPy pixel operations over the unchanged source scene.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is generated entirely with local NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV pixel operations. No external imagery, model, engine, mask, or reference is used.

  1. first_frame.png is decoded as RGB and copied unchanged into output frame 00.
  2. A clean plate is reconstructed only under the photographed hanging rig. The smooth wall is filled by row-wise interpolation from narrow left/right source strips with a small amount of borrowed local texture. The old acrylic edge at the table horizon is locally inpainted from neighboring pixels.
  3. The acrylic box is redrawn as a transparent front pane plus narrow side panes. Low-alpha cool tinting preserves the original scene and smiley ball beneath it. Asymmetric edge highlights, a dark top rim, connector hardware, and a faint near-table footprint provide depth cues.
  4. The top hook stays fixed. An orange-brown cord extends down to the moving connector while the entire box follows a quintic ease-in/ease-out path of 38 pixels. It reaches the tabletop around frame 16 and remains still through frame 19.
  5. The first two generated frames crossfade gently from the photographed rig into the locally reconstructed movable version at nearly the same geometry, avoiding a one-frame appearance discontinuity.
  6. artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes an amplified transition-difference sheet and verifies frame 00, every PNG, and the saved tensor against the in-memory RGB frames.
