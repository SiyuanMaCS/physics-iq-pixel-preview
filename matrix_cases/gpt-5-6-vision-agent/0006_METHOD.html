OBSERVATION.md

Observation and animation plan

Source-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB, locked camera, eye-level/very slightly elevated tabletop view.
  • Wall/table boundary: approximately (0, 84) -> (50, 66) -> (256, 96). The wall is neutral gray with a broad soft shadow behind the apparatus; the table is warm medium brown with fine horizontal/diagonal wood grain.
  • White cardstock: slightly trapezoidal photographed sheet, corners near (67,47), (155,51), (150,126), (62,116). Its visible center is near (108.5,86.5). It is matte off-white, subtly darker toward the lower edge, and casts/receives soft gray shading.
  • Black rotating platform: partly occluded by the sheet, roughly an ellipse/body spanning x=74..143, y=107..143; visible rim begins near y=113. It has a near-black top and a dark brown-black curved front.
  • Central black support behind the sheet: about x=124..134 at the top edge; it narrows upward and exits frame at (130,0). It remains fixed with the platform.
  • Cable: emerges near (137,128), runs to (169,134), then off the bottom-right; thin black curved line.
  • Lighting: diffuse from upper-left/front; shadows and object undersides fall down/right. Focus is modestly soft, with no hard graphic edges.
  • No ball or grabber is visible in frame 00. The orange ball and blue-black grabber therefore enter from above after the first frame.

Motion and physical interpretation

The cardstock is mounted upright on the motorized black platform and rotates clockwise in its own picture plane at a slow, steady rate. A blue-black grabber enters from the upper left holding an orange tennis ball, moves it over the upper edge, then lowers it on the far side of the card. Correct layer order is: rear grabber/ball, rear support, rotating card, then the platform foreground. Once the ball crosses the upper edge, progressively less of it is visible; it becomes fully hidden behind the card before release. The empty grabber rises and exits. The camera, wall, table, platform, cable, exposure, and frame remain fixed.

METHOD.md

Method

This continuation is generated entirely with local deterministic raster operations in generate.py; no external images, models, masks, engines, or network resources are used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as RGB and is copied byte-for-byte into frame 00.
  2. Pixels hidden by the source cardstock are reconstructed from same-row neighboring source colors, with low-amplitude deterministic analytic grain. Concealed portions of the fixed black support and turntable are rebuilt with softly antialiased local shapes. The untouched source photograph remains the background everywhere else.
  3. The photographed cardstock pixels and their polygon mask are projectively warped for frames 01–19. A vertical-axis clockwise yaw is modeled by horizontal foreshortening and opposite vertical displacement of its left/right edges. This keeps the sheet upright on the physical turntable rather than rotating it as a flat 2D graphic.
  4. A blue-black grabber and orange tennis ball are rendered locally with supersampled lines, polygons, ellipses, diffuse sphere shading, deterministic fuzz, and a curved tennis seam. Their path follows the event plan in OBSERVATION.md.
  5. Layer order is fixed apparatus/background, rear grabber and ball, soft cardstock shadow, then opaque cardstock. Thus the ball is naturally and progressively occluded as it is lowered behind the sheet. The jaws open while hidden, and the empty tool retreats.
  6. artifact_io.save writes all PNGs, the packed 0xRRGGBB uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. transition_check.png shows selected before/after pairs plus amplified deltas. The generator then reopens every PNG and verifies it against both the in-memory RGB frames and pixels.npz.

The camera, exposure, tabletop, wall, platform, and cable are held fixed. Motion is limited to the card, ball, and grabber described by the prompt.
