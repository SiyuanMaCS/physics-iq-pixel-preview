OBSERVATION.md

Source observation and animation plan

Locked scene measurements

  • Source/frame size: 256 x 144 pixels; output is a fixed 4:3-style wide crop at 4 fps.
  • Camera: static, slightly downward view. The neutral gray wall fills the upper background. A honey-brown wooden tabletop begins at about (0,108), runs behind the bowl, and reaches about (256,61) at the right edge.
  • Clear glass bowl: outer silhouette approximately x=78..205, y=71..143. Its upper rim is an ellipse centered near (140,99), with radii about (61,29). The visible water surface is approximately the inner ellipse x=88..192, y=80..124; highlights and the transparent/refractive bowl edges must remain stationary.
  • Grabber: enters from the top at about x=108..151. The black stem bends down into two orange-edged jaws. The jaw/paper area occupies roughly x=111..151, y=0..65.
  • Crumpled paper: irregular pale gray/tan wad centered near (134,49), approximately x=123..146, y=34..66. It hangs about 30 pixels above the near water surface. Its folds are represented by small gray, tan, and white ridges.
  • Illumination: soft from upper left. The left background is brighter than the right; the bowl has sharp white highlights. Added paper and water effects should inherit that direction.
  • No camera motion, cuts, text, new props, hands, or changes outside the grabber/paper/water interaction.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, five seconds)

Frame Event
00 Exact source image: jaws hold the crumpled paper above the bowl.
01 Static establishing hold; the paper remains pinched.
02 Final steady held moment before release begins.
03 The grip loosens and the paper shifts down about 1 px.
04 Final instant of contact; paper is poised directly below the jaws.
05 Release: a small air gap appears below the open jaws.
06 Paper falls vertically, with slight clockwise rotation, still well above the rim.
07 Faster fall; the paper reaches the upper part of the bowl opening.
08 Paper approaches the water surface and its lower edge nearly touches it.
09 First water contact: the wad compresses slightly and a small bright central splash/crown appears.
10 Paper settles into the surface; short low ripples expand from the impact point.
11 Ripple ring expands and dims; paper rebounds upward by about 1 px.
12 Paper floats low, darkening subtly as it wets; second weaker ring expands.
13 Ripples continue outward within the bowl, respecting the elliptical perspective.
14 Ripples weaken; the wet wad rotates/drifts slightly right.
15 Only faint broad ripples remain; paper stays afloat near center.
16 Water approaches calm; wad rests lower and slightly darker.
17 Residual highlight arcs fade.
18 Nearly static final state: jaws open above, wet paper floating in bowl.
19 Calm end hold with the same fixed framing.

The fall is deliberately concentrated into several frames so gravity reads clearly at 4 fps, while the remaining frames show impact, damping ripples, wetting, and a stable final state.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence is generated entirely with local Python pixel operations using Pillow and NumPy. No external model, engine, reference image, or network resource is used.

The first frame is decoded from first_frame.png, placed unchanged into the frame tensor, and its original PNG byte stream is copied to frames/00.png. The paper silhouette was measured by hand from the supplied image. Pixels belonging to the real crumpled wad are extracted with a feathered polygon while orange pixels belonging to the grabber are excluded. The small patch of wall uncovered after release is reconstructed from horizontal interpolation between untouched wall samples on both sides of the object.

The extracted original paper pixels are translated with an accelerating vertical displacement, rotated slightly, and bicubically resampled. On contact they are compressed vertically, desaturated, and darkened gradually to suggest wetting. A blurred ellipse supplies the approaching/settled shadow. The water response consists of perspective-flattened, broken elliptical highlight arcs clipped to the measured water ellipse, a very small three-frame splash crown, and rapidly decaying secondary ripples. These effects are intentionally subtle so the photographic source remains dominant.

artifact_io.save writes the RGB PNG sequence, a (144, 256, 20) uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection sheet, and the standalone player. The generator additionally writes transition_check.png, replaces frame 00 with the exact source PNG bytes, and verifies every decoded PNG against the tensor. It also asserts frame-00 pixel identity, frame-00 file identity, dimensions, modes, dtype, and sequence variation.
