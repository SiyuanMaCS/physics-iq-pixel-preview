OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 pixels, landscape, RGB output required.
  • Camera: fixed, eye-level/slightly elevated view across a wooden tabletop; no zoom, pan, tilt, roll, or focus change.
  • Table/background boundary: nearly horizontal at about y=47 on the left and y=45 on the right. The light-brown wood fills the lower roughly 68% of the image; a plain warm gray-white wall fills the upper region.
  • Duck: centered slightly right of the image midpoint. Its visible silhouette spans approximately x=114..158 and y=51..101. The head is centered near (134, 69), the body near (136, 88), and the bottom contact is near y=101.
  • Duck details: saturated yellow body; orange bill around x=125..140, y=71..80; one visible dark eye near (122, 67); a tiny dark nostril; subtle yellow highlights and olive/orange shaded edges.
  • Illumination: soft light from upper left/front. The wall and tabletop have broad natural gradients. A soft contact/cast shadow extends mainly rightward from the duck over roughly x=143..183, y=88..106.
  • Other scene features: subtle wood grain and knots; no other objects, people, text, overlays, or moving elements.

Temporal interpretation

The prompt explicitly calls the shot static and says the rubber duck sits stationary. Therefore, the physically correct result is a constant sequence: neither camera nor subject nor illumination changes. Reusing the observed source pixels also avoids inventing motion, compression shimmer, sensor noise, or unrequested actions.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

This sequence uses direct local pixel preservation. generate.py opens first_frame.png with Pillow, converts it to RGB, checks that it is 256 x 144, and repeats that exact RGB array for all 20 frames. No generative model, external engine, interpolation, synthetic texture, camera transform, or motion effect is used.

This is intentional rather than a placeholder: the prompt locks the camera and explicitly says the yellow rubber duck sits stationary. Keeping all pixels fixed preserves the photographed duck, wooden surface, background, cast shadow, lighting, and sensor/render texture without introducing an action that was not requested.

Outputs

  • pixels.npz contains a uint32 tensor with shape (144, 256, 20), packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png are 20 RGB frames at 256 x 144 and 4 fps.
  • frames/00.png is copied back from the source after artifact generation, making it file-identical as well as pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • inspection.png is the standard eight-sample contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png compares the first and last frames and displays a black absolute-difference panel. It also reports the changed-pixel and maximum-channel-delta totals.
  • index.html is a standalone 4 fps player containing the packed frame data.

Verification

The script reloads every generated PNG, repacks each pixel to uint32 0xRRGGBB, and asserts exact equality with the corresponding pixels.npz plane. It also asserts exact source equality for frame 00 and zero changed channel values across all 19 consecutive transitions.

Run with:

python3 generate.py
