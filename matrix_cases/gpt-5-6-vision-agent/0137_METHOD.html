OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, 256 x 144 RGB. The camera is static and the composition is centered.
  • Background: softly lit gray-green wall from y=0 to about y=112. Illumination is brighter behind the vessel (roughly x=95..178) and darker toward both sides.
  • Table: the rear edge runs from approximately (42,113) through (211,113), descending toward both lower corners. The tabletop is warm brown with fine horizontal/diagonal grain.
  • Vessel: a tall clear glass centered near x=139. The outer silhouette is about x=113..164. Its cropped neck enters through the top edge, the shoulder/body begins around y=79, and its thick bottom sits at y=139..143. The side walls subtly bow outward around the liquid chamber.
  • Blue liquid: bounded by the vessel, approximately x=115..162 and y=88..138. It is cyan-blue near the top and darker teal toward the bottom. A narrow dark meniscus lies immediately below the green marker.
  • Green water-level marker: a saturated green band around y=85..93, thickest at the outer sides and visually interrupted by glass reflections. Its top is slightly curved/elliptical.
  • Glass optics: bright vertical highlights occur around x=122..128 and x=145..153; the vessel also has dark refractive edge strips and a bright thick base.
  • Grabber: blue-black prongs enter at the cropped upper rim, chiefly around x=118..125 and x=154..160. Their visible portions are small, so the release is expressed by a subtle outward pivot rather than translating the whole tool into the scene.
  • Paper: a small pale crumpled piece at/just inside the upper rim is low-contrast against the glass highlight. For continuity it will become distinctly readable only after release, retaining a small irregular folded silhouette rather than a smooth ball.

Motion and physics assumptions

  • The paper starts near x=139, y=8, falls under gravity through the empty upper part of the vessel, reaches the liquid surface near y=88, partially submerges, and then bobs back to float at/just below the marked level.
  • At 4 fps, a literal free fall over roughly 80 image pixels would finish in too few frames to read. The image-space timing is therefore eased but remains accelerating: release/opening, clearly readable fall, surface impact, short submergence, upward recovery, damped bob.
  • The paper experiences slight horizontal flutter (at most 2 px), modest rotation, and small deformation. It does not pass through the glass wall.
  • The liquid reaction is local and restrained: a small dimple/crown at impact, then two low-amplitude ripples that damp rapidly. The green band remains a fixed marking on the vessel rather than moving with the water.
  • All pixels outside localized grabber/paper/liquid-effect regions remain copied from the source frame to preserve the locked shot.

METHOD.md

Generation method

Actual method used

The sequence is a deterministic local Python/Pillow/NumPy render built only from first_frame.png and the written prompt. No network resources, external images, masks, models, or rendering engines were used.

  1. The source frame is loaded as 256 x 144 RGB and copied exactly for frame 00.
  2. Every later frame begins from the same photographed source, preserving the locked camera and all background/table/vessel texture.
  3. The visible inner tips of the blue-black grabber are drawn as anti-aliased layered curves. Their tips pivot apart over frames 01–04 and then remain open. The long outer portions already present in the source are retained.
  4. The crumpled paper is an irregular polygon with several independently shaded facets, fold lines, a soft contact shadow, and a small highlight. A per-frame center/angle schedule creates accelerating descent, slight side-to-side flutter, impact compression, submergence, recovery, and damped bobbing.
  5. While underwater, the paper palette is shifted toward blue-green and reduced in contrast. A fraction of the photographed glass specular highlights is composited back over it, making it appear inside the transparent vessel instead of pasted on its front.
  6. The water response is procedural: flattened perspective ellipses form restrained ripples, two short arcs form the impact crown, and tiny outlined circles form bubbles during submergence. A vessel-shaped mask prevents these marks from crossing the glass walls.
  7. Pixels from the photographed green level band are partially restored over the new action, so the external marker correctly occludes the paper and water effects.
  8. Rendering is performed at 4x resolution and reduced with Lanczos filtering. After reduction, the original source pixels are explicitly restored everywhere outside compact tong, paper, and water-effect masks. This prevents supersampling from introducing false motion into the wall, table, or stationary glass.

Timing

  • 20 frames
  • 256 x 144 RGB
  • 4 frames per second
  • 5 seconds total playback

The paper is released near the beginning, contacts the marked liquid level in frame 09, reaches maximum submergence in frame 11, and settles into a small floating bob by frame 19.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py separately writes transition_check.png, which pairs enlarged action crops with consecutive-frame difference heat maps.

The generator then verifies all of the following with exact NumPy comparisons:

  • frame 00 equals first_frame.png byte-for-byte after RGB decoding;
  • every frames/00.png–frames/19.png decodes to its in-memory RGB frame exactly;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and every value equals packed 0xRRGGBB from the corresponding PNG;
  • every consecutive frame contains a nonzero localized change.

The final inspection.png and transition_check.png were visually reviewed after the localization correction. The reviewed sequence shows a stable locked scene, readable paper descent, contained water response, and a damped final float.
