OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured composition

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB, fixed camera, landscape view.
  • A pale laminated wood coffee-table top occupies roughly y=62..135. Its far edge is nearly horizontal, while its right edge recedes diagonally from about (220, 63) to (255, 134).
  • A dark pipe/wheel-like outlet is partly cropped at the far left, approximately x=0..18, y=54..78; it stays completely static.
  • The mustard sofa and dark room background occupy the upper part of the image and stay completely static.
  • The grey-blue tennis ball in frame 00 is centered at approximately (108, 76), with a visible body radius of about 7 px. Its soft contact shadow extends mostly to the lower-right, to about (119, 84).
  • The tabletop's diagonal right boundary means a ball traveling deep into the right side must also move downward in the image. A projected path from (108, 76) toward (234, 104) remains visibly supported by the tabletop and reads as rightward motion in perspective.
  • Lighting is soft and warm from above/front-left. The ball has a pale upper-left highlight, a darker lower-right side, and a low-opacity diffuse shadow.

Motion and 20-frame event plan

The camera and every non-ball pixel remain locked. Frame 00 is copied exactly from first_frame.png. For subsequent frames, the original ball area is reconstructed from nearby horizontal wood texture; a shaded, subtly rotating ball and its contact shadow are drawn at the planned positions.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

This sequence was generated locally with deterministic Python pixel operations; no external model, engine, reference image, mask, or network resource was used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is a direct copy of that array and is never redrawn.
  2. For frames 01–19, the source ball and its attached shadow are removed once with two explicit elliptical masks and OpenCV Telea inpainting. This works well here because the hidden table is dominated by broad horizontal wood bands.
  3. The ball follows 20 hand-measured image-plane centers from (108, 76) to (234, 104). The downward component is deliberate perspective compensation for the tabletop's diagonal right boundary.
  4. A diffuse elliptical contact shadow is composited under the ball at every generated position.
  5. The ball is rendered analytically as a 4x-supersampled grey-blue sphere. It uses fixed upper-left Lambertian/specular lighting, a soft fuzzy silhouette, restrained object-space texture, and a faint seam. Object-space normals rotate by traveled distance divided by radius, giving a no-slip rolling cue while the illumination remains fixed in scene space.
  6. The supersampled RGBA ball is Lanczos-filtered to the 256 x 144 frame and alpha-composited over the repaired tabletop.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py separately writes an all-frame transition sheet.

Verification and review

  • Array shape and type are asserted as (20, 144, 256, 3) and uint8.
  • Frame 00 is asserted pixel-identical to first_frame.png; its saved PNG byte hash is also asserted equal to the source PNG hash.
  • Every frames/00.png–frames/19.png is decoded and compared pixel-for-pixel with the in-memory frame.
  • pixels.npz is checked for shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and exact equality with RGB packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png, transition_check.png, frames/01.png, and frames/19.png were actually reviewed with the image-view tool. The review confirmed a fixed camera/background, a continuous monotonic down-right roll, a stable contact shadow, acceptable restoration of the original ball location, and full tabletop support through the final frame.

Run python3 generate.py from this directory to reproduce and re-verify all generated artifacts.
