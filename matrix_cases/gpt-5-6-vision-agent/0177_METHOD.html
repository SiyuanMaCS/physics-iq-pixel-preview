OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and motion plan

Locked scene measurements

  • Source is an RGB image at 256 x 144 pixels; the requested camera is locked for all 20 frames.
  • The table/wall boundary runs diagonally from about (0, 91) through (82, 66) to (255, 96). The warm wooden tabletop fills the lower portion; the softly lit gray wall fills the upper portion.
  • The black powered turntable/platform occupies approximately x=100..165, y=86..125. Its main cylindrical base, cast shadow, small front highlight, and the cord leaving toward the right are stationary.
  • The rotating teapot/pedestal assembly occupies approximately x=102..164, y=10..105, with a visual rotation axis near x=132. The blue rounded body is about x=104..158, y=25..87; the lid/knob is about x=118..145, y=10..35; the yellow-green foot/top plate is about x=116..150, y=86..103.
  • The teapot is highly glossy and decorated: dark cobalt body, red-orange spout on the viewer's right, small red/orange/green floral and character motifs, yellow-green trim, bright specular highlights, and a small black lid knob.
  • Illumination is soft from the upper left. The wall has a diffuse bright patch at upper left, and the teapot/platform cast a soft shadow down-right on the table.
  • No camera move, zoom, reframing, new object, hand, or text is permitted. Only the teapot and its directly attached upper pedestal/foot rotate; the black base, cord, background, and existing environmental shadows remain stable apart from a subtle contact-shadow modulation immediately beneath the rotating foot.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00 (0.00 s): exact byte-for-byte visual copy of first_frame.png.
  • Frames 01-03 (0.25-0.75 s): rotation eases into a slow clockwise turn; motifs begin drifting rightward across the near face while the spout begins moving toward the far/right side.
  • Frames 04-07 (1.00-1.75 s): steady clockwise rotation; the round body and lid remain centered, visible surface decoration progresses smoothly, and the spout foreshortens slightly.
  • Frames 08-11 (2.00-2.75 s): steady turn continues; a different portion of the blue decorated body rotates into the near-facing center. Gloss highlights remain tied mostly to the fixed light/camera rather than sliding rigidly with the painted texture.
  • Frames 12-15 (3.00-3.75 s): clockwise rotation continues at the same slow physical rate. The asymmetric spout is more side-on/partly receding, providing the clearest depth cue; attached trim and foot rotate consistently.
  • Frames 16-19 (4.00-4.75 s): the teapot reaches about 28 degrees clockwise from its initial pose, still turning at the end rather than stopping. Inter-frame displacement remains small and continuous with no loop reset.

Intended pixel method

Use only local Python/Pillow/NumPy operations on first_frame.png. Preserve the source as the base of every frame. Build hand-measured soft masks for the teapot body, lid, spout, and upper foot; use a cylindrical inverse warp plus angular texture advection for the rounded painted body, affine/perspective foreshortening for asymmetric attachments, fixed-screen relighting/specular overlays, edge feathering, and local source-derived background reconstruction where the moving silhouette uncovers pixels. Keep frame 00 untouched.

METHOD.md

Generation method

Actual method used

The animation is a local 2.5D pixel reconstruction made from first_frame.png with NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. No external images, models, engines, masks, or network resources were used.

  1. I measured and hand-authored soft polygon masks for the cobalt body, lid/knob, green upper foot, and large red spout. The black powered platform and cord are not part of the rotating masks.
  2. The source image is the base for every generated frame. A narrow locally inpainted clean plate is used only behind the rotating silhouette so that subpixel edge changes do not reveal duplicate source pixels. All other background and fixed-platform pixels come directly from the source.
  3. The red spout is isolated using a hand-bounded HSV selection and connected-component extraction. Its source pixels are locally inpainted out of the underlying blue body layer.
  4. The rounded body is inverse-warped as a visible cylindrical/spherical surface. Painted decoration is advected by longitude as yaw increases. Newly revealed longitudes fade toward source-matched cobalt ceramic instead of mirroring high-contrast decoration at the limb.
  5. The lid and upper green foot use the same rotation phase with smaller cylindrical texture displacement appropriate to their shallower shapes.
  6. The asymmetric spout is composited separately with progressive horizontal foreshortening toward its attachment point, a small positional recession, and modest far-side darkening. This provides the main depth cue for clockwise rotation.
  7. Screen-space ceramic highlights are reintroduced lightly, limb shading stays consistent with illumination from upper left, and a subtle contact-occlusion ellipse remains directly above the fixed black base.
  8. Motion eases in over the opening frames and then continues at constant speed, reaching approximately 28 degrees clockwise at frame 19. The animation does not stop or reset at the final frame.

Timing and output

  • 20 RGB frames, 256 x 144 pixels, at 4 fps (5 seconds as specified by the playback convention).
  • frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • artifact_io.save writes the frame PNGs, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz, the inspection contact sheet, and standalone index.html player.
  • transition_check.png shows three consecutive-frame pairs alongside 5x amplified absolute differences for motion-locality and continuity review.

Visual review and revision

I reviewed both inspection.png and transition_check.png with the image viewing tool. The first render used a 42-degree turn; its newly revealed limb became too visually busy and the spout recession was too weak. The final render reduces the turn to 28 degrees, darkens the revealed cobalt limb, tightens the highlight mask, and increases independent spout foreshortening. The final sheets show a continuous slow turn with a locked background and no frame jump.
