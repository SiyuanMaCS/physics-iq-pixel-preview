OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Reference measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB, locked camera.
  • Table/wall boundary: approximately (0,96) -> (97,90) -> (168,88) -> (206,81) -> (255,95); the surface fills the lower part of frame.
  • Basket: roughly x=98..168, y=61..131. It is a warm tan cylindrical weave, about 68 px wide and 66 px high, with a dark base/contact shadow.
  • Basket top: an elliptical woven rim near y=62..76; a black circular magnet/attachment sits near x=122..154, y=58..69.
  • Suspension: dark chain centered around x=139, running from about y=10 to the magnet; a short orange cord enters at the top edge near x=140.
  • Lighting: soft overhead/front-left light. Basket's left/front face is brighter; its right edge and underside are darker. The tabletop cast shadow falls mostly down-left.
  • Ball: fully occluded in frame 00. It will be reconstructed under the basket center at about x=135, with radius about 10 px and table contact near y=121. It is orange with a pale curved tennis-ball seam, a small upper-left highlight, and a soft down-left contact shadow.
  • Static frame: no camera motion, no new scenery, no people, and no objects beyond the stated demonstration.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic local Python pixel composite built from first_frame.png. No external imagery, models, masks, engines, or network resources are used.

  1. The basket, magnet, chain, and orange cord are isolated from the source using a hand-measured antialiased silhouette plus local luminance/color selection for the thin irregular chain and cord.
  2. A locked clean plate is reconstructed only where the original assembly and its contact shadow occlude the gray wall and wooden tabletop. Local diffusion fills the textured table; horizontal interpolation and gentle smoothing restore the nearly textureless wall.
  3. A stationary orange tennis ball is rendered behind the moving assembly with supersampled radial shading, two clipped cream seam arcs, and a soft tabletop contact shadow.
  4. The source assembly pixels and their alpha matte are translated vertically through a hand-tuned eased 20-frame trajectory. Its broad basket shadow fades and softens with height. Layer order is clean plate, shadows, ball, then moving source assembly, so the ball is progressively uncovered by actual occlusion.
  5. Frames 00-02 preserve the source exactly; frame 00 is asserted byte-for-byte equal to first_frame.png. The saved PNGs are decoded and compared against the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz.

The output is 20 RGB frames at 256 x 144, representing 5 seconds at 4 fps with a fixed camera.
