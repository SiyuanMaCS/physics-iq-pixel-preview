OBSERVATION.md

Scene observations and animation plan

Source and fixed geometry

  • The source is a 256 x 144 RGB photograph. The camera is locked off, with shallow depth of field and mild photographic blur.
  • A warm wooden tabletop occupies roughly y=70..143. A cool light-gray wall occupies the upper background; a dark vertical strip is at x=239..255.
  • The Newton's-cradle base is a dark, glossy trapezoid spanning about x=88..205, y=102..143. Its front chrome edge runs from approximately (97,138) to (197,127).
  • Two chrome rectangular support loops are visible. Their important uprights are near x=87, x=108, x=168, and x=200, and the top rails lie around y=8..20. They and the base remain completely fixed.
  • The compact row of cradle balls lies at about y=99..106, with bright steel highlights between x=128 and x=169. Their dark reflections lie near y=119..137.
  • The released steel ball begins near center (79,110), radius about 8 px. It is held from the left by a blue-and-black grabber occupying x=0..74. The ball begins well left of the resting row, at the visible swing amplitude.
  • The pulled ball's suspension is extremely fine and low contrast. Its effective pivot is near (128,18), with a perspective-projected rest/collision position near (128,103). The ball therefore follows a pendulum arc, not a straight translation.
  • Lighting is soft from the upper-left. Steel balls have a dark lower-left body, a pale gray top, and a tiny near-white highlight toward the upper-right. Any synthesized moving ball must retain that lighting and the source blur.

Motion and compositing decisions

  • Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from first_frame.png.
  • From frame 01 onward, the original held ball is removed from its source location with a locally reconstructed tabletop/base/support background. The original grabber remains at the left edge but its jaw tip is progressively shifted left a few pixels, reading as a small release/retraction rather than a camera or scene cut.
  • A steel ball is rendered along an analytic damped pendulum path. It accelerates toward the row, transfers its impulse at contact, and then the far-right ball swings outward. Subsequent transfers alternate between the two end balls with decreasing amplitude. The middle balls only receive tiny compression/jitter offsets.
  • The moving string is drawn as a subpixel, low-opacity double suspension line before its ball. Moving-ball shadows/reflections are softly warped on the glossy base. Occlusion order is background, string/shadow, balls, then any foreground support elements.
  • Motion blur is strongest during the lowest/fastest portion of each swing and is made from several short temporal samples. It stays modest so the photographic ball remains recognizable at 4 fps.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

The animation is a deterministic local Python/OpenCV/Pillow pixel-compositing pipeline. It does not use a renderer, external model, network source, or content from another case.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as the sole visual source. Frame 00 is retained exactly, without re-encoding inside the frame tensor before output.
  2. A clean locked-off plate is made by applying OpenCV TELEA inpainting only to the original held-ball ellipse and the far-right end-ball ellipse. The fill is softly blended only inside those masks. The blue/black grabber is otherwise kept fixed, so it reads as an opened tool remaining just outside the swing path.
  3. A feathered RGB/alpha ball texture is sampled from the source photograph's isolated rightmost steel ball. It is resized slightly along the path to preserve the photograph's perspective and shallow-focus character.
  4. Hand-measured ball centers describe a physically motivated Newton's-cradle sequence: the released ball accelerates along a curved approach, contacts the row, the opposite end rises, and later impulses alternate with decreasing amplitude. Middle balls remain nearly fixed apart from a very small contact glint.
  5. Fine two-line suspension strings are drawn at low opacity from measured top-rail points to each moving ball. Short, weighted temporal samples add restrained motion blur during fast travel. Soft elongated reflections are placed on the glossy black base.
  6. Source strips for the near chrome uprights are restored last. This puts moving strings and balls behind the foreground supports wherever their paths cross, preserving scene depth.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNG frames, the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. The script additionally creates transition_check.png and verifies every PNG against pixels.npz.

Reproduction

Run:

python3 generate.py

The script asserts a (20, 144, 256, 3) uint8 frame stack, exact equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png, and exact equality among the PNG pixels and the (144, 256, 20) packed tensor.
