OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured composition

  • Source is an RGB image at exactly 256 x 144 pixels. The camera is locked off at table height, looking slightly downward.
  • A black rectangular Newton's-cradle base occupies approximately x=51..157, y=91..126. Its bright front rim runs diagonally from about (52,116) to (139,130).
  • The chrome support frame occupies approximately x=48..157, y=8..120. The upper rails are near y=9 and y=20, with uprights near x=50, x=80, x=141, and x=154.
  • Five balls are visible. The two lifted balls are clustered around (75,89) and (81,91), held at the left amplitude by a blue-black grabber entering from the left. The three resting balls are centered around (110,96), (120,97), and (129,98). Ball diameter is about 8 pixels.
  • The suspended strings converge on the ball centers from the upper rails. They are thin, gray/chrome, and partially translucent against the wall.
  • The grabber occupies x=0..87 and y=70..97. It has a black handle/arm, bright blue upper and lower jaw surfaces, and dark tips around the lifted balls.
  • Background is a plain warm gray wall above a honey-brown wood tabletop. Lighting comes from upper left; metal balls have compact white highlights and dark lower rims. Shadows fall mostly down-right.

Motion and compositing approach

  • Preserve the photographed frame, support, base, tabletop, wall, and lighting as much as possible.
  • Retain the photographed grabber body, remove the source ball/string positions with local background reconstruction, then composite shaded metal balls, analytic thin strings, contact shadows, and subtly opening jaw-tip overlays.
  • Use pendulum motion about measured upper pivots. During the first downswing the two left balls move together. At bottom contact their velocity transfers to the two rightmost balls; later they return for a smaller reverse transfer.
  • Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from first_frame.png; generated compositing begins at frame 01.

METHOD.md

Method

I generated the continuation locally from first_frame.png using deterministic Python pixel operations only.

The photographed wall, table, Newton's-cradle frame, base, lighting, and grabber body are retained from the source. OpenCV local inpainting removes the five original ball locations and their thin source suspension threads. Inpainting the lifted-ball disks from their immediate neighborhood reconstructs the blue/black jaw material that was hidden beneath them; there is no large synthetic tabletop replacement. Small jaw-tip overlays suggest the opening action. The released balls are rendered at 4x resolution as small shaded steel spheres with a dark rim, reflected wall/base/table bands, and compact white highlights, then downsampled for antialiasing. Each ball has two thin perspective suspension threads and a soft base shadow.

Motion uses measured resting positions and pendulum-like sampled displacements. Frames 1–5 bring the two left balls from the photographed left amplitude to contact. On that sampled impact, displacement transfers to the two rightmost balls for frames 6–12. A damped reverse transfer sends the two left balls outward in frames 13–18, and frame 19 begins the next smaller rightward response. The blue-black grabber tips open over frames 1–3 and remain clear of the pendulum path while the photographed tool stays at the left edge.

artifact_io.save packs every RGB frame into a uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor and builds the standalone player. The generation script then restores the original PNG bytes as frames/00.png, produces an adjacent-frame difference sheet, and verifies every decoded PNG pixel against pixels.npz. Frame 00 is additionally checked for byte-for-byte identity with the input.
