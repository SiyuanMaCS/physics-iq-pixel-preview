OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Scene measurements

  • Source is RGB, 256 x 144. Camera is static and the frame must remain locked.
  • Wooden tabletop begins at an oblique back edge running approximately from (0, 96) through (54, 71) to (255, 88).
  • Green kinetic-sand mound occupies approximately x=73..165, y=89..132; its crown is near y=88..93 and its visual center is near (119, 111).
  • Blue tennis ball occupies approximately x=104..132, y=46..76, with center near (118, 61) and visible diameter about 27 px.
  • Blue-black grabber occupies the upper-center region, approximately x=97..143, y=0..47. It is already open, and remains suspended/fixed after release.
  • The starting clearance is about 13 px from the ball bottom to the sand crown.
  • Lighting is soft, from above/front-left. The ball has a cyan-blue highlight on its left/center and a darker teal rim on the right/bottom. The sand has small, irregular granular clumps and a soft contact shadow on the table.

20-frame event plan (4 fps)

  • Frame 00: exact source frame; open grabber, released ball at its starting position.
  • Frames 01-02: ball accelerates downward under gravity, crossing the short air gap; grabber and camera remain fixed.
  • Frame 03: first contact with the mound crown; slight vertical ball squash and first shallow indentation.
  • Frames 04-06: impact/compression phase. Ball pushes into the sand, squashes subtly, and creates a darker central cavity; green sand bulges outward in a low irregular ring.
  • Frames 07-09: kinetic sand absorbs most energy. Ball makes a tiny damped rebound/relaxation while recovering its round shape; disturbed sand remains heaped around its lower sides.
  • Frames 10-13: ball settles back a few pixels into the cavity. Local sand clumps creep inward and upward against the ball.
  • Frames 14-19: final stable state. Ball is partially embedded and motionless, with a persistent indentation, irregular green rim, and slightly broadened mound footprint.

Only the ball, its local shadow, and the nearby sand deformation change. No cuts, camera motion, new objects, or tool motion are introduced.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence is generated entirely with local Python pixel operations from first_frame.png; no external models, engines, images, masks, or references are used.

  1. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from the supplied RGB source.
  2. A clean plate for later frames is formed by masking only the original tennis-ball ellipse and reconstructing each covered row by color interpolation between pixels immediately to its left and right. Row-wise reconstruction preserves the slanted wall/table boundary without pulling table color upward. The grabber, mound, and all other source pixels remain fixed.
  3. The ball layer is extracted from the source using the color/difference evidence between the original and clean plate, constrained by a soft measured ellipse. It is translated along a hand-tuned gravity/impact trajectory. During first contact it receives a small, temporary anisotropic scale to represent squash, then recovers.
  4. The kinetic-sand response is constructed from locally sampled green mound colors. A shaded cavity, low lateral displacement lobes, irregular granular clumps, foreground rim, contact seam, and approach/contact shadow are alpha-composited in physical depth order. The motion uses one strong compression, a very small damped rebound, and final settling.
  5. artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNG frames, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py separately creates transition_check.png from adjacent frame pairs and amplified pixel differences.
  6. The generator verifies dimensions/type, exact equality of frame 00 to the source, every saved PNG against the in-memory RGB frames, and pixels.npz against the same frames packed as uint32.

python3 generate.py
