+def soft_ellipse(h, w, cx, cy, rx, ry, feather=1.4):
+    """Return an antialiased elliptical alpha matte in [0,1]."""
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:h, :w]
+    d = np.sqrt(((xx - cx) / rx) ** 2 + ((yy - cy) / ry) ** 2)
+    edge = max(feather / max(rx, ry), 1e-4)
+    return np.clip((1.0 + edge - d) / (2.0 * edge), 0.0, 1.0)
+
+
+def over(base, layer, alpha):
+    """Float alpha composite layer over uint8 RGB base."""
+    a = np.clip(alpha, 0.0, 1.0)[..., None]
+    return np.clip(base.astype(np.float32) * (1.0 - a) + layer.astype(np.float32) * a, 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)
+
+
+def make_clean_table(source):
+    """Remove only the source ball and its cast shadow using local table pixels."""
+    mask = np.zeros((H, W), np.uint8)
+    # The region covers the body (x≈40..61) and shadow (x≈52..71), but
+    # deliberately stops short of both the tube and lampshade.
+    cv2.ellipse(mask, (53, 49), (19, 14), 0, 0, 360, 255, -1)
+    mask = cv2.GaussianBlur(mask, (3, 3), 0)
+    clean = cv2.inpaint(source, mask, 4, cv2.INPAINT_TELEA)
+
+    # Suppress the slight radial texture left by inpainting without flattening
+    # the surrounding horizontal wood grain. Blend a horizontally filtered
+    # version only in the central repaired area.
+    repaired = cv2.GaussianBlur(clean, (9, 3), 0)
+    inner = soft_ellipse(H, W, 53, 49, 17, 12, 3.0) * 0.32
+    clean = over(clean, repaired, inner)
+    return clean

+def extracted_ball(source):

+    x0, y0, x1, y1 = 38, 35, 64, 61

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Frame and camera

  • Source is an RGB image at 256×144. The camera is static, with no zoom, pan, or cut.
  • The tabletop fills approximately y=9..143. Its front edge is outside the frame; pale horizontal wood grain runs mainly left-to-right.
  • The back wall / bench boundary is near y=8..25. Illumination comes from upper-left, producing soft shadows toward lower-right.

Measured scene elements

  • Black tube/nozzle: enters from the left edge, approximately x=0..29 and y=25..54. Its round opening/nearest dark rim is centered near (20, 42). It stays fixed.
  • Grey tennis ball in frame 00: center approximately (52, 47), visible body radius about 8–9 px, with a soft contact shadow extending to about x=40..65, y=43..59. It has a brighter upper-left face and darker lower-right edge.
  • White lampshade: fixed foreground obstacle. Its top quadrilateral has corners near (73,42), (104,20), (137,31), (112,55); its body extends to a bottom point near (120,111), with side extents around x=74..145. Along the ball's trajectory at y≈47, the shade occupies approximately x=78..140, so the ball must be occluded behind it.
  • Clear tabletop to the right continues through x=255. A plausible straight travel line is horizontal at ball center y≈47, consistent with the tube mouth and the initial ball location.

Motion and compositing model

  • Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from first_frame.png.
  • For subsequent frames, the original ball and its shadow are removed from a clean background using a small local inpaint restricted to their original footprint.
  • The observed ball appearance is extracted from frame 00 with a feathered circular matte. It is translated along the tabletop; the soft shadow is rendered separately so its contact with the table remains believable.
  • A lampshade occlusion mask is taken from the unchanged source pixels. The ball layer is placed behind this mask, so it disappears while traversing x≈79..143 and emerges on the right.
  • Motion is constant-speed and left-to-right. Subpixel placement and a very small directional blur avoid harsh 4-fps jumps. No other object changes.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic local pixel composite derived only from first_frame.png.

  1. Frame 00 is the unmodified source array.
  2. For frames 01–19, a tight ellipse covering the original ball and its right-cast shadow is reconstructed with OpenCV's local Telea inpaint and a mild horizontal smoothing blend that retains the surrounding wood appearance.
  3. The observed ball body is extracted from the source with a feathered elliptical matte, keeping its real shading and surface texture. It is translated by 10 pixels per frame along y=47.6, from x=62 in frame 01 to x=242 in frame 19.
  4. A mathematical two-Gaussian contact/cast shadow is composited below and to the right of the moving ball, matching the upper-left source illumination.
  5. The measured lampshade silhouette is restored from exact source pixels after the moving layers are drawn. The ball is therefore progressively occluded behind the shade and then emerges on its right without changing any stationary object.
  6. artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py also creates a selected adjacent-frame transition sheet and reloads every PNG and pixels.npz to verify equality against the generated RGB arrays. The tensor is explicitly checked as uint32 packed 0xRRGGBB, and frame 00 is checked for exact equality with the input.

No external assets, models, masks, references, engines, or network services were used.
