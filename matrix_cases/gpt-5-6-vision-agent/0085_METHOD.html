OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

Locked scene measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB, fixed camera, realistic tabletop lighting.
  • Baking tray: dark gray interior begins near (87, 91), runs to the right edge, and fills most of the lower-right image. Its raised rim is already present and remains unchanged.
  • Rubber duck: yellow body spans approximately x=162..215, y=60..109.
    • Head center is about (188, 77), radius about 20 px.
    • Body is an oval around (186, 95), about 50 x 28 px.
    • Orange-red bill spans about x=201..215, y=76..84; black eye is near (200, 70).
  • Woven basket/dispenser base occupies the upper middle background. The blue tap and its red fitting are near x=169..193, y=0..27.
  • A narrow bright-red liquid thread is already visible below the tap, around x=191..196, y=23..61, ending just above the duck's crown. This existing setup motivates an immediate continuing pour.
  • Light comes from upper left. New liquid therefore needs pale pink highlights on its left/center and dark crimson shading on its right/lower edges.
  • All pixels outside the stream, duck, and small tray-pool area must remain locked to the first frame.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00: exact source frame with no modification.
  • Frames 01-03: the red thread lengthens to the duck's crown and begins a tiny impact splash; the stream subtly varies in width.
  • Frames 04-06: continuous liquid strikes the crown. A glossy red cap spreads across the upper-right head while thin rivulets begin beside the eye and behind the bill.
  • Frames 07-09: the coating advances down the right cheek and neck; several small outward splash droplets appear briefly; the first red runoff reaches the upper body.
  • Frames 10-12: a broader red sheet runs across the right side of the head and body, while yellow remains visible on the left/front. The first shallow pool forms at the duck's base on the tray.
  • Frames 13-15: coating and rivulets thicken, with animated specular highlights. The tray pool expands irregularly around the right and front of the duck.
  • Frames 16-18: steady pouring continues. More runoff connects the duck to the pool; the pool grows wider and develops a glossy reflection and a darker edge.
  • Frame 19: final sustained pour, wet red-coated right half of the duck, active crown splash, and a settled bright-red puddle on the dark tray.

The animation will use only local analytic pixel drawing and alpha compositing over the locked source image. Shapes will be supersampled for antialiased, photographic-scale edges.

METHOD.md

Method

I generated the animation entirely with local Python pixel operations using Pillow and NumPy. The source photograph remains the locked background in every frame, and frame 00 is copied exactly from first_frame.png.

The bright-red liquid is assembled from supersampled analytic layers at 4x resolution and downsampled with Lanczos filtering for natural antialiasing. The layers comprise:

  1. a subtly oscillating shaded stream from the existing tap to the duck's crown;
  2. frame-varying impact lobes and tiny ballistic droplets;
  3. progressive masks clipped to approximate head and body silhouettes, filled with translucent crimson and separate dark-edge/specular strokes;
  4. narrow runoff connecting the body to an irregular perspective puddle;
  5. a growing tray puddle with an organic outline, dark wet contact edge, red interior, and pale reflections. The puddle mask subtracts the duck silhouette so it reads as lying behind and around the duck rather than painted over it.

No generative model, external engine, network resource, optical-flow source, or external reference was used. artifact_io.py writes the pixel tensor, individual PNGs, contact sheet, and standalone player. The generator then verifies every saved PNG against its corresponding uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor plane and verifies frame 00 against the source pixels.
