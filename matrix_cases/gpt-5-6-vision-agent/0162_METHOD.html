OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

Measured scene

  • Source is a 256 x 144 RGB photograph. The camera is locked and the full background must remain fixed.
  • Warm wooden tabletop begins near y=57 at the right and slopes to about y=64 at the left. The pale wall occupies the upper field.
  • Newton's-cradle base is a black trapezoid spanning approximately x=49..163, y=96..128. Its chrome outer frame rises to y=9; the inner suspension bar is around y=22.
  • Four resting balls are visible in a tight row at approximately (105,99), (114,99), (123,99), and (132,99). Their diameter is about 8 px. The raised/released left end ball is centered near (60,84), also about 8 px in diameter.
  • The left ball's equilibrium center is approximately (96,99). Its suspension length is about 49 px, so the raised pose corresponds to a roughly 48-degree leftward displacement.
  • The blue-black grabber lies/extends in from the left, mainly x=4..57 and y=61..85. Its tip is adjacent to the raised ball in frame 00.
  • Lighting is soft and warm from upper left. Balls have bright near-white highlights, dark lower rims, and subtle reflections/shadows on the glossy black base.

Motion and compositing approach

  • Frame 00 is preserved byte-for-byte from first_frame.png.
  • Later frames use a clean background derived only from the source frame, with small moving-object regions reconstructed by local texture interpolation/inpainting.
  • Chrome balls, paired suspension wires, contact shadows, and restrained motion blur are synthesized with analytic geometry, sampled source colors, and antialiased supersampling.
  • The cradle follows a pendulum-style, momentum-transfer animation: only an end ball has large displacement at a time; the three middle balls remain in contact. The amplitude decays after each impact.
  • Once the ball separates, the grabber remains parked at the left edge. Its release is communicated by the growing gap to the descending ball, without inventing a large tool motion that is not supported by the source pixels.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a local pixel compositing reconstruction made only from first_frame.png and the written prompt.

  1. I measured the raised and resting ball centers directly from the source photograph.
  2. Frame 00 is copied without modification.
  3. For later frames, the original raised-ball region is removed with a small OpenCV Telea inpaint mask sampling the surrounding wooden tabletop. A separate clean plate similarly removes the right end ball only while that ball is swinging.
  4. The chrome balls are feathered circular cutouts from the real source image. They are translated along hand-tuned pendulum arcs with fractional-pixel interpolation, preserving the photograph's genuine reflections, blur, and color response.
  5. Paired suspension wires are analytic antialiased line segments with scene-band-dependent color and opacity. Low balls receive a soft contact reflection on the black base. Fast portions of the swing receive a very restrained two-sample shutter echo.
  6. The event timing follows idealized Newton's-cradle momentum transfer with damping: left impact, right excursion and return, left excursion and return, then a smaller right excursion.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, individual PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone player. The generation script also creates transition_check.png around the three momentum-transfer moments.

No external images, models, masks, engines, or network resources were used.
