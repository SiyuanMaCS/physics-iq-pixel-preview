The animation is a local, source-derived pixel continuation made entirely by generate.py. No external images, models, engines, masks, or references were used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as the only scene image. Frame 00 is copied from it without modification.
  2. A small mask is constructed from measured acrylic-box edge coordinates and the hanging collar. OpenCV's local TELEA inpainting reconstructs the narrow wall/table regions hidden by those edges. The rest of the source frame is never replaced or repainted.
  3. The signed RGB difference between the source and that clean plate captures the source photograph's real antialiased box edges, highlights, and black hanging hardware.
  4. For frames 01-19, this signed residual is translated vertically with bilinear subpixel resampling and added to the clean plate. This lets the original clear-box detail combine with the wall, table, and ball at its new position without pasting an opaque source rectangle.
  5. A very faint translucent gray face and source-matched edge reinforcement make the acrylic readable over the table while leaving the smiling tennis ball visible. The exposed suspension extension is drawn as a narrow dark orange-brown cord behind the moving hardware.
  6. The 19 displacement samples use an ease-in, smooth descent, deceleration, and a restrained subpixel settle. The ball and all non-apparatus scene pixels remain stationary.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the RGB PNGs, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py then reloads every PNG, packs it independently, and asserts exact equality with the corresponding tensor plane.

Visual review was performed on first_frame.png, inspection.png, transition_check.png, and the full-resolution final frame. The reviewed final frame shows the box resting around the stationary ball with the face unobscured and no camera motion.
