OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Reference measurements

  • Canvas: 256 × 144 pixels, landscape, fixed camera.
  • Background: pale cool-gray wall above an ochre-brown wooden tabletop. The wall/table boundary slopes from about (0,81) through (83,54) to (255,99) and is partly occluded by the objects.
  • Main dispenser: ribbed clear glass body, approximately x=115..198, y=0..72. Its bright red drink occupies roughly x=125..192, y=34..65; the upper boundary is almost horizontal and the vessel bottom curves upward at the sides.
  • Woven basket/stand: approximately x=99..219, y=40..127, with the dispenser centered on top. Its many tan, ochre, brown, and muted red strands must remain static.
  • Spigot: dark black/red tap assembly around x=116..144, y=37..62. A narrow, mostly vertical metallic outlet descends at about x=122..127 to y=104.
  • Receiving glass: clear, slightly tapered tumbler approximately x=99..139, y=58..136. Its side rims are strongest near x=100..105 and x=134..139. It already contains red liquid from about y=106 to the curved base near y=133.
  • Pour path: the stream exits beneath the outlet near (124,62) and follows the vertical metal/glass outlet region to the drink surface near (124,106). A visible moving red core can be added mostly below the outlet tip and immediately above the rising surface without moving the hardware.
  • Lighting: soft diffuse light from upper left. Shadows fall gently to the lower right. No camera motion, focus change, exposure change, or new objects.

Animation design

Only the liquid changes. The dispenser, basket, glass contour, table, background, tap, and camera remain pixel-locked to frame 00. The receiving glass fills gradually: its liquid surface rises from about y=106 to about y=91 over five seconds. A narrow translucent red stream fluctuates by roughly one pixel, with a bright specular core, tiny impact crown, and subtle surface ripples. The stream begins already established, matching a demonstration captured during pouring, and remains flowing at the end.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is generated locally from first_frame.png with deterministic NumPy and Pillow pixel operations. No external images, models, masks, engines, or network resources are used.

Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the decoded RGB pixel level. For frames 01–19, the source frame remains the locked background and only a conservative mask inside the receiving glass plus the already-visible stream is changed. The liquid surface follows a smooth 15-pixel rise over five seconds. Newly filled pixels retain the source basket/glass texture but receive a red refraction transform whose channel coefficients were matched to the existing liquid in the bottom of the glass. As the input surface becomes submerged, its old reflective band is faded into real liquid texture sampled from lower in the same source glass. A curved meniscus, low-amplitude traveling ripple, narrow pulsing stream highlight, and occasional one-pixel impact glints supply real-time motion without moving any object or the camera.

artifact_io.save writes the frame PNGs, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection contact sheet, and standalone HTML player. The generator separately builds transition_check.png. It then reloads every PNG and verifies exact equality to the corresponding pixels.npz plane, checks exact equality of frame 00 to the input, and confirms that changes stay localized.

Run with:

python generate.py
