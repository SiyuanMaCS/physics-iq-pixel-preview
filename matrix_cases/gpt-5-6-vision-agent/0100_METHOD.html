magnet-wrench · 生成方法

I generated the sequence locally from first_frame.png with deterministic Python pixel operations (NumPy, OpenCV, and Pillow). No external images, masks, models, engines, or references were used.

The photographed wrench was isolated with a hand-measured support polygon and a soft contrast-based alpha matte. Its original footprint and contact shadow were removed by fitting separate quadratic RGB surfaces to unaffected pixels on the platform's elliptical top and dark cylindrical side. Modeling those two surfaces separately prevents the nearby light tabletop from bleeding into the black platform.

For each animated frame, the extracted wrench is transformed around (160, 59.5). The transform first deprojects the image's vertical axis into the tabletop plane, applies clockwise rotation there, and projects it back with a vertical foreshortening factor of 0.58. This makes the wrench's apparent length and angle change consistently with the fixed oblique camera. A soft contact shadow is derived from the transformed wrench alpha, offset in the fixed light direction, and composited onto the reconstructed platform.

The angular path is a cosine ease-in/ease-out from 0 to 130 degrees across frames 00 through 19. Frame 00 is explicitly restored from the unmodified source array to guarantee exact identity. All scenery outside the wrench footprint remains copied from the source photograph.

I visually reviewed inspection.png, an enlarged wrench crop, the reconstructed clean platform, the alpha mask, the final pose, and transition_check.png. The first draft was revised after review because generic inpainting drew light tabletop color onto the platform. A later overlap issue in the support polygons was also corrected so the wrench neck stays intact throughout the turn.

Finally, generate.py reloads every PNG and pixels.npz, verifies the 20-frame RGB uint8 shape, checks frame 00 against first_frame.png, confirms the stored tensor is uint32 with shape (144, 256, 20), and compares every packed 0xRRGGBB value against every PNG pixel.
