OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

Source measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, 256 x 144 RGB. The camera is fixed in a slightly elevated, three-quarter view.
  • The light oak tabletop fills the frame. Its grain runs mainly upper-left to lower-right; the strongest broad plank/seam line crosses from roughly (0, 55) to (183, 144).
  • The navy glazed mug occupies about x=126..218, y=37..119. Its cylindrical body is about x=126..192, y=38..118; the handle occupies x=181..218, y=70..105.
  • The dark brown liquid is an ellipse inside the rim, about x=132..187, y=51..90. The front rim occludes the liquid along the lower edge. A warm highlight/reflection lies near the left-center of the liquid.
  • A white ceramic domino/tile is held at a slight clockwise lean. Its visible polygon is approximately (164, 19), (179, 29), (172, 76), (155, 64). The bottom is already at/through the apparent liquid surface.
  • The blue-black grabber enters from the top at x=157..184. Its two prongs surround the tile near x=163..177, y=0..25. Both tool and tile cast coherent soft shadows/reflections into the liquid.
  • Illumination comes from the upper-left. The mug and handle cast a soft shadow toward the lower-left; this remains static throughout.
  • Locked regions: the camera, tabletop, mug exterior, handle, and broad mug shadow must remain unchanged. Only the tool/tile area, liquid interior, and small splash region above the rim may change.

Motion and physics

At 4 fps the actual free fall is necessarily brief. The tile is released at the opening, descends and rotates slightly clockwise, strikes the liquid, and rapidly becomes occluded first by the liquid and then by the front wall/rim. A small displacement splash and damped concentric ripples follow. The empty grabber rebounds upward and leaves the frame. The ceramic tile sinks fully, so the final shot is the unchanged mug with a nearly settled dark surface.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic local Python pixel composite built only from first_frame.png.

  1. The source frame is loaded as RGB and copied byte-for-byte into frame 00.
  2. Soft polygon/chroma masks isolate the white ceramic tile and the blue-black grabber. Their source pixels are retained as sprites so their original lighting, texture, antialiasing, and camera character carry into the motion.
  3. A clean stationary plate is reconstructed under those masks with geometry-aware local filling: real neighboring tabletop texture is color-matched and cloned into the vacated area, the hidden rear rim follows a measured projected ellipse, and hidden liquid uses row-wise statistics from unoccluded liquid pixels. A conservative local inpaint is retained only as fallback outside those classified materials. No part of the mug, table, camera framing, or broad shadow is globally transformed.
  4. Frames 01-03 translate and slightly rotate the ceramic sprite downward. A vertical visibility/tint field makes the lower portion darken and disappear behind the liquid/front rim. The split grabber sprite opens by a few pixels and recoils upward through frame 05.
  5. The liquid response consists of clipped antialiased ellipses and small irregular crown strokes sampled from the brown liquid/rim palette. Ring radius expands while opacity and contrast decay, producing an impact dimple, brief splash, outward wave, reflection, and settling surface.
  6. artifact_io.save packs each RGB frame into an exact uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, writes the PNG sequence, inspection contact sheet, and standalone HTML player. The generator additionally writes a selected before/after transition sheet and reloads every output to assert exact agreement among the source, PNGs, and tensor.
