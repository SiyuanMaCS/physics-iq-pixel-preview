+def soft_ellipse(cx, cy, rx, ry, feather=1.25):
+    """Return an analytic antialiased ellipse mask."""
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[0:H, 0:W].astype(np.float32)
+    d = np.sqrt(((xx - cx) / rx) ** 2 + ((yy - cy) / ry) ** 2)
+    # Convert feather in pixels to an approximate normalized edge width.
+    edge = feather / min(rx, ry)
+    return np.clip((1.0 + edge - d) / (2.0 * edge), 0.0, 1.0)

METHOD.md

Generation method

The animation is a deterministic local pixel edit of first_frame.png. No external assets, reference images, generative models, or rendering engines were used.

Image construction

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as a 256 × 144 RGB array. It is inserted directly as frame 00 without modification.
  2. A small mask covers the photographed ball and its right-cast shadow. OpenCV Telea inpainting reconstructs only that covered tabletop region, producing the clean plate used for frames 01–19. The rest of the source image is unchanged.
  3. The real photographed ball pixels are retained under an analytic, softly antialiased elliptical alpha mask. This preserves the source highlight, blur, color, and shading rather than replacing the ball with a drawn circle.
  4. The real shadow is represented by the RGB difference between the source and reconstructed table, restricted with two soft ellipses. It is translated separately from the body so that the brief rebound hop can lift the ball while its shadow remains attached to the tabletop.
  5. Full-frame inverse warps translate the body and shadow along the trajectory documented in OBSERVATION.md. At impact, the body is compressed horizontally to 76% and expanded vertically to 110%, with its right edge anchored at the kettlebell. The next three frames add a 2–3 px hop as the ball rebounds.
  6. The 30 lb kettlebell remains fixed because the tennis ball has negligible momentum relative to it. The duck, pipe, table, sofa, camera, exposure, and framing also remain fixed.

Timing and physics

  • 20 frames at 4 fps represent 5 seconds.
  • The ball moves from x≈60 to contact at x≈116 over the first 0.75 s.
  • It rebounds left with lower speed, briefly hops, lands, then rolls down under table friction and settles near x≈26.
  • Frames 17–19 hold the settled state, providing a stable ending without a camera or lighting change.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, individual PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz, reconstructs each packed pixel value, and asserts exact equality with the in-memory RGB frames. It also asserts that frame 00 exactly equals the supplied source image.

I visually reviewed inspection.png, transition_check.png, and an enlarged contact-sequence crop with the provided image viewing tool. The checks confirmed a locked background, a single ball throughout, visible contact/compression, clean rebound direction, and no interaction with the duck.
