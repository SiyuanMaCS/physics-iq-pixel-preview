OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • Source is 256 × 144 RGB, a locked-off landscape camera view.
  • A white/very pale blue projection screen fills most of the frame. Its lower black rail runs diagonally from about (0, 124) through (238, 80) because of perspective.
  • Weathered horizontal wooden deck boards occupy the lower part of the frame. These and the screen must remain completely static.
  • The yellow ceramic mug occupies approximately x=115..133, y=43..70. Its body is about 16 × 20 px; a small darker handle is visible on its right. The mug center is near (124, 58).
  • A blue-black grabber/tool enters from the upper edge around x=110..121, y=0..12. It remains suspended and fixed after release.
  • The gray concrete brick occupies about x=106..152, y=103..127; its top contact surface is near y=104 beneath the mug.
  • The mug's bottom begins roughly 34 px above the brick. The scene lighting is soft and cool, with a slightly blurred/low-resolution photographic appearance and a subtle object shadow cast onto the screen.
  • No camera motion, zoom, new props, text, hands, or people are allowed.

Motion/physics choice

The mug is released from rest and falls nearly vertically under gravity. At this image scale, the first three quarter-second samples use an accelerating displacement of about 4, 16, then 33 px. Its bottom meets the brick at frame 03. Because ceramic striking concrete is brittle, the cup fractures at impact: a few ochre/yellow ceramic pieces rebound briefly, then fall and settle on the brick/deck. The fragments are deliberately few and image-scale appropriate, with no exaggerated explosion. A soft screen shadow follows the intact mug downward and becomes a small diffuse impact shadow.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is generated locally from first_frame.png with deterministic Python pixel operations (NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV); no external images, models, engines, masks, or references are used.

  1. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from the supplied RGB source.
  2. A clean background plate is made by masking the original mug and its broad local screen shadow, fitting the pale screen's smooth low-frequency illumination from surrounding valid screen pixels with a local polynomial, adding a trace of shifted source texture, and feathering the reconstruction back into the photograph. The reconstruction ends above the black rail, preventing rail pixels from bleeding upward. The camera, grabber, rail, brick, and wooden deck otherwise retain their original pixels.
  3. The intact mug is extracted from the difference between the source and clean plate. A soft alpha matte retains its native low-resolution shading and antialiasing. The photographed sprite is translated along an accelerating vertical path (4, 16, then 33 pixels), with a slight contact tilt/squash and a restrained moving shadow.
  4. On contact with the concrete brick, the brittle ceramic is represented by five small hand-drawn polygonal fragments. Their colors are sampled/approximated from the original ochre-yellow mug, with subdued highlights and soft subpixel edges. Hand-keyed ballistic poses create a small outward rebound, gravity-driven descent, one chip falling to the deck, a damped secondary bounce, and complete rest for the final four frames.
  5. artifact_io.save encodes the 20 RGB frames into the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, numbered PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally creates the early-transition difference sheet and then reloads every PNG and asserts exact equality with its corresponding tensor slice.
