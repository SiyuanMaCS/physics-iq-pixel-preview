light-on-mug · 生成方法

This sequence was generated locally from first_frame.png with deterministic Python pixel operations. No external images, models, masks, engines, or network resources were used.

Construction

  1. The RGB source is loaded unchanged and used verbatim for frame 00.
  2. Two compact clean plates are made with OpenCV Telea inpainting: one removes the source mug handle, and the other removes its wall-shadow handle. The rest of the photographed scene remains source pixels.
  3. The yellow handle is isolated at source scale. A rounded ceramic ring mask completes the small part hidden against the mug body, while source yellow pixels and a yellow glaze gradient retain the original appearance.
  4. The large wall-shadow handle is extracted from the difference between the source and its inpainted wall plate. This preserves the photographed grey/brown shadow color and its soft penumbra.
  5. For frame n, phase is 2*pi*n/20, corresponding to a constant clockwise 360-degree turn in 5 seconds. The horizontal projection of both handle sprites scales with abs(cos(phase)) and their centers move from the left side, through edge-on, to the right side and back. The sprites are mirrored after crossing to the opposite side.
  6. Depth ordering follows the sine of the phase: the rear half is composited before the original mug body and is therefore occluded; the front half is composited after it. The enlarged wall projection follows the same phase. Soft masks restore the original mug body and main wall-shadow body without restoring the old handle.
  7. Three extremely subtle low-contrast flecks rotate on the projected ellipse of the platform top. The platform outline, glossy lighting, fixed spotlight circle, table, cable, and camera remain stationary.

Timing and outputs

  • 20 RGB frames, 256 x 144, at 4 fps.
  • Frame 00 is pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, pixels.npz, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player.
  • transition_check.png shows four representative adjacent frame pairs plus 4x amplified absolute differences.

Verification and review

generate.py reloads every saved PNG and verifies it against the in-memory uint8 frames. It also packs the frames as uint32 0xRRGGBB, transposes them to (144, 256, 20), and asserts exact equality with pixels.npz. The source/frame-00 equality is asserted separately.

I visually reviewed first_frame.png, enlarged mug/shadow crops, the complete multi-frame contact sheet, inspection.png, and transition_check.png using the available image-view tool. A first revision exposed a residual duplicated source handle where the wall-shadow restoration overlapped the foreground; the restoration mask was corrected and the handle sprite was cleaned before the final render.
