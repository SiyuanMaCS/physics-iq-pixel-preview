+def clean_tabletop(source: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
+    """Remove the source ball with row-aware interpolation and texture restoration."""
+    clean = source.copy().astype(np.float32)
+    cx, cy = SOURCE_CENTER
+
+    # A generous mask includes the fuzzy edge and the small down-right contact shadow.
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[0:H, 0:W]
+    core = ((xx - cx) / 16.5) ** 2 + ((yy - (cy + 1.0)) / 12.5) ** 2 <= 1.0
+
+    # The tabletop grain is predominantly horizontal.  Interpolate each affected
+    # scanline between clean samples on either side, while retaining fine row texture.
+    for y in range(39, 66):
+        xs = np.flatnonzero(core[y])
+        if not len(xs):
+            continue
+        left, right = int(xs[0]), int(xs[-1])
+        sample_l = source[y, left - 3:left].astype(np.float32).mean(axis=0)
+        sample_r = source[y, right + 1:right + 4].astype(np.float32).mean(axis=0)
+        for x in range(left, right + 1):
+            u = (x - left + 1) / (right - left + 2)
+            clean[y, x] = sample_l * (1.0 - u) + sample_r * u
+
+    # Blend the reconstruction softly at its edge, then restore a restrained amount
+    # of neighboring wood microtexture so the repaired patch does not look airbrushed.
+    soft = cv2.GaussianBlur(core.astype(np.float32), (0, 0), 2.0)[..., None]
+    repaired = source.astype(np.float32) * (1.0 - soft) + clean * soft
+    row_detail = source.astype(np.float32) - cv2.GaussianBlur(source, (0, 0), 2.0).astype(np.float32)
+    donor = np.roll(row_detail, 35, axis=1)
+    repaired += donor * soft * 0.28
+    return np.clip(repaired, 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observations and event plan

Measured scene

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 pixels, landscape, fixed camera.
  • Table: pale laminated wood, spanning nearly the full frame; its front edge runs from about y=126 at the left to y=99 at the right because of perspective.
  • Black pipe: enters from the left edge, approximately x=0..21 and y=40..69, oriented horizontally toward the right. Its visible right lip is near (17, 55).
  • Gray tennis ball: centered near (118, 52), with an apparent radius of about 9 px. It is slightly motion-softened and casts a subtle shadow down/right.
  • 30 lb black kettlebell: the main bell occupies roughly x=176..213, y=24..55, with handle above it. The ball's plausible impact point is the bell's lower-left side near (181, 48).
  • Yellow rubber duck: centered near (226, 40), behind/right of the bell. It remains stationary.
  • Lighting: soft overhead illumination; the tabletop grain and all background objects remain locked throughout.

Animation/event plan (20 frames at 4 fps)

  • Frame 00: exact source frame, as required.
  • Frames 01-05: ball continues swiftly right from its source-frame position toward the kettlebell, with increasing proximity and subtle horizontal motion blur.
  • Frame 06: first contact with the kettlebell at its lower-left side.
  • Frames 07-08: brief collision compression/deformation; ball speed drops sharply.
  • Frames 09-19: ball rebounds a short distance left, rolls forward again, and settles beside the kettlebell with diminishing motion. The heavy kettlebell stays visually fixed, consistent with its much greater mass; the duck and pipe never move.

All generated frames after frame 00 use a locally reconstructed clean tabletop under the source ball, then composite a shaded gray ball, its soft contact shadow, slight rotation cues, and speed-dependent directional blur. No external assets or models are used.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is generated entirely with local Python pixel operations from first_frame.png.

  1. The gray ball and its soft shadow were measured at approximately (117, 52).
  2. A clean plate was made by replacing only the ball area with row-wise interpolation from adjacent tabletop pixels, soft edge blending, and a small amount of neighboring wood microtexture.
  3. The original ball appearance was retained as a signed RGB difference layer between the source and clean plate. This preserves the photographed lighting, fuzzy edge, and shadow more naturally than redrawing the ball.
  4. That layer was translated along a straight path into the kettlebell, with speed-dependent horizontal directional blur. At impact it is briefly compressed horizontally and expanded vertically, followed by a small damped rebound and settling motion.
  5. A very low-contrast rotating arc provides a restrained rolling cue. The much heavier 30 lb kettlebell, duck, pipe, camera, and background remain fixed.
  6. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, PNG sequence, inspection sheet, and standalone player. The generator separately creates transition_check.png and verifies every PNG against pixels.npz after saving.

Frame 00.png is copied exactly from first_frame.png without alteration.
