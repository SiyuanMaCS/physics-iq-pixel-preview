OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured source frame

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB; locked, eye-level/slightly elevated camera.
  • Light wood tabletop occupies approximately y=31..131, with its front edge at y=119..133.
  • A black pipe enters from the left edge, approximately x=0..18, y=36..58, with its visible opening/end directed toward the right.
  • Grey tennis ball: center approximately (95, 56), apparent radius 7 px; its floor contact/shadow lies around y=61..64.
  • 30 lb black kettlebell: main body approximately x=154..186, y=42..72; left collision boundary on the ball's path is near x=155. Its handle rises to about y=10.
  • Yellow rubber duck: approximately x=199..221, y=47..68; it is not in the ball's trajectory and remains static.
  • Ball travel line: nearly horizontal, center y=56, from pipe/rightward toward the kettlebell.
  • Lighting is soft and diffuse from above/front-left. The frame has mild photographic blur/noise and warm wood reflections.

Physical interpretation

The ball is much lighter than a 30 lb kettlebell. It rolls quickly rightward, compresses on the kettlebell at about center x=147, and rebounds left. The kettlebell receives only a tiny, short-lived visual jolt (subpixel/one-pixel deformation cue rather than a translation), while the duck and all other scene elements remain fixed. The rebound loses speed, includes a small hop from elastic deformation, then returns to rolling and gradually slows.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is a deterministic 2D photographic composite generated locally from first_frame.png.

  1. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from the supplied image.
  2. For frames 01–19, the original ball and its small contact shadow are removed with a tightly bounded, feathered wood-texture patch synthesized from equal-distance pixels to its left and right on the same image rows. A light local horizontal smoothing suppresses copied knots while retaining the tabletop grain. The pipe, kettlebell, duck, camera framing, and the rest of the photograph remain untouched.
  3. A blue-grey tennis ball is rendered analytically at 4x spatial resolution using a spherical normal field, diffuse upper-left lighting, a subdued curved seam, fine deterministic mottling, antialiased edges, and a height-dependent soft tabletop shadow. The overlay alone is area-downsampled before being composited onto the untouched photographic plate.
  4. Motion follows hand-authored lightweight collision physics: fast rightward travel, horizontal compression against the kettlebell on frame 03, reversed velocity, a small damped hop, two increasingly soft table contacts, then rolling drag to rest. The 30 lb kettlebell does not visibly translate because its mass is overwhelmingly larger than the ball's.
  5. A five-sample short shutter average provides mild motion blur during fast travel. The collision frame is intentionally sharp so contact is readable at 256 x 144.
  6. artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs, packed 0xRRGGBB uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. The generator then reloads every PNG and checks every packed pixel against pixels.npz, and verifies that frames/00.png has the same SHA-256 as first_frame.png.
