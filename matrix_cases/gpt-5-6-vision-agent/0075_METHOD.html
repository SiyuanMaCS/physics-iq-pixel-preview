Actual generation method

The animation is generated locally by generate.py from only first_frame.png and the written prompt/rules.

  1. Frame 00 is copied directly from first_frame.png with no resampling or edits.
  2. For later frames, two small clean plates are reconstructed from real neighboring pixels:
    • the initial upright block-shadow region on the lit wall is replaced by scanline interpolation between the real wall pixels on its left and right, then softly feathered;
    • the original blue block is removed by scanline interpolation through the horizontal shadow, tabletop, and black platform regions behind it.
  3. The real black platform-top pixels are rotated clockwise in ellipse-normalized coordinates using inverse bilinear sampling. This retains the original platform texture and perspective ellipse instead of substituting a synthetic disk.
  4. A 6 x 4 unit rectangular-prism cross-section is advanced by exactly 18° per frame. Its four plan-view corners are projected into the image with fixed perspective, and the two camera-facing vertical faces receive fixed-light Lambert-style cyan/blue shading. A small top face and soft contact shadow keep it grounded on the platform.
  5. The same prism yaw controls the wall-shadow width. Its projected horizontal extent is enlarged by 1.42x, extended vertically from the platform shadow toward the top of the spotlight, and rendered with a dense core plus a broader Gaussian penumbra. The existing wide horizontal platform shadow remains from the photographed source.
  6. Prism and shadow geometry are rasterized at 4x resolution and Lanczos-reduced for stable antialiased edges. The locked camera, spotlight pool, wall, table, and wall/floor boundary remain fixed.

Timing

  • 20 frames
  • 256 x 144 RGB
  • 4 fps
  • 5 seconds
  • Pose angle: 18° * frame_index, giving 342° at frame 19 and a short 18° loop step back to the exact source pose.

Validation and review

generate.py writes all deliverables through artifact_io.save, then reloads and checks every PNG against the in-memory RGB frames and checks pixels.npz as a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor packed as 0xRRGGBB. It also asserts that frame 00 is exactly equal to first_frame.png.

I visually reviewed inspection.png, individual rendered frames 01, 05, and 14, and transition_check.png with the available image-view tool. The review covered shadow continuity/reconstruction, block grounding, face-order changes through the yaw, platform motion, and transition locality.
