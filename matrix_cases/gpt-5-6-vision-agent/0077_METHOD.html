Scene observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB pixels; fixed camera and fixed crop.
  • Back wall/floor boundary: approximately y=103, with a dark horizontal seam.
  • Wooden tabletop: y=104..143, warm brown with perspective grain and a diagonal seam near x=195.
  • Black rotating platform: centered near x=157, spans roughly x=132..183 and y=116..140. Its visible top is an ellipse approximately 49 x 19 pixels; the lower glossy rim occupies y=126..139.
  • Yellow mug: centered near x=157, body approximately x=140..174 and y=83..124. The opening/top is an ellipse around y=81..89. The handle projects left to about x=133 and occupies y=91..113.
  • Lighting: a strong warm-white spot is centered on the mug/lower wall. The lit wall forms a soft circular field about x=42..255, y=0..108; the table has a narrow radial pool extending from the platform toward the lower edge.
  • Large cast shadow: dark mug silhouette on the wall, centered near x=158. The body spans approximately x=137..182 and y=40..86; its handle is on the left around x=121..140 and y=47..82. A small platform/stand shadow lies close to the wall boundary around y=91..104.
  • The source is somewhat soft/noisy and has strong natural gradients; generated compositing should retain that texture and avoid crisp vector edges.

Motion interpretation

“Clockwise” is interpreted from above. The mug and black platform rotate together about the platform's vertical center at x≈157. In the locked camera view, the handle starts on the viewer's left (roughly the 9 o'clock side), sweeps through the rear where it is progressively occluded, emerges on the viewer's right, crosses the front with increased visibility, and returns toward the left. The large wall shadow follows the same phase but is broadened and softened by the spotlight. The spotlight and camera remain fixed.

The five-second shot uses one smooth revolution (18° per frame interval, 342° reached at frame 19); this keeps the motion clearly clockwise without duplicating frame 00 at the end. The circular platform receives subtle rotating highlights/marks so its spin remains readable even while its outline stays fixed.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is a deterministic local Python pixel animation made from first_frame.png; no external assets, models, masks, or engines were used.

Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the RGB pixel level from the supplied image. For later frames, OpenCV inpainting removes only the original left-side yellow handle and the original left-side lobe of the wall shadow. The original wall, wood grain, platform, spotlight pool, mug body, and body-shadow pixels otherwise remain locked to the camera.

The mug handle is modeled as a vertical C-shaped loop attached at two radii to a cylindrical body. Its horizontal coordinates are projected with cosine over one 20-frame revolution, causing it to move left → rear/occluded → right → front/overlaid → left. A soft supersampled alpha raster provides antialiased edges; source-like warm yellow shading and very light deterministic grain are applied only to the newly painted loop. During the rear half of the revolution, a color-derived mask restores the original mug cylinder over the handle to produce occlusion. The large wall-shadow loop uses the same rotational phase with a larger projection, broad Gaussian softness, and wall-relative darkening.

A small low-contrast scuff rotates around the elliptical top of the black platform to disambiguate the clockwise platform motion. The strong illumination and platform silhouette remain fixed, as required for a stationary spotlight and camera.

artifact_io.save writes the RGB PNG sequence, packed tensor, contact sheet, and standalone player. generate.py then verifies that frame 00 equals the source, every saved PNG equals its in-memory RGB frame, and pixels.npz equals the frames packed as uint32 0xRRGGBB with shape 144×256×20.
