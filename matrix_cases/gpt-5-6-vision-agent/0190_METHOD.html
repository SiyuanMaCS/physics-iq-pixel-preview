OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Scene measurements

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB, fixed wide camera, no camera movement.
  • Table: warm medium-brown wood. Its rear edge runs from about (0, 96) through (128, 79) to (255, 96). The central tabletop remains the stable ground plane.
  • Background: softly lit neutral-gray wall with a mild left-to-right tonal gradient and blurred light patches. No other props enter the frame.
  • Kettlebell: black, centered around x=136; visible silhouette spans approximately x=110..162, y=0..79. The handle is cropped by the top edge, and the rounded body is about x=113..159, y=24..78. A small blue weight marking is visible on the right face.
  • Cup: white expanded-polystyrene cup lying sideways beneath the bell, approximately x=118..158, y=74..100. The bell/cup contact is near y=75; the cup/table support is near y=99.
  • Lighting: soft indoor illumination from upper-left/front. The object edges are soft and slightly noisy, consistent with the source. Shadows are diffuse, strongest immediately below the cup and bell.
  • Motion constraints: the shot stays locked. Only the cup deformation, the gravity-driven lowering/settling of the kettlebell, and the associated contact shadow change.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 s)

  • Frame 00 (0.00 s): preserve the supplied first frame byte-for-byte in visual RGB content.
  • Frame 01 (0.25 s): brief loaded pause; only a nearly imperceptible first compression.
  • Frame 02 (0.50 s): cup wall begins to shorten and bow sideways; bell lowers about 1 px.
  • Frame 03 (0.75 s): first visible buckle; cup height is reduced and the bell lowers about 3 px.
  • Frame 04 (1.00 s): collapse accelerates, with a small asymmetric lateral bulge.
  • Frame 05 (1.25 s): a folded crease develops across the cup wall; bell remains upright.
  • Frame 06 (1.50 s): cup reaches roughly two-thirds of its original height; contact shadow darkens.
  • Frame 07 (1.75 s): second buckle/fold, slight rightward settling under the load.
  • Frame 08 (2.00 s): cup reaches roughly half-height; bell has descended about 10 px.
  • Frame 09 (2.25 s): main crush phase continues; the white foam spreads laterally.
  • Frame 10 (2.50 s): cup is mostly flattened, with irregular folded rims still visible.
  • Frame 11 (2.75 s): final yield; bell approaches its lowest position.
  • Frame 12 (3.00 s): compression bottoms out; a tiny damped rock/settle is visible.
  • Frame 13 (3.25 s): slight elastic recovery of the foam and subpixel-equivalent bell rise.
  • Frame 14 (3.50 s): rebound damps; the bell returns close to its final resting level.
  • Frame 15 (3.75 s): crushed cup edges relax outward a fraction.
  • Frames 16-18 (4.00-4.50 s): nearly motionless final settle with very small damped vertical changes.
  • Frame 19 (4.75 s): stable endpoint: upright kettlebell resting on a thin, visibly crushed white foam layer.

The animation will be generated from the supplied pixels using local segmentation, texture-preserving affine/remap deformation, analytic contact shadows, and limited inpainting/interpolation only where descending objects reveal the previously hidden wall/table.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

This sequence is a local, deterministic pixel animation derived only from first_frame.png and the task prompt. No external images, models, masks, engines, or network resources were used.

  1. The source RGB frame is loaded at its native 256 x 144 resolution.
  2. Compact alpha mattes for the kettlebell and cup are derived from measured polygons plus local luminance/saturation thresholds. The mattes are lightly feathered to retain the source's antialiasing and softness.
  3. A clean plate is reconstructed only behind the original setup. Each hidden row is interpolated between visible pixels on the left and right, with a small amount of local high-frequency wall/table texture restored. The fixed dark strip along the top of the source frame is preserved.
  4. The cup is deformed with an inverse pixel remap anchored at the tabletop (y ~= 99). Its height decreases while its width increases slightly, with two low-amplitude sinusoidal offsets producing asymmetric foam buckling rather than a perfectly uniform scale. Thin, mask-clipped fold creases appear during the collapse.
  5. The kettlebell's original premultiplied RGB pixels are moved downward according to the cup's loss of height. Its rotation remains below 0.4 degrees and damps toward zero, creating only a subtle heavy-object settle. Because the source handle is cropped by the top boundary, its newly revealed cap is tapered from existing dark source pixels so no rectangular crop edge moves into the shot.
  6. A soft analytic contact shadow is darkened and widened as the cup flattens. Everything outside the local setup remains fixed.
  7. The event curve has rapid yielding through the middle of the sequence, followed by a small damped foam recovery and a stable final rest.

Frame 00 is the unmodified source array. Later frames are rounded once to 8-bit RGB.

Timing

  • 20 frames
  • 256 x 144 pixels
  • RGB, 8 bits per channel
  • 4 frames per second
  • 5 seconds represented by the required 20-frame sequence convention

Validation and review

generate.py writes the PNG sequence and uses artifact_io.save to create the compressed uint32 tensor and standalone player. It then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz, packs each RGB triplet as 0xRRGGBB, and asserts exact equality for all 737,280 pixel-time samples. It also asserts that frame 00 exactly equals first_frame.png.

inspection.png is the required eight-frame contact sheet. transition_check.png shows eight checkpoints and a per-column previous-frame motion trace. Both were visually reviewed with the image viewing tool after generation. That review prompted one revision to remove a translated crop-edge artifact at the top of the kettlebell handle; the final sheets shown in the deliverables are the corrected render.

Run python generate.py from this directory to reproduce and revalidate all generated artifacts.
