ball-in-sand · 生成方法

Task: 20 RGB frames (256×144, 4 fps, 5 s), frame 00 byte-identical to first_frame.png, showing a suspended blue-black grabber tool releasing a blue tennis ball that drops onto a green kinetic-sand pile. All work is local Python pixel synthesis (numpy / PIL / scipy); no external models, engines, references or subagents. first_frame.png was inspected only numerically (RGB statistics, masks, ASCII classification maps) — no image viewing tool was used, so no "visual review" is claimed beyond numeric and ASCII diagnostics.

Scene measurements (from frame 00)

  • Wall: smooth warm grey, near-constant across the tool span (~(165,156,154) left, mild vignette past x≈150), grain σ≈1.4–2.
  • Wooden table with perspective far-edge diagonal from ~(195,60) to ~(0,88); colour ~(95–140,67–99,44–67).
  • Green kinetic-sand mound x 90–181, y 84–125; surface y≈84–86 over the landing column (x135 → y86); mean colour (36,108,42), clumpy grain σ≈(9,12,7).
  • Grabber: trigger-loop handle (y0–24, ends ~y37), dark hinge block (x≈123–141, y≤27), and two ~5 px curved claw jaws cupping the ball, tips meeting at y≈55; palette (26,83,110)/(73,106,126)/(111,128,145).
  • Ball: sphere centre (134.5,37.7), r≈12.5 px, lit upper-right ((51,123,157) highlight → (29,103,139) mid → (26,83,110) shade), felt grain.
  • Soft cast blob on the wall under the tool, ~(125–146, 53–58).

Synthesis pipeline (generate.py)

Each frame (except 00) is composited over a static background, with no camera motion and a fixed field:

  1. Static background — copy of frame 00 with the closed-jaw/ball polygon (x100–162, y27–58) and the cast-blob mask inpainted by a per-row wall model (median of clean strip x96–106, slight right-side vignette term) plus a deterministic smooth grain field; original handle and hinge pixels are stamped back verbatim from frame 00.
  2. Open claw jaws — quadratic-Bézier tubes (dark shell r3.4, blue body r2.5, offset highlight r1.0, analytic distance-band coverage for anti-aliasing). Control points interpolate between measured closed anchors and an open pose (tips splay to x≈122/147): 35% at frame 01, fully open from frame 02.
  3. Ball sprite — a 40×40 table whose colours are the real frame-00 ball pixels copied where the pixel is ball-coloured (k-means centre distance vs. jaw colours), and elsewhere a degree-2 polynomial shading model in (nx,ny,nz) least-squares-fitted on those pixels (residual MAE ≈2.5–3.2), plus correlated grain. Stamped with 4×4 supersampled disk coverage; a squash (1.10/0.85) is applied at impact via inverse-scale sampling.
  4. Motion — release at t=0.5 s, constant gravity g≈23.4 px/s² (centre 37.7 → 73.5 at contact, t=2.25 s, frame 09), damped embed to centre y≈76.9 (bottom ≈y89.4, ~3.5 px into the pile), recovered round by frame 12. The acceleration is deliberately modest so the 9-frame fall is legible at 4 fps (true real-time fall at this image scale would finish within one frame interval).
  5. Dynamic shadow — soft multiply ellipse interpolated from the held blob position (y≈56) onto the sand crest, shrinking/darkening on approach.
  6. Sand crater — per-column heightfield edit: Gaussian dent (depth 1.6 frame 09, 4.1 after) with two raised side ridges (2.3 at |dx|≈11.5). Newly exposed interior is filled from deeper pile texture darkened ~(0.78,0.86,0.74); ridges use frame-00 sand colour lightened ~10%; edges are 1 px feathered. Nearest non-sand / nearest-sand distance-transform lookups supply local background and texture colours. A few ridge clumps are stamped over the lower ball edge to sell embedding; two tiny sand clumps stick to the ball after impact.
  7. Granules — 10 small (1.1–1.6 px) sampled-colour clumps launched on ballistic parabolas at frame 10 (per-frame gravity 1.454 px/f², vy −1.7…−3.0, vx ≤2.2 px/f), each clamped to its analytic flight time and settled onto the deformed surface by frames 14–15.

Frames 15–19 are intentionally identical (settled final state).

Outputs

frames/00.png…19.png, pixels.npz (H×W×20 packed 0xRRGGBB tensor), inspection.png (8 key thumbnails), transition_check.png (10 labelled key frames), index.html (standalone delta player, produced via artifact_io.py), plus OBSERVATION.md and this file.

Verification performed

  • frames/00.png array-equals first_frame.png exactly.
  • All 20 exported PNGs re-packed to uint32 equal the pixels.npz tensor.
  • The player's run-length delta reconstruction in index.html was replayed in Python and equals the tensor for all 20 frames.
  • Ball tracking: blue-pixel centroid below y58 moves (134,60)→(134,73)→ (134,76) on the gravity schedule, then static; changed-pixel bbox stays inside x100–162, y23–91 (the action region; untouched background elsewhere).
  • Sprite continuity: mean abs difference between frames 00 and 01 over the ball core (r<8.5) is ~1.2/255.
  • Frames 15–19 pairwise identical; crater, ridges and settled granules lie within the original sand footprint.

Known limitations: gravity is slower than strict real-time for legibility at 4 fps; the empty open claw and crater are procedural reconstructions matched to the frame-00 palette rather than recovered hidden geometry; ball shading inside the previously jaw-covered lower arc is model-extrapolated.
