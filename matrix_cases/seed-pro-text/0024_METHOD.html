OBSERVATION

  • Light wood coffee table: visible tabletop roughly y≈62→127 at the left, sloping to y≈59 at the centre; front edge/apron y≈116→128. Warm light wood RGB ≈ (145,138,120) mid-table with fine film grain; back edge near y≈59–64. At the far right a dark vertical surface (dark furniture/shadow) begins at x≈212–214 for y≈66–78 (brightness falls 130 → ~70 across x≈210→214).
  • Black pipe: dark (RGB mostly < 25, near black with a few faint blue rim highlights) object on the left end. Silhouette spans x≈0–28, y≈0–77, with its mouth at the lower-right around (28, 68), resting on the tabletop. A few cool glints RGB ≈ (39,62,92) sit inside the mouth near x≈5–6, y≈68–69.

    • Diameter ≈ 14.9 px. Colours: cool grey body RGB ≈ (95–135, 110–145, 120–180), bright neutral rim RGB ≈ (147,145,142) on the right, warm light highlight RGB ≈ (140,133,125) upper-left, dark blue-grey lower shading RGB ≈ (82,85,95). No felt seam is resolvable at this size.

METHOD.md

METHOD

Task: 20 RGB frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s), continuing first_frame.png. Prompt: a grey tennis ball rolls out of a black pipe on the left end of a light wood coffee table, across the whole table toward the right. Camera locked off, constant framerate, real time.

Everything was produced with local Python pixel operations only (numpy, Pillow, OpenCV, scipy). No external images, models, engines, network, or subagents were used. first_frame.png was inspected only numerically (RGB statistics, ASCII brightness/class maps, circle fitting) — no image Read/View tools were used.

Measurements (from frame 00 alone)

  • Ball: circle fit on the colour residual vs. a reconstructed wood field (boundary-gradient grid search): centre (111.0, 75.05), radius 7.45 px. Cool grey body with a bright neutral right rim and a darker blue-grey lower crescent. Soft elliptical contact shadow centred near (110, 83.4) (semiaxes ≈ 8 × 2.2 px), darkening the wood to roughly 0.6–0.9× locally.
  • Black pipe: near-black body x≈0–28, mouth near (28, 68), left table end; untouched in every frame.
  • Table: light wood tabletop y≈62–127; clear travel corridor to x≈210; dark furniture/shadow begins around x≈212–214 at the ball's height.

Motion model

Constant-velocity roll along y = 75.05:

x(f) = 111.0 + 4.947·f,  f = 0..19   (19.79 px/s)

At f = 19 the centre is x = 205.0 (right edge ≈ 212.4), i.e. the ball reaches the dark surface at the right end. Rotation is not rendered: at a 15 px diameter no surface marking is resolvable in the source, so a spinning texture would be invented; rolling is conveyed by translation + finite-exposure blur.

Reconstruction

  1. Static wood background. Known wood pixels (table band excluding pipe, ball, shadow, and the right-hand dark furniture) diffuse their colour by iterated masked box-blur (300 iterations) into a smooth low-frequency wood field. High-frequency grain (patch minus its σ≈2.2 Gaussian blur) from a clean table patch (x 130–205, y 66–88, tiled by modulo) is added back inside the ball/shadow fill region, feathered over ~1.1 px. This fills only the ball's vacated spot; all other pixels keep their original values.
  2. Object layer. One premultiplied RGBA sprite is taken verbatim from frame 00: alpha = union of the ball disc (hard core, 1.7 px feathered rim) and a Gaussian-soft ellipse for the contact shadow. Keeping ball and shadow in one moving layer reproduces frame 00 exactly and keeps the shadow attached.
  3. Finite exposure. Each frame averages 9 sub-samples of the translated layer over a 60 % shutter (±0.3 frame ⇒ ≈ 3 px horizontal motion blur), then composites the average "over" the static background.
  4. Frame 00 is a byte-identical copy of first_frame.png (verified file-equal); it is never reconstructed.

Outputs

  • frames/00.png–19.png, pixels.npz (H×W×20 uint32, 0xRRGGBB, the exact packed tensor; verified equal to every PNG), inspection.png (contact-sheet from artifact_io), transition_check.png (frames 00/03/06/09/12/15/17/19 with ×4 difference rows against the previous keyframe), index.html (standalone delta player from the provided template), generate.py, OBSERVATION.md.

Verification performed (numeric, no visual tool)

  • frames/00.png byte-identical to first_frame.png; all 20 PNGs match the uint32 tensor channel-by-channel.
  • Ball centroid (cool-grey detector) advances ~4.95 px every frame, y ≈ 75.7 constant; changed-pixel bbox stays inside the corridor y 63–90 and moves x 103→213; zero changed pixels outside the corridor.
  • Frame-to-frame changed-pixel counts are stable (207–232), no flicker.
  • Ball edge gradient stays close to the source (≈ 45–53 vs 52.5 in frame 00; 65.7 at f 19 where the ball meets the darker right-end surface, which is physically expected).
  • Contact-shadow darkening stays consistent along the path (shadow core ≈ 130–135 mean vs ≈ 146–151 adjacent wood).
  • Vacated position ring gradient 24.2 vs ≈ 22.1 mean for control wood rings — no visible seam; residual differences inside the vacated disc are ≤ ~8 mean (local wood-grain level).

Known simplifications

  • Ball spin is not drawn (markings unresolvable at 15 px).
  • Inpainted wood at the vacated spot reuses grain from a nearby clean patch rather than recovering the true occluded grain; it is static after f 00, so no temporal artefacts are introduced.
  • The ball ends at the right end at t = 4.75 s rather than leaving frame, matching "rolls … over the entire table towards the right" while keeping the whole action inside the 5 s clip.
