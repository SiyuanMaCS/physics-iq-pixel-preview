METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0017

What happens

Locked-off shot, 20 frames at 4 fps. The blue-black grabber stays suspended and motionless; a blue tennis ball is released from its open claw (jaw centre measured at x≈130, y≈51), free-falls onto the crest of the green kinetic-sand mound at y≈79, and embeds ~half its diameter with a small rebound, ejecta and dust, then rests in a crater. Frame 00 is the untouched first_frame.png.

Actual method (honest statement)

Everything is procedural local Python pixel work on top of the pristine first frame (numpy + Pillow). No external models, engines, reference images or generators are used. The first frame is never repainted where nothing happens; the grabber, wall and table are copied unchanged into every frame.

Scene measurements (from numeric RGB inspection only)

  • Segmentation masks: sand G>R+15 & G>B+10 (2737 px, bbox x93–181 / y79–119, mean colour (35,109,41)); tool B>R+15 & B>G+5 (one component x113–153 / y0–51, teal-blue ~(20–40,80–115,95–130) with near-black outlines).
  • Per-column pile top profile precomputed from the sand mask; used both for the contact-shadow placement and for grain landing detection.
  • Wall ~(160,155,160) grey; wooden table from y≈67–70, warm brown ~(125,92,66) with grain noise. Light comes from the upper-left (tool highlights).

Ball (radius 5.5 px, rendered once as an 18×18 sprite)

  • 4× supersampled sphere: Lambert shading from direction (−0.45,−0.55,0.70), dark fresnel rim, tight specular spot + broad soft highlight upper-left.
  • White felt seam drawn as two cubic-Bézier S-arcs meeting at the poles; deterministic hash-noise felt mottling and a faint fuzz fringe; area-averaged back down to native resolution for anti-aliased edges.
  • Vivid blue base #2668be (kept distinct in hue from the teal tool), alpha- composited each frame. Two extra faint ghosts above the fast-falling ball give a short motion smear; on impact the sprite squashes (1.12×/0.88×) then stretches slightly during the damped rebound.

Physics

  • Free fall y(t)=54.5+½·38·t², so contact with the crest (centre y 84.5) is at t=1.25 s (frame 05), impact speed ≈47.5 px/s. Piecewise embed to centre y87.5 (f06), rebound to 86.1 (f07), settle 85.6 (f08 onward).
  • Contact shadow: soft dark ellipse on the pile/table only (never on the wall), broad and faint when the ball is high, tightening and darkening as it nears the surface.
  • 26 deterministic seeded grains: random launch angle/speed/size/shade, ballistic arcs under g=42 px/s²; each is frozen forever at its first pile-profile crossing (8-step bisection), so landed grains remain fixed and the tail frames are perfectly static.
  • Six procedural dust puffs (olive-cream soft ellipses) expand, drift outward/up and fade out between frames 05 and 12.
  • Crater: darkened depressed ellipse (colour mixed toward (12,44,16)) masked to the original sand region, ramping in 0.55→1.0 depth across f05–f06; two bright displaced-sand lips at x≈120.5/139.5 with hash-grain texture.

Verification

  • frames/00.png is byte-identical in RGB to first_frame.png.
  • Every PNG was checked against the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz (shape 144×256×20): all 20 match exactly.
  • Per-frame changed-pixel counts (in transition_check.png) show the expected pattern: small moving-ball deltas during the fall, peak at impact, ejecta/ dust settling through f13, then zero deltas f14–f19 (static aftermath).
  • All modifications are confined to the impact column x≈110–149, y≈68–95; the grabber and background remain pixel-static.
  • No image viewing tool was used; scene understanding came solely from numeric RGB masks, profiles and sampled pixel values.
