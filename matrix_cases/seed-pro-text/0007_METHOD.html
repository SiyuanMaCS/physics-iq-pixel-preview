ball-hits-duck · 生成方法

Task

20 RGB frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s), continuing first_frame.png: a brown tennis ball rolls straight out of the black pipe on the left and hits the yellow rubber duck on the right. Locked-off camera, constant framerate, real-time.

Everything was produced with local Python pixel operations only (NumPy, OpenCV, SciPy, Pillow). No external models, engines, references, or network access. first_frame.png was inspected exclusively through numeric RGB analysis (threshold masks, connected components, per-pixel RGB dumps, luminance ASCII maps printed to the terminal); no image viewing tool was used, so no claim of visual review is made.

Measurements (from numeric inspection)

  • Pipe mouth x0–23 / y38–68, bore axis y ≈ 52.
  • Ball: shaded disc, center (84, 51), hard radius ≈ 8.5 px, soft fringe + contact shadow to ≈ 9.6/10.4 px; core RGB ≈ (43, 21, 16).
  • Duck: silhouette x202–222 / y30–50 (base ≈ y50.5), orange beak at x206–210 / y38–40 facing the pipe, small dark eye near (211, 38).
  • Travel gap from ball right edge (x92) to duck left edge (x202): 110 px.

Pipeline (generate.py)

  1. Ball sprite. Coverage alpha is measured from the frame-0 darkening of each pixel against the per-row tabletop median outside the halo (the real silhouette is slightly asymmetric, extending to x96 on the lit side), intersected with a disc/contact-shadow support and lightly feathered. The sprite RGB is split into a fixed smooth sphere shading (Gaussian-weighted mean inside the disc) and high-frequency surface detail; only the detail is rotated for the no-slip roll (spin = −travel/r), while shading and the soft contact shadow stay locked to the ball.
  2. Duck sprite. Core mask = yellow (R−B > 28) plus orange beak pixels, restricted to the body connected component, closed and hole-filled; alpha is a 0.8-σ feather. Fringe colours are un-mixed from the Telea-inpainted local background (front = (obs − (1−a)·bg)/a) so the sprite can move without a beige/yellow halo, and semi-transparent dark fringe (contact shadow) is removed so it cannot lift into the air when the duck tips.
  3. Static background. One inpainted plate (cv2 INPAINT_TELEA, radius 3) covers the full measured ball + shadow footprint and the duck footprint only; static near-black couch pixels touching the duck's top/right edge are excluded from the mask so they are never smeared. Table-like grain (σ ≈ (1.5, 1.8, 2.2), spatially 0.6-σ correlated) is re-added inside inpainted table areas.
  4. Animation. Ball center x = 84 + 7.857·t for t = 0…14 (right edge reaches x202 at frame 14 exactly), then eases 194 → 201 over frames 15–19 while staying in contact. The duck is restored as exact frame-0 pixels through frame 14 (first contact, no displacement), then for frames 15–19 slides right 1.5 → 7 px with an ease and rotates clockwise 5° → 32° about its base pivot (correct push-from-the-left torque). Clip ends mid reaction, duck still tipping/sliding.
  5. Compositing. Per frame: inpainted background → (warped) duck RGBA → translated ball RGBA; for frames 0–14 the exact original duck pixels are restored last over any ball fringe overlap. Frame 00 is the untouched source image.
  6. Outputs go through artifact_io.save (PNGs, pixels.npz packed as uint32 0xRRGGBB in (144,256,20) layout, inspection.png, standalone index.html). transition_check.png shows five frame pairs (00→01, 06→07, 13→14, 14→15, 18→19) over per-pixel mean-abs-difference heat maps.

Verification (numeric)

  • frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png.
  • All 20 PNGs exactly match the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz.
  • Pipe, couch and table outside the action corridor (y26–63, x60–240) are unchanged in every frame; duck pixels are identical to frame 0 through frame 14.
  • Ball centers are monotonic and contact occurs at frame 14; duck yellow centroid moves right after impact; per-transition changed-pixel counts are steady (~530–560) during the roll and rise (~885–930) once the duck reacts.
  • Limitations: inpainted table is smooth with re-synthesised grain (no external texture); ball spin detail is the original surface detail rotated analytically; post-contact motion is a hand-authored ease, not a rigid-body simulation.
