METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0011

Task

Continue the locked-off shot: the brown/orange tennis ball rolls straight out of the black pipe and across the light wooden coffee table toward the right side. 20 frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s); frame 00 must be identical to first_frame.png. All pixel work is local Python/NumPy/PIL; no external models, engines, or image-view tools. first_frame.png was inspected only through code (histograms, per-pixel hex dumps, colour masks, least-squares fits).

What the first frame contains (measured, not guessed)

  • Wood tabletop band around the action (y≈42–60) is a grayish warm tan with a slow per-row horizontal gradient and fine horizontal grain; a black pipe enters from the left with its elliptical mouth near (115.5, 32.5); a smaller static red ball sits at ≈(109.5, 36), r≈4.2.
  • The moving tennis ball: centre (54.5, 50.6), core radius ≈7.2, fuzzy support to ≈7.6 with a loose fuzz wisp to ≈9 on the lower-right. Lit from upper-left (peak #E98F53 near (−3,−1)); no seam is resolved.
  • No measurable cast shadow outside the ball footprint. Full numbers and the 20-frame event plan are in OBSERVATION.md.

Pipeline (generate.py)

  1. Table model. For each row y=38..63 a quadratic RGB-vs-x trend is least-squares fitted to clean table pixels (excluding ball, red ball, pipe and table edges). This gives a smooth table colour T(x,y); the residual F0 − T is the fine wood grain.
  2. Static background. The initial ball footprint is located from an empirical coverage map (step 4, threshold 0.12, 1-px dilate) and inpainted as T(x,y) + grain(x+29, y) — same-row grain copied from the clean donor strip at x+29 (its statistics match the surrounding horizontal grain). A 1-px 50% feather ring hides the boundary.
  3. Ball shading (fitted, not invented). Interior frame-0 ball pixels (d < r−0.5) are fit per channel by least squares on the basis [1, nx, ny, nz, (1−nz)nx², (1−nz)nx, (1−nz)ny, (1−nz)ny²] where n=(dx/r, dy/r, √(1−(d/r)²)) is the analytic sphere normal (r=7.6). The extra rim terms capture the dark warm limb; fit RMSE is 4.0/1.8/3.9 in R/G/B.
  4. Fuzzy silhouette. Per-pixel coverage is unmixed from frame 0, α = median_c (obs−table)/(shade−table) over channels with strong separation, then separable-smoothed. This empirical alpha keeps the real fuzz outline (including the lower-right wisp); bilinear lookup allows sub-pixel translation. Alphas <0.02 and samples above the template support (screen y<42) are hard-zeroed, so the ball never bleeds onto the pipe or off-table edge.
  5. Rolling felt. Two-octave trilinear 3-D value noise (deterministic integer hashing, frequencies 4.3 and 8.6 over the unit sphere) is amplitude-calibrated to the frame-0 interior residual (≈5.4/2.6/5.0 in R/G/B) and sampled on a point that is rotated by Rodrigues' formula about the screen-projected table-depth axis a=(0,−cos40°,−sin40°) by φ = −(cx−54.5)/7.6 (clockwise roll toward +x, no slip). Lighting stays world-fixed while the felt turns.
  6. Motion blur and compositing. 50% shutter centred on each timestamp, integrated with 7 temporal × 2×4 spatial sub-samples; colours are alpha-averaged and composited over the static background.
  7. Frame 00 is the untouched input file (byte-identical). Frames 1–19 use cx = 54.5 + 8.55·t, cy = 50.6: constant speed straight roll; frame 19 centre 217.0, fuzz ending ≈x225, just inside the dark table edge.
  8. Outputs go through artifact_io.save (pixels.npz as the H×W×20 uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, 20 PNGs, inspection.png, standalone index.html); transition_check.png is written in addition (endpoints at 2× plus all 20 ball-centred crops at 3×).

Verification performed (numeric, no visual claims)

  • frames/00.png equals first_frame.png exactly; every one of the 20 PNGs equals its slice of the uint32 tensor in pixels.npz.
  • Warm-ball centroid per frame matches the linear trajectory within 0.11 px; cy is constant.
  • All pixels with y≤40 (wall, pipe, red ball) and y≥63 (front table edge/floor) are byte-identical to frame 0 in every later frame; no dark off-table edge pixel is ever changed (the ball stays on the tabletop); frame-to-frame changes stay inside the y41–62 corridor.
  • Re-rendered ball at the frame-0 pose agrees with the real ball to mean |Δ| ≈ 3.2 over α>0.5 pixels (dominated by stochastic fuzz); inpainted vacated area matches neighbouring table grain statistics with zero warm residue.
  • Ball internal total variation stays in 11.1–12.4 across all frames (frame-0 ball 12.4), i.e. texture/sharpness is stable along the roll.

Honest limitations

  • The ball is a shaded sphere + noise felt; it does not reproduce the exact frame-0 fuzz fibre pattern after the ball starts moving (it rotates a fresh, statistically identical texture), and no seam is drawn because none is resolvable in the input.
  • Table inpaint is a per-row smooth trend plus translated grain, not a generative fill; it is invisible at this scale and is immediately covered again as the ball passes.
