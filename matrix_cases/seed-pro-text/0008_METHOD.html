METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0008

Task: 20 RGB frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s). A brown tennis ball rolls straight out of a black pipe on the left of a beige coffee table and hits the small yellow rubber duck; a mustard-yellow couch sits in the background.

Everything was produced with local Python pixel operations only (NumPy, SciPy, Pillow). No external models, engines, network access or image-view tools were used; the input frame was analysed purely through numeric RGB inspection (threshold masks, connected components, profile dumps). first_frame.png was never modified.

Measurements from frame 0 (see OBSERVATION.md)

  • Ball: dark brown matte disk, centroid (65.74, 51.88), radius ≈ 7.2 px, median RGB ≈ (55, 40, 36), soft contact shadow offset to its lower-right.
  • Duck: saturated-yellow sprite bbox x 183–205, y 46–67, single dark eye at (189, 53), so it faces left toward the pipe. Body base ≈ y 66–67.
  • Closest point of the duck's left breast (183, 50) gives first contact at ball centre x ≈ 176.0. Roll distance 110.3 px.

Frame construction (generate.py)

Static plate. Frame 0 is copied exactly for output frame 00. For frames 01+ the ball's original footprint (analytic disk r=8.3 plus the photographed shadow ellipse, clipped to x 52–82, y 42–63) is inpainted once: inpaint_silhouette searches horizontal donor shifts inside a clean table band (x 82–172), keeps the shift whose donor boundary ring best matches the canvas (minimum mean absolute RGB error), applies a small per-channel gain (0.92–1.08) to match exposure, and feathers the seam one pixel. This produces a static photographic table with the ball removed and no moving texture.

Ball sprite. A 21×21 patch around the measured centre is converted to soft-alpha RGBA: coverage α = clip(r+0.75−d, 0, 1); the per-row median table colour is used for the unpremultiplied background term, and foreground RGB is recovered by (crop − (1−α)·bg)/α. The sprite is rasterised at sub-pixel positions with bilinear interpolation, centre y fixed at 51.88 (constant depth, constant apparent size). Schedule: constant speed (176.0−65.74)/16 = 6.89 px/frame through frame 16; then +7.4, +1.2, +1.2 px as the ball drives into the duck and decelerates (centres 176.0, 183.4, 184.6, 185.8).

Contact shadow. A fixed 19×9 soft multiplicative stamp (gaussian ellipse, up to 48 % darkening, centred 4.5 px right and 6.5 px below the ball centre, matching the photographed shadow direction and extent) is applied to the table before the ball is composited, so it travels with the ball and stays under it.

Duck reaction (frames 16–19 only). The duck is cut from frame 0 as RGBA from the crop x 180–210, y 44–69: alpha from the yellow chroma key clip((R−B−20)/22, 0, 1) with the dark eye forced opaque, foreground unpremultiplied against the per-row table colour. Each contact frame first inpaints the duck's resting footprint (same donor routine, band x 100–170), then composites a rigid clockwise rotation about the body-base pivot (195, 66) via an inverse affine bilinear warp, plus a rightward translation: tip/slide = 2°/0, 4°/+1, 9°/+2.5, 14°/+4 px (frames 16–19). The eye is re-stamped at its warped location so it stays a crisp dark pixel. Rotation is clockwise because the impact is on the duck's left breast, tipping it away from the ball. Composite order per contact frame: inpainted plate → shadow stamp → rotated duck → ball, giving slight rubber-compression overlap at the breast and a near-tangent rest pose at frame 19.

All pixels outside the dynamic corridor (x 58–212, y 44–71) remain byte-identical to frame 0 in every frame; the camera is locked.

Outputs and verification

  • frames/00.png–19.png (256×144 RGB), pixels.npz with the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor of shape 144×256×20 (H×W×T, via artifact_io.save).
  • inspection.png: key-frame sheet (0, 2, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 19).
  • transition_check.png: frames 0/8/15/16/17/19 plus ×4 amplified absolute differences across the contact transitions.
  • index.html: standalone delta-encoded player from player_template.html.
  • Programmatic checks in generate.py: frame 00 equals first_frame.png exactly; every saved PNG repacks to the tensor in pixels.npz.
  • Numeric QA: changed-pixel bbox stays inside the dynamic corridor; brown-disk tracking gives a straight line (cy 51.7–52.0) at the designed speed; duck yellow bbox and eye track the tip/slide after contact.
