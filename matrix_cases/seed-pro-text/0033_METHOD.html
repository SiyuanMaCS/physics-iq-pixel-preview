OBSERVATION — case_0033 (PixelWorld-Text)

All measurements come from numeric RGB inspection of first_frame.png (256×144, RGB) with local Python only. No image viewing tool was used.

Scene layout (frame 0)

Static, locked-off view of a light grey-beige wooden tabletop flanked by two large pipes at the left and right edges.

  • Left pipe: dark interior at the left edge (luminance < 30), far rim arc crossing the top of the frame around y≈4–17; near/lower lip runs down the left edge.
  • Right pipe: dark opening x≈226–255, y≈17–83, cut by the right edge; near lip bulges left to x≈226 around y≈58–78.
  • Blue-lit patch inside the right pipe mouth: small crescent x≈236–255, y≈66–74 (peak (175,203,241)), static pipe-mouth lighting; the moving balls never reach it.
  • Back panel: warm dark wood strip across the top (y≈0–~20, e.g. RGB ≈ (60,46,2)), meeting the table in perspective around y≈20–35.
  • Tabletop: smooth light grey-beige wood with strongly horizontal grain (horizontal residual correlation ≈ 0.99 at lag 1; ≈ 0.12 vertical), grain residual σ ≈ 9–11. Luminance grows from ≈ 100 far/left to ≈ 135 near/right; the front edge darkens below y≈134.
  • Lighting: key light from the right and slightly front/high — the yellow ball's measured cast shadow runs to the left and slightly up (fitted key azimuth ≈ 50–58° from +x, elevation ≈ 44°). The blue ball sits in open shade at the left-pipe mouth: no direct key, cool ambient + warm table bounce.

Equal speeds, so both reach the midpoint of the fitted frame-0 centres simultaneously. Midpoint (112.9, 53.8), apparent radius there r ≈ 7.66, contact centre gap 2r ≈ 15.3 px.

Rendering method (local pixel math only)

  1. Clean plate: inpaint ONLY the two frame-0 ball + shadow footprints — horizontal wood-grain runs interpolated from run endpoints, then two vertical fill-aware blend sweeps. Every other background pixel (pipes, back panel, table) stays byte-identical to frame 0 (verified).

Ball extraction: numerical fit of centre/radius/soft edge (grid + Nelder–Mead) against plate-composited predictions; a 9-lobe non-negative sphere shader (ambient, three key lobes, Blinn specular, bounded fresnel, table bounce, two occlusion lobes) with key azimuth/elevation searched; plus a smooth rotating felt-mottle albedo map. Two bounded rim-only terms (grazing darkening, dark-side terminator) are fitted last against plate-composited rim rows.

  5. Frame 00 is written byte-for-byte from first_frame.png.

METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0033 (PixelWorld-Text)

Two tennis balls (blue in pipe-mouth shade, yellow sunlit) roll out of opposite edge pipes, collide head-on at equal speed at the midpoint, and rebound. Everything is procedural local Python (numpy / scipy / PIL); no external assets, engines, network, or AI models. The generator is generate.py; run python3 generate.py. first_frame.png was inspected only through numeric RGB code — no image viewing tool was used at any stage.

1. Frame 00

frames[0] = first_frame.png bytes verbatim (verified byte-identical), guaranteeing exact reproduction of the observed starting image.

2. Clean plate (static background)

The background only needs filling where the frame-0 balls and their shadows sit. build_plate defines small elliptical/rectangular footprints around the two balls (table rows only, y = 35…132), and for each horizontal run of hole pixels interpolates the wood colour linearly between the run endpoints (wood grain is horizontally coherent: measured lag-1 correlation 0.99 vs 0.12 vertically). Two fill-aware vertical blend sweeps (plate + filled_up·up + filled_down·down) / (1 + f_up + f_down) smooth the fills using only other filled rows. Every pixel outside the holes remains byte-identical to frame 0 (asserted numerically).

3. Ball models (fitted to frame 0)

BallModel fits, per ball, centre, radius and soft-edge width, a view-dependent shader, and a felt albedo map.

  • Geometry. A local planar table colour is least-squares fitted to a clean right-side annulus (the left side carries the cast shadow), and corrected with the modelled ground shadow. Predictions are α·ball + (1−α)·plate, with analytic coverage α = clip((r − d)/w + 0.5, 0, 1). Parameters are searched on a coarse grid, then refined by Nelder–Mead over SSE. Fits are interleaved with shader/albedo refits: shader → geometry → shader → albedo → geometry → shader → albedo.
  • Shader (9 non-negative lobes). Constant ambient, key lobe powers n·L ^ {0.6, 1, 2.5}, Blinn–Phong specular (n·H)^12, bounded fresnel rim (1−z)^3 (coefficient capped at 90), a warm table-bounce lobe, and two negative-only occlusion lobes (pipe side and underside). Coefficients are per-channel non-negative least squares on dense polar samples (260 angles × 84 radii) well inside the disk; the key direction is grid searched over azimuth −25…65°, elevation 44…82°. Fitted key for the yellow ball ≈ azimuth 50–58°, elevation 44°.
  • Felt albedo. albedo = observed / shaded on the interior, smoothed tangentially (σ = 7 angular samples) and lightly radially, wrapped periodically; the last interior band is held to the geometric edge.
  • Rim-only finishing terms (indices 9–10). Fitted last and never fed back into geometry/albedo: a bounded isotropic grazing darkening −(1−z)^1.6 and a bounded dark-side terminator −max(0,−n·L)^1.5, each per channel, selected between joint and single least-squares solutions under caps (70 / 90). They match fuzzy-felt limb self-occlusion without perturbing the fitted interior.
  • Frame-0 rerender fidelity: mean absolute RGB error over the whole disk 6.4 (blue) / 6.9 (yellow) levels, inner core 3.4 / 3.6, worst pixel 46 / 35. Residuals concentrate in 1–2 px AA rim bands where ball, shadow and wood grain mix.

4. Shadows

Four additive per-channel gaussian lobes in radius-relative coordinates (centres, widths and amplitudes) are fitted to the exact target max(plate − observed, 0) of the yellow ball (non-negative least squares; the fourth compact crescent lobe was located by a 4-D position/width grid search on the 3-lobe residual). The additive model matches the data better than multiplication (a multiply field under-darkens the bright wood). Fields move rigidly with each ball's pose and radius. Yellow strength 1.0 always; blue ramps 0.30 → 1.00 as it exits pipe-mouth shade. Fields below 1e-4 are zeroed so far gaussian tails cannot subtract a sub-ULP amount that quantizes a static integer pixel one level darker.

5. Blue ball's sunlit appearance

Frame 0 only shows the blue ball in open shade. Its intrinsic colour is estimated from its ambient response relative to the yellow ball's ambient/lit-centre ratio (≈ (10,26,140)). Its hypothetical sunlit shader uses the yellow ball's measured key/ambient/specular lobe structure rescaled by the intrinsic colour ratio (specular and the darkening rim terms scaled near-neutrally). Each frame the blue shader is a convex blend (1−w)·shade + w·sun, w = 0, .22, .48, .72, .88, .96, 1, … (t = 0…6+), which numerically gives a smooth core-lightness ramp (blue channel mean 123 → 191 by t5) with no popping. This is an extrapolation, not a measurement (see limitations).

6. Motion and physics

trajectory() places contact at the midpoint of the two fitted frame-0 centres (112.9, 53.8) at t = 10, radius given by the linear law through the two fitted radii (r ≈ 7.66 at contact), so the centre gap at contact is exactly 2r. Equal per-frame displacement gives equal speeds (2.943 px/frame for both, ratio 1.000). Post-contact velocities reverse and scale by COR = 0.80. Rolling spin angle is φ = ∓arc-length/r, reversed after contact; render() rotates the albedo map accordingly. At t = 10/11/12 an on-axis squash 0.86/0.93/0.98 is applied, and the centres are pulled together by r·(1−s) so the shortened ball halves actually stay in contact (avoids a visible table sliver between them). Depth ordering (shadows and balls) uses the fitted y positions.

7. Compositing

For t = 1…19: copy the float plate → subtract both additive shadow fields (far ball first) → paste each 3× supersampled ball patch (subpixel patch origin, analytic coverage and analytic sphere normals, rolling albedo, squash transform along the velocity axis, blue shade/sun blend) with straight alpha → round to nearest and clip to uint8. Frame 00 bypasses compositing.

8. Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz (144×256×20 uint32 0xRRGGBB), frames/00.png…19.png, the 8-frame inspection.png sheet and the standalone index.html player (delta-encoded). make_transition_check writes transition_check.png (19 amplified frame-difference tiles).

Checks run (numeric only):

  • frames/00.png byte-identical to first_frame.png; all 20 PNGs decode to the packed 0xRRGGBB tensor (all equal).
  • Equal-speed trajectories; contact gap = 2r; rebound speed = 0.8× incoming; smooth blue relight ramp; motion confined to the two ball/shadow tracks; pipes, back panel and front edge exactly unchanged across all frames (max RGB difference 0).
  • Frame-0 model rerender errors as in §3; shadow crescent rows match the observed plate-minus-frame darkening to within a few levels.

Limitations

  • The blue ball's sunlit shading is an extrapolation from one shade-only observation plus the yellow ball's measured environment; its lit colour is a deep saturated blue and a few highlight pixels clip at 255 around t5–7.
  • Shadow lobes are fitted to one ball/one pose and moved rigidly; small crescent residuals (a few levels) remain at AA rim pixels.
  • Felt albedo mottle is very low contrast; spin is physically phased but visually subtle by construction, to preserve frame 0's smooth look.
  • No human/image-tool visual review was performed; validation is numeric.
