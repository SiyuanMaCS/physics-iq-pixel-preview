The table surface is a smooth, mildly grainy gradient: warm pale wood (~(120,110,90)) on the upper-left, neutral/cool lit wood (~(145,155,170)) on the lower-right, grain amplitude ~±2–4 RGB.
  • Outside the table edges are near-neutral dark background corners: top-right triangle ~x226–255, y0–25 and bottom-left triangle ~x0–27, y52–143 (plus tiny bottom-right / top-left slivers). These are the only places the two edge pipes are visible — just dark mouths at the frame corners; the pipe bodies lie off-frame.

  • Center-to-center axis (blue → yellow unit vector): n = (0.9039, −0.4278); center distance 72.47 px.
  • Key light comes from screen-right/slightly-above: ball highlights on the right side, soft contact shadows cast to the left (slightly down):
    • Yellow: distinct soft elliptical shadow, core factor ≈0.65 of ambient, spanning offsets roughly x−22…−9, y−3…+9 from center.

  • Other bluish pixels at the right edge (x243–255, y91–134) and at (246,14) are static cool-colored fringe on the dark table-edge/background structure, not balls (elongated, non-disc shapes) — left untouched.

Rendering method (numeric pixel operations only)

  1. Build a shadow-free clean table background by inpainting the two ball discs and their contact shadows in frame 0 (robust local quadratic surface fit seeded Laplacian fill + matched table grain).
  2. Extract each ball's exact RGBA sprite (disc + 1.5 px feathered edge via chroma unblending) and its own soft shadow factor map from frame 0.
  3. Frames 01–19: paste clean background, multiply in the two moving shadow maps and paste resampled sprites at sub-pixel centers from the table; add a subtle rotating tennis seam for the rolling cue.
  4. Frame 00 is written byte-identical to first_frame.png.

METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0031

Everything was produced with local Python pixel math only (NumPy, SciPy ndimage, PIL for PNG I/O). No network, external data, reference images, or rendering engines were used. first_frame.png was inspected numerically (RGB arrays); no image viewing tool was used to judge the frames.

1. What the source frame contains

Numeric inspection (see OBSERVATION.md) finds a locked-off light wooden tabletop with two dark pipe-mouth triangles in opposite corners (top-right and bottom-left; pipe bodies off-frame), and two 18 px-diameter tennis balls on the diagonal between them:

  • blue center (120.5, 71.5), yellow center (186.0, 40.5), radius 9 px, initial center gap 72.44 px;
  • diagonal unit vector (blue→yellow) n = (0.9039, −0.4278);
  • key light from screen-right: lit rim on the right side of each sphere and a soft elliptical contact shadow cast to the left and slightly down (yellow core factor ≈ 0.55–0.65; blue weaker, ≈ 0.7–0.9).

2. Motion model

Equal speeds, equal masses, head-on along the diagonal:

  • speed v = 2.2694 px/frame (so each ball travels (72.47 − 18)/2 = 27.23 px and the discs first touch at frame 12, t = 3.00 s);
  • blue moves +n, yellow moves −n; at the elastic contact at frame 12 the velocities reverse, so frame 12+k retraces frame 12−k (k = f for f ≤ 12, else 24 − f);
  • both sprites keep their screen-space sphere shading (the light is fixed); a very subtle procedural felt-seam band rotates by the no-slip rolling angle θ = ±d/9, with its sign reversing after rebound.

Positions for all 20 frames are tabulated in OBSERVATION.md.

3. Frame-0 decomposition (the only scene data source)

generate.py decomposes first_frame.png into three layers that are re-composited for frames 01–19.

  1. Ball masks. Conservative chroma tests in RGB (B−R > 25 ∧ B−G > 8 ∧ B > 60 for blue, G−B > 35 ∧ R−B > 20 ∧ G > 110 for yellow), plus r < 10.4 discs. A static cool fringe on the right table edge (x ≥ 240) is explicitly excluded from the blue mask.

  2. Clean table plate. Each disc plus an elliptical shadow-support region to its left (center offset (−15, +3), axes 11.5 × 9.5) is filled: masked pixels are seeded from a robust iteratively-reweighted quadratic RGB surface fit in a 53×53 window (the ball, its shadow box, and chroma excluded), then harmonically inpainted with 2500 Jacobi relaxations of the discrete Laplacian. Fine wood grain (source minus a σ=0.9 Gaussian blur) is copied from nearest clean donor patches beside each footprint (×0.9) and the fill edge is feathered over ~2 px.

  3. Contact-shadow factor fields. Over a 59×35 window per ball, the per-channel multiply factor obs / plate is measured wherever the geometric shadow support is valid and r ≥ 10.2 (excluding ball pixels), using σ=0.9 Gaussian-denoised obs/plate and a short σ=0.9 normalised-convolution pass, so the field is grain-stable without the core bleeding into the soft edge. Immediately under the rim (r < 10.2) where a shadow is detected just outside it, the field continues toward the contact point (factor floor 0.80 at r ≈ 7.4) instead of snapping back to the plate.

  4. Ball RGBA sprites (25×25). The interior r ≤ 8.2 is fully opaque with its original colour. In the 8.2–10.4 antialiased rim, coverage is estimated by chroma unblending against the true local background (plate × own shadow factor), comparing each rim pixel to the fully-covered interior colour sampled at radius 7.3 along the same angle. Reliable per-angle samples (all three channels agree within 0.3 and lie in [−0.15, 1.15]) populate a shared radial coverage profile (0.15 px bins, medians, interpolated, σ=0.35 smoothed), which supplies sensible coverage at shadowed angles where direct unblending is ambiguous. Sprite colours are recovered by inverse alpha compositing over that same shadowed background.

4. Frame compositing (f = 0…19)

For each frame: start from the clean plate; multiply the two shadow factor fields (bilinearly resampled at the sub-pixel translated window origins); composite the yellow then blue RGBA sprites with bilinear sub-pixel resampling at the analytic centers; multiply on the subtle rotating seam inside each disc (max 10 % darkening, fading at the rim). Results are clipped to 0–255.

5. Outputs and exactness guarantees

  • frames/00.png is overwritten with a byte-for-byte copy of first_frame.png after saving; the frame-0 entry of pixels.npz is the decoded source array, so tensor and PNG agree exactly.
  • artifact_io.save writes the (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor packed as 0xRRGGBB, the 20 PNGs, inspection.png, and index.html; transition_check.png is a 3×3 contact sheet of key frames (0,3,6,9,11,12,13,16,19) with timestamps.
  • Numeric verification (.tmp/verify.py) confirms: tensor/PNG equality for every frame, byte-identical frame 00, ball chroma centroids within ~0.5 px of the analytic centers for all 20 frames, no stray ball pixels, discs inside the frame, center gap 18 px at the contact frame, post-collision symmetry frames 13–19 vs 11–5 (MAE ≤ 0.02 RGB, from the seam flip), ~650–830 changed pixels per step, and zero changes outside the 30 px ball/shadow corridors and at both static pipe mouths.

6. Known approximations

  • The contact shadow is treated as a fixed soft pattern translating with each ball (correct for a ball rolling on a plane under one fixed light), and the frame-0 field has a handful of edge pixels matching the source within ~10–20 RGB; frame 00 itself is the exact source regardless.
  • Seam shading is a subtle procedural cue; no real seam pattern was recoverable from the source, so it is intentionally faint.
  • Collision physics is idealised: equal masses, perfectly elastic and central, instantaneous contact at frame 12 (no compression or slow-motion around impact).
