METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0053

Task: a blue-black grabber releases two raised metal balls of a five-ball Newton's cradle; 20 RGB frames, 256×144, 4 fps. Frame 00 is byte-identical to first_frame.png (asserted in generate.py and re-verified against pixels.npz). All work is local numpy / OpenCV / PIL pixel math; no external assets, models, engines or image-viewing tools were used. Geometry was measured numerically (RGB masks, connected components, Canny + least-squares circle fits); see OBSERVATION.md.

1. Measurements driving the model

  • Five balls r≈5.2 px on 12 px centres; rest row y=97 at x=102,114,126,138,150.
  • Frame 0 holds the left pair at (88.5,94.6),(100.5,94.6): 13.5 px left and ≈2.4 px above rest, implying string length L=45 px to the rail at y=52 and release angle θ₀=arcsin(13.5/45)=0.305 rad.
  • The cradle sits on a glossy black base (x104–166, y92–126) on a brown wooden table, with soft dark reflections + bright specular flecks of the three resting balls at y≈115–121.
  • Grabber pixels are isolated with the vivid-blue mask (B>R+40, B>85) union dark/navy pixels (B−R>15, value<58, or value<66) restricted to three hand- traced silhouette polygons (two scissor grips around the balls, the black handle on the left, the navy shaft down the base). Ball interiors are excluded.

2. Clean background plate

All dynamic objects (three tool polygons, five ball disks, the reflection ellipses/flecks) form an inpaint mask. A synthetic background field is composited with a 3×3 feather:

  • y<92: per-row median wood colour from the clean strip x108–160;
  • 92≤y≤126, x<104: per-row median brown tabletop; x>166 kept as-is;
  • black base x104–166: vertical gradient (44,44,43)→(26,26,26) + σ≈1 grain;
  • y≥127: clean-row medians (shadow gap, wooden apron).

A Telea inpaint pass cleans the thin mask seams. This static plate is shared by frames 01–19. Static zones (upper wall, apron) provably never change across the sequence (max abs diff 0, checked numerically).

3. Ball physics and rendering

Continuous pendulum motion θ(t)=−θ₀·0.9^n·cos(ωt), ω=π rad/s (quarter swing 0.5 s = 2 frames), n = number of bottom crossings elapsed. Collisions at t=0.5,1.5,… s transfer the pair's motion to the opposite side (Newton-cradle rule): the moving pair stops, the far pair exits, the centre ball stays; the amplitude envelope is 0.9 per half-cycle. Ball centres map through x=c+L sin θ, y=97−L(1−cos θ).

The ball sprite is the untouched 13×13 patch of the middle resting ball (138,97) from frame 0 — its own dark chrome body, white specular fleck and black lower crescent are preserved (a median over the three balls blurred the specular in an earlier revision). A radial alpha (r=5.2, 0.9 px feather) composites it at sub-pixel positions with warpAffine. Each frame also draws that ball's tabletop reflection (soft dark ellipse at y116, bright fleck at y120, both Gaussian-blurred), fading with swing height and at the black-base edges.

4. Grabber release and retreat

The tool mask is split into two scissor halves by the sign of (p−pivot)·v̂ (pivot (70,90), v̂=(−0.247,0.970)). Each half rotates ±angle about the pivot and translates up-left on keyframes: f1 10°/(−3,−2), f2 18°/(−13,−8), f3 24°/(−38,−22), f4 28°/(−112,−80), gone from f5 on. The jaws visibly open and the handle leaves the frame while the released pair swings; tool pixels never overlap a ball disk (checked numerically).
