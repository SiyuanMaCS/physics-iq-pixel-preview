METHOD.md

METHOD

What this is

The 20-frame sequence is produced by analytic 2D pixel compositing in local Python (generate.py, numpy + OpenCV + Pillow only). There is no physics engine, no renderer, no 3D model, and no external/AI model: every output pixel comes from either the original first_frame.png, an analytically inpainted static plate built from it, or a sprite cut from it and affine-warped by hand-measured geometry. The camera is locked off, so the problem reduces to keeping the static world byte-still and moving three sprites (stick, white domino W3, black domino B3).

All geometry, colours, masks and angles were measured from first_frame.png with numeric RGB inspection (column traces, connected components, polygon probing, ASCII dumps); the measurements and the 20-frame event plan are recorded in OBSERVATION.md. No image viewing tool was used on the first frame.

Pipeline

1. Static plate (build_plate)

The dynamic pixels — stick (green grip + warm shaft keyed by colour inside a geometric corridor), W3 polygon, B3 polygon (front face + warm lit flank), B3's standing cast shadow, and a base sliver — are erased only for x ≥ 137 (the near row is never touched). Holes are filled analytically:

  • platform top: linear interpolation between two clean anchor columns plus σ≈1.8 grain;
  • platform front face: per-row medians of surviving face pixels, with the measured quadratic near-edge lightening, plus σ≈1.5 grain;
  • table: per-channel robust least-squares wood-colour plane fitted on clean warm-wood pixels, plus fine grain tile-copied from a clean table patch (x205–250, y56–92);
  • a cv2.INPAINT_TELEA pass seeds the blend, then plate = orig·(1−α) + fill·α with α=1 inside erased geometry and 0.5 on the dilated ring.
  • The deep dark strip immediately right of W3 (platform face/table shadow) was verified to be static background, not W3's side face: it is kept as exact original pixels except where the stick passes through.

2. Sprite extraction (extract)

Three RGBA sprites are cut from the original:

  • stick — green head + warm shaft; alpha from a colour key (G−R>14 & G−B>12 & G>80 for green, R−B>12 & v>60 for shaft, 0.5 on warm AA edges), inside its geometric corridor;
  • W3 — front/top face polygon; alpha 1.0 on measured core polygons, 0.55 on AA rims;
  • B3, split in two: the dark front face and the 6 px warm side/flank strip. Keeping these separate is what lets the flank become the warm end cap of the slab after tipping; warping it with a fixed width offset made cap pixels jump in the last frames (this was a measured defect, fixed).

Sprite colours are alpha-unblended from the plate: c = (orig − (1−α)·plate)/α, so warped edges composite cleanly. Rim colours (0 < α < 0.9) are mixed toward the core median (0.5 W3, 0.35 B3) and any residual green is forced to G ≤ R+6 — the original AA rims carry a faint greenish/olive pull from anti-aliasing that otherwise reappears as stray green pixels when the sprite rotates.

3. Animation (frames 3–19; frames 0–2 are the untouched original)

Each frame starts from the plate. Moving pieces are affine-warped RGBA sprites (cv2.warpAffine, bilinear) composited "over" the plate:

  • stick: rotation about measured pivot (186, 27.5); clockwise θ from vertical sweeps the tip left into W3, holding at 67°.
  • domino: rotation about its base hinge. Local up maps to v = cosα·(0,−1) + sinα·FALL with measured screen fall direction FALL=(−0.96,+0.28); the width axis EW=(1,−0.05) stays fixed, giving cosine foreshortening of a rigid slab hinged at its base. B3's side strip uses columns (−FALL, −v) about edge anchor (157, 66.35), so it becomes the end cap. W3's lit face darkens slightly with lean (gain 1 − 0.12·sinα).
  • Angles per frame (THETA, A3, AB lists in generate.py) implement the OBSERVATION.md plan: stick moves frames 3–10; W3 tips frames 5–17; B3 follows frames 8–17; at 88° both lie in the gap with the tip short of the static near row. Frames 17–19 are pixel-identical (settled rest).
  • While a domino still stands its pixels are an exact hard copy from the original inside its (dilated) stand mask — sprite-over-plate can only approximate the original blend.
  • Shadows: B3's standing cast shadow is restored as exact original pixels (feathered mask, weight clip(1−1.5·sinα)) while it is vertical, crossfading into an analytic soft moving contact shadow (dark warm polygon under the slab, alpha ramping with sinα, clipped to x ≥ 120). W3 gets the same moving contact shadow.
  • Near-row protection: a mask of the static dominoes P/W1/B2/W2 is built by connected components (cool-bright or near-dark bodies, area > 60, x < 140) and dilated; every warped layer has zero alpha there and canvas[PROT] = original is enforced after compositing, guaranteeing the near row never changes.

4. Output (artifact_io.save)

The 20×144×256×3 uint8 array is written as:

  • pixels.npz: uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor transposed to H×W×20;
  • frames/00.png … 19.png (lossless PNG, frame 00 = exact first frame);
  • inspection.png: contact sheet of frames 0,2,5,8,11,14,17,19;
  • index.html: standalone frame player (template, tensor embedded).

transition_check.png is built separately by .tmp/make_transition_check.py: key frames 0,3,5,7,9,12,15,19 with, beneath each, the amplified per-pixel difference from frame 0 (×4), giving a numeric map of where and when the scene changes.

Verification performed (numeric, no image viewing tool)

  • Frames 0, 1, 2 are byte-for-byte equal to first_frame.png; frame 00 PNG is the original.
  • Every frames/NN.png decodes to exactly the same pixels as the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz (unpacked and transposed); shape and dtype assertions in artifact_io.save hold.
  • Static-near-row protection mask: 0 changed pixels in all 20 frames.
  • Changed-pixel bounding boxes stay inside the physical action envelope (x 118–196, y 17–82; the extreme pixels are the rotated head AA fringe at the top and B3's soft contact shadow, pure darkenings d ≤ 8, on the front table band y ≥ 76); W3/B3 mass bboxes follow the planned cascade and stop at the measured gap geometry; frames 17/18/19 are identical.
  • Every green pixel in every frame lies within 8.1 px of the stick pivot (the rigidly rotating head, 25–30 px vs 30 at rest); there is no green anywhere else. Diffs vs frame 0 are zero for frames 0–2 and confined to the stick corridor at frame 3.
  • Sprite coverage/alpha means printed at generation: stick 92 px/ᾱ0.71, W3 302 px/0.81, B3-front 511 px/0.90, B3-side 187 px/0.72.

Limitations: motion is authored from measured geometry and a hand-timed event table, not simulated physics; inpainted plate regions are statistically matched (plane + tiled grain) rather than recovered; the moving contact shadow is an analytic approximation.
