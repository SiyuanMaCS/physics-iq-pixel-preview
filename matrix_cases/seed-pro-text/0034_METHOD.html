METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0034

Fully procedural, local Python pixel math (NumPy / SciPy / OpenCV / Pillow). No network, no external models, no reference imagery, no subagents. The only scene input is first_frame.png. All inspection was numeric (per-pixel RGB statistics and ASCII luminance maps); no image viewing tool was used.

1. Scene read (numeric, see OBSERVATION.md)

Gray wall, grained wooden table, black platform slab (dark-gray top, near-black front face) on the right; six painted wooden cubes in a perspective row on the table's near edge — yellow, red, pink, green (the struck "first" block), blue, violet — and a black stick/paddle on the platform, its head already overlapping green's upper-right edge at frame 0. The row recedes up-right: bases lie on the near-edge diagonal y≈125 (x55) → y≈112 (x135).

2. Segmentation (frame 0 → RGBA sprites)

  • Stick: darkest component (L<38) inside the head zone x139–159/y43–72, largest CC, dilated 1 px; a dilation ring that matched painted neighbours was clipped back so no coloured pixels ride with the arm.
  • Four movers (yellow/red/pink/green): strict per-hue painted-face predicates, largest CC inside each block's bounding rectangle, vertical morphological closing + hole fill. Silhouettes are then grown column-wise from the topmost seed down to a fitted table-edge baseline y=k·x+q (least squares on the tall core columns), adding:
    • relaxed shaded paint of the same cube,
    • the cube's shaded natural-wood side faces (R>125, G<106, R−G>38, B<95, L<125) and lit natural-wood tops (R>148, R−G>45), always within the block rectangle and within 2–5 px of the painted core; gap-table columns (no core in the column stack) are excluded. Foreign-paint exclusion uses the neighbour's filled core kept inside its own rectangle — global hue predicates also match ordinary table wood, which had carved each cube's own shaded face out of its sprite.
  • Blue, violet, platform: never segmented; their pixels are protected and copied verbatim into every frame.
  • Green under the stick head: the occluded paint sliver is completed with cv2 Telea inpainting so the cube stays intact once the arm swings away.

3. Clean background plate

The union of the five sprites and their small table contact-shadow / coloured bounce-light strips defines the inpaint hole; static blocks and the platform are protected. Revealed surface is filled row-wise by linear interpolation between wall or wood anchors (chosen by horizon y), then given photographic grain from the high-frequency residual (image − Gaussian blur σ1.1) of the nearest clean surface donor; painted/shaded cube faces are banned as donors. The 1-px hole ring is kept as the untouched photo for a seamless seam.

The stick's head also hides dark-gray slab top and the deeply shaded left face of the blue cube, which the wood/wall row fill cannot reconstruct (no anchor on the stick's left). Those reveal pixels are filled from the nearest slab-gray / neutral near-black / blue-shaded-face donor plus the same grain residual, so the uncovered slab and the blue cube's dark side read correctly after swing.

4. Motion model

  • Pivots. Stick axle inferred at (188, 48) on the platform housing. Each cube pivots at its front-left table-edge corner on the fitted baseline.
  • Stick: plain image-plane clockwise affine (screen-clockwise = +θ with y-down) about the axle, bicubic resampling, keyframed 0→21° with a small settle to 19°.
  • Cubes: a perspective-foreshortened hinge transform rather than an in-plane rotation. With lean angle θ and floor vector (fx, fy) (where the formerly vertical unit edge lands at 90°), the forward map of local axes is F(1,0)=(1,0), F(0,−1)=(fx·sinθ, −cosθ+fy·sinθ); rendered through the inverse affine. Vectors (e.g. green (−0.43, +0.086)) were measured from the row geometry, so a toppled cube lies along the near-edge line (mostly −x, slightly +y) with depth foreshortening instead of standing up rotated.
  • Timeline (domino): green starts f03 (contact), pink f06, red f09, yellow f12; lean keyframes 4,12,23,36,49,61,71,78,82° — accelerating fall, easing to rest. Blue, violet and platform never move.
  • Shadows/light: per-block contact shadow and coloured bounce-light strips fade out with lean; a blurred dark ellipse stretches along the fall vector while each cube tips.

5. Compositing

Per frame: clean plate → fading static shadows/bounce → dynamic stretch shadows → mover sprites back-to-front (green farthest → yellow nearest) → stick on top, straight alpha compositing with bicubic sampling. Frame 0 is the verbatim first_frame.png (asserted byte-identical).

6. Outputs and verification

  • Programmed checks (numeric, no visual tools): frame 00 equals first_frame.png; every PNG equals the corresponding tensor column; motion bounding boxes stay inside the stick corridor and the four fall footprints; all changed pixels above y40 lie under the rotated stick layer; painted-core ghosts left behind in vacated standing regions are zero for red/pink/green (the remaining relaxed-"yellow" hits are ordinary table wood, verified by RGB statistics); the plate seam ring is byte-identical to frame 0; changed pixels over static objects are limited to the stick's antialiased edge and the correctly reconstructed blue shaded face.
