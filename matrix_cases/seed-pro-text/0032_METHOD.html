METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0032

Task (from prompt.txt): locked-off single shot of a light wooden tabletop with two pipes at the edges; a blue and a yellow tennis ball roll out of the pipes at equal speed and collide. 20 RGB frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s); frame 00 is byte-identical to first_frame.png.

All work was done with local Python pixel operations (numpy, Pillow, OpenCV). No external models, engines, network, or image-viewing tools were used; the first frame was analysed purely through numeric RGB inspection (see OBSERVATION.md).

1. Scene measurement (numeric, from first_frame.png)

  • Strong-chroma masks B − max(R,G) and G − B, least-squares circle fits on per-row extents, and gradient-energy ring fits locate:
    • blue ball centre (92.8, 57.0), felt radius 8.1 px (AA halo ≈ 8.5);
    • optic-yellow ball centre (178.0, 56.2), radius 8.1 px.
  • Both contact the table (soft occlusion crescent directly under each disc, fitted darkness up to ≈34 % at the contact line, gone within ≈1 px onto the table).
  • Dark static pipe structures at x≈0–14 and x≈242–256; faint blue crescents inside the rims. The scene background is otherwise smooth grained wood with a gentle left→right brightness gradient.

2. Physics (analytic, flat tabletop)

  • Equal pre-collision speed so the balls touch at the midpoint: v = 3.45 px/frame = 13.8 px/s. Centre trajectories x_b(t) = 92.8 + 3.45 t, x_y(t) = 178.0 − 3.45 t for t = 0…10; contact at frame 10 (t = 2.50 s), blue centre 127.3 / yellow 143.5.
  • Equal-mass head-on impact, tennis-ball restitution e = 0.8: velocities reverse, post speed 2.76 px/frame, t = 11…19.
  • Rolling without slipping: sprite angles ±arc-length/r (±24.3°/frame), blue clockwise while moving right and yellow counter-clockwise while moving left; signs reverse after rebound.
  • Frame 10 carries a subtle impact deformation (scale 0.96 horizontally about the contact plane, 1.03 vertically about the ground-contact line); normal spheres again from frame 11.
  • Heights are constant; no bounce.

3. Rendering pipeline (generate.py)

  1. Static background. The two ball discs (r 10.0) and their contact shadows (ellipse 11×4) are masked and removed from frame 0 with OpenCV Telea inpainting (radius 4). High-frequency wood grain lost in the fill is restored by adding a spatially shifted copy of the frame’s own band-pass residual (Gaussian high-pass, σ 1.6), gain matched to the residual variance of the surrounding ring and feathered at the hole edge. This background is shared by frames 1…19, so pipes, walls and untouched tabletop never change.
  2. Ball sprites. Each 25×25 sprite is cut from frame 0 with an analytic anti-aliased disc alpha (r 8.2). Source and background are bilinearly sampled at sub-pixel positions and the background colour is alpha-unmixed (ball = (src − (1−α)·bg)/α), so sprite edges composite cleanly over any table location without a fringe.
  3. Per frame: an analytic soft contact shadow (Gaussian ellipse fitted to the frame-0 darkness maps) is multiplied under each ball; each sprite is rotated about its centre (rolling spin), translated to its sub-pixel centre with linear interpolation, and alpha-composited; frame 10 adds the squash warp described above.
  4. Frame 0 is the untouched source image.

4. Outputs and verification

  • frames/00.png…19.png, pixels.npz (H×W×20 packed 0xRRGGBB uint32), inspection.png, index.html via artifact_io.save, plus transition_check.png (key frames, amplified |Δ| panels around contact, and a full 20-frame strip).
  • Verified programmatically: frame 00 equals first_frame.png; every PNG equals the in-memory frame; the packed uint32 tensor matches pixels.npz.
  • Numeric QA: measured chroma centroids match the planned trajectories to ≤0.7 px; vertical centroids stay within 0.5 px; the balls have a clear edge gap at frame 9 (≈6 px) and touch at frame 10, then separate; pipe/background regions are byte-identical across all frames; vacated ball positions show zero residual chroma; dark felt marks rotate around both discs with the correct rolling sense and reverse after the rebound.
