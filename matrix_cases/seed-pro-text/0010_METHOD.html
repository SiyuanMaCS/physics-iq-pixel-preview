ball-hits-nothing · 生成方法

How the 20-frame clip was actually produced. Everything was done with local Python (numpy, scipy.ndimage, PIL) operating on pixel values; the first frame was inspected only through numeric RGB statistics (thresholds, connected components, fitted geometry) — no image viewing tool was ever used, and no external model, engine, reference image, mask or network asset is involved. generate.py is the single reproducible source (python3 generate.py, deterministic seed) and regenerates every artifact.

1. Measurement from first_frame.png (256×144)

  • Warm/saturated pixels (R−B, R−G thresholds) + connected components isolated the orange ball and the smaller red ball; near-threshold thresholds plus luminance binned the pipe, wall and table regions. Component bounding boxes, centroids and colour statistics gave the geometry:
    • ball centre (72.5, 51.5), silhouette radius 8.0 px (warm bbox x64–81, y44–59; antialiased fringe reaches ~8.6–8.7);
    • pipe mouth: near-black component x0–20, y40–67, axis y≈53;
    • static red ball at (116.5, 22.5), r≈5 — the track band under it is clear;
    • the horizontal track y≈51.5 to the right edge is uninterrupted wood; the dark top-right background polygon ends at y≤43, so the ball stays on wood.
  • Sphere shading was fitted per channel as a linear function of the camera normal n on the disk: colour(n) = AMB + KEY·n (least squares, with nz = sqrt(1−(r/R)²)), RMSE ≈ (3.3, 2.4, 6.5): AMB = (128.9, 77.0, 41.3), key rows (8.1, 1.2, 86.4), (10.0, 0.1, 59.5), (7.9, −4.1, 48.0) — light almost head-on, slightly upper-left. Residuals (σ ≈ (3, 2, 6)) drove the felt model.
  • The original ball's leading (right) edge shows a ~1–2 px soft fringe beyond the fitted radius, interpreted as exposure motion blur (BLUR = 1.8 px).
  • Wood on the track band: neutral warm grey ≈ (105–135, 100–130, 90–115), slow brightness ramp downward (~+0.3 level/px), fine near-isotropic grain with residual std 3–5 levels plus broad tonal streaks.
  • Contact shadow measured as 5–12 levels of darkening in an ellipse ~1–2 px below the contact line.

2. Motion plan

Constant real-time rate, straight horizontal path out of the horizontal pipe, constant depth → constant screen-y and constant angular size:

  • x(t) = 72.5 + 8.65789·t, y = 51.5, t = 0…19; final centre x = 237.0 (disk still fully inside the frame at t = 19).
  • No-slip clockwise roll: φ = (x − 72.5)/8.0 radians (~1.08 rad/frame, ~3.27 turns over the clip).

3. Cleaned table background (one-time inpaint of the t=0 footprint)

The ball's starting footprint must become plain wood after it leaves:

  1. Repair hole = disk r = 8.7 at (72.5, 51.5) ∪ shadow ellipse (rx 10.5, ry 3.4) centred at (72.5, 60.0). The disk radius is deliberately 8.7: a larger hole (9.5–10 was tried) bites into a real wood-knot/grain feature at x78–90, y43–49. Those dark neutral pixels (mean ≈ (93, 86, 79), R−B ≈ 14) all lie at r ≥ 8.75 from the ball centre — outside the measured silhouette — so they are background grain, not ball fringe, and must stay.
  2. "Valid wood" mask = everything outside the hole that is also non-warm (R−B < 28, R−G < 20), eroded 1 px, so neither ball nor red ball colour can bleed into the fill.
  3. Low-frequency baseline = per-channel gaussian (σ 9) of valid pixels divided by the gaussianed valid mask (normalised convolution), reproducing the broad tonal field.
  4. Fine grain = (wood − baseline) sampled from the same y-band shifted +55 px (clear wood there) and added to the baseline — grain statistics match the neighbourhood instead of looking blurred.
  5. Fill is alpha-feathered over the outer ~1.5 px of the hole. Pixels outside the hole are asserted byte-identical to the original (assert in generate.py). Boundary fill-vs-neighbour luma mismatch measured mean 5.9 / p95 13.9 / max 20.3 levels (256 scale).

Known residual: 13 dim antialiased fringe pixels at radius 8.75–9.3 from the old centre (mean RGB ≈ (129, 98, 80), partial-coverage blends) stay frozen on the background rather than widening the hole and damaging the adjacent wood knot; they are sub-pixel-coverage edge specks.

4. Ball rendering (frames 1–19)

For each frame the analytic sphere is composited onto the cleaned background:

  • Shading stays fixed to the light (convex Lambert sphere): AMB + KEY·n with the fitted constants.
  • Rolling texture is fixed in object space: the camera normal is counter-rotated by φ before evaluation, so surface markings rotate.
    • a faint tennis-seam groove, exp(−((qz − 0.45·sin(2·az + 0.9))/0.085)²), darkening up to 7% (and 3% extra desaturation in G/B);
    • broad felt mottle from summed low-frequency sines (±a few levels);
    • fine fuzz from a deterministic 3-D hash (amplitudes (6, 4.5, 9) levels), giving per-frame deterministic, surface-fixed noise.
  • Motion smear: each pixel integrates 3×3 subpixel samples × 5 temporal samples across a 1.8 px forward exposure window (sphere position and roll phase advance together). Coverage is the fraction of samples inside the disk; the pixel colour is blended by coverage against the background, using the mean colour of covered samples only (so partially covered edges keep correct saturation).
  • Contact shadow travels with the ball: soft ellipse at (cx + 0.4, y + 8.4), radii (8.0, 1.5), wood multiplied by 1 − 0.05·exp(−3.5·d²) — a narrow ~5% darkening tucked under the contact, matching the measured original.

Frame 00 is the untouched first_frame.png (asserted byte-identical after save/reload). Nothing else in the scene moves: pipe, wall, table edges, red ball and all other pixels are copied from the first frame; the only frame-to-frame changes are the ball, its shadow and its vacated footprint.

5. Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, 144×256×20), frames/00.png…19.png, inspection.png and index.html; the script additionally writes transition_check.png (enlarged key frames 0/4/9/ 14/19 plus 4× amplified consecutive-frame difference strips).

Checks performed in generate.py and interactively:

  • frame 00 PNG equals first_frame.png exactly;
  • every PNG, reloaded, packs to exactly the pixels.npz uint32 tensor;
  • background outside the repair hole is byte-identical (assert);
  • changed-pixel bbox per frame stays inside the ball/shadow envelope and travels right at 8.66 px/frame (counts ≈ 480 at frame 1 settling to ≈ 590–610 vs frame 0); the frame-19 ball remains fully inside the frame;
  • frozen residue at the old site quantified (13 dim fringe pixels, above);
  • difference strips show motion confined to the ball envelope with no background chatter.

Approximations, stated plainly: the ball is an analytic shaded disk rather than a re-projection of the original pixels; wood behind the starting disk is synthesised from surrounding statistics; the motion is ideal constant-speed no-slip rolling. These were chosen to match measured frame-0 values and physics of a ball rolling straight out of a pipe on a flat table.
