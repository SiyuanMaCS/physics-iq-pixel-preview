METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0048 attempt_01

Blue-black grabber releases a white ceramic domino; it free-falls into a blue mug of dark red-brown liquid (impact, crown, droplets, expanding ripples, settle). 20 frames, 256×144, 4 fps. Everything is local numpy/PIL/scipy pixel work driven by generate.py; no image viewing tool, no network, no external models. Frame inspection during development used numeric RGB queries and classified ASCII dumps only.

1. Moving object and background model (from first_frame.png)

Measured numerically (see OBSERVATION.md for full numbers):

  • domino hard white component: bbox x99–117, y11–52, capsule, 499 px, core ≈ (221,240,254) with a 1–4 px blue-tinted antialiased fringe (white composited over wood / navy rim);
  • mug opening ellipse: centre (119.5, 67), axes (32.5, 24);
  • liquid ellipse: centre (120, 71.5), axes (26.5, 15); liquid is opaque, mean ≈ (32,5,2); back edge of the surface at the entry column (x≈107) is y≈59;
  • genuine ceramic inside the opening is dark navy (5–16, 14–41, 41–87); pale values there are domino AA fringe, not rim highlights.

2. Clean plate (revealed background)

A work band = 4 px dilation of the hard mask, clipped to x[DX0−6, DX1+7], y[DY0−2, DY1+4]. For each band pixel a background colour is modelled once:

  • Above/outside the outer ellipse (table): per-row linear wood trend between medians sampled at x88–96 and wood-filtered pixels at x120–128, plus 0.8× a real per-row grain residual taken from x160–252, so the fill carries genuine table grain.
  • Inside the ellipse, rim rows (y≥44): mirror the symmetric visible pixel across the ellipse centre (xᵣ = round(2·119.5 − x)) and copy it with a faint dim (×[0.90,0.92,0.95]) — the revealed back rim is solid ceramic, and the matching right-arc pixel is its true colour (verified against matched visible arc pairs). Mirrored wood/AA-grey sources are routed to the wood model. At the outer ellipse boundary (q ≥ 0.96) a short wood→navy ramp provides edge AA; tiny σ=0.6 noise breaks exact symmetry.

A "plate" is the input with the occluded pixels replaced by this model. The replacement mask reclaims: the hard mask, a soft skirt (3 px over wood, 2 px in rim rows), and any remaining pale white-over-navy fringe pixels (R>85, B>140, key > 0.55) on both edges of the mask — needed because the rounded domino fringe reaches 3–4 px and otherwise leaves a bright sliver against the navy rim after the body passes.

3. Sprite extraction and motion

  • Coverage alpha: white colour-key key = clip((B−B_model−8)/(253−B_model)) against the plate model, forced to 1 inside the hard mask, zeroed outside the reclaim mask, σ=0.5 Gaussian with small alpha cut at 0.05. The sprite rect is x96–119, y10–55 (RGB cropped from the input, alpha as above).
  • Pasted with integer vertical shifts only (the fall is vertical at x≈107). Free-fall schedule dy = 0.73·n² px, n = frame−2: [0,0,0,1,3,7,12,18,26,36,47,59] for frames 0–11. A mild vertical shading ramp (1.0 → 0.85 lower in the mug) approximates interior darkening.
  • Occlusion by liquid: sprite pixels whose row is below the analytic back surface surf_y(x) of the liquid ellipse are discarded (opaque liquid). On frame 6 only, pixels within 4 px of the surface are pasted at 0.35 alpha as a one-frame refracted entry glimpse. The sprite is not drawn after frame 11 (fully submerged).

4. Splash and ripples

All effects are analytic and touch only measured liquid pixels (inside the inner ellipse, q < 0.98):

  • Rings: Gaussian ridges A·exp(−½z²)·cos(1.9z) in ellipse-normalised uv space about the impact point U0,V0 = (107−120)/26.5, (59−71.5)/15, σ = 0.075. Positive lobes mix toward lit crest (92,33,28), negative lobes toward trough (9,2,1); 1–3 concentric packets per frame with radii/amplitude hand-scheduled (frames 5–19), radii grow and amplitudes decay so the surface is essentially back to its frame-0 state by 19.
  • Entry mound/flash: compact Gaussian mound at impact on frames 5–7 plus a short-lived crest flash at the impact point.
  • Crown: a few hand-placed 1 px dark/lit ridge pixels along the near contact arc, frames 5–8.
  • Droplets: 1 px beads, dark (58,15,12) or glinted (108,48,40), on short hand-traced arcs above the surface, frames 6–10, landing back in the liquid; droplets behind the falling sprite are hidden by the later sprite composite.

5. Frame assembly and outputs

Per frame: plate → rings/mound → droplets → domino sprite (frames 3–11) → crown. Frames 0–2 are byte-for-byte copies of the input (hold, then release instant with no visible change). artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB packed tensor pixels.npz (shape 144×256×20), the 20 PNGs in frames/, the inspection.png contact sheet, and the standalone index.html player from the supplied template. transition_check.png shows labelled frames 4–13 plus an amplified |frame7−frame6| difference strip around impact.

6. Verification

Built into the end of generate.py, plus separate numeric re-checks:

  • frames 00, 01, 02 are byte-identical to first_frame.png;
  • pixels.npz tensor equals the per-frame packed (R<<16)|(G<<8)|B for all 20 frames, and each frames/NN.png decodes back to the same array;
  • changed-pixel bbox per frame stays inside x85–156, y8–95 (nothing else in the scene moves);
  • after submersion (frames 10+) there are zero bright/white residue pixels in the vacated corridor/rim band, and all inpainted corridor pixels above the rim classify as wood-coloured;
  • ripple strength decays monotonically and the liquid region is indistinguishable from frame 0 by frame 19.

Known simplifications

  • The domino fully submerges (the opaque liquid hides it from frame 11); the real object might rest partially visible — chosen because the entry column is well inside the opening and the liquid is opaque.
  • Rings are 2-D elliptical Gaussian packets rather than a fluid solve; droplets and crown pixels are hand-scheduled 1 px elements.
  • The grabber head stays in frame after release (it does not retract), matching a static mechanism simply opening its jaws.
