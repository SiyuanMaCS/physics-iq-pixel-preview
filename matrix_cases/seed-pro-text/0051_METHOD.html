METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0051

Local pixel synthesis only (Python + NumPy/OpenCV/SciPy/PIL). No external models, engines, references, or network access. first_frame.png was never opened with an image-viewing tool; all scene understanding came from numeric RGB inspection (threshold masks, connected components, PCA, ASCII colour maps). See OBSERVATION.md for the measurements.

Scene model

  • A thin wooden stick (light tan, half-width ≈ 2.8 px) runs from free tip (105.9, 53.9) into a dark slot on the black platform on the right; its axle pivot is the green cap at (210, 57.5). PCA of warm rod pixels gives its initial axis (+3.9° screen angle).
  • Four dominoes on the back/far row (B1, W2, B3, W4, ground line y 63→73) and three on the front/near row (B5, W6, B7, ground line y 80→86), with an empty gap between rows. Colours alternate black / white.
  • Action: the stick rotates clockwise in screen coordinates (its free end rises), strikes the top of W4 ("the first domino"), and the far row topples leftward in a chain W4 → B3 → W2 → B1. The near row, platform and axle never move.

Static background

For every moving pixel (four far-row domino silhouettes and the stick band, both dilated a few px) a "clean" background is built once from frame 0:

  1. A 2×-upscaled TELEA inpaint fills the wall region (y < ≈46), where smooth blue-grey is required.
  2. Over the table, each removed pixel takes the colour of its nearest surviving pixel (Euclidean distance transform), lightly Gaussian-blurred. This prevents wall or domino colours from bleeding across the thin stick band — a problem seen with plain TELEA.
  3. Inside the stick band over the table, fill colours are instead sampled along rays perpendicular to the rod axis, accepting only bright background samples, so the strip revealed as the rod lifts away is always wood-toned.
  4. Removed table pixels are re-seeded with high-frequency grain taken from a clean table patch (donor minus its local blur, randomly tiled), so fills keep the table's σ ≈ 5–7 noise.
  5. Fill is alpha-blended (feathered mask) into the untouched original, so pixels outside removal regions stay byte-identical to frame 0.

Domino sprites

Each far-row domino is extracted from frame 0 by a colour rule inside a tight search window (black: max channel < 72; white: min > 105 with low saturation, warm pixels excluded), morphological close, and largest connected component. Internal silhouette holes are filled from the sprite's own nearest visible pixels (no neighbour bleed). Ground-contact pivots and top reference points were measured numerically (see OBSERVATION.md).

A falling domino is its frame-0 RGBA sprite rotated with cv2.warpAffine about its measured ground pivot; colour is premultiplied by alpha before the bilinear warp and divided back afterwards, so transparent edges never pick up black fringes. Upright dominoes are pasted from the exact frame-0 cutout. Rotation is screen-counter-clockwise (positive cv2 angle), which swings the top leftward. A soft quadrilateral contact shadow is projected from pivot toward the rotated top, blurred, with opacity growing with the tilt. Dominoes composite back-to-front (B1, W2, B3, W4).

Stick

The rod colour is sampled directly from frame 0 along its axis at 1-px spacing (105 × 10 grid). Samples hidden behind the near row, far dominoes or the green axle are flagged as occluded (near-row mask dilated to catch its AA fringe). Occluded cells are reconstructed from a canonical across-rod profile: the per-across-position median over the fully visible span s = 6–20, plus a per-row brightness offset from that row's visible cells. This keeps the reconstructed rod a uniform light tan instead of inheriting dark slab-fringe colours. A capsule alpha gives a rounded tip and feathered edges.

Per frame the textured strip is warped with an affine map that rotates about the axle (210, 57.5) by φ, so the base end stays anchored in the slot. The stick composites behind both domino rows, matching its frame-0 occlusion.

Static elements

The near-row dominoes are composited from exact frame-0 pixels through their extracted masks, and the platform front/top, slot and green axle from an exact frame-0 cutout — so every static pixel is byte-identical to frame 0 in all 20 frames (verified numerically).

Motion schedule (4 fps, keyframed)

Hand-tuned angle keyframes, linearly defined per frame in the script: stick φ = 0 → 16° (frames 0–7, then held); W4 tilts 0 → 83° (f02–f08), B3 0 → 85° (f04–f09), W2 0 → 85° (f06–f11), B1 0 → 82° (f08–f12). Contact moments match the geometry (a falling top reaches the next slab's upper body). Frames 12–19 are the settled scene and are pixel-identical.
