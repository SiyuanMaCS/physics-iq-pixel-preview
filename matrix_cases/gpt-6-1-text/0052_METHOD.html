The continuation uses only the supplied first frame and scene prompt. Inspection was performed through Pillow/NumPy RGB arrays, printed luminance and color-category grids, numeric coordinate samples, and local contrast measurements. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed. No network, external reference, pretrained model, external rendering or physics engine, or subagent was used.

generate.py is a deterministic local Python renderer. It combines photographed ball/tool texels with a mathematical pendulum trajectory and local background reconstruction. NumPy and SciPy perform coordinate sampling, interpolation, masks and filtering; Pillow draws thin lines and writes PNGs. OpenCV's local Telea pixel inpainting fills the old ball silhouettes. The provided artifact_io.py packages the generated RGB frames into PNGs, a packed tensor, an inspection sheet and an embedded HTML player.

The image measurements and initial 20-frame plan were recorded in OBSERVATION.md before generation code was written. The three unobstructed ball centers determine a perspective line of equilibrium. The two held balls are measured about 17 pixels to the left of their equilibrium positions. Their initial angle is inferred using a 79-pixel horizontal projected pendulum length. A separate 84-pixel vertical projection and the perspective slope determine the screen arc.

At 0.05 seconds the held pair is released. The model approximates the small-angle pendulum with a 1.30-second period and exponential amplitude damping of 0.043 per second. A signed angle alternates the two active outer pairs: negative angles move the left pair; positive angles move the right pair. At the zero crossings the idealized equal-mass contact transfers pair velocity through the middle ball. The middle ball remains at equilibrium. This is an analytic two-in/two-out approximation, not a solved Hertzian collision simulation. Physical dimensions and period are assumptions inferred from the single image.

Both wires of each ball connect to fixed estimated anchors on the original chrome rails. The moving balls follow projected circular arcs. Their radii remain nearly constant, with a very small perspective adjustment. The original chrome reflections from the free balls are reused, preserving the photographed specular highlights; the obscured left pair uses the nearest free-ball texture. Area sampling of the original texels keeps the tiny bright highlights. Local rim shading and a small lower warm reflection integrate the texture into the new locations. The far balls are drawn before the near balls, and the original front post is overlaid to preserve occlusion.

The tool is extracted with a locally measured silhouette, an interior opening, and masks that exclude the balls and front post. It withdraws 24 pixels left and 22 pixels down with smooth acceleration/deceleration over 0.65 seconds. Part of it remains at the lower left. Exposed wood uses a quadratic RGB illumination fit to unobstructed table samples, with grain borrowed from the same image. The hidden portion of the charcoal base uses a measured planar color fit. Only the affected regions are reconstructed. Short hidden rear-post segments are restored with narrow shaded chrome strokes. Ball silhouettes are inpainted locally; old low-contrast wire segments are interpolated across narrow bands. Soft local shadows follow the moving balls.

Rendering uses six subpixels per image dimension. Changes are averaged onto a native-resolution clean background so static areas keep their original pixels. An early revision corrected an overly broad front-post mask and removed source-ball remnants that the first compositing pass retained. Frame 00 is assigned the exact input RGB array rather than rendered.

Files and validation

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB frames, 256×144, sampled at 0.25-second intervals. Playback is 4 fps and lasts 5 seconds.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32. Each value is packed 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: standalone player containing the first frame and lossless packed delta packets; no remote assets are needed.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: six pairs of adjacent frames with amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • .tmp/verification.json: numeric centers, tool displacement, frame-difference statistics and export verification results.

The generator verifies that every exported PNG matches both its RGB frame and the packed tensor, and that frame 00 equals the input pixel for pixel. The embedded HTML packets are also decoded independently and compared to the tensor. Numeric checks confirm that the left and right pairs alternate, the middle ball stays fixed, all centers remain inside the image, all 20 frames are distinct, and background regions outside the apparatus and tool remain unchanged. Review is numeric; the sheets were generated for inspection but were not opened through an image tool.
