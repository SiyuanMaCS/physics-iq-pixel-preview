ball-hits-duck · 生成方法

This sequence was made with deterministic local Python pixel operations. The scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. GENERATION_RULES.md supplied the output requirements. The provided artifact_io.py and player_template.html were used for output packaging. No network, other cases, external reference images, external models, physics/rendering engines, or subagents were used. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed.

Input analysis

Pillow decoded the source as 256 × 144 RGB. NumPy RGB samples, color thresholds, row-wise numerical listings, and connected-component measurements identified the dark pipe on the left, the already visible brown ball at approximately (66.1, 52.2), and the yellow duck around (195, 60). OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation implementation, including a plan for all 20 frames.

The first frame already has the ball beyond the visible pipe opening. The animation continues from that measured position. It does not rewind the ball to the pipe or change the mandatory initial pixels.

Background reconstruction and object pixels

The source photograph is the static plate. For subsequent frames, only local tabletop regions that become exposed are repaired. Robust linear RGB fits along each affected row use uncontaminated tabletop strips on either side; nearby source grain is added at reduced amplitude. This preserves the source's horizontal brightness bands. The repair regions are feathered, and the original duck pixels remain exact during the approach.

RGB thresholds and hole filling recover the original brown sphere and the duck silhouette. Edge coverage is inferred from local background-to-object color differences. The duck's eye, bill, head, and body come from source pixels, with foreground colors recovered by unpremultiplying edge coverage.

The moving ball uses a slightly elliptical sphere, with projected radii 8.25 × 7.55 pixels. Its local source texture is divided by mild analytic sphere illumination, sampled in a rolling spherical coordinate map, then lit in the fixed scene coordinates. The unseen hemisphere is a mirrored continuation of the observed local texture. This is a pixel-based approximation: the hidden side is not an independently observed or physically reconstructed surface. Four-times spatial sampling and area reduction antialias the ball silhouette.

The duck uses an affine translation and a small rotation about its measured base, with premultiplied alpha and four-times sampling. Its texture and lighting remain attached to the original sprite. Soft source-derived shadow and warm reflection residuals translate across the tabletop. Shadows are composited underneath the solid objects. These are local 2.5D operations, without a full three-dimensional scene or new camera view.

Motion and contact

The ball follows a straight projected tabletop line with slope 0.057. Initial horizontal speed is 66 pixels/second with 8 pixels/second² deceleration. Contact is defined when its center reaches x=176, at t=1.879168 seconds, bringing the ball's right edge into the duck's left side.

A one-dimensional impulse uses nominal ball/duck masses 58/35 and restitution 0.13. The incoming speed is 50.966656 pixels/second. The resulting speeds are 29.292126 for the ball and 35.917792 for the duck. Their subsequent tabletop decelerations are 23 and 27 pixels/second². Position and speed are continuous across the analytic collision except for the instantaneous impulse's speed change.

The duck moves 23.89051 pixels right and 1.36176 pixels downward along the projected path. It rocks by approximately −10.89° in the first sampled contact frame and then settles through a small damped oscillation. The ball stops at approximately (194.65280, 59.52751). The duck stays upright and on the table; the ball stays on the table behind it. Frames 17–19 are the same settled arrangement.

The frame rate is constant at 4 fps. Samples occur at t=0.00, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds. Each sample displays for 0.25 seconds, giving 5 seconds of playback. Frame 00 bypasses all synthesis and is an exact copy of the decoded input RGB.
