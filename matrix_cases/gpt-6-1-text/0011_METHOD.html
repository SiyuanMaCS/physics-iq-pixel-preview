METHOD.md

Method

This is a local pixel continuation of the supplied photograph. It uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy numerical image operations. The scene inputs are only first_frame.png and prompt.txt; the provided output helper and HTML template serialize the results. No network, external reference images, image models, external rendering engines, subagents, or image Read/View tools were used. Review consisted of numeric RGB sampling and computed checks, not visual inspection.

Source reconstruction and ball appearance

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code and records the measured setup and a full 20-frame plan. Numeric chroma segmentation identifies the orange-brown ball and the smaller stationary red ball. Fitting the larger ball's red-minus-green falloff gives center (54.6738, 51.1538), horizontal radius 7.1655, vertical radius 6.7779, and photographic edge blur sigma 1.2666 pixels.

The photographed frame supplies the fixed background, tabletop, pipe, red ball, and all existing stationary objects. Only the wood concealed by the moving ball, its cast shadow, and a faint reflection is locally reconstructed. The reconstruction interpolates neighboring wood colors within each row, adds texture copied from nearby unobstructed wood, and blends through a feathered mask. This affects 668 pixels of the static plate after quantization; the hidden grain is an estimate.

The ball is extracted by alpha dematting the source RGB against that plate and a fitted shadow. Uncertain fringe colors are extrapolated from reliable foreground samples. Bilinear subpixel placement retains the photographed lighting and soft silhouette. A small mathematical felt texture and a low-contrast curved seam vary using rotating sphere normals. These surface details are added procedurally; they are not recovered observations of an unseen part of the ball. The underlying photographed shading remains fixed relative to the lighting. This is a source-based composite with mathematical sphere detail, rather than a full 3D reconstruction.

The main shadow is a soft Gaussian opacity field fitted to source/table RGB ratios outside the ball: offset (11.2467, 0.8340), standard deviations (3.9343, 2.5928), peak opacity 0.3477. A second narrow contact shadow lies under the lower rim. The final shadow fit reduces source ratio RMSE from 0.0452 to 0.0389. Shadows darken the existing wood multiplicatively, and a weak warm reflection follows beneath the ball. A support mask derived from the source RGB prevents shadows from extending into the dark background at the table's right boundary.

Motion and timing

For frame n, t = n / 4 seconds. The projected travel distance is d = 43t − 0.6t² pixels. The ball center follows (54.6738 + d, 51.1538 + 0.08d), a straight line along the table with small constant rolling deceleration. Angular rotation equals projected distance divided by the mean ball radius, including the path slope, so translation and rotation remain coupled.
