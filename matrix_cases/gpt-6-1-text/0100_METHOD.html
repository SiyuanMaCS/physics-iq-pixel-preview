OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Inspection uses local Python and numeric RGB values only. No image Read/View tool is used.

Measurements

  • Input: RGB, 256 × 144 pixels. Pixel coordinates are x rightward, y downward.
  • Camera, table, backdrop, magnet, platform, and their existing shadows remain fixed. The wooden tabletop occupies most of the image; its RGB values are generally warm brown. The narrow strip at the top is blue gray.
  • The dark magnet assembly lies to the left of the platform, approximately x=102–140, y=46–73. A purple feature extends approximately x=85–114, y=51–107. These pixels will be preserved.
  • The black platform is a low circular disk seen obliquely. Its top is approximately x=140–214, y=25–65, with a dark side extending to y=81. Most top pixels are RGB approximately (38,45,61).
  • A conic fit to unobstructed top-face boundaries gives center (177.06,45.15) and ellipse covariance [[1344.17,176.64],[176.64,384.53]]. This corresponds to horizontal radius 36.66 px, y shear 0.1314, and vertical foreshortening 0.5185.
  • The small steel wrench occupies approximately x=141–180, y=48–68. Its visibly bright connected component has 192 pixels, bounds x=142–179, y=49–67, centroid (160.97,58.39). The shaft runs down and right from the open jaws near (148,53) to the handle tip near (177,65).
  • Typical metal top RGB is around (129,150,181); lower bevel pixels are gray and darker. The jaws contain an actual dark opening that must remain open.
  • The wrench rests close to the front-left rim. Its trailing handle tip will serve as an eccentric contact pivot, around (177,64.5), so the clockwise sweep brings the head inward across the disk instead of sweeping off its rim.

Planned synthesis

Extract the existing metal pixels with a carefully bounded fractional alpha mask, preserve their detail, reconstruct only the hidden platform pixels using the measured ellipse and local surface/side color samples, and transform the wrench in the inferred tabletop plane. Add a small contact shadow and metal bevel consistent with the fixed lighting. Use an eased monotone clockwise rotation through 92 degrees about the handle contact point. The table and magnet are stationary. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the supplied input.

METHOD.md

PixelWorld-Text method

Generated locally from first_frame.png and the scene description in prompt.txt, following GENERATION_RULES.md. All frame inspection was through local Python and numerical RGB measurements. No image Read/View tool, network resource, external model, external engine, or subagent was used. The contact sheets were generated as output artifacts; they were not visually reviewed.

Scene and motion

The photographed table, magnet assembly, backdrop, circular black platform, and fixed scene illumination are retained. The small steel wrench makes an eased, monotone 92-degree clockwise turn in the inferred platform plane. An eccentric contact point near the trailing handle at pixel (177, 64.5) keeps the head sweeping inward over the disk. This pivot is a synthesis choice. The initial rim overhang becomes smaller as the wrench turns.

The 20 samples occur at t = 0, 0.25, ..., 4.75 seconds and play for 5 seconds at 4 fps. For frame i, s = i/19 and the angular displacement is 92 × s² × (3 − 2s) degrees. Its velocity starts and ends at zero without reversing. Frame 00 is an exact copy of the supplied RGB pixels.

Pixel construction

  1. Numerically identify the blue-black top of the disk from RGB contrasts and fit an ellipse to its unobstructed boundary. The fit yields center (177.058, 45.147), horizontal radius 36.663 pixels, vertical foreshortening 0.51846, and y shear 0.13141.
  2. Bound the original wrench with a manually measured polygon and derive fractional metal coverage from its RGB contrast against the reconstructed background. The dark gap between the open jaws remains transparent. Reuse the source metal colors, including its existing gray bevel and surface variations, as a premultiplied color layer.
  3. Reconstruct the small background patch occupied by the wrench and its shadow. Robust polynomial RGB fits use 1,510 unobstructed top-face samples; local side-face fits use 464 samples. An antialiased ellipse edge separates the top and side. A small residual texture from the input prevents a perfectly flat fill. The repair is restricted to the original wrench neighborhood.
  4. Extract the original contact shadow as fractional attenuation. Rotate the wrench and this shadow with the affine map P R(θ) P⁻¹, where P describes the measured plane projection. This preserves area and the rigid planar shape while changing its projected aspect. Bilinear interpolation and four closely spaced subpixel samples stabilize edges; sampling spacing ramps up during the initial 10 degrees to avoid a sudden first-frame softening.
  5. Composite onto the fixed photograph. Apply a modest orientation-dependent metal reflection multiplier, 1 − 0.035 sin²(θ), while retaining the original texture. Round and clip to uint8 RGB. Pack each frame as uint32 0xRRGGBB.

This is deterministic image-based synthesis with analytic planar motion. The angle schedule and contact point are authored; no magnetic-field calculation or full three-dimensional dynamics simulation is claimed.

Verification and revision

An initial generation was numerically inspected and then revised to ramp the subpixel sampling at motion onset and protect all pixels left of x=140. The zero-angle reconstruction error improved to 0.01933 mean RGB code values over the full frame. The delivered frame 00 is exact regardless of this reconstruction metric.
