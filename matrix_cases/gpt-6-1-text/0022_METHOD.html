OBSERVATION.md

Observation and frame plan

Inputs: only prompt.txt and numeric RGB values read from first_frame.png with Pillow/NumPy. No image reader, viewer, image model, network, or external scene source was used.

Measurements

  • The source is 256 × 144 RGB. The camera is fixed. The image is dim, with warm brown background tones and a light, desaturated wood tabletop in the foreground.
  • The black pipe occupies approximately x = 0–29, y = 72–99. Its stationary silhouette and all background pixels will be retained.
  • The one small cool-grey ball is already on the tabletop in frame 00. Its visible surface is approximately x = 154–168, y = 73–86; estimated projected center (160.5, 79.6), radius 6.4 pixels. RGB values on its lit right side reach approximately (115, 145, 173); the left side is approximately (102, 105, 117). Soft source blur extends outside this core.
  • The existing ball/contact shadow extends left and below the core, approximately x = 150–171, y = 80–88. It will be reconstructed once to make a stationary clean tabletop plate for subsequent frames.
  • The tabletop back edge runs approximately from (0, 73) to (204, 61). Its right edge runs approximately from (204, 61) to (256, 85). The near table edge runs through approximately (130, 143), (208, 130), and (255, 120).
  • A roll toward the right parallel to the long table edge projects with slope about −0.067. The ball contact starts around (160.5, 86); its projected center therefore gently rises as x increases. It reaches the right table edge at approximately x = 245.

Motion and rendering plan

The first frame is copied byte-for-byte. The ball has already emerged from the pipe in the supplied starting image, so it continues from that position without resetting. Keep the camera, pipe, table, and background fixed. Use approximately 25 pixels/second projected rolling speed with slight friction. Couple spherical texture rotation to distance/radius. Use source-derived color and soft shaded rendering, a small attached contact shadow, and short exposure blur. After the ball crosses the right edge, allow gravity to pull it down and let it leave the right side of the frame. There are no additional objects or actions.

METHOD.md

Actual method

This is a deterministic local Python pixel animation made from first_frame.png and prompt.txt. Pillow reads and writes RGB pixels; NumPy performs compositing and packing; SciPy supplies local interpolation and Gaussian filtering. No external assets, masks, models, engines, network requests, or subagents were used. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed. The source was inspected numerically through RGB samples and luminance/color text grids.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generate.py. Numeric inspection found that the supplied first frame already contains the ball on the table near (161, 80). The continuation therefore starts there. It keeps the fixed camera, pipe, wood table, and background from the source, then moves that ball toward the right table end.

The animation uses the following pixel operations:

  1. Copy frame 00 exactly. frames/00.png is also a byte-for-byte copy of the original PNG.
  2. Reconstruct the tabletop hidden beneath the original ball and shadow by interpolating corresponding wood rows from adjacent clear areas. Add weak fine texture from nearby photographed wood and blend within a feathered local footprint. The clean plate changes 362 source pixels, within x = 148–172, y = 71–89, and stays constant throughout subsequent frames.
  3. Fit the photographed ball's RGB values to the basis (constant, horizontal normal, upward normal, front normal) over the sphere's interior. The fitted shading retains the source's dim grey surface and cool illumination. Extract small source RGB residuals for surface detail.
  4. Render a small analytic sphere with a soft edge using 4× supersampling. Rotate the source-derived residual detail about the rolling axis using Rodrigues rotation, with angle tied to distance/radius. A curved seam adds approximately two RGB levels of tennis-ball detail. Lighting stays fixed as the surface rotates. Apply a compact shadow with a cool ambient tint while the ball contacts the table.
  5. Average three subframes at offsets −0.018, 0, and +0.018 seconds to approximate a short exposure. Composite these local overlays onto the unchanged source plate. No whole-frame warp, camera motion, or background resampling occurs.

Motion is an analytic screen-space approximation, rather than a calibrated 3D simulation. The source provides neither physical dimensions nor a measured launch speed. The initial ball center is (161.2, 79.5), projected radius 6.45 pixels. While supported, x(t) = 161.2 + 25t − 0.30t². The contact path has slope −0.067, with a slight radius reduction for perspective. The measured right edge is approximated by y = 0.46153846x − 33.153846. Contact crosses that edge at x = 245.7801 and t = 3.5330 seconds. The ball then follows continuous horizontal velocity and a downward parabola with projected gravity of 200 pixels/s². It becomes partially clipped by the right frame border at frame 16, has only three changed pixels remaining at frame 17, and is out of view in frames 18–19. No new object enters the scene.

The twenty samples are at t = 0, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds. Holding each for 0.25 seconds gives 4 fps and a five-second sequence. pixels.npz contains the array named pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, packed as 0xRRGGBB. artifact_io.py exports the PNG frames, the eight-frame inspection.png sheet, and a standalone HTML player with the first frame plus lossless pixel deltas. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png, showing consecutive frames and amplified RGB differences.

Verification is numerical. All twenty PNGs have RGB mode and 256×144 dimensions and equal their tensor slices. Frame 00 equals the source at both pixel and PNG-byte levels. Every decoded HTML frame equals the tensor. Pixels outside the local reconstruction/current overlay regions equal the source. Center x increases monotonically, contact and falling trajectories meet continuously, and the final two frames are identical. Detailed positions, difference counts, and checks are saved in validation.json.

Run from this workspace with python generate.py. The process overwrites the generated artifacts deterministically and preserves the inputs.
