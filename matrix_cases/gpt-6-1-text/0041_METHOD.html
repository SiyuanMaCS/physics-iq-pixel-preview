cut-orange · 生成方法

This is a deterministic, local pixel animation of the supplied scene. It uses the original image's textures, manually measured image-space silhouettes, mathematical interpolation, and subpixel image warps. It is not a 3D simulation or a learned video generation method.

Source inspection

The only scene inputs were prompt.txt and first_frame.png. The frame was examined using Pillow/NumPy RGB arrays, coordinate samples, chromatic row spans, and numeric connected-component checks. No image Read/View tool was used. Neither the input nor the output sheets have been visually reviewed through an image tool. The scene interpretation and the original twenty-frame plan were recorded in OBSERVATION.md before writing the generator.

Pixel construction

generate.py uses Pillow, NumPy, SciPy's local numerical image operations, and the supplied artifact_io.py output helper.

  1. Measured antialiased polygons isolate the tangerine and its narrow reflective knife. The knife's leading edge slopes from the top near x=132 toward a faint tip near (120,110). The tangerine silhouette is split along the blade's cutting line into two portions.
  2. Areas of board originally hidden by the fruit and knife are filled with row-wise boundary-color interpolation and small grain variations sampled from clear board pixels in this same image. Existing texture outside the edited region stays fixed.
  3. Fruit hidden under the knife is initialized from nearby known fruit pixels, then filled using 160 local harmonic interpolation iterations. Small texture variations from the source orange are retained. This estimates the previously occluded surface; it cannot recover unseen ground-truth pixels.
  4. Premultiplied-alpha affine sampling moves the knife along its entry direction and moves the two fruit portions slightly apart. The forward slicing stroke reaches 14 projected pixels at frames 07–08, followed by smooth withdrawal. The knife leaves the frame by frame 17. The pieces end with outward translations of 3.9 and 5.5 pixels, small rotations, and damped settling. Slight vertical compression accompanies cutting pressure.
  5. Narrow shaded orange/pith strips reveal the cut edges during withdrawal. Their texture and hue come from the source fruit plus analytic shading. Local differential soft contact shadows follow the portions while preserving the supplied illumination. Warm blade reflections transition toward gray steel away from the orange, preventing orange-colored patches from traveling above the fruit.
  6. A small initial mask-edge compositing correction fades over frames 00–04. Frame 00 is explicitly copied from the supplied RGB frame so it is byte-exact. No camera transform, new props, hands, overlays, or frame interpolation are added.

The motion is an authored plausible cutting/withdrawal trajectory, not a measured physical reconstruction. Geometry and material appearance are inferred from one small source image; the new cut surfaces and hidden board are synthesized locally.

Output format

  • Twenty RGB PNG frames: frames/00.png through frames/19.png, each 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, dtype uint32, shape (144, 256, 20). Each value packs a pixel as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight selected output frames in a 1024 × 330 contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: eight selected transitions in a 1024 × 432 sheet, with amplified absolute RGB difference strips. These are diagnostic artifacts, not claims of visual review.
  • index.html: the supplied standalone player template with embedded first-frame pixels and nineteen delta packets. Playback uses a constant 250 ms interval, so twenty frame intervals last five seconds at 4 fps.
  • generate.py: reproducible generator and verification checks. Run python generate.py from this workspace.
  • .tmp/validation.json: numerical validation results.
