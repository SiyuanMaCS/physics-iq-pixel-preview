Pixel generation

generate.py loads the source RGB pixels without resizing. It fits a smooth seven-term polynomial background in a local 48-row neighborhood, using neighboring gray pixels outside the flame and the top four image rows. The fitted RGB background has residual RMS errors of approximately (1.2644, 1.0211, 1.1061) in the sampled background pixels. This is an estimate of the gray surface under the flame, rather than a new scene render.

Subtracting that estimate yields a signed RGB contribution for the existing flame and warm halo. A low-contrast threshold protects gray background grain. Lightly filtered halo pixels and the sharp source core are combined with smooth spatial feathering. The signed contribution includes the orange base's lower blue values. The original frame supplies all stationary geometry and texture.

For frames 01–19, cubic inverse sampling deforms this extracted light contribution around the fixed anchor (131.3,31). The upper flame bends more than the base, with nominal lateral shifts between -1.6 and +1.7 pixels. Height varies from 0.93 to 1.09 of the source, width from 0.94 to 1.06, and gain from 0.97 to 1.04. A small additional curvature follows the bend. The deformation and intensity values come from the deterministic, irregular schedule in OBSERVATION.md; they are intended to approximate mild flame flicker, not a combustion-fluid simulation.

Each frame is the original plus the difference between the warped and original light contribution. A gain-dependent local RGB adjustment of about one code value also affects the fixed candle rim and nearby surface. Final values are rounded and clipped to 8-bit RGB. Operations are performed directly in source RGB code values. Frame 00 bypasses all transformations and is an exact copy of the input.
