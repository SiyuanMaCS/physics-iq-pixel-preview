METHOD.md

Generation method

This is a local analytic pixel-compositing animation. It uses only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. No network, external model, external rendering/physics engine, reference image, supplied mask, or subagent was used. The first frame was inspected through local numeric RGB samples and text grids only. No image tool was used and no visual review is claimed.

generate.py preserves the input as the static photographic layer. It creates liquid layers at 4× spatial resolution using NumPy fields and Pillow polygons, then averages only their RGB residuals into the original pixels. Frame 00 bypasses rendering and is exactly the original RGB image. Coordinates and the intended event sequence were written in OBSERVATION.md before the generator was coded.

The source already shows red liquid falling from the blue outlet near the upper right of the cropped dispenser. The animation continues that trajectory with narrow time-varying stream widths, subpixel bending, red shading, and moving specular highlights. Time-dependent wetting fronts move down measured curved paths over the duck. A soft crown patch feeds a main rivulet and smaller branches. Translucent red blends preserve the duck's original surface shading and texture, and separate geometric masks protect the eye and bill.

Small deterministic droplets follow analytic ballistic trajectories with gravity. Runoff starts a shallow red pool below the duck after approximately 1.6 seconds. The pool's projected area grows approximately linearly with elapsed influx time; an irregular elliptical boundary, translucent red depth shading, moving elliptical ripples, a weak inverted duck reflection, and narrow light streaks represent the puddle on the dark baking tray. The background, woven basket, cropped glass dispenser, camera, and duck position remain fixed.

This is a plausible authored pixel continuation, not a measured or fully simulated fluid dynamics solution. Droplet paths are analytic; wetting and puddle growth are simplified geometric models. The dispenser's cropped liquid level is held constant because its free surface is outside the visible frame.

Artifacts and reproducibility

Run python generate.py in this workspace. Dependencies are local Python, NumPy, and Pillow. The output helper artifact_io.py packs the frames and builds the standalone HTML player. After saving, the source PNG is copied verbatim to frames/00.png to preserve both its pixels and its file bytes.

  • pixels.npz: pixels is a uint32 tensor of shape (144,256,20), encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty RGB PNG frames, 256×144.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: four adjacent-frame pairs, amplified RGB differences, and enlarged duck details.
  • index.html: self-contained player with play/pause, replay, scrubbing, and looping, at 4 fps.
  • verification.json: programmatic checks and frame-by-frame transition statistics.

The generator verifies every PNG against all three color channels decoded from the packed tensor, verifies exact pixel and file-byte equality of frame 00 to the input, and checks that pixels outside the liquid region remain unchanged. It independently decodes the HTML player's initial frame and all nineteen transition packets and verifies their equality to the tensor. It also reports per-frame changed-pixel counts and mean and maximum adjacent-frame RGB differences. Inspection sheets are produced for review, but were not opened with an image tool.
