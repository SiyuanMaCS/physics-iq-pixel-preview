OBSERVATION.md

Numeric observation and event plan

The scene was inspected only with local Python/Pillow/NumPy RGB measurements. No image Read/View tool was used. Coordinates below are at the source resolution, 256 × 144, with x increasing rightward and y downward.

Measured scene

  • The input is an RGB image of shape (144, 256, 3). The camera is fixed.
  • A soft gray/lavender background occupies the upper approximately 33 pixels. Typical RGB values are (173, 170, 183). Its lower boundary meets a warm brown wooden tabletop; typical table RGB values are (125, 94, 66). The original texture and exposure will be retained.
  • The transparent drinking glass is centered around x=135.5. Its mouth spans approximately x=107–164 and y=34–62. The sides taper slightly toward a thick reflective base around x=111–157, y=120–141. The surface of the water is represented by the darker elliptical region approximately x=108–163, y=55–80. White highlights occur at (120,68) and (155,68). These glass and water features must remain recognizable.
  • The wooden match enters from the upper edge around x=43 and slopes down and right. Color segmentation above the table fits the shaft centerline x=42.645 + 1.60456*y, an angle of 31.93 degrees below horizontal. The visible shaft is approximately 3.3 pixels thick measured perpendicular to that line.
  • The charred match head occupies approximately x=140–151, y=61–68, centered near (146.5,65.5). The darkest core is RGB (28,23,20). A small amber flame is visible above/alongside the head at x=140–146, y=56–61, peaking at RGB (240,187,114) at (144,60). It must not become a large unrelated fire.
  • A plausible contact point on the visible near half of the water plane is around (146,74). The initial head is already near the water. The continuation should therefore show a short approach, extinction at entry, and a longer controlled lowering/hold.
  • No hand is visible in the initial frame; the match's holder remains beyond the upper boundary.

Motion and physics

Use one rigid match, with its shaft gradually steepening while its head moves down into the glass. The shaft continues to pass through the opening near the left/central rim instead of passing through the sidewall. Keep the head near x=144–147, lowering from y=65.5 to about y=94. The motion eases into a stationary final hold. The contact plane is y≈74 at the head's location. The flame flickers on the approach, collapses as the head reaches water, and stays extinguished. A few faint bubbles and low-amplitude elliptical ripples follow contact, then dissipate. The submerged wood receives a restrained apparent displacement and softened contrast through the water. The glass, camera, tabletop, and background remain fixed.

Intended local pixel method

Recover the narrow original match/flame footprint with local inpainting, extract the original shaft and charred-head texture with a geometric matte, and move that texture using rigid coordinate transforms. Use analytic soft flame fields and small water-surface displacement/ripple fields. Render antialiased local changes and preserve the initial pixels outside the affected regions. Save and check the required packed RGB tensor, PNG sequence, contact sheets, and standalone HTML player. This is a procedural pixel continuation, not a learned video model or a physics-engine rendering.

METHOD.md

Method and verification

Completed a 20-frame RGB continuation at 256 × 144, 4 fps, with a five-second playback duration. The match moves downward, progressively steepens, enters the water between frames 04 and 05, loses its small flame, and settles submerged. The camera and the photographic setup stay fixed.

Actual generation method

generate.py uses local Python pixel operations only: Pillow, NumPy, SciPy coordinate sampling, and OpenCV's local Telea inpaint. It does not use an AI model, network access, reference images, a video generator, an external rendering/physics engine, or subagents. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and the action in prompt.txt. artifact_io.py and player_template.html are supplied output-format helpers.

  1. Measurement. Local numeric RGB sampling identifies the upper gray wall, brown tabletop, transparent glass, water ellipse, diagonal wooden match, tiny amber flame, and dark burned head. A fit to the wooden shaft above the table gives x = 42.64488636 + 1.60456378*y. The measured scene and a plan for every frame were written to OBSERVATION.md before creating the generator.
  2. Photographic background. A geometric soft matte covers the shaft and rounded burned head, and a warm-color selection isolates the small flame. A narrow 794-pixel footprint is restored from immediate neighbors with local inpainting. In the upper gray background, straight interpolation across the shaft preserves its smooth shading. All pixels outside this footprint remain the original background until covered by the moving match or affected by a water ripple.
  3. Match motion. The original wooden and charred-head RGB texture is recovered against the restored background, then transformed as a rigid object. Its apparent anchor descends from y=65.5 to y=94.0 while shifting left by 3.7 pixels. The shaft steepens from 31.93° to 59.47° and continues through the glass opening. Inverse mapping and a 3× sampling grid antialias the narrow shaft. The visible match remains connected to the upper frame boundary.
  4. Water and glass. At the inferred local water-contact plane y=74, a continuous inverse warp compresses the apparent submerged vertical extent by a factor of 0.88 and introduces at most 0.72 pixels of lateral displacement. Submerged contrast is attenuated by 18%. The original near rim and bright glass reflections partially overlay the relocated match. These are approximate photographic refraction and transmission effects, not a full ray tracer.
  5. Flame and contact. The initial flame texture is warped independently of the shaft so that it continues to rise upward. Small width, height, and lean changes provide flicker. A subpixel Gaussian amber emission core keeps the source's tiny bright center legible after interpolation. The flame shortens near water contact and is fully absent starting at frame 05. Two faint subpixel air bubbles briefly rise and disappear. Small elliptical traveling ripples locally displace and shade the original water pixels, with exponential decay and an explicit fade to still water. The final frames hold the extinguished match stationary.
  6. Frame 00. The first frame is copied directly from the source RGB array, bypassing every reconstruction and rendering step.

The motion is an authored continuous-looking trajectory sampled at 4 fps. The contact plane and depth effects are inferred from the single frame; this is a procedural pixel animation with approximate physics.

Numeric inspection and revision

No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed. Inspection consisted of local RGB measurements, masks computed within the generator, motion-coordinate checks, warm-pixel counts near the flame, charred-head location measurements, and per-frame pixel differences.

A revision added a restrained subpixel amber emission core and adjusted the final pre-contact flame strength. The numeric check then retained warm flame pixels in frames 01–04 and found none near the tip in frames 05–06. The measured peak flame RGB at frame 01 was (217,166,106), compared with the original bright core (240,187,114). The change keeps the fire small while making extinction coincide with contact.

The mean absolute RGB differences between consecutive frames decrease as the match slows. Frames 18 and 19 are identical, consistent with the final stationary hold. The entire region at x≥174 remains exactly the source throughout all 20 frames.
