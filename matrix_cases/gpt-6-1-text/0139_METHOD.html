Intended construction

Use the first photograph as an immutable background plate. Local pixel masks will identify the measured paper facets for progressive scorch and a very small deformation. Procedural, time-dependent flame density fields will create tapered, curved orange/yellow tongues rooted to the burning paper. White smoke will use a continuously integrated two-dimensional buoyant density/advection field with local vortices and diffusion; its density will be composited translucently over the photograph. No external models, engines, assets, or network sources will be used. Verify saved PNGs numerically against the packed RGB tensor, and save the required inspection and transition contact sheets without claiming image-tool visual review.

METHOD.md

Generation method

This is a local, deterministic pixel animation of the provided photograph. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The generation rules were read before work, and OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator. No network, additional scene references, external masks, learned models, external rendering or physics engines, or subagents were used.

Scene reconstruction and motion

The source image was read as RGB data with Pillow. NumPy patch means, color thresholds, and SciPy connected-component coordinate statistics identified the paper's creases and approximate boundaries. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed.

Every generated frame starts with the original photographic background. Locally drawn polygon masks follow the paper's upper face and neighbouring shaded fold. An irregular burn front advances down the upper face over the five-second clip. It changes the existing paper texture through tan scorch, dark charcoal, a little ash shading, and tiny orange edge embers. The burn texture is fixed in paper coordinates. A small inverse-coordinate warp curls the charred ridge by at most 1.1 pixels; a normalized Gaussian estimate from surrounding original pixels fills the tiny exposed rim. Lower white folds remain recognizable, and the paper stays on the board.

Fire is made from three continuously changing tapered tongues attached to the burning fold. A fourth, smaller tongue appears farther down the fold late in the clip. Analytic centerlines bend with time, while flame height, width, boundary ripples, and radiance fluctuate at several frequencies. Hot cores are yellow and the edges are orange. The fields are evaluated at 768 x 432, alpha-composited, and downsampled to 256 x 144. A small blurred glow and very faint local amber illumination/reflection affect pixels near the paper and on the glass.

White smoke is a two-dimensional density field, also at 768 x 432. It is integrated continuously at 48 steps per second with semi-Lagrangian backtracing, bilinear sampling, diffusion, and gradual dissipation. Density enters near the hot paper/flame, with a narrower emission thread at the crease. A prescribed upward velocity, leftward drift, multiscale stream-function perturbations, and rising counter-rotating eddies carry the density into rolling wisps. This is a kinematic, buoyancy-inspired smoke approximation; it does not solve the full Navier–Stokes equations or combustion chemistry. Smooth exponential optical-depth compositing gives the smoke a soft maximum opacity of 0.66 and keeps the background visible. The final refinement replaced a hard opacity cutoff with this smooth response.

The camera, background, and board geometry do not move. All random texture uses the fixed NumPy seed 139. Frame 00 bypasses all effects and copies the source RGB array exactly.

Timing and output

There are 20 RGB frames at 256 x 144, played at 4 fps. Samples correspond to physical times 0.00, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds, each displayed for 0.25 seconds, yielding a five-second clip. The standalone player's elapsed-time label counts each displayed interval and ends at 5.00 seconds.

artifact_io.py writes:

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20), with each pixel packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB PNGs.
  • inspection.png: eight selected output frames in a contact sheet.
  • index.html: a self-contained 4 fps canvas player with embedded initial RGB data and lossless frame deltas, playback, seek, replay, and loop controls.

generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, showing six adjacent-frame pairs alongside amplified absolute RGB differences, and validation.json, recording numeric frame diagnostics and verification results. These sheets are review artifacts, not evidence of image-tool visual inspection.

Verification

The generator checks the frame count, resolution, RGB dtype, tensor shape and dtype, and packed color range. It verifies frame 00 against the original RGB array, then decodes every tensor slice and compares it exactly with the corresponding saved PNG. It also independently decodes the HTML player's initial frame and all 19 delta packets and compares all 20 reconstructed frames with the tensor.

Numeric transition diagnostics track changed-pixel counts, per-frame and adjacent-frame RGB differences, smoke opacity/support, flame area, and progressive char area. Exact array comparisons check that x=0–144, x=215–255, and y=115–143 remain identical to the source throughout the sequence. Final values and boolean verification results are in validation.json.

Reproduce all generated artifacts by running python generate.py in this workspace with Python, NumPy, SciPy, and Pillow available. No downloads are required.
