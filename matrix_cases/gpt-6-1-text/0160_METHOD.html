METHOD.md

Generation method

The five-ball Newton's cradle is animated for 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, sampled at 4 fps. The camera, tabletop, cradle base, background, and support structure remain fixed. A blue-black grabber opens and retracts, releasing the left end sphere. The left sphere reaches the touching row, transfers motion to the right end, and the end spheres alternate swings with gradually decreasing amplitude.

Source and inspection

Only first_frame.png and prompt.txt provided scene information. GENERATION_RULES.md, artifact_io.py, and player_template.html supplied local task/output instructions and serialization helpers. No other cases, references, videos, masks, scores, network resources, AI models, subagents, external physics engines, or 3D rendering engines were used.

The source was loaded with Pillow as a numeric RGB array. Dimensions, RGB samples, luminance character grids, chroma thresholds, and horizontal contrast residuals were used to estimate sphere centers, tool silhouettes, and stand geometry. OBSERVATION.md and its preliminary 20-frame event plan were written before the generator. All subsequent inspection was numeric. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed.

Pixel construction

  1. Frame 00 is the unmodified source RGB array. The provided output serializer also reproduces its PNG exactly in this environment.
  2. The grabber's two photographed parts are extracted with measured polygon boundaries and RGB/chroma thresholds. Their source pixels are composited with small opposing rotations for opening and a smooth translation toward the lower left. The tool fully leaves the frame at about one second.
  3. The source tool and held sphere are removed from the background. Exposed wood is reconstructed from a robust local polynomial fit to visible wood colors, with fine texture resampled from unobstructed wood in the same image. The far end sphere is removed when it moves using OpenCV's local Telea pixel inpainting. The narrow rear upright it previously occluded is extended from a source cross-section, blending the pole alone into the restored background.
  4. Moving sphere appearances are resampled from photographed chrome textures. The left sphere's originally jaw-occluded portion is filled with texture from the neighboring unobstructed sphere. Four-times supersampling provides boundary coverage. Small reflection shifts, directional brightness changes, and rim darkening are computed from analytic sphere normals; this is pixel shading, not a 3D engine.
  5. Suspension lines are drawn by analytic subpixel distance coverage between inferred pairs of top-rail anchors and each end sphere. Shallow, soft shadows are drawn using Gaussian profiles. Original rail and front-upright pixels are restored after the moving sphere composites so the spheres pass behind the foreground supports.
  6. The middle three spheres retain their source appearance. Their inner four-pixel-radius regions are verified pixel-identical in every output frame.

Motion

The active end follows θ'' = -(9.81/0.335) sin(θ) - 0.025 θ'. The initial angle is -0.44 rad and release occurs at 0.025 seconds. The 0.335 m pendulum timescale is an assumed parameter, since an absolute physical scale cannot be uniquely recovered from a single frame. It gives approximately 0.59 seconds for each outgoing-and-returning end swing.

Fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration uses steps no larger than 0.0005 seconds. Zero-crossing times are interpolated. At contact, the angular velocity is transferred to the opposite end and multiplied by 0.983. Only one end sphere is displaced at a time; the central three stay at their rest centers. Eight contacts occur within the sampled interval. This idealized momentum-transfer model omits the very short individual compressions and rebounds of the middle balls.

Sphere positions use a perspective-compatible two-dimensional projection:

  • dx = H sin(θ), with H fitted to the initial held sphere's horizontal displacement.
  • dy = s dx + V (cos(θ) - 1), where s is the measured slope of the ball row and V is fitted to the source's initial vertical displacement.
  • Sphere radius varies slightly with projected displacement to approximate changing depth.

The first contact occurs at 0.319261 seconds. The final sample at 4.75 seconds shows the left sphere returning after its last outward reversal. Motion continues beyond the five-second clip.

Validation passed for all 20 PNGs against the packed tensor and generated arrays. Every embedded HTML frame was decoded and compared with the tensor. Frame 00 matches the source exactly. Designated fixed background regions and the three middle sphere cores match the source in every frame. Five sphere centers are represented in every state; the active end always lies on the outward side of its resting position. Contact speed decreases monotonically. Numeric source-to-output and successive-frame differences were examined for 00/01/02 and the eight inspection sample times, with a complete transition check across all 19 frame boundaries.

Limits

This is a source-pixel reconstruction with an idealized pendulum/contact model. Occluded wood, base details, and chrome reflections are approximate. The paired suspension anchors and physical scale are inferred. Some end-ball samples are partly hidden behind the front uprights. At 4 fps the brief impacts occur between samples and are represented by the resulting outgoing sphere motion. Numeric checks establish consistency and stationary geometry, but do not establish perceptual realism.
