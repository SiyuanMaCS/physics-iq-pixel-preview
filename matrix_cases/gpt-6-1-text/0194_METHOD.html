METHOD.md

Method

This is a local pixel-based continuation of the supplied photograph. generate.py uses Pillow, NumPy, SciPy numerical morphology, and OpenCV image transforms. It does not use external scene assets, generative models, a 3D renderer, or a physical simulation engine.

The first image was inspected through numeric RGB samples, threshold component measurements, and character maps printed by local Python. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed.

The kettlebell, green sheet, and existing narrow top holders are extracted from the source using color/luminance masks with a fractional boundary ring. Premultiplied alpha compositing preserves the photographed object texture. A scanline interpolation between neighboring gray pixels reconstructs the small background areas revealed by motion. Green reflections under the original sheet and the original weight's diffuse shadow are reduced locally.

A prescribed 20-frame motion curve lowers both objects into contact in frame 04, at one second. The kettlebell moves vertically about 26 pixels overall. Its first 7 pixels approach the cushion; the remaining 19 pixels represent compression. The original left fabric is remapped through the inverse of a monotone displacement field. The center sinks, adjacent folds bend, and the lower seam remains fixed. Soft bowl/contact shading and a fractional front cloth occlusion help connect the weight to the fabric. A small overshoot decays before the resting frames.

The green paper first lowers vertically by 16 pixels. After contact, a perspective homography moves its original quadrilateral into a shallow trapezoid on the right cushion, around x=166–202 and y=74–88. The existing paper RGB texture is reused, including local interpolation over the area originally hidden by the holder. The right cushion is not deformed; only a small contact shadow is applied. The initial narrow dark and blue holders extend with the placement, release after frame 08, and withdraw above the frame by frame 13. Their pixels come from the original holder strips.

The motion and deformation are analytic, hand-specified approximations inferred from the prompt and numerically measured scene. They are not a calibrated simulation of a 30-pound weight or a measured pillow stiffness.

Outputs:

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB PNGs, each 256 × 144, displayed at 4 fps for 5 seconds.
  • pixels.npz: pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32. Each element packs RGB as 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: a standalone player embedding frame 00 and lossless pixel delta packets. No server or external assets are required.
  • inspection.png: a contact sheet of selected frames.
  • transition_check.png: six pairs of successive frames with amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • OBSERVATION.md: numeric scene observations and the event plan, written before generation code.
  • .tmp/motion_report.json and .tmp/verification.json: generated motion parameters and numeric artifact checks.

Verification confirms frame 00 is pixel-for-pixel identical to first_frame.png, all 20 PNGs exactly reproduce the packed tensor, dimensions and RGB types are correct, the bottom 19 rows stay exactly fixed, and the final settled frames are identical. The embedded player data is also decoded locally and compared with the tensor. Inspection and transition images were generated as review artifacts; they were not opened through an image tool.
