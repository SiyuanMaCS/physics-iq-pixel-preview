+def reconstruct_table():
+    """Harmonic fill of a numeric ball/shadow region plus source wood micrograin."""
+    rb = np.sqrt(((XX - CX) / 12.7)**2 + ((YY - CY) / 10.3)**2)
+    rs = np.sqrt(((XX - 110.2) / 12.6)**2 + ((YY - 56.7) / 4.9)**2)
+    radius = np.minimum(rb, rs)
+    feather = np.clip((1.08 - radius) / 0.14, 0.0, 1.0)

+    indices[hole] = np.arange(len(locations))

+    rhs = np.zeros((len(locations), 3))
+    for row, (y, x) in enumerate(locations):

Planned pixel method

Use the supplied photograph as a locked background. Remove only the initial ball and its shadow using neighboring wood pixels and a local smooth interpolation; preserve the rest of the source exactly. Extract the ball's photographic appearance numerically, with an antialiased soft boundary. Translate that body along the measured trajectory, carry its contact/cast shadow, and rotate its subtle surface detail with the rolling displacement while keeping the lighting fixed. At contact use a small, brief deformation against a numeric kettlebell silhouette, then restore the round body. METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is a local, image-based pixel continuation, generated by generate.py using Pillow, NumPy, and SciPy. The only scene inputs were prompt.txt and first_frame.png. No network, external references, pretrained models, external masks, rendering engines, or subagents were used. The helper artifact_io.py only writes the already generated frames, packed tensor, contact sheet, and standalone HTML player.

Inspection and planning

The source was inspected through Python RGB arrays, numeric pixel samples, blue-minus-red thresholds, and luminance character grids. No image Read/View tool, image display, or browser screenshot was used. OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator, and contains measured object locations and the twenty-frame plan. inspection.png and transition_check.png are generated diagnostics; their creation does not constitute visual review.

The source already places the grey tennis ball outside the pipe, near (119.2, 51.9). The continuation preserves that starting state exactly and continues its rightward roll. The black pipe, wood, yellow duck, and heavy kettlebell use the original photographic pixels throughout.

Background and photographic ball layer

  1. Two numerically defined, feathered elliptical regions cover the initial ball and its lower-left shadow. A discrete harmonic interpolation fills those pixels from the surrounding wood. Half-strength fine wood grain, copied from a clean nearby part of the same image, restores small texture variations inside that fill. The region is local; the rest of the photograph remains unchanged.
  2. Subtracting this reconstructed table from the original gives an additive photographic residual. This layer contains the source ball's body, soft boundary, lighting, and cast/contact shadow. Translating this residual avoids moving a rectangular patch of wood with the ball.
  3. A soft, elliptical body weight corrects for small differences in the table RGB at the new position, so the ball retains its original grey/blue-lit color. Broad shading is inherited from the source image.
  4. Fine surface detail is isolated with a Gaussian high-pass filter. Its rolling rotation is sampled with analytic sphere normals and an angle proportional to rolling displacement divided by the ball radius. The unseen hemisphere reuses mirrored source detail. This changes only subtle texture; it is not a reconstruction of unseen photographic content.
  5. The moving residual is sampled at four times the output resolution and averaged back to 256 × 144. Five temporal samples across a 12 ms shutter produce mild motion blur. Only the residual is supersampled; the stationary source table is not resampled. Original dark kettlebell pixels are restored after compositing to preserve the foreground boundary at contact.

Motion and collision

An explicit one-dimensional trajectory keeps the ball on the tabletop, with screen y=51.9. Its initial screen speed is 103 px/s. It reaches the curved left side of the kettlebell at 0.50 s. At that sampled contact, the body compresses horizontally by 6.5% and expands vertically by 2.5%; the center advances about 0.52 pixels to retain side contact.

After contact, a coefficient of restitution of 0.42 gives a leftward speed of 43.26 px/s. Constant rolling deceleration of 26 px/s² brings the ball to rest at x=134.711, t=2.164 s. Frames 09–19 hold that settled state exactly. The 30 lb kettlebell remains planted, consistent with its large mass relative to a tennis ball and the frictional tabletop. The duck is outside the path.

This is a manually calibrated screen-space physical approximation, not a measured three-dimensional dynamics simulation. The input does not reveal the hidden pipe transit, the back of the ball, or metric camera calibration. The continuation therefore starts from the ball position actually present in frame 00 and retains the visible setup.
