+def harmonic(image, mask, iterations=750):

+            out[...,c][mask] = smooth[mask]
+    return out

METHOD.md

Method

This continuation uses only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. It was made with local Python pixel operations (Pillow, NumPy, and SciPy), not a trained image/video model, a remote service, a renderer, or a physics engine. The frame was inspected using numeric RGB values, chroma masks, and component coordinates. No image Read/View tool or visual review was used.

Source reconstruction

The photographed pipes, table, lighting, and framing are retained as a fixed background. A union of two local elliptical regions removes the initial balls and their cast shadows. Jacobi harmonic interpolation estimates the table under those regions; a small high-frequency residual copied from the same photographed table 24 pixels below supplies local grain. All source pixels outside the cleanup and subsequent ball/shadow footprints remain exact.

Each sphere is extracted with a soft elliptical alpha estimate. Its edge color is unpremultiplied against an estimated shadowed table. A least-squares fit to an ambient/normal lighting basis separates the photographed shading from small surface color variations. The extracted surface residual is sampled through rotating sphere normals; the unobserved hemisphere uses a reflected extension of the observed texture. A faint analytic undulating seam and deterministic felt grain supplement the photographed detail. These are reconstructed sphere surfaces rather than newly observed details of the source.

The shadows are estimated from the source after separately interpolating away the balls. Their smooth multiplicative darkening fields translate and scale with the spheres. Ball rendering uses nine subpixel samples and a soft edge to match the low-resolution photographed silhouette. Frame 00 bypasses all these operations and is copied directly from the supplied RGB image.

Motion

The initial ball centers are (76.2, 49.9) and (149.1, 58.55). A projective map along their connecting tabletop track uses the initial apparent-radius ratio 7.55 / 8.95. The scalar track coordinate represents normalized physical distance; both balls have equal and opposite physical speeds, so perspective accounts for the different pixel displacements.

Continuous, piecewise analytic motion is evaluated at t = 0, 0.25, …, 4.75 s. Both incoming velocities decrease under equal rolling resistance of 0.035 in normalized track units per second squared. A root solve places tangent contact at 2.00 s, frame 08, accounting for the measured projected ellipticity. At contact, equal masses exchange opposite velocities with coefficient of restitution 0.84. The spheres then roll back toward their starting sides while slowing under the same resistance. Sphere rotation is signed traveled distance divided by radius, so it reverses consistently with the rebound.

The projective map, sphere shape, illumination, and unseen surface texture are local approximations. There is no claim of a calibrated reconstruction of the original camera or of visual inspection of the result.
