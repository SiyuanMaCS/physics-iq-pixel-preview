OBSERVATION.md

Observation from numeric RGB inspection

The input is an RGB image of 256 × 144 pixels. No image Read/View tool was used. Measurements below come from Python/Pillow/NumPy RGB values, row intervals, and text color classifications.

  • The camera is fixed. A warm, dim surrounding wall contains a broad, cool illuminated pool: its upper boundary is near (118, 20), its bright region extends toward (187, 90), and its outer right boundary is near x=232 at y=105. Preserve all of this illumination.
  • The upright cyan-blue block has a small apparent width. Blue pixels start at y=106, span x=116–124 through y=120, and x=116–125 through y=131. Its lower edge is around y=133–134. A dark top surface occupies approximately x=117–124, y=105–107. The left surface is dimmer than the cyan front/right surface, with a bright longitudinal highlight near x=120. Representative mid-height colors are (55,181,250), (95,229,251), and (70,216,253).
  • The platform top is a dark elliptical disk centered approximately (119,134), with radii about (27,11). It is partly cropped by the lower image edge. Its top surface is neutral charcoal, around RGB 35–48; the outer front rim is much darker. The blue pixels below y=134, fading toward y=142, are treated as a reflection on the disk, not additional block height.
  • The wall shadow is an upright dark silhouette approximately x=126–139, y=65–104. Its left boundary moves slightly right with height down the image. Its central RGB values are approximately (20,17,15), whereas nearby illuminated wall values are approximately (150,148,161). The top and lateral boundaries have a short penumbra. The dark diagonal bands across y=100–128 belong to the stationary surrounding setup/base shadow and are preserved, except where they overlap the moving upright silhouette.
  • The spotlight does not move. Its direction is inferred from the enlarged upward/rightward shadow. An image-space projection with magnification about 1.65 and an effective emitter near (102,172) maps the block toward the measured wall shadow. This is an approximate image-space reconstruction, not a recovered camera or lamp calibration.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence uses only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. The first image was inspected through local Python RGB samples, numeric row intervals, and text color classifications. No image Read/View tool, network access, reference imagery, external model, rendering engine, or subagent was used. The observations and twenty-frame motion plan were written in OBSERVATION.md before the generation code.

The implementation is a source-pixel, 2.5-D reconstruction, not a fully recovered three-dimensional scene. A mathematical rectangular footprint rotates at a constant 24 degrees per second clockwise. Its long and short dimensions are 13 and 3.5 in pixel-scaled coordinates. The upright block's initial projected height is about 27 pixels. The initial orientation is 65 degrees, and it decreases six degrees per frame to -49 degrees. A fixed vertical pivot and approximate depth projection keep the block upright on the disk.

The measured block and reflection are removed from the background with narrow scanline interpolation using neighboring RGB values. The source-minus-background RGB residual retains the input's texture, cyan highlights, dark cap, and edge softness. Inverse piecewise warps deform the two visible faces according to the rotating rectangle, including a small depth-dependent vertical displacement. At a frontal pose, the narrow face vanishes and the opposite end appears. That unobserved end uses an inferred mirrored copy of the observed end texture with slightly lower brightness. Gentle analytic light factors alter the original face RGB values. The measured top cap is carried by the same pixel deformation. The body and dim disk reflection overlap with complementary weights at the contact area.

The wall's upright shadow is isolated with the same local background-repair approach. Its source RGB residual is warped according to the evolving rectangular breadth and projected top depth. The approximate shadow magnification is 1.65; its position is fitted to the input. Original penumbra and ambient shadow color come from the input pixels. This image-space shadow approximation is synchronized with the cuboid orientation; it does not perform ray tracing or reconstruct hidden geometry. The original broad setup shadow and the spotlight's illuminated pool remain stationary.

The disk's circular outline and broad shading stay fixed. A bounded high-frequency residual from its source texture rotates clockwise in normalized elliptical coordinates. Thus the disk can turn without rotating the stationary illumination or adding an artificial marker. The input's blue reflection changes with the block's face proportions.

All transforms use local NumPy calculations and OpenCV bilinear pixel resampling. SciPy supplies a small Gaussian filter for the disk detail extraction. Pillow writes RGB PNGs and the inspection artifacts. artifact_io.py only packs and exports the completed frames and creates the standalone HTML player. Running python generate.py reproduces the artifacts. Temporary inspection work used only the workspace; no shared temporary directory was used.

Revision and verification

Numeric inspection of the first generated sequence exposed background antialiasing being copied into an internal face junction after the frontal pose. The final mapping samples interior face texels at that junction and applies explicit subpixel coverage at the newly revealed outside edge. The transition detail sheet was also enlarged so that its crops are complete. These revisions were followed by regeneration and validation.

  • There are exactly 20 RGB PNG frames, each 256 × 144, sampled at 4 fps for a five-second playback interval.
  • Frame 00 has exactly the same RGB pixels as the input. Its PNG file is also copied byte for byte from first_frame.png.
  • pixels.npz contains a pixels array of shape (144,256,20) and dtype uint32. Each value is 0xRRGGBB, with no alpha channel.
  • Every PNG was decoded and compared exactly with its corresponding packed tensor slice.
  • All twenty frames decoded independently from the HTML player's initial frame and delta packets matched the tensor. Its timer is 250 milliseconds.
  • Mean absolute RGB change between adjacent frames ranges from about 0.037 to 0.332 on the 0–255 scale. The first transition is about 0.332. No transition is identical to its predecessor.
  • The RGB region above y=60 is unchanged in every frame. Numeric samples also confirm stationary outer wall and spotlight pixels, a centered upright blue region, and a widening then narrowing enlarged shadow.

validation.json records the numeric checks. inspection.png is an eight-frame contact sheet. transition_check.png contains frames 00–03, amplified adjacent differences, and enlarged block/shadow pixel crops. These files were generated as review artifacts. They were not viewed with an image tool; review claims are limited to numeric verification and text classifications.
