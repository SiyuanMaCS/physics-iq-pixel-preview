METHOD.md

Method

This is a deterministic local pixel animation of the supplied setup. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt; GENERATION_RULES.md defined the output requirements. Source inspection and output review used Python and numeric RGB measurements exclusively. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed. There were no network calls, other-case references, external models, external rendering or simulation engines, or subagents.

Reconstruction and motion

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV for local image processing. It keeps the source mug, handle, wood, and fixed camera view. Manually measured row masks isolate the blue-black jaws. A small ceramic region is separated into premultiplied RGB and alpha by estimating blue-channel contrast against the reconstructed background. This retains the actual source ceramic shading and highlights instead of replacing the piece with a flat rectangle.

The uncovered areas are reconstructed locally. OpenCV Telea inpainting initializes the small holes. Same-row pixel interpolation restores the wood and the inner mug wall/liquid. A measured analytic upper-rim contour corrects the two rows where ordinary inpainting mixed wood color into the blue rim. The reconstruction cannot recover unseen original texture; it supplies a consistent local estimate from neighboring source pixels.

The grabber jaws separate laterally and withdraw through the upper frame edge. The ceramic follows a prescribed accelerating downward path, then continues sinking, with a small rotation and lateral drift. An opaque projected liquid plane clips its submerged portion, and a compact meniscus covers the immersion boundary. The object is fully submerged by frame 07. The mug body, handle, and table remain fixed.

The surface effect is computed from radial travelling waves, damped reflected waves, and a small basin oscillation. Their perspective projection is elliptical. Local height gradients perturb the liquid pixels by subpixel remapping and shaded brown highlights. The wave field is masked and feathered inside the original liquid region. A supersampled small splash crown and six deterministic droplets add entry motion. The droplets follow gravity-like parabolic paths and return to the liquid. No foam, additional props, or camera motion are added.

These are authored, physics-inspired trajectories and pixel effects. They are not a calibrated fluid simulation or a reconstruction of unavailable future frames. Each frame is a fixed-rate sample, with no camera shake or changing frame dimensions.

Timing and revisions

The 20 frame samples are at t=0.00, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds and each displays for 0.25 seconds: 4 fps, 5 seconds total. Frames 00–01 hold the original view. The jaws begin opening at frame 02; release is at frame 03. Entry begins at frame 04, the splash peaks around frame 05, and the last ceramic section submerges during frame 06. Ripples decay through frame 17. Frames 18–19 hold the settled surface.

Numeric review led to two content revisions: reconstructing the hidden rim with its measured oval boundary, and extending low-amplitude surface motion so the settling sequence reaches the planned duration. An OpenCV remapping dtype error was corrected before outputs were produced. All final artifacts were regenerated together after the revisions.
