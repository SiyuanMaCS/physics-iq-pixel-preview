OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation

The only scene inputs are prompt.txt and the 256 × 144 RGB first_frame.png. Inspection used local Pillow/NumPy RGB samples, brightness character tables, and color-threshold coordinates. No image Read/View tool was used.

The scene is a fixed perspective view of a five-ball Newton's cradle on a brown wooden table. The pale, softly varying background occupies the upper image; the table boundary slopes from approximately y=47 at x=55 to y=63 at x=255. The black rectangular cradle base projects approximately through (57,91), (144,99), (142,143), (54,119). Chrome uprights run near (48,20)–(58,119), (78,9)–(91,124), (135,25)–(140,140), and (150,13)–(148,126). The crossbars and all original static pixels will remain fixed.

The blue-black grabber enters from the left. Its upper handle is visible near x=0–18, y=50–72; its working jaws/other handle occupy x=0–83, y=72–99. Saturated blue samples include (60,78)=(0,140,222) and (40,90)=(13,125,208). Chrome-ball highlights are substantially brighter and approximately neutral: (80,90)=(247,252,253). The two held balls are estimated near (74,90) and (85,92), with apparent radius about 6 px. The three resting balls are near (110,96), (122,98), and (134,100). Thus the equilibrium row has roughly 11.8 px spacing and 2 px vertical offset. The held pair is displaced about 12–13 px left of equilibrium. Thin suspension lines connect the balls to the two upper crossbars, but many are subpixel and low contrast.

These estimates are constrained by numeric pixels, rather than an image display. The held balls overlap the tool, and their exact silhouettes and highlights cannot be measured as cleanly as those of the resting balls.

Continuation model

Release at approximately 0.10 seconds. The grabber opens slightly and withdraws to the left over the next second. The two left balls fall along pendulum arcs, collide around 0.50 seconds, and transfer motion to the two right balls. The center ball stays essentially stationary. The two end pairs alternate excursions, with weak energy loss at successive impacts. A nominal 1.60-second pendulum period gives readable real-time motion at four frames per second. Wire endpoints move with their balls; frame, table, lighting, and camera remain fixed. Original sphere texture will be reused where possible, with local mathematical shading and local background reconstruction in revealed areas.

METHOD.md

Method and output

This is a local pixel-based continuation of first_frame.png, guided only by prompt.txt. Scene geometry was measured through RGB values, color thresholds, coordinate lists, and luminance character tables using Python. No image Read/View tool, network, reference image, external mask, trained AI model, physics engine, renderer, or subagent was used. The generated inspection sheets were saved, but were not visually viewed.

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy local array filters, and OpenCV image operations. It produces a fixed-camera photograph continuation rather than a newly rendered three-dimensional scene:

  1. Reuse the supplied photograph as the static scene. Preserve the input PNG verbatim as frames/00.png.
  2. Identify the grabber, five sphere positions, suspension endpoints, and chrome supports from numeric RGB inspection. Build local geometric alpha regions in code. Remove the initially moving objects with local OpenCV Telea inpainting. Reconstruct the larger revealed wooden area using a quadratic RGB lighting field fitted to visible source-table pixels, adding fine grain sampled from that same image.
  3. Retain the original chrome-ball texture inside antialiased elliptical sphere silhouettes. Add a small mathematical rim correction and highlight; sample these textures at moving centers with four-by-four subpixel averaging. Keep the center sphere's original pixels fixed. Repair the few pixels of rear support hidden behind a held sphere using RGB profiles sampled above and below it.
  4. Open the two jaw branches slightly about their hinge, then translate the original blue-black grabber texture left until it leaves the frame. Composite the front uprights over moving objects, and the rear uprights behind the spheres.
  5. Animate two equal-angle ball pairs with an ideal Newton's-cradle impulse-transfer approximation. Release occurs at 0.10 s; the first transfer is at 0.50 s. A cosine describes the initial descent; alternating half-sines describe excursions of the two right and two left balls. The nominal period is 1.60 s and projected maximum displacement is 12.4 px. Each transfer reduces excursion by a factor of 0.974. A quadratic vertical-rise term approximates the perspective projection of a pendulum arc. Thin antialiased strings connect both rail endpoints to every moving ball; soft local shadows remain on the existing dark base.
  6. Save the RGB frames, packed tensor, contact sheet, transition sheet, and self-contained HTML player with the provided output-only helper.

The apparatus scale and three-dimensional camera calibration are not supplied. The timing, hidden background, subpixel strings, and sphere contours are therefore estimates. This is an analytic image animation with idealized collision transfer, rather than a measurement-calibrated dynamical simulation. Frame sampling is fixed at 4 fps; frame 19 is held for the final quarter second, making a five-second playback.
