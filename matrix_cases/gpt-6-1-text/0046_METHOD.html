METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was made with local Python pixel operations using Pillow, NumPy, SciPy, and OpenCV. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external references, external models, rendering engines, image Read/View tools, or subagents were used.

The source was measured numerically through RGB row samples, color thresholds, connected components, and ellipse fitting. OBSERVATION.md records these measurements and the event plan written before the generation program.

The stationary scene is copied from the input. Locally estimated opacity separates the ceramic domino and the blue-black grabber from their background. Foreground colors are corrected with the compositing equation before affine resampling, which reduces colored fringes when they move. The grabber jaws separate slightly and retract upward. The domino follows an image-space ballistic fall with acceleration 175 pixels per second squared and a small rotation during entry. A sloping surface mask hides progressively submerged portions; the domino is fully hidden by frame 04 and remains submerged.

Previously covered wooden pixels are reconstructed by interpolating nearby wooden row colors and transferring a small amount of local grain. The covered mug rim and interior wall are filled with source samples at matching ellipse radius. A quadratic color fit to measured dark liquid pixels restores the small patch under the ceramic. This reconstruction is local and approximate: it estimates surfaces that cannot be observed in the first frame. The mug body, handle, fixed shadows, and all surrounding pixels outside the allowed edit support remain identical to the input.

The liquid uses bounded analytic wave packets and a decaying surface oscillation within an ellipse fitted to the opening. Surface gradients drive small pixel refraction and soft lighting variations. A brief low crown and four small ballistic droplets show the displacement during immersion. The droplet layer is drawn at four times the frame resolution and downsampled with Lanczos filtering for subpixel edges. The liquid settles during the latter part of the sequence; very weak motion can quantize to identical RGB frames.

This is a source-pixel animation with analytic physics and shading, rather than a full three-dimensional fluid simulation. The scene's original perspective, texture, and lighting are retained where visible.

Artifacts and verification

  • generate.py deterministically regenerates the output in this workspace.
  • pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 tensor of shape (144, 256, 20). Each value is packed as 0xRRGGBB; axes are height, width, frame.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png are 20 RGB frames at 256 by 144, played at 4 fps for 5 seconds. The first PNG is copied directly from the source and is byte-for-byte identical.
  • index.html is a standalone player containing the first packed frame and exact delta packets. No network assets are required.
  • inspection.png is an eight-frame contact sheet. transition_check.png shows consecutive early frames, the final transition, and amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • verification.json records dimensions, rate, tensor type, exact PNG/tensor equality, exact first-frame PNG bytes, player/tensor equality, stationary-pixel equality, trajectory values, and consecutive-frame RGB differences.

Verification was numeric only. The contact sheets were generated as requested but were not visually reviewed with image tools. The program checks every PNG against its corresponding uint32 tensor slice and decodes every player delta to confirm all 20 displayed frames match the tensor. Numeric bright-pixel counts confirm the ceramic descends through frames 01–03 and has no remaining bright pixels in its local region from frame 04 onward.
