METHOD.md

Method

The animation was generated locally from the supplied photograph, using Python, NumPy and Pillow. The source was inspected through numeric RGB values, coordinate tables, and RGB-derived text grids. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed. There were no network requests, other scene inputs, external models, external engines, reference pictures, or subagents.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generation code and records measured geometry and the twenty-frame plan. All dynamic object boundaries are estimated from the source RGB data; transparent glass makes the exact rim/surface boundary uncertain at this resolution.

Pixel construction

  • The camera and photographed setup are fixed. Every generated frame starts with the original 256 × 144 RGB photograph. Edits are confined to the narrow outlet-to-glass stream and the glass contents. Existing wall, rim and base reflections are protected with soft analytic geometry and measured high-frequency source detail.
  • Water starts leaving the dispenser at 0.20 seconds. A short rounded leading tip establishes the stream; the first modeled arrival at the juice is approximately 0.32 seconds. The continuous flow then remains active through frame 19. There is no added hand, prop or camera movement.
  • The clear falling column is evaluated at four times the output resolution. A gravity acceleration model gives increasing fall speed; conservation of flux contracts its radius. It refracts the current photograph/juice pixels through a small displacement field. Asymmetric bright and dark cylinder edges and traveling highlight bands show flow. The premultiplied color and coverage are area averaged to the output grid.
  • The liquid's modeled surface center rises monotonically from y = 86.50 to 80.49, a 6.01 pixel rise. New volume is integrated at a constant rate after stream arrival. A backward pixel warp raises the original liquid-cap texture and compresses its body gently toward the anchored base.
  • Gradual color desaturation and increased luminance approximate dilution of orange grapefruit juice. A soft, fluctuating interior plume represents mixing beneath the incoming stream. Small damped elliptical surface waves, a subtle impact depression, and a few subpixel bubbles create local motion.
  • This is an analytic photographic pixel composite, not a fluid solver or a full 3D reconstruction. Liquid volume, stream scale and transparent-object geometry are plausible estimates from a single frame. The large reservoir's expected depletion is below reliable pixel resolution, so its photographed bands stay fixed.

Artifacts and playback

Run python generate.py from this workspace to regenerate the outputs. The only visual input read is first_frame.png; the supplied output helper and player template serialize the generated frames.

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty RGB PNGs at 256 × 144. Frame 00 is copied from the input file and is both byte-identical and pixel-identical.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, a uint32 array with shape (144, 256, 20) and axes (y, x, frame). Each integer encodes 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight sampled frames assembled by the supplied helper, with frame labels.
  • transition_check.png: original/next/amplified-difference comparisons for transitions 00→01, 01→02, 08→09 and 18→19.
  • index.html: a standalone embedded-data player with play, pause, replay, scrubbing and loop controls, operating at 4 fps.
  • .tmp/verification.json: numerical states and consecutive-frame RGB change measurements, plus validation results.

Samples are at t = frame/4, from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds. Holding each frame for 0.25 seconds makes a 5-second sequence. The supplied inspection/player labels display each frame's interval-end time, from 0.25 to 5.00 seconds.

Verification performed

Generation asserts that every PNG exactly decodes from its corresponding uint32 tensor slice; all values lie within 24-bit RGB. The complete HTML first-frame and delta-packet sequence is independently decoded and compared with all twenty tensor slices. The original frame's bytes and RGB values match exactly. All pixels outside x = 98–140, y = 57–130 remain exactly equal to the original photograph in every frame. The modeled liquid height is monotonic and the final surface center retains more than three pixels of vertical separation below the estimated mouth center.

The numerical transition check found 18 changed pixels during initial stream emergence, with 1,413–1,659 changed pixels per later transition. The first-transition whole-frame mean absolute RGB change is about 0.0054; subsequent values are about 0.056–0.079 on a 0–255 channel scale. These checks confirm fixed-frame consistency and small, localized changes. They do not constitute image-tool visual review.
