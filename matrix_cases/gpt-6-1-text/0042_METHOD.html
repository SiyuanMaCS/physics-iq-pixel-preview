Result and playback

The output contains 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, shown at 4 fps for a five-second playback. The fixed shot continues the supplied photograph: the existing knife makes a short diagonal cutting stroke, pauses at the glass board, withdraws toward the upper right, and reveals two portions of the original half tangerine separated by a small opening. The fruit settles while the background and camera remain fixed.

frames/00.png is a byte-for-byte copy of first_frame.png. Its RGB values also exactly equal frame 0 in the tensor and player. Frames 09–10 form the brief cutting pause; frames 18–19 hold the settled end pose. These holds are part of the same constant-rate sequence.

Actual generation method

generate.py uses local NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV pixel operations. The only scene input is first_frame.png, with the action supplied by prompt.txt. No network access, external scene assets, masks, AI models, simulation engines, or subagents were used.

The first frame was inspected by printing numeric RGB samples, color-threshold connected component measurements, and row boundary coordinates. These measurements informed manually specified contours for the glass board context, diagonal knife silhouette, whole fruit, and the front-facing fruit surface. OBSERVATION.md recorded the scene measurements and twenty-frame action plan before the generation program was written.

The program separates the source photograph into a fixed background, a photographic knife layer, and photographic fruit layers. It reconstructs pixels initially hidden behind the objects using local Telea inpainting with high-frequency texture sampled from the uncovered glass. Pixels outside the reconstruction and moving-object regions retain the source photograph. The broad board reflections and original soft fruit contact shadow stay fixed.

Initially hidden tangerine flesh is synthesized with ellipsoidal shading, subtle radial segment membranes, a small central pith region, peel shading, deterministic grain, and texture sampled from the visible fruit. The observed exposed fruit pixels are preserved. Hidden steel behind the front-facing fruit is reconstructed from blade samples farther up the same source knife, so the moving knife does not carry a duplicate fruit surface. Warm reflections on the steel fade gradually during withdrawal.

The knife follows an analytic smoothstep trajectory, using subpixel affine warps and premultiplied alpha. Its cutting movement combines a seven-pixel slide toward the lower left, a ten-pixel downward displacement, and a small rotation about the offscreen handle. Withdrawal ends at a displacement of (48, −48) pixels relative to the initial knife. The original front-facing peel is composited in front of the steel to preserve the measured occlusion relationship. A weak moving shadow ties the blade to the glass. Frame 01 blends the first subpixel pose with the original to reduce edge quantization.

After cutting, two masks on opposite sides of a diagonal plane through the fruit move apart by approximately five pixels in total, with small rotations and less than one pixel of settling displacement. A narrow pulp-colored side surface and restrained contact shading expose the completed cut. This is a source-pixel animation with approximate perspective and analytic motion; it is not a three-dimensional mechanical simulation.

Artifacts

  • generate.py: deterministic generation, output writing, and numerical validation.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32; each value encodes 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: the twenty RGB output frames.
  • inspection.png: eight-frame contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: selected consecutive pairs with amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • index.html: self-contained player with lossless first-frame and delta data, playback, seeking, and looping at 250 ms per frame.
  • OBSERVATION.md: initial numeric observations and event plan.
  • .tmp/numeric_validation.json: numerical transition statistics and motion controls.

The provided artifact_io.py and player_template.html are used only to serialize the generated pixels and construct the player and contact sheet.

Verification actually performed

All twenty PNG files were decoded and compared exactly to their corresponding packed tensor frame. The first RGB frame was compared exactly to the source, and its PNG is copied directly from the source file. The HTML player's initial data and all nineteen delta packets were independently decoded and compared exactly against pixels.npz; its playback interval is 250 ms. Both inspection sheets were checked for PNG integrity.

Numeric transition measurements show a peak whole-frame mean absolute channel change of approximately 2.00 / 255 during withdrawal, and 0.23 / 255 at the first transition. The deliberate pause and final hold have zero frame-to-frame change. Conservative background regions at the left and lower right were verified to remain pixel-identical throughout. Numeric samples down the final diagonal opening confirm exposed board colors between the two portions.

No image Read/View tools were used. The contact sheets were generated as requested, but no visual review is claimed. Validation consisted of numeric RGB inspection, pixel equality checks, structural checks, and player-data reconstruction.
