METHOD.md

Method

This is a local, source-image-based pixel animation. It uses Pillow and NumPy and does not use a generative model, outside reference, external rendering engine, network access, or subagent. Source inspection was entirely numerical: RGB samples, color thresholds, and luminance-bin text grids. The generated inspection sheets have not been viewed with an image tool.

generate.py copies frame 00 directly from the source RGB array and preserves the original PNG file bytes for frames/00.png. For the remaining frames it keeps the photograph fixed and composites motion inside the receiving glass and in a narrow region beneath the existing dispenser outlet. A fourfold subpixel grid provides antialiasing. It averages only the generated pixel differences back to native resolution, so areas outside the effects keep their exact source values.

The juice level rises according to a short flow onset followed by constant inflow. At frame 19 the modeled level has risen about 9.2 pixels. An inverse vertical warp extends the original liquid column upward, including its meniscus and surface shading. The warp fades out above the heavy glass base. Fixed sidewalls and the two bright glass highlights are protected, with the brightest highlight centers restored from native source pixels. Small color changes approximate the reduction in optical density as water mixes with the juice. A soft time-varying filament approximates the local mixing under the stream. These are analytic compositing effects, not a solved fluid simulation.

The water stream is attached to the measured outlet near (154.7, 60). Its width decreases using a gravity-dependent velocity and approximate continuity of flow. Local source sampling approximates refraction; translucent shading, a thin bright edge and a dim opposing edge make it clear water under the existing subdued light. The stream's highlights change with time. Small elliptical ripple rings, an impact depression, and a few staggered subpixel bubbles are confined to the liquid surface. The pour continues through the last frame, and the liquid stays inside the glass.

artifact_io.py writes the 20 RGB PNGs, the compressed uint32 pixel tensor, the eight-frame contact sheet, and a self-contained HTML player. The tensor has shape (144, 256, 20) and packs each RGB pixel as 0xRRGGBB. The player uses four frames per second; the twenty frames provide five seconds of playback. transition_check.png shows three adjacent-frame pairs alongside amplified numeric difference maps. It is a diagnostic artifact, not part of the animation.

Validation in generate.py reads every saved PNG and compares all RGB values with the corresponding packed tensor slice. It verifies exact source RGB equality for frame 00, shape, dtype, and unchanged pixels outside the measured effect region. It writes frame-by-frame numeric transition statistics and the modeled level changes to validation.json. Review is numerical only; no visual-review claim is made.

The transparent glass rim and overlapped objects have approximate manually measured boundaries. The optical effects and ripples are a restrained image-based reconstruction, with no claim of full three-dimensional or fluid-physical accuracy.
