cut-orange · 生成方法

This continuation uses local, deterministic pixel synthesis from first_frame.png and the action in prompt.txt. The source was decoded with Pillow. Inspection consisted of numeric RGB samples, RGB-derived text grids, color runs, connected components, and array comparisons. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed. There was no network access, external reference, supplied mask, AI model, subagent, external renderer, or physics engine.

Image construction

generate.py creates antialiased, coordinate-based silhouettes for the diagonal knife complex, the physical tangerine lobe, the reflected orange patch, and the contact neighborhood. The large glass-board/background region stays fixed. The interpretation of the lighter orange lobe as a knife reflection and the division between knife and glass were inferred from the source RGB and geometry; they are not visually confirmed.

OpenCV's local Telea inpainting reconstructs only source pixels that the moving objects uncover. Most original wood and glass pixels are carried through unchanged. The knife and rind use the actual source texture, resampled with premultiplied alpha and bilinear, subpixel coordinate transforms implemented with SciPy. Hidden knife pixels beneath the fruit are reconstructed with a dark local steel shade, so the moving knife does not carry duplicated rind pixels. A separate inpainted patch attenuates the orange reflection during withdrawal.

The knife follows a smooth, prescribed single cutting stroke: approximately 9 pixels left and 8 pixels down, with a small draw along its axis. It then lifts to a final offset of 6 pixels right and 15 pixels up. A cutting-plane depth mask controls knife/fruit occlusion. This is an image-space kinematic approximation of slicing; material fracture, forces, and optical reflections are not simulated by a physical solver.

After the lower rind is cut, the source fruit silhouette divides along the cutting plane. The left portion slips approximately 6.5 pixels left and 3 pixels down and pivots 4.7 degrees around its support. A small exponentially damped displacement models settling on the glass. The right portion shifts less than one pixel. Soft contact shading follows the supported pieces. There is no camera movement or added object.

The newly exposed faces are projected ellipses with analytic shading. Their orange rind, pale pith, flesh, segment membranes, and fine vesicle variation are drawn mathematically in NumPy with fixed random seeds. These faces are synthesized pixels rather than recovered hidden source details. They fade into visibility as the blade finishes the cut and withdraws. A small initial source-compositing residual fades over the opening stroke to keep the initial transition continuous.

Revision and numeric checks

The first generated sequence was checked with color components and RGB text grids. A dark area left behind around rows 48–56 was traced to a silhouette that excluded part of the source knife/shadow boundary. The silhouette was expanded to the measured boundary and the local inpainting margin increased from one pixel to two. The sequence was then regenerated and checked again. This is a numeric inspection and correction, not an image-tool review.

The generator verifies the following on each run:

  • Exactly 20 RGB PNGs at 256 × 144; playback uses a fixed 250 ms interval, corresponding to 4 fps and 5 seconds for 20 display intervals.
  • frames/00.png is copied directly from first_frame.png, with both file bytes and decoded RGB pixels equal.
  • pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20) using 0xRRGGBB packing. Every frame PNG is compared pixel by pixel with its tensor slice.
  • The standalone HTML player's first frame and all 19 delta packets are independently decoded and compared with the tensor.
  • Background regions outside the motion remain exactly equal to the source. All 19 adjacent frame pairs have nonzero change, and their RGB difference statistics are saved.

Artifacts and reproduction

Run python generate.py from this directory. Dependencies are NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV, all used locally. artifact_io.py only handles output encoding, the inspection contact sheet, and the supplied standalone player template.

  • pixels.npz: the packed RGB tensor.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: the twenty RGB frames.
  • inspection.png: the generated eight-frame contact sheet for the user to inspect.
  • transition_check.png: four transition pairs with amplified RGB difference maps.
  • index.html: self-contained, offline player with seek, playback, replay, and loop controls.
  • OBSERVATION.md: pre-code numeric measurements and the twenty-frame event plan.
  • .tmp/verification.json: detailed numeric verification report.
  • .tmp/trajectories.json: per-frame motion and reveal parameters.

The input-to-object interpretation, hidden-board reconstruction, shaded cut faces, and prescribed trajectories limit the physical and photometric accuracy. The source framing, visible textures, cadence, and first frame are preserved as described above.
