METHOD.md

Method and validation

This is a locally constructed continuation of first_frame.png guided by prompt.txt. Scene analysis used Python/Pillow RGB arrays, pixel samples, color-region measurements, and numeric coordinates. No image Read/View tool was used, and neither diagnostic PNG has been visually reviewed through an image tool. No network, outside reference, external asset, learned model, subagent, or external physics/rendering engine was used.

Pixel construction

generate.py reads the source as a 256 × 144 RGB array. Frame 00 is copied directly from that array and bypasses all reconstruction and rendering.

The measured block corners define six small cuboid meshes. The projection uses an inferred camera elevation whose table-plane foreshortening is 0.31, plus source-derived corner residuals to preserve the initial perspective and irregularities of the wooden blocks. A local triangle rasterizer implements supersampling and a per-pixel camera-depth buffer with NumPy. All moving faces use colors and perspective-warped texture samples from the original front, narrow side, or top face. Source outliers are replaced by the face's measured median. Initially dark side faces brighten as they turn upward; the original top faces darken as they become vertical. A narrow edge highlight approximates the painted wood's softened edges.

Still blocks retain their original pixels. The wall and unoccluded table remain copied from the input. Areas exposed by moving blocks are repaired with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting, with high-frequency wood grain sampled from other visible table pixels in the same image. The partly occluded cylinder is repaired with samples from its visible opposite half. Old source shadows are removed as the cascade advances. New soft shadows are projected analytically from the blocks onto the table and blurred with a local Gaussian filter. No generative completion model is involved.

The stick is a shaded wooden rod whose tip travels on a foreshortened circular arc centered near (180, 41). Its angle decreases from approximately +0.05 to −3.75 radians; in the image this moves from downward, to leftward, to upward, and then to the upper right: a clockwise sweep. A small tan end cap and dark attachment match the corresponding source pixels. The source cap overlaps the purple block's top; that patch is repaired with adjacent purple paint before the new rod is drawn.

Motion model

The impact order is purple → blue → green → pink → orange → yellow. Each block rotates rigidly about its leading lower edge on the table. Its unconstrained angle follows a continuous accelerating trajectory, theta = 0.40 * age + 0.5 * 5.2 * age², capped at 90 degrees. Successive launch times are derived from the first contact angle of the previous pair. They are approximately 0.460, 0.617, 0.852, 1.114, 1.390, and 1.648 seconds.

A separating-axis test on rectangular cross sections locates first neighbor contact. Lookup interpolation limits each predecessor's angle against the current next-block pose, processed from the last block backward. This produces a supported cascade rather than six independently falling sprites. The yellow block ends flat on the table; the other five remain supported by their neighbors at approximately 51.2, 61.2, 63.3, 63.6, and 65.6 degrees from upright. The last block reaches its resting pose between 2.25 and 2.50 seconds. The stick uses a smooth angular trajectory ending at 3.50 seconds, followed by an entirely stable ending.

This is an analytic, contact-limited animation, not a full force/impulse rigid-body simulation. Hidden surfaces, exact camera geometry, stick length, contact location, lighting, and occluded tabletop detail are inferred from the single frame. The model does not simulate free translation, rebound, or grain-scale deformation. Those approximations are the limits of the actual method.

Each moving output is evaluated at three times spanning a 1/40-second exposure and averaged. Geometry is rendered at four times the output resolution and box-filtered to 256 × 144. Frame 00 remains exact; all other frames are deterministic. Temporal sampling is fixed at four frames per second, with 20 frames and a five-second playback interval.
