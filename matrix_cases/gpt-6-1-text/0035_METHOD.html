Image construction

generate.py uses Pillow, NumPy, OpenCV pixel transforms/inpainting, and SciPy Gaussian filtering. It extracts the source pigments, face textures, top-cap textures, and the wooden arm strip. Six fitted projected cuboids use those source textures. Face triangles are rasterized with barycentric texture coordinates and a local depth buffer at four times the output resolution, then area-downsampled for edge antialiasing. The source face colors receive small orientation-dependent shading changes.

Original block locations are removed with polygon masks supplemented by local RGB pigment masks, followed by a one-pixel expansion and OpenCV Telea inpainting from neighboring source pixels. The occluded arm and its original shadow on the platform are cleared locally. Small newly exposed parts of the platform are reconstructed from its numerically measured ellipse, gray top, black front, and low-amplitude deterministic pixel variation. The original attachment patch remains at its measured axis.

The background is copied from the source. Nearest-neighbor supersampling preserves unchanged background RGB values exactly through the final area reduction. A standing block retains its original source pixels until it starts moving. Source wood grain and existing table shadow/spill are retained; additional soft contact shadows are computed for moving blocks. This is local compositing and fitted geometry, rather than a reconstruction of full hidden scene geometry or complete physically based relighting.

Motion and contact

The arm rotates clockwise in the image about (204,49). Its measured initial endpoint is (175,76), next to the purple block. It follows a smooth angular profile with a 70-degree sweep over 2.25 seconds, then stops. The first contact occurs near the beginning of the sequence. At frame 01 the arm tip is near (171.3,71.4), close to the moving purple block's right face.

A custom one-dimensional hinge model integrates angular motion at a 0.001 s step. A kick to purple initiates the wave; gravity-like angular acceleration, damping, neighboring contact constraints, angular impulses, and a tabletop angle limit transfer the motion through blue, green, pink, red, and yellow. For neighboring blocks, the corner/side non-penetration expression is g = spacing*cos(theta_left) - width_left - height_right*sin(theta_right-theta_left). Position projection and an impulse opposing closing velocity enforce g >= 0. This is a reduced mathematical contact model, not a general rigid-body engine. The initial measured lean of each source face is included in the simulation.

The leftmost block settles horizontally. Following blocks remain inclined, supported by the neighboring fallen blocks. The row has settled by frame 06; the arm stops at frame 09; frames 10–19 hold the final scene. The simulation is sampled at t = frame_index / 4 with no interpolation of the completed images.

Artifacts and validation

Run python generate.py from this workspace to reproduce all generated frames and output artifacts. The seed for reconstructed texture variation is fixed.

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB PNG frames, each 256×144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, each value packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-data player, 4 fps, 20 displayed intervals, five seconds total, with play/pause, replay, seek, and loop controls.
  • inspection.png: eight selected rendered frames in a labeled contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: eight labeled inter-frame absolute RGB heatmaps.
  • verification.json: frame equality checks, activation times, pose angles, and all 19 adjacent-frame difference statistics.
  • OBSERVATION.md: source measurements, the pre-coding event plan, and its geometry-driven timing revision.

The generator checks the exact source equality of frame 00 and validates every saved PNG against all three channels unpacked from the uint32 tensor. Additional local checks decode the HTML player deltas back into the same tensor, check RGB dimensions and frame counts, and evaluate the contact/table constraints. Validation is numerical; perceptual quality is limited by single-frame geometry estimation and local inpainting, and was not reviewed using an image tool.
