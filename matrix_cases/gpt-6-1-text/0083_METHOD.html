light-on-statue · 生成方法

The sequence uses local Python image operations on first_frame.png. No image-viewing tool, network request, external reference, mask, AI model, simulation engine, or subagent was used. Scene measurements were obtained from numeric RGB reads, per-row brightness intervals, and regional pixel statistics.

generate.py uses Pillow, NumPy, and SciPy. It segments the photographed porcelain with manually measured row contours and local brightness alpha, approximates it as horizontal elliptical cross sections, and rotates those sections clockwise about the stationary vertical axis at x=146.5. The angular speed is 17 degrees per second. The sampled poses run from 0 to 80.75 degrees, at 0.25-second intervals. Surface colors are sampled from the source frame with bilinear interpolation. Source texture is remapped around the visible part of each ellipse. Newly exposed sides use alpha-normalized, blurred texture from the inner porcelain region, with a gradual transition to avoid copying dark background pixels onto the side. Analytical ellipse normals supply a modest shading adjustment under a fixed frontal-left light. The thin isolated upper relief is included in the source alpha; its unobserved side geometry is only approximated.

The illuminated wall is recovered beneath the original shadow by per-row quadratic fits to unaffected lit pixels on its two sides, followed by mild spatial smoothing. The difference between this recovered wall and the source provides an RGB shadow residual. Source sculpture pixels are removed from that residual by local interpolation. This residual is deformed by the same rotation angle and elliptical width law, using an approximate correspondence between sculpture height and wall height. The large shadow thus changes together with the sculpture. Shadow magnification and projection are source-matched approximations; this is not exact point-light ray tracing.

The hidden tabletop beneath the sculpture is filled from nearby source RGB values. The darkest support region is retained, and source reflections inside the measured platter footprint are advected in elliptical coordinates. A symmetric rotating black platter has a fixed outline under the locked camera. All pixels outside the local sculpture, support and shadow regions stay fixed.

A numeric revision widened the transition to synthesized side texture, moved its sampling inward from the silhouette boundary, and replaced contaminated contact-area fill with darker source support pixels. This reduces a texture-wrap seam and bright stationary remnants beneath the moving lower details.

Artifacts

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images at 256×144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, channel packing 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: self-contained canvas player with 4 fps playback, pause, seek, replay and looping controls. It embeds the first frame and subsequent pixel deltas.
  • inspection.png: eight-frame contact sheet generated for human inspection.
  • transition_check.png: frames 01, 02, 10 and 19 with four-times-amplified absolute RGB differences from the preceding frame.
  • OBSERVATION.md: source measurements and the pre-generation 20-frame event plan.
  • .tmp/validation.json: numerical checks and transition statistics.

Verification and limits

Frame 00 is copied directly from the original RGB array and tested for exact equality. Every saved PNG is tested against unpacking its corresponding uint32 tensor slice. The packed tensor dimensions, dtype, frame count and sampling rate are checked. The HTML's embedded pixel packets are independently decoded and compared against the same tensor. Outside x=90–204 in the wall and the narrow support footprint, the original scene remains pixel-identical. Temporal differences are measured numerically. The final sampled time is 4.75 seconds; twenty frames displayed for 0.25 seconds each occupy five seconds.

The scene's hidden sculpture surfaces, depth, and lamp position cannot be uniquely recovered from one image. The reconstruction therefore approximates volume, side texture, and shadow projection while preserving the observed setup. No visual review is claimed: the generated diagnostic images were saved but were not opened with an image tool.
