Completed a fixed-camera, 20-frame RGB continuation at 256 × 144, 4 fps, with five seconds of playback. The orange-brown tennis ball rolls from the pipe exit across the tabletop to its right side. The red ball and black pipe remain fixed. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the decoded input image.

Actual method

All scene reasoning used the supplied text and local numeric RGB inspection of first_frame.png. Pillow, NumPy, and SciPy perform local pixel operations. No image Read/View tools, network access, external scene references, learned models, physics engines, rendering engines, or subagents were used. The sheets were generated as artifacts, but were not visually reviewed.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generation code. RGB thresholding identified the two balls; sampled table boundaries and the dark pipe established an approximate projected rolling direction. The orange ball is already outside the visible pipe in the first frame, so motion begins immediately.

For frames 01–19, a small union of elliptical masks removes the original orange ball, its cast shadow, and warm contact reflection. A sparse four-neighbor harmonic solve interpolates the hidden tabletop from the actual surrounding RGB pixels. Small high-frequency grain copied from another tabletop area in the same image is added inside the filled region. Everything outside this local restoration and moving ball/shadow remains copied from the source.

An analytic sphere-like disk with a soft optical edge is fitted to the source colors by bounded nonlinear least squares. Its RGB shading is a polynomial of analytic surface normals. The fit also estimates an elongated cast shadow and a compact contact shadow. Material texture comes from the source sphere's residual RGB values after removing the fitted shading. Inverse Rodrigues rotation moves that texture over the sphere as rolling proceeds, while illumination stays fixed. Low-contrast periodic color variation fills material coordinates on the hemisphere absent from the input. The procedure evaluates these mathematical expressions directly into pixels; it does not create or render a 3D scene.

The projected center advances by (9.57895, 1.10000) pixels per 0.25-second frame. Total horizontal travel is 182 pixels. Apparent radii grow 8% across the sequence to approximate perspective. Rolling phase follows distance divided by the initial projected radius. Three nearby shutter samples give mild subpixel exposure smoothing. The shadow moves with the ball and is clipped at an estimated tabletop boundary. The final contact point remains supported near the right edge.

The fitted disk center is (37.7722, 44.2696), with initial radii (7.2684, 6.4215) and edge softness 0.7038 pixels. This fitted shading center differs slightly from the observed orange-color centroid. The latter moves from (36.9792, 44.9444) to (219.0380, 65.9430), matching the intended event plan.

Limits

A single low-resolution still does not determine launch speed, camera calibration, table depth, or the hidden material texture. The trajectory, perspective change, rolling phase, and shadows are inferred approximations. The inpainted table and hidden-hemisphere texture are synthesized locally, so their fine detail is approximate. Motion ends at the right side of the tabletop; the sequence does not infer an additional fall. Numeric validation establishes artifact correctness and measured continuity, rather than perceptual realism.
