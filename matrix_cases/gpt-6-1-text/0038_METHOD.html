METHOD.md

Method

The animation uses local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png, with the action taken from prompt.txt. The source photograph supplies every visible object, material, reflection, and background. No network, external reference, AI model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used.

Image construction

generate.py reads the input as RGB uint8. Local RGB measurements identify the black balloon above the hose. A row-wise soft matte covers the photographic body and its original antialiased boundary. Background colors hidden by the original balloon are estimated by horizontal interpolation between nearby clear pixels in the same row. The two photographic highlights and the dark latex texture remain opaque inside the silhouette. Premultiplied foreground colors are recovered algebraically so that the unwarped foreground/background composite reconstructs the source exactly.

For frames 01–19, a dense, monotonic coordinate map expands the balloon upward and sideways around the fixed attachment near (137.5,86). Horizontal expansion reaches 1.63× and vertical expansion reaches 1.39×. The deformation smoothly diminishes near the neck. All pixels at y≥86 are restored directly from the original image, preserving the complete hose, blue detail, green apparatus, support/shadow, and wooden tabletop. Pixels outside the affected upper balloon region are also preserved exactly.

The source alpha and premultiplied colors are sampled with bilinear interpolation on a 4×4 subpixel grid per output pixel, averaged, and composited over the reconstructed background. This maintains photographic reflections with soft moving boundaries. Frame 00 bypasses rendering and is copied exactly from the supplied image.

Motion

The approximate balloon volume follows a constant volumetric air-inflow schedule:

V(i)/V(0) = 1 + (3.693091 - 1) * i/19, for i = 0…19.

The horizontal and vertical powers of this volume ratio are chosen to reach the specified final scales while satisfying sx² * sy = V/V0 for the main body. The body expands more horizontally than vertically, becoming rounder. The photographic taper stays connected to the fixed hose. The crown moves from approximately y=31 to y=10 and remains within the frame. The balloon remains intact as pumping continues.

This is a two-dimensional photographic deformation guided by an approximate air-volume law. It does not solve three-dimensional rubber membrane stresses or rerender changing reflections. The original reflected-light texture stretches with the latex.
