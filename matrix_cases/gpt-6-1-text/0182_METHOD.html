METHOD.md

Method

This is a local pixel synthesis continuation of first_frame.png, guided by prompt.txt. The first frame is copied without modification. The source was inspected through local Pillow/NumPy RGB values, color thresholds, connected-component bounds and coordinate samples. No image Read/View tool, external reference, network, external AI model, external physics engine or subagent was used. The output sheets were generated, but were not visually reviewed with an image tool.

Motion and geometry

generate.py implements a small two-dimensional rigid-body contact solver directly with NumPy. Yellow and blue are moving rectangles with relative masses of one each; red is a fixed support. Dynamics run at 480 steps per second, with gravity of 240 image-coordinate units/s². The image does not provide a calibrated real-world length, so this is a plausible image-space dynamics approximation rather than a measured reconstruction of the experiment.

The grabber holds the placement through 0.50 s, opens its photographed jaws, and withdraws upward. Yellow then pivots clockwise past the red support's right edge. Blue initially slips slightly relative to yellow, reaches the tabletop and eventually tips right. Collisions use separating-axis tests, convex polygon clipping, angular impulse response, low restitution, Coulomb friction and penetration correction. The table plane slopes upward slightly to the right in image coordinates, matching the first-frame projection. The numerical solution settles with yellow partly across blue; the final second holds the settled pose exactly. Red's fixed pose is an explicit modeling assumption.

The visible blocks are rendered as textured extruded quadrilaterals. Their front faces rotate and translate according to the solver, while projected depth vectors stay fixed. This supplies changing visible side faces without rotating a whole photograph as a flat patch. Photographed face textures are extracted with local perspective transforms. Small occluded blue regions and newly exposed block faces use sampled block colors with deterministic subtle grain. Side-face brightness follows simple directional normals. The blue clamp shadow is removed from newly exposed material. The grabber uses two separately extracted photographed jaw mattes with lateral opening and vertical withdrawal.

Pixel reconstruction

The stationary background behind the original moving objects is reconstructed once by per-row interpolation between neighboring RGB samples. Table regions additionally receive locally copied, attenuated grain residuals. All operations remain within the workspace. Soft table shadows change with height and contact; a short-lived local shadow follows blue on yellow. Rendering uses 3× supersampling and area reduction to 256 × 144. Unaffected background pixels retain their exact source RGB values. Static source regions are not repeatedly synthesized. The first two continuation frames preserve measured photographed block pixels during release.

This method approximates camera projection, material illumination and contacts; it is not a full three-dimensional reconstruction. All pixels are produced by local code and source sampling.
