duck-falls-in-box · 生成方法

This is a local source-pixel animation of the supplied photograph. Inputs were only first_frame.png and prompt.txt; the output format followed GENERATION_RULES.md. Scene measurements and the event plan were written to OBSERVATION.md before the generation program. The original frame was inspected through Pillow/NumPy RGB samples and textual coordinate grids. No image Read/View tool, visual inspection, network access, external reference, external AI model, external engine, or subagent was used.

Pixel construction

generate.py loads the RGB source with Pillow. It estimates the warm gray wall behind the duck and the two narrow grabber jaws by interpolating RGB scan lines between unaffected wall samples near x=120 and x=142. The rest of the image is copied unchanged.

A local matte is extracted for each moving object from its channel deficits relative to that wall. The greatest relative channel deficit supplies opacity; low-amplitude wall noise is excluded. The original RGB values are converted to premultiplied foreground color by subtracting the estimated background contribution. This preserves the photographed yellow duck, orange beak, and orange/black jaw pixels, including soft edges. The matte is computed directly from the allowed source image; no supplied or external mask is used.

The two jaws open through small opposing affine shears, anchored at the top edge. Each lower tip moves approximately 2.8 pixels outward by 1.25 seconds. The duck remains in its source pose through the release sample at 1.0 second, then follows projected ballistic motion: downward offset = 120 × (t − 1)² pixels. Rotation is at most 5 degrees, and lateral drift is less than half a pixel. SciPy performs local inverse affine bilinear resampling of opacity and premultiplied RGB. Seven sub-exposure samples over 1/120 second provide slight motion blur during the fall; frame samples remain spaced at 1/4 second.

The original front wall of the box occludes the duck below its sloped lip, approximately y=86 at x=130. A small darkening is restricted to the exposed aperture as the duck approaches. At 1.75 seconds only the upper duck remains visible; by 2.0 seconds it is fully concealed. The duck's floor contact and settling are inferred beneath the opaque box front. No hidden 3D collision simulation or visible bounce is generated. The open grabber and fixed setup remain still for the remainder.

These are manual measurements, simple local wall reconstruction, and a projected motion model. They do not reconstruct an unseen 3D scene. The opening is only a few pixels deep in this camera perspective, so the box masks most of the entrance.
