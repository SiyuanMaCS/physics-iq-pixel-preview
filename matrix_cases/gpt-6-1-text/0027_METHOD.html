METHOD.md

Method

This continuation uses local Python pixel operations, adjacent-pixel reconstruction, mathematical sphere rotation, and alpha compositing. PIL, NumPy, and SciPy are used; artifact_io.py writes the tensor, PNG frames, contact sheet, and standalone player. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No image Read/View tool, network access, external references, external model, rendering engine, or subagent was used. The review is numeric RGB inspection and artifact verification, not visual review.

Static scene and source reconstruction

Frame 00 is the original RGB image, exactly preserved. Subsequent frames retain the fixed camera, exposure, wood texture, wall, transparent pane, and existing glass edges from that image. The initial ball and its cast shadow are removed within a local feathered mask. Robust quadratic fits to unoccluded wood on each affected scanline estimate the missing tabletop colors. Small detrended texture residuals are copied from adjacent unoccluded wood in the same scanline. The original ball shadow is measured as brightness attenuation against this repaired plate and fitted to a rotated Gaussian; the contact shadow is a narrower Gaussian. New ball, shadow, and reflection layers are the only animated content.

Ball appearance and rolling

The fitted ball center is (81.8, 57.8), with hard silhouette radii 10.5 and 10.45 pixels. Four-times supersampling and approximately 0.66-pixel optical blur reproduce the source's antialiased silhouette. Background contributions are removed from high-opacity original ball samples. A robust fit in sphere-normal coordinates separates fixed illumination from blue felt and pale seam residuals. Those observed residuals become the material texture; low-confidence rim values are filled from the nearest confident sample.

For each frame, the sphere's visible normals are inverse-rotated to sample the material. The unobserved rear hemisphere uses a folded projection of the observed texture. This repeats source material on the back rather than claiming to recover unseen detail. The fitted illumination stays fixed in camera coordinates while the measured felt and seam rotate. The ball is generated as a shaded sphere, not a rotating photograph rectangle.

The projected contact path has slope 0.174. An assumed support-plane elevation with sine 0.38 lifts that path into three dimensions. The rolling axis is the support normal crossed with the travel direction. Rotation angle is lifted travel distance divided by the ball radius, enforcing no-slip rolling in the assumed geometry. This includes the visible clockwise turn. The pane and ball do not deform.

Timing, contact, and optics

Twenty samples use t=i/4 seconds and play for 5 seconds at 4 fps. Initial horizontal speed is 40 pixels/second. Effective rolling resistance is 6 pixels/second² on wood and 3 pixels/second² on glass, with continuous position and velocity at the crossing. The ball crosses x=126 at approximately 1.216 seconds and ends near (222.85, 82.34), moving at about 22.1 pixels/second. The ball stays wholly visible and supported by the pane. Gradual resistance makes successive center steps decrease from 9.960 to 5.704 pixels.

The cast shadow follows the original measured projected light direction. Over clear glass its strength decreases smoothly; a faint compressed blue reflection is clipped to the inferred pane boundaries. A short-range contact shadow anchors the ball at the surface. The pane's original transparent background and edge highlights remain part of the static plate.

The absolute scene scale, exact pane overlap, unseen ball texture, and camera elevation cannot be uniquely recovered from one frame. The geometry and rear texture completion above are explicit modeling assumptions. The chosen event is simply a continuous roll onto and across the stationary pane, ending before the far edge.
