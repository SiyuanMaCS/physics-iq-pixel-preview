METHOD.md

Method and verification

The continuation uses deterministic local pixel compositing of the supplied photograph. It uses no external references, network resources, learned model, external rendering engine, or subagent. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. Inspection was numerical RGB inspection in local Python; the saved images were not viewed through an image tool, and no visual review is claimed.

Pixel construction

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV. The source is loaded as a 256 × 144 RGB uint8 array. Frames 00 and 01 retain those exact pixels. A small patch of the gray wall behind the grabber and paper is reconstructed by interpolating unobstructed source RGB samples from the same rows, with a feathered patch boundary. The photographed tabletop, glass bowl, background, and shaft stay fixed.

Numeric RGB spans define the orange/black grabber matte. The matte is divided into a stationary central actuator and two arms. The arms open by rotating their original photographic texture around nearby hinge points, reaching 13.5 degrees on each side. An irregular paper outline follows the measured RGB boundary. Its original creases, highlights, and shadows are retained. Both cutouts use soft mattes, edge color decontamination, and premultiplied affine resampling to reduce edge fringes.

Release occurs at 0.90 seconds. The falling paper follows y = 0.5 × 3600 × (t − 0.90)² in pixel units, with a small rotation. This corresponds to an approximate scale of 367 pixels per meter. The bottom of the paper reaches the estimated water surface after a 38-pixel fall at 1.0453 seconds. At 4 fps, frame 04 captures the paper about 18 pixels into the fall and frame 05 captures it after contact.

The paper then remains buoyant. A damped cosine supplies a small bob, an exponentially settling angle supplies a slight rotation, and total sideways drift remains about 1.2 pixels. The paper becomes slightly flatter vertically after impact. Its lower folds darken gradually by at most about 9 percent to suggest wetting. The lowest folds receive a weak water tint. These are explicit mathematical animations, not a fluid or material simulation.

The water plane is an approximate ellipse centered near (129,103), inferred from numerical rim/reflection structure in the input.
