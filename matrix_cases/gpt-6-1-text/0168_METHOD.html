+CENTER = (139.0, 64.2)
+RADIUS = 17.3
+SPEED_DEG_PER_SECOND = 12.0

+def sample(array, x, y, order=3):
+    return np.stack([map_coordinates(array[..., c], [y, x], order=order,
+                                    mode='nearest', prefilter=(order > 1))
+                     for c in range(3)], axis=-1)
+
+
+def basis(u, v):
+    z = np.sqrt(np.maximum(0, 1-u*u-v*v))
+    return np.stack([np.ones_like(u), u, v, z, u*u, v*v, u*v,
+                     u*z, v*z, v*v*v], axis=-1)
+
+
+def main():
+    source = np.asarray(Image.open(ROOT/'first_frame.png').convert('RGB'))
+    assert source.shape == (144, 256, 3)
+    raw = source.astype(np.float64)
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:144, :256]
+    dx, dy = xx-CENTER[0], yy-CENTER[1]
+    radius = np.hypot(dx, dy)
+    u, v = dx/RADIUS, dy/RADIUS
+    design = basis(u, v)
+    # Fit only unmarked yellow felt. The ink and pale stitching are excluded.
+    r, g, b = raw.transpose(2, 0, 1)
+    felt = ((radius < 15.8) & (g > 80) & (g-r > 1) &
+            (g-b > 68) & (b/g < .32))
+    coefficients = []
+    for channel in range(3):
+        A = design[felt]
+        target = np.log(np.maximum(raw[..., channel][felt], 1))
+        initial = np.linalg.lstsq(A, target, rcond=None)[0]
+        fit = least_squares(lambda c: np.r_[A@c-target, .035*c[4:]],
+                            initial, loss='soft_l1', f_scale=.045)
+        coefficients.append(fit.x)
+    coefficients = np.stack(coefficients, axis=-1)
+    # Keep numerical behavior outside the fitted ball bounded.
+    shading = np.exp(np.clip(design@coefficients, np.log(8), np.log(245)))

OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The source was inspected only using local Pillow/NumPy RGB arrays, numeric samples, threshold counts, and text color classifications. No image read/view tool was used.

The image is 256 by 144 RGB pixels. It contains a yellow-green tennis ball on a dark cylindrical platform, a brown wood surface, and a gray/beige background. The camera is fixed. The ball's approximate center is (139.0, 64.2), with a roughly 17.3-pixel radius and a soft silhouette spanning x=121..157 and y=46..82. A chroma-threshold contour fit gives (138.87, 63.85), radius 16.82; the soft RGB fringe extends beyond that fit. Its green-channel values range from about 100 near its lower edge to 228 on its brighter right side. The light remains strongest on the right/upper surface. The black platform top is approximately x=101..181, y=68..94, and its body reaches approximately y=113. The ball contact and shadow occupy roughly x=125..153, y=80..91.

The face is painted on the left visible portion of the sphere. Dark eye regions are around (126,54) and (133,54); the dark smiling mouth curves around x=123..135, y=65..71. A pale tan seam bends from approximately (150,55), through (136,64), to (139,78). Background RGB and every platform pixel outside the ball's soft contour will stay fixed.

Interpret clockwise in the fixed camera's image plane: rotate the spherical material around the camera-facing axis while retaining the stationary center, spherical silhouette, contact, and lighting. Use 12 degrees per second, or 3 degrees per frame. Divide out a fitted smooth illumination field, rotate the extracted material texture, and apply the illumination at the destination. This carries the original seam and face together and avoids rotating the highlights or platform. Any soft edge extraction uncertainty will be handled locally. No new objects, translation, bouncing, cuts, or camera changes.

METHOD.md

Method

Generated 20 RGB frames at 256×144 pixels, at 4 fps. Frame timestamps are t/4 for t=0..19; each frame lasts 0.25 seconds, for a five-second clip. Frame 00 is the original RGB array, without modification.

The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. GENERATION_RULES.md supplied the output requirements. All source inspection was done in local Python with numeric RGB samples, threshold masks, connected-component measurements, and text classifications. No image Read/View tools, external references, network, external models, engines, or subagents were used. The contact and transition PNGs were produced for the user; they were not visually reviewed.

The implementation is deterministic local pixel processing using NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. The ball is modeled as a stationary circular projection centered at (139.0,64.2), with a nominal radius of 17.3 pixels. The soft matte reaches 18.15 pixels from the center and becomes fully opaque within 16.5 pixels. Rotation means clockwise as seen by the fixed camera, around its viewing axis. Its rate is 12 degrees per second: 3 degrees per frame, ending at 57 degrees.

To keep light attached to the scene, the script fits a smooth illumination model separately to each RGB channel. It uses 639 samples of yellow felt, excludes dark ink and the pale seam, and robustly fits logarithmic intensity to a basis of projected coordinates and the analytic spherical normal component sqrt(max(0,1-u²-v²)). This separates the moving material detail from the fixed illumination approximately.

Local radial samples outside the ball estimate background color for unmixing its antialiased edge. The foreground is divided by the fitted illumination to obtain a material texture. Unreliable low-coverage samples are extended radially from well-covered source pixels. For each frame, an inverse clockwise coordinate transform samples that same material texture with cubic interpolation; every frame is sampled from the original extraction rather than the preceding frame. The destination illumination is applied afterward.

The final frame is the original source plus a soft-matte-weighted difference between the rotated and initial relit textures. The zero-angle difference vanishes, and source detail outside the matte is preserved exactly. The platform, contact shadow below the contour, wooden tabletop, and backdrop are stationary. This is a texture-based reconstruction with fitted illumination; the rotation axis is an interpretation of the prompt and is not recovered from a second view. Edge unmixing and illumination separation are approximations, and there is no inferred unseen backside or full dynamic physics simulation.

Run python generate.py from this directory. The script writes pixels.npz, the PNG sequence, the inspection sheet, the transition diagnostic, and the standalone HTML player using the supplied output helper. Byte-identical outputs are reused on repeated runs, avoiding unnecessary overwrites in environments that protect existing artifacts. It then performs the numerical verification and writes .tmp/verification.json.
