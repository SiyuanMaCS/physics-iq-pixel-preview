METHOD.md

Generation method

generate.py creates a five-second, fixed-camera sequence with 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144 and 4 fps. The first sample is at t=0; frame 19 is sampled at t=4.75 seconds and occupies the last quarter-second of playback. Frame 00 uses the input pixels directly.

Actual pixel method

The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. All measurements were made locally with Python, numerical RGB inspection, color predicates, connected components, and coordinate text maps. No image Read/View tool, network resource, external model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. The saved inspection images were not visually viewed.

Each block is represented by a calibrated projected rectangular prism. Its three initially visible faces are extracted from the input using OpenCV perspective transforms. Source edge pixels from the table or a neighboring block are repaired with nearest valid color samples from the same face. The photographed painted textures are then warped onto the rotating faces. Back-face culling hides faces as their projected orientation changes. A small analytic lighting adjustment brightens the broad face as it turns upward.

The original moving silhouettes and their contact shadows are removed in bounded regions with local OpenCV inpainting. Fine residual wood texture from the unobstructed lower part of the same table is added to smooth repaired areas. The arm's original footprint is repaired separately because it crosses the tabletop and the platform lip; the obscured portion of that lip uses a numerically fitted elliptical boundary and nearby original platform colors. The remaining platform, table, and background retain the original pixels. Unreached blocks retain their source appearance until the cascade reaches them.

The tan arm texture is sampled from the input and mapped onto a thin projected arm that rotates clockwise along an elliptical image trajectory around its fixed attachment. It drives into the purple block, clears the row, and brakes to a stop after a single sweep. Its uppermost free end temporarily occludes part of the wall as the elevated arm rotates away from the viewer.

Blocks tip in the order purple, blue, green, pink, red, yellow. Their initial motion follows staggered analytic acceleration curves. Separating-axis tests on idealized rigid two-dimensional cross-sections limit each angle against its left neighbor; binary searches find the contact angle. This prevents the blocks from passing through one another in the inferred cross-section model and produces an overlapping resting line. Yellow lies flat; the other blocks rest against their neighbors at approximately 65–66 degrees from vertical. Yellow has a small damped recoil before settling. This is constrained analytic animation using inferred geometry, rather than a full dynamics simulation or a reconstruction of unseen geometry.

Moving shadows are projected from the block vertices, blurred locally, and composited on the table. Geometry and face warps are rasterized at 4× resolution and reduced with area filtering for smooth edges. Pixels outside the moving objects, exposed tabletop repair regions, and shadows are copied exactly from the source. The fixed framing never pans or zooms.

Revision and numerical validation

An initial numeric inspection found shaded paint holes in the removal masks. The solid repair masks were expanded, and the sequence was regenerated. The final clean plate has zero pixels meeting the six paint predicates within its repair mask, avoiding remnants of upright blocks in the exposed table.

The final run passed:

  • 20 RGB PNGs, each exactly 256 × 144.
  • Exact pixel equality of frame 00 and first_frame.png.
  • pixels.npz contains pixels with shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32, encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • Every PNG agrees exactly with the corresponding packed tensor slice.
  • Neighbor collision checks at 1,141 uniformly spaced times pass for the inferred two-dimensional cross-section model.
  • The unoccluded upper wall and lower table pixels remain exactly fixed.
  • Frames 14–19 are identical, providing a stable final hold.

Machine-readable pose and transition metrics are in .tmp/validation.json. The largest average adjacent-frame RGB change is 4.6801 on the 0–255 scale during the falling wave; changes diminish to zero after settling. These are numerical checks, not a claim of visual review.
