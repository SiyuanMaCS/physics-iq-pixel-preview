ball-in-basket · 生成方法

This is a local, source-derived pixel animation of the basketball dropping into the crate, rebounding with diminishing height, and settling inside. The scene interpretation is based on numeric RGB observations: the source ball overlaps the projected crate rim, and an orange patch is already visible through its front lattice. The five-second continuation is an inferred physical outcome, rather than observed footage.

Input inspection

Only prompt.txt and first_frame.png supplied scene information. The image was loaded with Pillow and inspected through array dimensions, RGB samples, chromatic connected components, row extents, least-squares ellipse fitting, and textual numeric classifications. No image Read/View tool was used. The generated contact sheets were saved but were not visually reviewed.

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code, with scene measurements and a 20-frame event plan. The ball's measured starting center is (108.6343,39.7453), with radii (29.0260,31.3302) pixels.

Pixel construction

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy for local pixel operations. It preserves the entire first frame as an exact RGB copy.

The missing wall behind the initial ball is reconstructed with a cubic polynomial fitted to nearby unoccluded gray-wall samples. A small residual texture is copied from the same wall. A narrow rear crate lip, formerly hidden by the ball, is reconstructed from the measured sloping upper edge with locally shaded black pixels. This is an approximation of unobserved background geometry.

The moving ball's color texture is extracted directly from the source. An ellipse and orange-to-gray boundary measurements estimate its antialias matte. Its visible seams, printed marks, source shading, and fine texture move with the ball. The source does not show the entire lower sphere: that missing surface is completed by reflecting the nearest unoccluded texture band, with additional lower-hemisphere shading. The completed lower texture is visible only through the existing crate apertures.

The front crate rail stays in front of the ball. Neutral brighter lattice pixels provide estimated transmission masks; the orange source patch at y=65–75 provides another inferred opening. Original dark plastic ribs remain fixed. The original orange color spill on the immediate rail is neutralized when the ball moves. Lighting becomes dimmer below the rim and near the sphere's contact region. These masks and lighting values are inferred from RGB data, rather than a measured 3D reconstruction.

The sphere layer is sampled at four times the output resolution in each direction and averaged to 256×144. Stationary source pixels are composited at their original resolution. A numeric revision removed unnecessary changes caused by resampling the static background and crate texture.

Motion

Motion uses a 2,000-Hz local analytic integration with downward acceleration 200 pixels/s², zero initial vertical velocity, and floor contact at center y=101.8. The measured sphere radius puts its bottom near the crate's lower edge. Restitution coefficients decrease through 0.82, 0.58, 0.45, 0.35, and 0.18. Contact times are approximately 0.7880, 2.0805, 2.8305, 3.1685, and 3.2870 seconds. The ball then rests.

There is about 1.3 pixels of horizontal inward drift and about 10 degrees of restrained texture rotation. Brief contact compression is evaluated at each output sample; since contacts occur between the sparse 4-fps samples, compression has little visible effect. The crate and table are stationary. The final samples are identical after the ball settles.

Twenty samples are saved at elapsed times 0.00–4.75 seconds, spaced by 0.25 seconds. Each is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving 4 fps and a five-second sequence.

Validation

Every saved PNG was decoded and compared to both its generated RGB frame and its tensor slice. Frame 00 matches the source pixels exactly. The HTML player's first frame and all nineteen delta packets were independently reconstructed in Python and match every tensor slice.

The table, crate feet, distant wall, left fixed strip, and outer background remain byte-exact in the checked stationary regions (all pixels at y≥116, x≤53, or x≥156). Untouched pixels elsewhere are also copied directly from the source. Frames 16–19 are identical, and adjacent changed-pixel counts decrease to zero as the ball settles. These are numerical checks; no visual-review claim is made.
