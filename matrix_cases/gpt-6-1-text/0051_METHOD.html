OBSERVATION.md

Numeric scene observation and event plan

The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The PNG was inspected using local Python RGB arrays, coordinate samples, threshold components, and text summaries. No image Read/View tool was used.

The input is 256 × 144 RGB. The locked camera sees a brown wooden tabletop below a blue-grey background. The table's far edge runs approximately through (8, 52), (71, 28), and (202, 43). The large dark platform occupies roughly x=192–255, y=43–83 and is clipped at the right border. Its visible top is blue-black (about RGB 52, 59, 65) and its front is nearly black (about RGB 12, 11, 9). The green attachment around (211, 57) is retained as part of the existing setup.

Seven distinguishable domino silhouettes occur in two separated groups along a diagonal from lower right to upper left. The left group has black, white, black, white pieces at approximate base centers (68, 62), (81, 65), (90, 67), and (102, 70). These remain upright because of the gap. The right group has black, white, black pieces, read from right to left, at bases near (159, 82), (146, 81), and (134, 78). Their visible heights are about 22 pixels and their transverse edge projects about (7, −4) pixels. The first black piece already has its top displaced leftward from its base; its upper silhouette is partly hidden by the stick. The gap between the nearest bases of the two groups is about 32 pixels.

The narrow tan stick runs from the platform attachment near (212, 57) to about (151, 64), with width roughly 2–3 pixels. Samples on it are approximately RGB (185, 174, 137), compared with the adjacent tabletop's RGB (130, 116, 85). Its clockwise motion is modeled in the platform plane, with perspective foreshortening. It pushes the nearest black domino toward the upper left. Falling lengths on the table are shorter than the row gap; the cascade therefore ends after the three right-group dominoes. No new objects, camera movement, or cuts are introduced.

METHOD.md

Method and verification

This continuation was generated locally with Python, NumPy, OpenCV, SciPy, and Pillow. Only first_frame.png and prompt.txt supplied scene information. The supplied artifact_io.py and HTML template were used solely to serialize pixels and construct the player. No external model, reference imagery, network resource, image Read/View tool, subagent, or simulation engine was used.

Before implementing the generator, OBSERVATION.md recorded measurements obtained from RGB arrays, component bounding boxes, coordinate samples, and text summaries, along with an explicit twenty-frame event plan. Seven visible pieces were interpreted as a four-piece left group and a three-piece right group. The exact hidden geometry and camera calibration are unavailable from the single frame, so the depth, domino fall lengths, clockwise turn in the platform plane, contact times, and resting angles are explicit modeling assumptions.

The static source image supplies the background, tabletop, platform, and untouched dominoes. Small polygon masks cover the original moving stick, the three moving dominoes, and their original shadows. OpenCV local inpainting estimates the newly exposed surface colour. A high-frequency wood-grain residual sampled from clear tabletop pixels below the apparatus gives the filled wood areas texture. The green attachment at the stick pivot is copied from the source and remains fixed. The source pixels to the left of x=112 are preserved exactly, including the entire left domino group.

The dominoes are projected rigid cuboids, with a seven-pixel transverse edge and an approximately twenty-two-pixel upright height. Their broad-face textures are sampled from visible source interiors. Perspective transforms map those textures onto the moving polygons; orientation-dependent brightness, darker side faces, narrow edge highlights, and blurred ground shadows provide depth. Rendering is performed at four times the target resolution and reduced to 256 × 144 with area filtering. The original pixels are reinstated outside the moving footprints so the camera and static scene remain fixed.

The stick is a narrow tan prism rotating clockwise on a foreshortened circular path about the measured platform attachment. Its projected width changes with its orientation. It moves the nearest black domino; the next white and black pieces tip after scheduled contact delays. Smooth analytic angle curves approximate gravity-driven acceleration and contact, followed by a small damped settling term. The first two pieces finish supported at about 79–80 degrees and the final black piece finishes nearly flat. The projected fall length of that last piece does not bridge the gap to the left group. The stick stops after approximately 2.25 seconds and the arrangement then holds. This is an analytic pixel animation, not a calibrated dynamical simulation or a learned video prediction.

Frame 00 uses an exact copy of the input RGB array. Frames 00–19 are sampled at 0.25-second intervals and played at 4 fps, giving five seconds including each frame's display interval. pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 tensor with shape (144, 256, 20), packed as 0xRRGGBB. The generator writes the twenty RGB PNGs, the inspection contact sheet, a transition-difference contact sheet, and the standalone HTML player.

Verification checked every PNG against its tensor slice, verified pixel equality of frame 00 with the source, checked RGB mode and resolution, and independently decoded all nineteen HTML delta packets to verify every player frame against the tensor. It also checked that the left group and table below y=95 stay exact, and that frames 09–19 form an identical final hold. Transition counts and motion parameters are recorded in verification.json. The stick-width revision was regenerated and these checks were repeated on the final artifacts. Review was numeric and code-based; no visual review is claimed. inspection.png and transition_check.png were created as requested artifacts but were not viewed through an image tool.

Run python generate.py from this workspace to reproduce the required outputs. Additional independent player decoding was performed after generation and its successful result is included in verification.json.
