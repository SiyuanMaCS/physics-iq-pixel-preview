+def clean_plate():
+    plate = SOURCE.astype(float).copy()
+    zones = [(56.5, 75.4, 22.5, 12.1, 30, 82),

+    for cx, cy, rx, ry, left, right in zones:
+        q = ((XX-cx)/rx)**2 + ((YY-cy)/ry)**2
+        region = q < 1
+        for y in np.flatnonzero(region.any(axis=1)):
+            xs = np.flatnonzero(region[y])
+            # Texture stays at the same y, preserving horizontal grain.
+            lcol = np.median(SOURCE[y,left-3:left+4].astype(float), axis=0)
+            rcol = np.median(SOURCE[y,right-3:right+4].astype(float), axis=0)
+            u = (xs-left)/(right-left)
+            donor_x = right + ((xs - xs[0]) % 12)
+            donor_x = np.clip(donor_x, 0, W-1)
+            detail = SOURCE[y,donor_x].astype(float)
+            detail -= np.mean(detail,axis=0)
+            fill = lcol[None,:]*(1-u[:,None]) + rcol[None,:]*u[:,None] + .65*detail
+            plate[y,xs] = fill

METHOD.md

Actual generation method

The scene inputs were only first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The generation requirements were read from GENERATION_RULES.md. I inspected the input with local Pillow/NumPy RGB samples, threshold connected components, and neighborhood statistics. I did not use any image Read/View tools, image models, external engines, network resources, other cases, or subagents. No visual review is claimed.

generate.py performs photographic pixel compositing with NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. The original photograph is the immutable scene plate. It reconstructs the table underneath two measured sphere/shadow footprints by interpolating undisturbed same-row colors and reusing same-row grain from nearby source pixels. This is local source-based inpainting, not recovered ground truth. Pixels outside those footprints and the moving balls/shadows retain the source photograph.

Both balls already appear outside their pipe mouths in the supplied frame. Their centers continue along separate projected straight lanes. Grey moves right/down at an initial 105 pixels/s; brown moves left/up at 130 pixels/s. Small constant rolling drag reduces speed. Perspective changes each ball's apparent radius with its projected depth. These velocities and the perspective model are reasonable continuation assumptions, not measurements from multiple source frames. There is no collision, camera movement, new apparatus, or repeated launch.

Each sphere's material comes from its original photographed disk. A local median filter estimates the fixed photographed illumination; dividing source colors by that estimate supplies a material texture. The material is sampled on sphere normals rotated about a rolling axis perpendicular to travel. The unobserved hemisphere is a mirrored procedural continuation of the visible material. The original lighting field remains fixed in camera coordinates. This approximates rolling felt/seam texture without rotating the light. The sphere layers use 3× spatial supersampling and five short-exposure samples over 0.012 seconds. Soft analytical contact and cast shadows, plus a faint warm surface reflection, move with each ball. The photographed left pipe occludes the far brown ball at the end of its path.

Brown passes behind the left pipe and leaves the shot at about one second. Grey leaves the lower/right boundary just after two seconds. The apparatus then remains still for the rest of the five-second sequence. Frame 00 is the unmodified supplied RGB image. The sequence is 20 frames at 256×144 and 4 fps; sample times are 0.00–4.75 seconds with 0.25-second display intervals.

Saved artifacts and checks

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32; each entry is 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 native-resolution RGB frames.
  • inspection.png: selected-frame contact sheet created by artifact_io.py.
  • transition_check.png: all 20 frames with adjacent changed-pixel counts.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-data player using the provided template, with 4 fps playback.
  • OBSERVATION.md: pre-generation measurements and the 20-frame event plan.
  • verification.json: pixel equality, static-region, and motion-position checks.

The generator reloads every PNG and checks exact equality against its corresponding packed tensor slice. It also checks exact decoded RGB equality of frame 00 with the source. Artifact review is numerical only; the contact sheets are supplied for the user's review.
