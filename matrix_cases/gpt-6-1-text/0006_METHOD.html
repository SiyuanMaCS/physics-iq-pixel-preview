METHOD.md

Method

This is a local, deterministic pixel synthesis based solely on first_frame.png and the scene description in prompt.txt. No network, external reference images, AI models, rendering engines, image Read/View tools, or subagents were used. Source and output inspection was numeric RGB inspection with Python. The contact sheets were created as artifacts; they were not visually reviewed.

generate.py uses NumPy, PIL, SciPy coordinate sampling, and OpenCV homographies/area resampling. The source image supplies the wall, wooden tabletop, black platform and its existing shadow, gray paper texture, and visible blue-black grabber texture. Only the original moving-object footprints are repaired. Hidden wall colors are interpolated from adjacent source pixels; hidden tabletop texture is sampled from exposed wood in the same image at equal distance from the rear edge. A small hidden platform-top polygon is reconstructed with colors measured on its visible surface.

The cardstock is rectified to a material texture and warped into a new projected quadrilateral each frame. It stays upright, with its bottom midpoint fixed on the platform. A mathematical rotation about a vertical axis changes its projected width and edge slope continuously, reaching 42 degrees clockwise relative to the source orientation. A small orientation-dependent shading adjustment and thin edge shade preserve its measured gray appearance. This is a projected planar approximation, not a reconstructed or simulated full three-dimensional scene.

The grabber's photographed shaft/head pixels are extracted with alpha estimated against adjacent wall pixels and translated with the ball. Its unobserved shaft continuation and cupping fingers are drawn analytically. The orange ball is an inferred 9.5-pixel-radius sphere, shaded with analytic surface normals, deterministic fine felt texture, and a pale curved seam. The opaque foreground paper occludes the ball and the grabber according to their projected positions. The ball has a subtle local support shadow near placement.

The initial image contains no numerically identifiable orange ball. Frame 00 is copied exactly. To show the requested held-ball/lowering demonstration, the continuation first lifts the initially concealed ball into view, reaching center (129,32) at frame 04. It then lowers the held ball smoothly behind the card, reaching center (114,110.5) at frame 13. The fingers open and the grabber withdraws upward. The ball remains fixed after placement and is completely occluded from frame 08 onward, while the card continues turning. Hidden ball geometry, hidden background, jaws, support position, and rotation-axis interpretation are inferred from the setup and prompt.

All moving layers use 4× supersampled alpha composition and area downsampling at the native 256×144 resolution. Background pixels are repeated into the working raster, so unmodified source regions survive downsampling exactly. Smooth cubic trajectories are evaluated at evenly spaced 0.25-second samples. There are 20 RGB frames at 4 fps, displayed for 5 seconds including the last frame's hold; sample times are 0 through 4.75 seconds.

A numeric inspection of the first render revealed a small residual of the original tool in the background repair. The adjacent source sampling was corrected and all artifacts regenerated. Final checks require the last frame to contain no residual dark tool pixels in its upper 60 rows, and require the outer left/right source regions to remain exact.

artifact_io.save packs pixels into a uint32 tensor with shape (144,256,20), stored as key pixels in pixels.npz. Values have the form 0xRRGGBB. It also writes frames/00.png through frames/19.png, inspection.png, and the self-contained index.html player. transition_check.png shows seven selected adjacent pairs and amplified RGB differences. OBSERVATION.md contains the source measurements and the pre-generation 20-frame plan.

Validation checks every PNG's RGB shape and exact packed-value equality against the tensor, first-frame equality against the decoded source RGB, value range, unchanged outer background, completion of tool withdrawal, and standalone player's reconstructed frame data. Numeric measurements and validation results are saved in .tmp/motion_measurements.json and .tmp/verification.json. No claim of visual review is made.

To regenerate, run python generate.py from this workspace. Outputs are deterministic.
