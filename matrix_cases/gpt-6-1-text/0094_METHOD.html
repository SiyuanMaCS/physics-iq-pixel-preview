  • The dark round platform occupies approximately x=133..211, y=26..81. Its top is foreshortened, about 78 pixels wide, with a dark curved side below. Top samples range from (46,50,62) near y=40 to (39,41,53) near y=55. The very dark lower-left and lower-front portions are consistent with the cylinder wall and will be retained.
  • The small plain plastic domino lies near the left edge of the platform. The bright component occupies x=128..156, y=39..53. Approximate top corners, in order, are (128.0,43.5), (151.1,39.7), (155.7,46.1), (132.6,49.9). The front side extends downward about 2.7 pixels. Typical top RGB is (173,182,233), with a brighter left end and a darker blue-gray front edge. These measured cool colors will be retained, although the prompt describes the material as white.
  • A small metal magnet assembly is visible farther left, approximately x=97..115, y=43..66. A narrow purple-blue part extends diagonally through approximately (86,51) to (109,109). All these source pixels remain fixed.

  • Occluded background beneath the original domino must be reconstructed locally. The surrounding tabletop gradient and platform top/edge colors provide the numeric basis. The platform wall below the original footprint is not a moving shadow and stays fixed.

METHOD.md

Generation method

generate.py creates a local pixel continuation from the supplied RGB image and text prompt. No network, other scene references, external AI models, external engines, subagents, image-read tools, or image-view tools were used. Source inspection used Pillow to decode RGB arrays, numeric row samples, color thresholds, connected components, and text representations of luminance/color classes.

The scene is a fixed source-image plate. The original domino's white-blue footprint and immediate antialiased edge are removed with a local color mask, expanded by one pixel. Hidden wood colors are estimated from nearby same-row source pixels; hidden platform colors come from nearby same-row dark platform pixels, with a reconstructed left lip. This fills 333 pixels. The rest of the original setup, including the magnet, table, platform wall, and existing broad shadows, is retained.

The moving domino is an analytic rounded cuboid, generated entirely by NumPy pixel math. Its length and width are 25.0 and 14.6 tabletop units; the camera's tabletop depth foreshortening is 0.48. Its thickness projects to 2.7 pixels. An orthographic camera maps the tabletop plane to the image, keeping the projected contact center fixed at (141.60,47.10). Rays intersect a rounded-box signed-distance function through sphere tracing, and finite differences provide surface normals. This is a lightweight mathematical rendering implementation, not an external rendering or physics engine.

The top face samples the original domino's RGB texture in its material coordinates. Side and rounded-edge shading uses an analytic fixed light direction and the measured cool white plastic colors. A restrained, blurred footprint shadow rotates with the domino. The object is sampled at six subpixels per image axis and area-averaged for smooth edges. The source platform and magnet do not move.

The domino turns clockwise by 225 degrees over 20 constant-rate frames, accelerating briefly, rotating steadily, then slowing. A positive angle in the foreshortened tabletop plane yields clockwise projected motion. The angles are the explicit 20-value schedule in OBSERVATION.md; this is a prescribed kinematic continuation, not a simulation of magnetic coupling. Rotation of a plain plastic domino is taken from the requested action. The method does not claim to explain a physical magnetic torque on unmodified plastic.

Frame 00 is the exact supplied image and its PNG is copied byte-for-byte. Frames 01 through 19 are composited RGB results. All 20 samples are displayed at 4 fps, each held for 250 ms, giving a 5-second playback. Sample times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds.

Verification and limits

Generation verifies all PNGs against the packed tensor and checks source equality for frame 00. All pixels that change during the clip lie within x=127..157 and y=38..54. The remaining 36,419 pixels remain identical to the source in every frame. The object's projected coverage varies with viewing direction as different side faces become visible, while its contact center stays fixed.

A same-angle calibration lowered whole-frame mean absolute RGB error against the supplied source from approximately 0.129 to 0.089 channel levels. The first transition's whole-frame mean absolute RGB change is about 0.113 channel levels. These measurements validate local continuity and pixel integrity; they do not establish photorealism. Occluded background and unseen side-face colors are local approximations.

Inspection sheets were generated, but no image tool was used and no visual review is claimed. The final validation also decodes the embedded HTML delta packets and compares every reconstructed player frame with the tensor.
