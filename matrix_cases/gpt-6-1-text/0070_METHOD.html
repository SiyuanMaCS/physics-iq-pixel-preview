METHOD.md

Method

This continuation uses the supplied photograph and local Python pixel operations. Scene interpretation came from numeric RGB samples, block averages, and edge differences in first_frame.png. No image Read/View tool, visual review, reference image, network service, external AI model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used.

generate.py preserves the photograph as a fixed background and computes the moving pixels with NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. It draws translucent liquid layers at four times the output resolution, then averages the subpixels to obtain antialiased 256 x 144 RGB frames. Frame 00 bypasses rendering and is copied exactly from the supplied image.

The narrow stream begins at the measured tap outlet, approximately (169.2,60.8). Its width decreases with increasing fall speed according to gravity and continuity; small deterministic centerline and highlight variations give it an irregular liquid surface. The original tap remains stationary and is treated as already open. The stream continues throughout the final frame. No hand, additional vessel, text, or camera motion is introduced.

The receiving glass's nominal surface rises from y=94 to y=78.8 under constant inflow. Its original water depth is approximately 39.5 pixels. Cumulative added height is 3.2 pixels per second, so the prescribed average juice fraction is added height divided by total liquid height, reaching about 27.8 percent. This is a geometric approximation inferred from the image, rather than a calibrated volumetric measurement.

Juice concentration is transported on a 120 x 180 grid using a semi-Lagrangian advection step, weak diffusion, and an analytic circulation field with a downward center jet and side return flow. The concentration is bounded and its mean is corrected after each step to match cumulative inflow. This scalar simulation approximates entrainment and mixing; it is not a full fluid-dynamics or free-surface solver. The time step is at most 1/80 second, and the video samples the evolving field every 1/4 second.

The liquid's cloudy pink/amber tint is composited using concentration-dependent optical attenuation. Original table colors remain transmitted through the liquid. Newly submerged areas are slightly blurred and refracted, and strong original glass highlights are retained as a reflection layer. A shallow elliptic surface, small impact cavity, subtle capillary ripples, and a few short-lived subpixel bubbles mark entry of the stream. The estimated dispenser boundary recedes by 1.15 pixels over the shot using a localized warp of its own source pixels.

The original background, table, dispenser exterior, faucet, and receiving-glass walls are retained outside two small active rectangles. Their pixels are numerically checked for exact preservation. Approximate scene geometry and optical shading are the main limitations of this single-photograph reconstruction.
