METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is deterministic local Python image synthesis using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. Pillow, NumPy, SciPy and OpenCV perform pixel operations; artifact_io.py supplies output serialization and the standalone player. No network, external imagery, reference scenes, AI models, simulation/rendering engines, subagents or image Read/View tools were used.

The input frame was measured with numeric RGB sampling, colour-threshold connected components and text coordinate maps. OBSERVATION.md records those measurements and the event plan made before the generation implementation. The original pixels form the fixed camera plate. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the input. The background, four left dominoes and the lower tabletop remain pixel-identical throughout.

Moving objects are removed with small local polygon masks and OpenCV Telea inpainting. Some masked tabletop pixels are replaced with colour-adjusted wood grain copied from a nearby clean strip of the same input. Original standing domino silhouettes and their local shadows are retained until the corresponding tile starts falling. The platform and green attachment use the original plate pixels; a small attachment mask is restored over the rod.

Three calibrated projected cuboids represent the right dominoes. Their front and top textures are sampled from the source image. Their initially hidden broad faces use source material colours, slight deterministic texture variation and an orientation-dependent lighting approximation. A four-times supersampled polygon rasterizer maps these textures onto the visible faces and composites them over the unchanged plate. The slender wooden rod is a shaded, textured prism rotating clockwise in an obliquely projected horizontal plane.

Motion uses explicitly prescribed rigid rotation curves with sequential onset times, increasing angular speed during descent, deceleration at contact, and brief damped settling. These are a mathematical animation approximation, not a numerical rigid-body simulation or a claim that every contact is solved exactly. The rod triggers the nearest black tile at about 0.27 seconds; the white tile starts near 0.63 seconds; the leading black tile starts at 1.00 second. Their final angles from vertical are approximately 76.8°, 81.5° and 88.5°, producing an overlapping leftward pile. Area-light shadow projections and a blurred contact shadow follow the moving cuboids. The leading tile cannot span the measured gap, so all four left tiles remain standing. The rod advances at a constant parameter rate of 37°/second for the full sequence; its projected screen motion is elliptical.

There are 20 RGB samples at t=0.00, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds, played at four frames per second for a five-second clip. All frames are 256 × 144. pixels.npz stores key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, with each pixel encoded as 0xRRGGBB. The PNGs, inspection contact sheet, transition sheet and HTML player derive from those same generated frame arrays.

generate.py verifies frame 00 against the decoded source, then decodes every exported PNG and checks every pixel against the packed tensor.
