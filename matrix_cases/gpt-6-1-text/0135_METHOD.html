Intended construction

Use manually measured polygons for paper and tool cutouts, numerical background interpolation for the small vacated wall regions, supersampled affine cutout motion, analytic falling motion with gravity and air drag, damped paper bobbing, and localized elliptical ripple/refraction/shading calculations. Keep the original bowl, table, camera, and lighting. Export the required RGB PNGs, packed uint32 tensor, two inspection artifacts, and standalone HTML player; verify all saved pixels numerically.

METHOD.md

PixelWorld-Text method

Generated 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, displayed at 4 fps for a five-second playback. Frame 00 preserves the input image exactly, including the original PNG bytes. The sample times are 0.00, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds.

Actual generation

The method is a local 2D photographic pixel continuation implemented in generate.py, using NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. artifact_io.py and the supplied HTML template only export the results. No network, external images, external models, simulation engines, or subagents were used.

The original frame was inspected by local code printing numeric RGB values and coordinate measurements. OBSERVATION.md records the measured setup and the 20-frame plan, written before the generation implementation. No image Read/View tool or visual review was used.

The original photograph supplies the stationary background, wood, glass bowl, water, shaft, tool colors, and crumpled-paper folds. Manually measured polygon masks separate the two jaws and paper. Masks are drawn at four times the final resolution, with numeric color residuals refining their edges. A small region of vacated gray wall is reconstructed by interpolation between neighboring unoccluded wall samples. The fixed shaft and hinge are composited from the original pixels.

The two jaw cutouts rotate outward around fixed pivots. The paper cutout detaches at 0.625 seconds and follows an analytic fall with gravity and linear air drag; first water contact is at approximately 0.793 seconds. This puts frame 03 in the fall and frame 04 just after contact. After contact, prescribed damped buoyant oscillations, small rotational settling, and slow lateral drift leave the paper afloat inside the bowl. Its lower folds darken gradually, and partial optical mixing represents the submerged underside. Affine cutout transforms are supersampled at four times the final resolution and reduced to 256 × 144.

Water disturbance uses expanding elliptical wave profiles inside the measured bowl mouth. Their slopes produce small local pixel refractions, dim pale highlights, and adjacent dark troughs. A short, low splash is drawn with supersampled translucent strokes and droplets. A small contact shadow and meniscus connect the paper to the photographed water. The waves and bobbing decay toward a calm final frame.

This is a measured 2D construction with analytic motion and approximate water optics; it is not a full 3D reconstruction or fluid simulation. Geometry and occlusion were inferred from numeric RGB transitions.

Validation

The generator checks frame count, dimensions, tensor dtype and layout, exact input-frame equality, and equality of every PNG against the corresponding unpacked tensor frame. Frame 00 is also copied byte for byte from the input PNG. Pixels outside the bounded moving-tool/paper and water regions are unchanged across all frames. Late-frame differences decrease to at most three RGB levels per channel between frames 18 and 19.

A separate numeric check decodes the HTML player's first frame and every delta packet, reconstructs all 20 packed frames, and compares them to pixels.npz. The required artifacts and RGB PNG modes are also checked. All checks passed. These checks establish file consistency and the specified motion parameters; no visual-review claim is made.
