liquid-on-duck · 生成方法

This is a local procedural pixel continuation of the supplied photograph. generate.py uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy numerical image operations. It does not use a video diffusion model, external AI model, simulation/rendering engine, external reference, network, or subagent. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt.

Inspection and planning

I read the source only through local code and numeric RGB inspection: image dimensions, tile RGB medians, connected color component bounds, and individual pixel/row values. OBSERVATION.md records those measurements and the complete 20-frame event plan, written before the generation implementation. No image Read/View tool was used. The contact sheets are generated artifacts; I do not claim to have visually reviewed them.

The measurements show that the first frame already contains a narrow red pour from the blue outlet at the top of the image to the duck's crown. The animation continues this existing action. The camera and setup remain fixed.

Pixel construction

  1. Preserve the original RGB photograph as the static plate. Reconstruct only the narrow basket strip covered by the old stream using nearby horizontal row colors. Preserve the cropped reservoir, blue tap, basket, table, tray, and duck positions.
  2. Use the measured source stream colors along its length. An anchored analytic centerline runs from approximately (127, 18) to (132, 54). Gravity narrows the jet, small sinusoidal perturbations vary its width/curvature, and moving highlights suggest liquid traveling downward. The first transition blends the existing source jet into these fluctuations.
  3. Derive duck coverage from the source's largest yellow RGB component, fill its internal holes, and include the measured orange beak and shaded base. Two analytic curved rivulets travel from the crown down the right shoulder and front of the duck. Their leading edges descend at 35 pixels/second; elapsed contact time controls wet width and film thickness. A soft thin film surrounds the thicker runoff. Optical absorption reduces green/blue under the red liquid while preserving the source's shading and rubber detail. Narrow moving glints follow the same paths. The existing eye and beak remain identifiable through local protection fields.
  4. Accumulated runoff creates an irregular shallow perspective ellipse on the tray starting around 1.25 seconds. Its final horizontal/vertical radii are 48.0/16.1 pixels, centered near (129.5, 104.1). The duck occludes the pool. The surface uses depth-dependent red tint, underlying source reflections, a dark meniscus, restrained specular reflections, and expanding local drainage ripples. It stays inside the tray.
  5. Deterministic small droplets follow ballistic trajectories with gravity and fixed birth times/lifetimes. Crown impact drops and short base splashes use the same trajectories across frames. Three short exposure samples provide slight motion blur without random frame-to-frame speckling.
  6. Rasterize fluid coverage at four times the output dimensions. Downsample only the composited RGB difference and add it to the exact original pixels; this preserves untouched texture even within the fluid's bounding region. A conservative spatial union further ensures fixed background pixels.

This is an approximate analytic fluid effect, not a solved 3D free-surface fluid simulation. It retains the supplied scene and models the requested pour using local pixel compositing and mathematical motion.

Timing and artifacts

Twenty RGB frames at 256 by 144 are sampled at t = 0, 0.25, ..., 4.75 seconds. Each is held for 0.25 seconds, producing a 5-second clip at 4 fps. Frame 00 is the exact input RGB, and frames/00.png also preserves the input PNG bytes. The pour continues at the final frame.

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, each value packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: the 20 RGB output frames.
  • inspection.png: eight sampled frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: all 19 transitions with the next frame beside its four-times-enhanced absolute RGB difference and numerical change statistics.
  • index.html: a self-contained canvas player with embedded lossless frame data, 4-fps playback, frame seeking, replay, and loop controls.
  • validation.json: machine-readable format, equality, motion, and transition checks, plus per-frame procedural state values.
  • OBSERVATION.md, generate.py, and this file: measurements, reproducible implementation, and method documentation.

Verification

The generator asserts output count, dimensions, RGB dtype, tensor shape and packing, exact frame-00 RGB equality, and agreement of every saved PNG with its corresponding tensor slice. It records consecutive-frame RGB differences and the spatial union of all changed pixels. A separate numerical verification also decodes the standalone player's first frame and all delta packets and compares its 20 reconstructed frames with pixels.npz.

These are local numerical checks, not image-tool visual review. The retained photographic plate and the local fluid effect provide continuity; realism of the newly generated liquid is limited by the analytic compositing method.
