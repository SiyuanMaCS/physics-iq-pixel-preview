ball-ramp · 生成方法

This sequence was generated by deterministic local Python pixel operations. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The procedure uses Pillow, NumPy, and SciPy for array processing, least-squares fitting, harmonic interpolation, and analytic sphere shading. No network, external references, pretrained models, external rendering/physics engines, subagents, or image Read/View tools were used.

Observation and reconstruction

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code. Measurements came from numeric RGB row profiles, coarse color means, and connected components. The initial tennis ball's yellow-green component contains 202 pixels and has bounds (78, 44)–(94, 59). Its center is approximately (86.3, 51.4). The cardboard foot is near x = 149, and its long edges slope upward to the right. The ball is already outside the cropped pipe in the source.

The original image supplies the stationary scene. A small union of feathered elliptical masks removes the original ball, its soft leftward shadow, and the weak tint below it. For that area, clean tabletop pixels on the same rows to the left and right supply a quadratic lighting/grain estimate. Fine residual grain is copied with mirror tiling from an adjacent clean wood strip. A sparse discrete Laplace solve corrects the reconstruction to match the original boundary. Pixels outside the local repair mask are copied exactly. This reconstructs an occluded tabletop; it cannot recover the unknown original wood pixels beneath the ball.

Ball appearance and motion

The moving ball is a four-times supersampled analytic sphere. A fixed diffuse light, soft opacity boundary, and measured source texture supply its appearance. High-frequency RGB variation from the original ball is sampled on sphere normals after applying a distance-driven rolling rotation. The unobserved hemisphere uses mirrored observed texture variation. Illumination stays fixed while that texture rotates. This is a pixel reconstruction with a mathematical sphere, rather than a full recovered three-dimensional scene.

Local bounded least squares fits the sphere's material, ambient light, light direction, edge softness, radius, shadow strength by RGB channel, and surface tint to the source pixels. The fitted radius is 8.263 pixels; fitted frontal material RGB is approximately (164.47, 204.38, 64.51). The weighted source reconstruction RMS error is 3.522 RGB levels. This fit is only for the moving representation: frame 00 uses the source directly and is exact.

The ball moves at 72 projected pixels/s on the tabletop, enters the ramp at t = 0.871 seconds, and decelerates uphill at 14 projected pixels/s². The center follows the ramp's measured projected slope, −0.385, with a small smooth contact adjustment at the foot. The radius decreases gently with recession. Rotation is proportional to traveled surface distance divided by radius. These are plausible chosen motion parameters inferred from a single still; neither true speed nor world-scale gravity can be measured from the supplied inputs.

An elongated Gaussian contact shadow follows the ball and shears with the ramp slope. On the ramp it is confined to the measured cardboard color region, with a soft boundary. A faint fitted tabletop tint follows the ball and is reduced on matte cardboard. A fixed 1/120-second exposure is averaged over three ball positions per frame for slight motion blur. The ball is partially cropped in frame 11 and fully outside the image in frame 12. Its trailing shadow fades out afterward; frames 15–19 are identical stationary views. The pipe and blue support stay fixed.

Artifacts and verification

Run python generate.py from this directory to recreate the outputs. The generator uses the supplied artifact_io.py and player_template.html as output helpers. No scene pixels are generated by those helpers.

  • frames/00.png–19.png: 20 RGB PNGs at 256 × 144, sampled at 4 fps. Frame 00 is a byte-for-byte copy of first_frame.png.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, axes [y, x, frame], each value packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: self-contained canvas player with embedded lossless first-frame data and subsequent run-length encoded pixel changes. It advances every 250 ms. Twenty displayed samples last five seconds.
  • inspection.png: eight representative frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: eight adjacent-frame comparisons, including the source transition, ramp entry, frame departure, and stationary end, with four-times amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • verification.json: machine-readable geometry, fit parameters, transition counts, and artifact verification results.

The generator verifies every PNG against the uint32 tensor, checks frame 00 against the original decoded RGB and PNG bytes, independently decodes every player delta against the tensor, checks the 250 ms playback interval, checks that no yellow ball remains in the reconstructed plate, and verifies the original pipe and blue-support connected components pixel-for-pixel across all frames. Numeric frame bounds confirm continuous rightward motion, the incline transition, and departure without a second ball. Dimensions and integer packing are asserted.

Review was numeric RGB inspection only. The saved image sheets were not opened with an image tool, and no visual-review claim is made.
