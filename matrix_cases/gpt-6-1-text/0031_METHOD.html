The animation is a deterministic local Python pixel synthesis using NumPy, Pillow and SciPy. Its only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external references, pretrained AI models, physics/rendering engines, image Read/View tools or subagents were used. Input and output appearance were inspected through numeric RGB arrays, sampled coordinates, character grids, color component bounds and numerical differences. inspection.png and transition_check.png are generated diagnostic artifacts; no visual review is claimed.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generate.py, with scene measurements and a complete 20-frame event plan. The photograph shows the two balls already emerged from their opposite edge pipes, so the continuation starts from their supplied positions. The original frame is copied directly into frame 00 and is never passed through the rendering process.

The fixed photographic background is retained. Only two compact unions of circular ball regions and leftward elliptical shadow regions are reconstructed (1,998 pixels with nonzero reconstruction weight). The pale wooden texture is interpolated from unoccluded source pixels in both directions along three nearby measured grain slopes, -0.36, -0.40 and -0.44. Interpolated texture receives a small amount of fine detail sampled from nearby parallel wood grain, and is feathered into the unchanged source. This local interpolation estimates the hidden tabletop; its original pixels under the opaque balls are unknown.

For each ball, a local numerical least-squares fit estimates its center, optical radius, soft matte and smooth RGB illumination using the supplied photograph. A nine-term spherical shading basis and a blurred circular edge are fitted together. The source RGB is then unmixed from the estimated shadowed tabletop to recover the foreground color. A smoothed version supplies fixed photographic lighting. The small residual supplies surface detail, which rotates by inverse Rodrigues reprojection on a sphere, with rotation angle equal to signed travel divided by radius. The unobserved hemisphere uses reflected source detail. This preserves the source color, soft focus and highlights while making the surface texture turn as it rolls. It is an approximate source-derived surface reconstruction, not a recovered complete 3D tennis-ball material.

Diffuse cast shadows translate with the balls. They are analytic elongated exponential fields, directed left and slightly down to match the supplied shadows, with a compact darker contact component and a mild warm RGB tint. Subpixel bilinear matte compositing keeps the contours smooth. The yellow ball is composited before the blue ball because its center lies farther up the tabletop.

A revision refined the collision geometry to the fitted 50% optical outlines: blue center (120.206,71.460), radius 8.692 px; yellow center (186.077,41.231), radius 8.523 px. The mean radius 8.608 px supplies the common rolling scale, and their radii sum 17.215 px supplies the contact separation. Both balls roll along the fitted line at equal and opposite projected speeds of 18.420 px/s. They touch at frame 06, t=1.50 s, then reverse with equal-mass restitution 0.88. Rolling resistance decelerates each ball at 1.2 projected px/s². There is no pause, time remapping, vertical jump, camera change or reset to the pipes. This is a mathematical head-on collision trajectory; the 2D source does not specify real metric scale or exact experimental coefficients.

The final 20 frames are 256×144 uint8 RGB. Frame n represents n/4 seconds and is held for 250 ms, totaling five seconds of playback. artifact_io.py saves the PNGs, montage and standalone HTML player, and packs each RGB value into uint32 0xRRGGBB in a tensor with axes (height,width,time), shape (144,256,20) and key pixels inside pixels.npz.

Verification in generate.py checks exact input/frame-zero equality, all 20 PNGs against the packed tensor, tensor type and shape, equal approach speed, contact separation and reversal. The independent .tmp/verify_outputs.py also decodes every HTML delta packet and checks it against all 20 packed frames, confirms that both pipe regions remain unchanged, and locates one blue and one yellow connected RGB component per frame near the calculated centers. It validates the diagnostic PNG files and required artifact presence. Numerical results and per-frame RGB measurements are in verification.json; trajectories are in trajectory.json.

Reproduce from this workspace with python generate.py. Run the independent artifact check with python .tmp/verify_outputs.py. Open index.html locally to play or scrub the twenty frames. No extra runtime resources are needed by the player.
