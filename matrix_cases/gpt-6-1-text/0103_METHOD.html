PixelWorld-Text generation method

This is a local pixel continuation of the provided photograph. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No image Read/View tool, network resource, other case, pretrained AI model, external engine, or subagent was used. All inspection and review used local code, RGB values, color-class text maps, and numerical checks.

Pixel construction

generate.py uses Python, NumPy, and Pillow. Frame 00 is copied directly from the source RGB array. Subsequent frames use one fixed photographic plate. A 60-pixel repair, bounded by x=98–107 and y=16–22, removes the original marble by interpolating nearby board/ramp-edge colors and continuing local texture. The rest of the photograph is retained exactly outside each moving marble's small shadow/rendering footprint.

The moving marble preserves the source's muted amber color and small, blurred silhouette. Its alpha matte is estimated from the blue-channel absorption relative to the repaired local background. Premultiplied foreground RGB is recovered algebraically from the source and matte. The lowest portion, originally hidden against the green lip, is continued from the upper silhouette with slightly darker color. Rendering combines 90% source-derived glass appearance with 10% analytic sphere shading. A weak refracted background contribution, dim highlight, and a very small changing internal vein suggest glass and rolling without increasing its photographed scale.

The ball is composited at fractional pixel coordinates with 5 × 5 subpixel samples. It casts a restrained local shadow. The fixed track rim occludes its lowest sliver while it is supported, and the original pink outlet wall can occlude the ball near that outlet. A five-sample 1/120-second shutter interval supplies slight motion blur. No ramp, whiteboard, support, background, or camera movement is animated.

Motion

The sphere starts at approximately (102.7,19.5). Six measured support paths guide it through upper green, purple, pink, blue, lower green, and yellow. Motion is integrated at 2,400 steps/second. On a ramp, gravity is projected along the slope with the 5/7 rotational-inertia factor and mild velocity drag. Between ramps, horizontal velocity is conserved and vertical motion accelerates under gravity. Receiving channels dissipate most impact momentum, so residual uphill travel stops and reverses toward the downhill direction. The short curved guides are represented by effective paths through their channels; outlet lips and channel capture approximate their unobserved depth.

The screen-space gravity is 1,250 pixels/second². This is an uncalibrated image-space approximation, not a recovered full 3D experiment or a measured physical scale. There is one continuous marble trajectory. It exits the lowest yellow ramp at about 4.599 seconds and falls toward the lower-left edge in the final sample.

The output contains 20 RGB frames, 256 × 144 pixels, played at 4 fps for 5 seconds. Samples run from t=0 to t=4.75 seconds, and each is displayed for 0.25 seconds. The refined per-frame positions and phases are recorded in OBSERVATION.md and verification.json.

Artifacts and validation

Run python generate.py from this workspace to regenerate the pixels and playback artifacts. The supplied artifact_io.py is used only for output encoding, PNG writing, the labeled inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player.

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32; each value is packed 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: the 20 RGB images.
  • inspection.png: eight selected full-resolution frames in a labeled contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: all 19 adjacent transitions, each with paired thumbnails and a boosted absolute RGB difference thumbnail.
  • index.html: standalone player with embedded frame packets, 250 ms playback, seek, replay, and loop controls.
  • verification.json: sample states, catches/exits, locality measurements, and integrity results.

Verification decoded the uint32 tensor and compared every pixel of every PNG against it. Frame 00 matches the source RGB exactly. The HTML's first frame and all 19 delta packets were independently decoded and compared to all tensor frames. Dimensions, RGB modes, frame count, packet count, packed value range, player timing, and prompt caption were checked. The repaired plate changes only the documented 60 source pixels; subsequent changes relative to that plate stay within the local ball footprint. All consecutive integration steps remain below 0.30 pixels, including mode changes. Adjacent output frames change 90–186 pixels in this final render.

A source-marble reconstruction diagnostic has a mean absolute channel error of approximately 1.748 over the original 9 × 5-pixel marble crop. That diagnostic checks appearance calibration; frame 00 itself is the exact source and has zero reconstruction error. No visual review is claimed, and the inspection PNGs were generated without opening them in an image tool.
