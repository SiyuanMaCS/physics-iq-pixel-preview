Its neutral grey interior spans approximately x=44–57, y=39–54. The upper-left/middle are lighter (roughly 85–99), the right edge is darker (roughly 45–60). Planned generation method

Retain the source photograph as the fixed background. Reconstruct only the original ball/shadow patch using local wood samples and a smooth spatial correction. Extract its photographed grey appearance and fit a softly antialiased spherical matte. Reproject the extracted texture with a rolling rotation and preserve the measured light/dark gradient with analytical shading. Translate a small soft contact/cast shadow. Restore lampshade source pixels in front of the moving ball. Use deterministic local NumPy/Pillow pixel operations only.

METHOD.md

Method

The continuation was generated entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations, using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy numerical/image-processing functions were used. No network, other scenes, external models, rendering engines, subagents, or image Read/View tools were used.

Scene interpretation

Numeric RGB inspection located the grey ball near (50, 47), with approximate radii 7.9 × 8.6 pixels. The fixed lampshade spans roughly x=74–143, y=20–110. The ball moves straight right at 46 projected pixels/second, retaining the same projected height and size. It passes behind the lampshade, then continues across the right tabletop and exits the frame. Foreground depth ordering is an assumption inferred from the source layout and the requested uninterrupted traverse.

Pixel synthesis

  1. Frame 00 is copied directly from the decoded source RGB array. It receives no edits.
  2. A small, feathered removal mask covers the original ball and its cast shadow. Its nonzero bounding box is x=40–75, y=36–58. A discrete harmonic fill reconstructs the missing wood from surrounding table pixels. Only small-scale grain is borrowed from the same photograph, 22 pixels below. Lamp-colored boundary pixels are excluded from the wood solver.
  3. The photographed ball is separated from the warm table using red-minus-blue chroma and an approximate spherical support. A regularized, robust least-squares fit estimates its fixed lighting from analytic sphere normals. The source residual retains irregular texture and the lighter seam-like variation.
  4. The residual is sampled with rotating surface normals. The unobserved hemisphere is approximated by a mirrored extrapolation of the photographed texture. Small deterministic surface grain and an antialiased silhouette soften the result. The recovered material brightness is bounded to prevent unstable edge extrapolation.
  5. A translated elliptical cast shadow and a narrower contact shadow accompany the ball. The photographed lamp silhouette is composited in front, preserving its original pixels and inner opening.
  6. Frames are sampled at t=0, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds. Holding each of the 20 frames for 0.25 seconds produces the requested 5-second playback. Translation is constant; texture rotation advances by travel/projected radius.

This is photographic compositing with inferred spherical shading and approximate projected rolling physics. The source photograph does not reveal the hidden tabletop, the rear material texture, metric scene dimensions, or exact 3D depth.

Revision and checks

The first numeric spot check found overly dark sphere-edge extrapolation and pale lamp-boundary diffusion into the restored wood. The final revision regularized and bounded the sphere shading, and supplied estimated wood rather than lamp RGB at the inpainting solver boundary. The artifacts were regenerated after those corrections.

All final checks passed:

  • Exactly 20 RGB PNGs, each 256 × 144, with frames/00.png pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • pixels.npz contains key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and packed color 0xRRGGBB.
  • Each of the 20 PNGs exactly matches its tensor slice.
  • Every independently decoded HTML delta packet exactly reconstructs its corresponding tensor frame.
  • The lamp interior and fixed background above y=30 and below y=65 remain exact source pixels throughout.
  • Numeric transition metrics agree with the planned constant trajectory, central occlusion, reappearance, and final exit. The identical transition while the ball is fully hidden is expected.

The image sheets were saved for inspection, but no visual review is claimed. Review used printed RGB values, masks/coordinate extents, material color samples, transition counts, and binary equality checks. Detailed results are in validation.json.
