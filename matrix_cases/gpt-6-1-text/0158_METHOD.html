METHOD.md

Method and delivered artifacts

This is a local Python pixel continuation of the provided RGB photograph. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external images, masks, pretrained models, external engines, or subagents were used. The first frame and generated frames were inspected using numeric RGB arrays, region statistics, and color classifications. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed.

Actual generation method

  1. Pillow loads the 256 × 144 RGB input. Frame 00 is copied exactly, without resampling or color changes.
  2. A soft matte for the pale folded region at upper left is estimated from its measured spatial bounds and the input's RGB differences. Existing local background pixels supply a small warped texture patch, matched to nearby unobstructed row colors, to restore regions revealed as the fabric moves. Unmodified parts of the photograph remain the base scene.
  3. A 59 × 43 material grid maps the initial narrow, diagonal cloth region to a wider final drape. Its final silhouette is a smooth interpolating curve fitted above the measured lid, left handle, right spout, and table. A cubic easing curve controls descent and unfolding over 3.5 seconds. The cloth initially retains sampled source folds and progressively changes illumination as it unfolds.
  4. Each grid cell is rasterized as two triangles. Barycentric interpolation supplies material coordinates for input texture sampling and surface shading. Rendering at three times the output resolution followed by area averaging antialiases the cloth boundaries.
  5. An analytic surface height combines a broad body bulge, irregular fan folds, and a wavy pooling hem. Parametric surface derivatives yield normals. Diffuse shading, a concentrated specular term, and fixed material grain give the off-white silk its folds and sheen. The suspended stage includes gentle sway, followed by a small exponentially damped adjustment after table contact.
  6. Soft projected cloth shadows and a low-opacity contact shadow attach the drape to the table. The teapot's original RGB pixels stay fixed underneath the composited fabric.

This is an image-space geometric construction with analytic surface shading. The support silhouette and motion are designed from the numeric measurements; it is not a solved cloth dynamics or collision simulation. Background reconstruction estimates unseen pixels using the supplied photograph.

Event realization

The teapot stays visible in the first frames. Its blue body begins substantial occlusion around frames 05–07 and is fully hidden by frame 09. The right spout remains visible longer; the last classified red teapot pixels disappear by frame 13. Frame 14 completes descent, and frames 15–19 show damped settling. The final cover retains elevated shapes around the lid, handle, and spout and extends onto the table.

Validation

The animation has 20 frames, displayed at a constant 4 fps for 5 seconds. Sampling times are 0.00–4.75 seconds; each frame is held for 0.25 seconds. Every PNG was decoded and compared exactly with its corresponding packed tensor plane. Frame 00 was compared exactly with the original RGB image. The embedded player data was independently decoded, and all 20 frames matched the tensor exactly.

Numeric color tests found zero remaining blue-body or red-handle/spout pixels in the final cover at their original locations. Mean absolute RGB changes per successive frame range from 1.04 during the onset through approximately 16.23 during the main descent, then decrease to 0.0075 on the final transition. These measurements check exact storage, coverage, and settling; they do not establish perceptual realism.
