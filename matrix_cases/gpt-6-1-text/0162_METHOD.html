METHOD.md

Generation method

This continuation uses only the supplied first-frame pixels, the text prompt, local Python pixel operations, and mathematical motion. No external model, reference image, network, simulation engine, or subagent was used. The source image was examined with NumPy numeric RGB, brightness, and chroma tables. No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generate.py; it records measured coordinates and the full 20-frame event plan. The generated scene keeps the camera, background, table, rails, base, and three middle balls fixed. The grabber releases the left ball and translates leftward out of frame. An alternating end-ball motion models the impulse passing through the touching row. The five balls remain present throughout.

The photographed left sphere supplies a premultiplied sprite with an antialiased elliptical silhouette. The right sphere supplies the other moving sprite; its narrow strip hidden by an upright is reconstructed from a different sphere in the same source. Bilinear sampling places the sprites at fractional coordinates. Static photographed uprights are composited over moving objects to preserve foreground occlusion. Thin paired suspension strings are drawn with analytic fractional pixel coverage. Subtle local shadows are added where the moving balls expose tabletop. Fine sphere reflections come from the original photographic pixels rather than a new rendered material.

Exposed sphere and thread regions are reconstructed using local OpenCV pixel inpainting. The broad area exposed by the departing grabber uses a quadratic fit to visible tabletop RGB values, plus sampled tabletop grain from the same source. The moving grip's small occluded strip is also locally inpainted. These operations are an approximation to the hidden background and surfaces.

Motion uses an initial angle of approximately 0.3369 radians, a 1.24-second pendulum period, a 72-pixel effective horizontal arc radius, and an amplitude factor of exp(-0.045t). The signed oscillator is assigned to the left end while negative and the right end while positive. At zero crossings, the active end reaches the row and the outgoing end takes over. The row slope is 1/6; the projected vertical rise is calibrated with a factor of 0.6753357641 so the mathematical initial ball position matches the measured source center exactly. This is an analytic approximation to an ideal cradle with mild damping, not a full rigid-body collision simulation. Actual physical dimensions and timing cannot be measured from a single image. Three samples across a 1/80-second exposure give slight motion blur while sampling at a constant 4 fps.
