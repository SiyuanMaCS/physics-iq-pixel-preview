duck-and-dominos · 生成方法

This sequence was generated entirely with local Python, NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV pixel operations. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network access, external images, masks, AI models, physics/rendering engines, subagents, or image viewing tools were used. The frame was inspected through numeric RGB samples, threshold spans, and numeric luminance grids; the generated inspection PNGs were saved but were not visually reviewed.

Scene continuation

A narrow photographed wood stick turns clockwise around the measured pivot on the dark platform. It triggers the black domino at the platform end. Four incoming black/white blocks tilt sequentially toward the duck. The nearest white block meets the duck, and the incoming blocks settle into a supported leaning arrangement. The duck makes a small damped rock and returns to its original pose. The four visible dominoes beyond the duck stay upright.

The duck acting as an obstacle is an interpretation of the described setup. Its motion and the supported domino angles are an approximate, deterministic animation, not the result of a full rigid-body simulation.

Pixel construction

  • The source photograph supplies the locked background, stationary objects, wood grain, duck sprite, and domino/stick texture.
  • Local measured quadrilaterals identify the moving domino faces. Their projected cuboid geometry uses fixed foot pivots, rotating height vectors, a table-plane fall direction, and rotating thickness vectors. Original face pixels are mapped with perspective transforms onto the projected broad, top, and side faces. A small occluded part of the nearest white block is filled from its visible source pixels.
  • Each domino follows an accelerating, impact-limited angular curve. The final angles, from the triggering block toward the duck, are approximately 65°, 54.5°, 38.5°, and 18.5°. Small exponentially damped angular corrections give contact settling. The cascade starts at 0.26, 0.56, 0.85, and 1.13 seconds.
  • The stick's source quadrilateral rotates 101° clockwise over 1.35 seconds. It retains the original measured pivot. Small platform areas exposed by the stick are filled with local OpenCV inpainting.
  • Vacated domino footprints on the table are filled using wood pixels from 35 rows lower in the same source photograph, adjusted to nearby row colors. Only local revealed regions are replaced. Soft contact shadows and modest orientation-dependent face shading accompany the blocks.
  • The duck mask comes from local yellow/orange RGB thresholds, hole filling, and a one-pixel dilation. Its original pixels are composited in front of the incoming blocks. During contact it rotates by less than half a degree in the sampled frames around a pivot at its foot; the underlying analytic envelope is 0.8°. Premultiplied alpha and a supersampled source preserve its texture without a discontinuity at rest.
  • Compositing uses fourfold spatial sampling and three temporal samples over a 1/50-second shutter. The original background is enlarged by nearest-neighbor replication so unchanged regions remain exactly unchanged after area reduction. Stationary dominoes retain their original pixels until their respective starts.
  • Frame 00 is copied directly from the input RGB array. Frames 01–19 are sampled at 0.25-second intervals. Twenty frames displayed for 0.25 seconds each give a five-second sequence at 4 fps.

Artifacts and verification

generate.py generates the sequence and calls the supplied output-only artifact_io.py helper. It writes pixels.npz, the twenty frames/00.png through frames/19.png, inspection.png, transition_check.png, and the self-contained index.html player. OBSERVATION.md records measurements and the event plan written before generator implementation.

The pixels array in pixels.npz is uint32 with shape (144, 256, 20). Each element encodes 0xRRGGBB. Every PNG is RGB and 256 × 144, and every PNG pixel was checked against its packed tensor element. Frame 00 was checked for exact RGB equality with first_frame.png.

Further numeric checks established that x < 119, y < 28, and y ≥ 90 remain exactly equal to the source throughout the sequence. The duck's masked pixels match the source exactly in the final frame. Frames 15–19 are identical. The HTML player's base64 first frame and delta packets were decoded independently and checked against all twenty tensor frames. Transition differences were measured numerically; an initial return-to-rest resampling discontinuity was corrected before the final artifacts were saved. Detailed numeric results are in .tmp/validation.json and .tmp/final_checks.json.
