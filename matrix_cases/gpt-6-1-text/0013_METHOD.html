ball-in-basket · 生成方法

The continuation is a deterministic local pixel composite generated by generate.py, using Python, NumPy, SciPy, and Pillow. It uses only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. No image Read/View tool, network source, learned model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. Review consisted of numeric RGB measurements and assertions; I do not claim visual review.

Measurement and rendering

  1. Orange RGB threshold runs measured the basketball silhouette. Least-squares ellipse fitting gave center (144.529, 24.905), radii (27.236, 28.684) pixels. The source's photographic color variation and bright markings are sampled with bilinear interpolation. A small inward sampling clamp extends clean ball color to the edge; the cropped upper few pixels are extended by reflecting the nearest source rows.
  2. The vacated ball region is reconstructed by solving a sparse discrete Laplace equation separately for the three RGB channels. Known surrounding wall pixels provide the boundary values. The top image boundary and the lower edge of the wall region use zero normal derivative, avoiding leakage from the black crate. A six-pixel enlargement of the fitted ellipse removes the warm source edge fringe.
  3. The very short crate lip segment originally covered by the ball is reconstructed from the photographed row immediately below it, blended against the gray background along a slightly sloped edge. All remaining crate texture comes directly from the source.
  4. The ball follows analytic vertical free-flight segments under constant gravity of 2200 px/s². Starting from rest, it contacts the interior bottom at about 0.231 seconds. The first impact has restitution 0.74, followed by more dissipative rebounds with velocity ratio 0.68. Its resting center is y=83.5. A brief Gaussian impact pulse compresses the vertical radius and reciprocally expands the horizontal radius, keeping the lower surface continuous.
  5. The original front wall occludes the ball below the lip. Bright neutral gaps in the photographed lattice, identified by their local RGB values, reveal small portions of the ball inside the crate. These portions use 69% of the source intensity plus a small color offset to approximate the interior shade. The dark ribs stay in the foreground.
  6. Frame 00 is replaced with an exact pixel copy of first_frame.png. Samples at t=0,0.25,…,4.75 seconds are held for 0.25 seconds each, yielding 20 RGB frames at 4 fps for five seconds. Frames 06–19 hold the settled state exactly.

The motion scale is estimated from the apparent diameter of a standard basketball. The hidden background, precise interior floor height, restitution, and lattice visibility are inferred from this single frame. This is a photographic texture continuation with mathematical motion and pixel compositing, not a full 3D simulation. The camera, wooden table, and pixels outside the compact ball/reconstruction region are exact copies of the input.

Files

  • OBSERVATION.md: numeric measurements, physical assumptions, twenty-frame event plan, and the reconstruction revision.
  • generate.py: reproducible pixel generation, packaging, transition-sheet creation, and verification.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 shape (144,256,20), axes (y,x,frame), packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty 256×144 RGB PNGs.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: the first six adjacent transitions, with the two RGB frames and their absolute RGB differences magnified by three.
  • index.html: standalone player with all twenty frames embedded, a 250 ms playback interval, seeking, replay, and looping.
  • verification.json: numeric trajectory, visibility totals, transition bounds, and the results of artifact assertions.

Validation results

Every PNG matches its corresponding uint32 tensor slice exactly. Frame 00 matches the input RGB array exactly. Every pixel outside x=110–180, y=0–113 is unchanged across all frames. Frames 06–19 are identical, confirming a stable settled hold. The table and camera therefore remain fixed. The final HTML payload was decoded independently and compared to all twenty tensor slices; all matched. All required image dimensions and RGB modes were checked numerically.
