+def hermite(t, times, points, velocities):
+    """Continuous position with explicit endpoint velocities, in pixels/s."""
+    i = min(max(np.searchsorted(times, t, side='right') - 1, 0), len(times)-2)
+    dt = times[i+1] - times[i]
+    u = np.clip((t-times[i])/dt, 0., 1.)
+    p, q = np.asarray(points[i]), np.asarray(points[i+1])
+    v, w = np.asarray(velocities[i]), np.asarray(velocities[i+1])
+    return ((2*u**3-3*u**2+1)*p + (u**3-2*u**2+u)*dt*v
+            + (-2*u**3+3*u**2)*q + (u**3-u**2)*dt*w)
+
+
+def flight_hit(p, v, gravity, intercept, slope):
+    # Solve y(t) = intercept + slope*x(t) for a descending intersection.
+    aa = .5*gravity
+    bb = v[1]-slope*v[0]
+    cc = p[1]-intercept-slope*p[0]
+    dt = (-bb + math.sqrt(bb*bb-4*aa*cc))/(2*aa)
+    return dt, np.array([p[0]+v[0]*dt, p[1]+v[1]*dt+aa*dt*dt])
+

METHOD.md

Method

This continuation was made entirely in local Python with Pillow and NumPy. The first frame was read as numerical RGB data; RGB tables, component bounding boxes, and text character maps supplied the scene measurements. No image Read/View tool, visual review, external model, network source, reference image, or physics engine was used.

Source preservation

Frame 00 is the original RGB image, without edits. Later frames reuse a stationary copy of that image. Only the original marble's small footprint is restored: same-row color samples from either side of the marble reconstruct the board and green ramp, with feathering and a small amount of nearby source texture. This restoration changes 59 source pixels. A numeric color check found nine strong amber pixels in the original footprint and none in the restored footprint.

All apparatus, whiteboard texture, reflections, frame elements, lighting, and background remain source pixels outside the original footprint and the current marble/shadow footprint. The moving marble is approximately 5.3 pixels in diameter, consistent with the small source marble.

Motion

The path is a two-dimensional approximation of the visible channels. It uses authored cubic Hermite contact paths, an uphill coast and reversal on the purple and blue ramps, a curved redirection through the purple cup, and a short leftward coast followed by rightward travel on the lower green ramp. The purple contact section uses a quadratic position function. These paths model the intended contact behavior and timing; they are not a full rigid-body simulation of an inferred three-dimensional apparatus.

Every open gap uses an explicit ballistic equation:

x(t) = x0 + vx * dt
y(t) = y0 + vy * dt + 0.5 * 550 * dt**2

Coordinates are pixels, time is seconds, and the projected gravity is 550 pixels/s² for every free-flight segment. Landing times are solved analytically by intersecting the flight with the next sloped running surface. Contact response includes authored energy loss and channel redirection. The source does not uniquely specify channel depth, material friction, or exact physical scale.

The marble starts near (115.3,12.9), enters the top green cup, drops onto the long purple ramp, passes through the smaller purple cup, reaches the blue ramp, slides onto the lower green ramp, then drops onto the yellow bottom section. Its remaining rightward momentum carries it off that section's open right edge in the final frame. Its final center is approximately (163.18,123.85). The detailed frame coordinates and phases are recorded in .tmp/verification.json.

There are 20 frames sampled at t = 0, 0.25, ..., 4.75 seconds. At 4 fps, each frame occupies 0.25 seconds and the playback lasts five seconds. The shot, framing, and image dimensions are fixed throughout.

Pixel rendering

The marble and shadow use 8 × 8 subpixel sampling per output pixel. Sphere normals supply a soft upper-left reflection, dark amber shading, subtle background-tinted transmission, a small rim reflection, and very faint rotating color variation. These are analytic pixel operations, calibrated to the dim yellow/amber RGB values of the original marble. A small Gaussian shadow is stronger on contact sections and weaker in flight. No additional objects or text are inserted into the video frames.

  • generate.py: deterministic local generator and validation.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, values packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty RGB PNGs at 256 × 144.

The generator checks every PNG's decoded RGB values against the corresponding uint32 tensor slice and checks exact frame-00 equality with the source. It also confirms that changes outside the two localized footprints are absent, checks dense trajectory samples for finite in-frame positions, and verifies position continuity at every phase boundary. The standalone player's embedded first frame and all nineteen delta packets are independently reconstructed and compared against the tensor. Inspection artifacts were generated for human review; they were not visually viewed by the agent.
