METHOD.md

Method

This continuation uses local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. Pillow reads/writes PNGs, NumPy performs the RGB arithmetic, and SciPy supplies bilinear interpolation and a nearest-pixel distance transform. No external images, masks, models, network services, or rendering engines were used. The scene was inspected through numeric RGB measurements only. Neither the input nor the outputs were reviewed with an image viewing tool.

Motion

The ball stays centered at (129.45, 57.50), with a fixed elliptical image footprint of approximate radii (19.35, 19.45) pixels. Its photographed face, seam, and felt texture turn clockwise as seen by the camera at 15°/second. There is no translation, camera motion, or platform movement. Samples have angles 3.75 × frame_number degrees: 0° through 71.25° at times 0 through 4.75 seconds. Each sample displays for 0.25 seconds, giving a five-second clip.

This is an image-based axial rotation of the visible spherical surface. It does not reconstruct a hidden hemisphere or simulate a motor/contact forces. The clockwise direction is defined in the camera's image plane.

Source-pixel rendering

  1. Copy the source RGB pixels exactly for frame 00.
  2. Build a fixed soft elliptical matte, with an approximately one-pixel transition around the silhouette. Estimate the backdrop under mixed edge pixels from robust median RGB values immediately outside each row of the sphere. No backdrop pixels outside the ball are repainted.
  3. Fit a smooth illumination field to 877 unmarked yellow-green interior pixels. Eight robust weighted least-squares iterations fit log green intensity using the basis 1,u,v,u²,uv,v²,v³,uv². Black ink, the pale seam, and mixed outline pixels are excluded. This field keeps the right-side brightness and lower-side shading stationary during rotation.
  4. Unmix edge pixels and divide the photographed foreground by that lighting field. Extend uncertain edge material from the nearest reliable interior pixel. The measured smiley, seam, and felt detail supply the material; no replacement face is drawn.
  5. For each later frame, inverse-map normalized coordinates with u_source = cos(θ)u + sin(θ)v and v_source = -sin(θ)u + cos(θ)v. Since image y increases downward, positive θ gives visible clockwise motion. Bilinear sampling avoids integer-coordinate jumps. Multiply the moved material by the fixed illumination field.
  6. Composite the material change over the original RGB image using the fixed matte. The reference reconstruction is subtracted before adding the rotated reconstruction. This preserves the original background and the same soft silhouette. Quantize to uint8 RGB only at the output step.

An initial version averaged four subpixel material lookups. Numeric transition checks showed that its added filtering made the first transition comparatively larger. The final version uses a single bilinear lookup, whose zero-angle coordinate map is the identity.

The platform and the dark contact area below the ball remain source pixels. The physical simplification is a fixed shaded ball footprint with moving visible material; the result is not a reconstructed three-dimensional scene.
