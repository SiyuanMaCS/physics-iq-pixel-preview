Planned pixel method

Use the source image as a fixed plate. Numerically locate a soft towel mask, recover the small hidden background/table/liquid region by interpolation and source-local texture copying, and deform the source towel texture with a triangulated flexible-sheet mesh. Use an accelerating fall followed by a damped collapse onto the liquid surface, perspective compression, small folds, and gradual wetting. Animate the existing tool's terminal pixels. Add low-contrast elliptical surface ripples clipped to the dish. METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This animation was generated with local Python pixel operations using Pillow, NumPy and SciPy. Its scene inputs were only first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The generation rules and supplied output helpers were read locally. No network, external references, masks, pretrained models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Inspection

The source image was decoded locally to an RGB array. Coordinates, RGB samples, medians, color differences, and categorical bins were printed to identify the gray background, brown tabletop, dish, liquid, grabber, and folded towel. No image Read/View tools were used. inspection.png and transition_check.png are generated review artifacts; they were not visually viewed by the agent. The towel's upper boundary blends with the background, so its inferred contour is approximate.

OBSERVATION.md was written before coding and contains scene measurements and a twenty-frame event plan.

Pixels and motion

  1. Preserve the first frame directly from the input RGB array. Use the original image as a fixed plate for the subsequent frames.
  2. Construct a source-local towel contour from the measured coordinates. Represent it with a 19 by 31 UV vertex grid and 1,080 triangles. Texture the mesh by bilinear sampling of the original towel pixels, including its bright folds and darker central crease.
  3. Recover the background hidden by the original towel within that contour. Gray background and tabletop colors are interpolated from adjacent source pixels. The occluded rear dish rim is approximated with an elliptical boundary and colors measured in the source. Hidden liquid colors come from source row medians on either side. A small amount of source-local grain and seeded scalar noise prevents an entirely flat recovered surface.
  4. Move the upper towel down with an accelerating initial displacement, keeping the lower edge near the liquid. Add a temporary bend as the sheet collapses. Ease it toward a foreshortened surface mesh, then apply exponentially decaying sub-pixel motion and a small settling displacement. This is an analytic flexible-sheet animation, not a cloth or fluid dynamics solver.
  5. Preserve the grabber's fixed shaft exactly. Warp the two existing terminal halves apart by up to about 2.1 pixels per side around the source's dark crease. Its colors come from the input image.
  6. Add low-contrast elliptical ripples clipped to the dish interior, together with a soft contact shadow under the towel. Gradually blend the damp towel toward a blue-gray color measured from the liquid and add small procedural fold shading. Wetting is an appearance approximation rather than a capillary transport simulation.
  7. Rasterize moving triangle edges at three times the output resolution and average down to 256 by 144. Fixed plate pixels are repeated during supersampling, preserving them exactly when no action overlaps. Pixels outside the towel, liquid ripple, and terminal action regions are copied from the original image. The final towel bounds are approximately x=112.5..175.8, y=97.2..120.6, inside the dish.

The sequence has twenty frames at four frames per second. Frame timestamps are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds; each frame is displayed for 0.25 seconds, making the playback five seconds long. The final frames show the wet towel settled in the liquid.
