OBSERVATION.md

Scene measurements and event plan

The input is 256 × 144 RGB. Inspection used PIL/NumPy RGB values, threshold coordinates, and textual luminance/color grids only; no image viewing tools were used.

  • The large dark circular platform is at the right, approximately x=119–190, y=43–91. Its upper surface has charcoal RGB around (44,43,48); the front face and shadow are darker. The perspective compresses tabletop depth by roughly 0.5 relative to horizontal distance.
  • The pale rectangular domino is the broad matte patch at x=110–132, y=53–61. Its top face occupies roughly (110.5,53.3)–(131.5,58.4), with a narrow side extending down to y=61.2. Interior top RGB is about (176,171,211), consistent with the frame's cool light. It has no resolved pip pattern. Its left end initially overhangs the platform edge.
  • The smaller bright and reflective setup on the left, around x=82–95, y=50–68, is interpreted as the facing magnet. The narrow colored support/marking beneath it, tabletop, platform, and all other input details will stay fixed.

Its overhanging left end therefore sweeps upward and then toward the platform interior. A short ease-in and longer ease-out give a continuous turn without sliding the entire setup or adding objects. The angle is measured in the uncompressed plane; projection preserves its rigid dimensions and its vertical thickness.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations. The inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt; GENERATION_RULES.md supplied the output contract. No network, external reference, AI model, rendering engine, or subagent was used.

Inspection and construction

The source was inspected through PIL/NumPy numeric RGB samples, color thresholds, bounding coordinates, and text grids. No image Read/View tool was used, so the output has not received image-tool visual review. OBSERVATION.md records the scene interpretation and the frame plan written before the generation code.

The broad pale rectangle at x=110–132, y=53–61 is interpreted as the domino on the dark circular platform. The smaller reflective setup to its left is interpreted as the magnet. The fixed photographic image supplies the surrounding scene for every frame. A narrow background patch reconstructs the brown table and charcoal platform under the original domino, using nearby same-row RGB samples and an estimated platform edge. Only that patch, the moving domino, and its contact shadow change.

The domino is a perspective-projected rectangular prism, with a 21-unit length, 10.6-unit width, and 2.5-pixel vertical thickness. Tabletop depth projects at a scale of 0.52, with a small horizontal shear. Its pale top and darker sides are sampled from the actual source pixels. The visible vertical sides are projected independently and remain vertical throughout the turn. An 8× supersampled foreground layer provides subpixel coverage. A blurred, dark, local footprint supplies the moving contact shadow. Premultiplied foreground compositing preserves stationary background pixels without resampling the whole photograph.

Clockwise angles follow the explicit 20-frame plan: 0°, 3°, 8°, 15°, 23°, 32°, 41°, 50°, 59°, 68°, 77°, 86°, 95°, 103°, 110°, 116°, 121°, 124°, 126°, and 127°. The supported contact pivot is fixed near (129,57.3); the initially overhanging end sweeps toward the interior of the platform. This is a prescribed kinematic continuation of the requested action, rather than a simulation of magnetic fields or plastic material response. No new hands, objects, camera motion, or background activity are introduced.

Frame 00 is the original RGB array, bypassing all reconstruction and rendering. Frames 01–19 apply the projected continuation. A numerical revision corrected the visible-side selection and matched the source side RGB gradient; the first transition's aggregate channel change dropped from 22,358 to 6,262.

Timing and artifacts

There are 20 RGB frames of 256 × 144 pixels. They play at a constant 4 fps, with a 0.25-second dwell per frame and a total duration of 5 seconds. Sample times run from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds.

  • generate.py: reproducible generator, artifact writer, and verification.
  • pixels.npz: uint32 pixels array with shape (144,256,20), packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: RGB frame sequence.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: six adjacent frame pairs and their amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • index.html: standalone player containing the packed first frame and lossless frame deltas, with play, pause, replay, seek, and loop controls.
  • verification.json: timing, geometry, per-transition statistics, and numerical validation results.
  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and planned events.

Verification

The generator verifies all of the following:

  1. The sequence is 20 × 144 × 256 × 3, uint8 RGB.
  2. Frame 00 exactly matches the source RGB pixels.
  3. The packed tensor is uint32 with shape (144,256,20).
  4. All 20 decoded tensor planes equal their PNGs and their generated RGB arrays.
  5. Pixels outside x=105–152, y=40–67 are identical to the source in all frames.
  6. Inverse projection preserves the 21 × 10.6 rigid planar dimensions, and all angular increments are clockwise.
  7. Adjacent-frame changes are local to the domino region. Their total channel differences grow during the main turn and decrease as it settles.

An additional independent decoder checks that the HTML player reconstructs exactly the same 20 tensor frames. The required files are checked for existence and the PNG frames for size and RGB mode.

The hidden background is estimated from local pixels, and the prism geometry is calibrated from a very small source footprint. These are explicit approximations; the contact sheets are generated diagnostic artifacts, not evidence of a visual review.
