METHOD.md

Method

This is a deterministic local pixel animation built with Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, reference images, external AI models, external rendering engines, subagents, or image Read/View tools were used. Scene interpretation and review relied on numerical RGB samples, connected-component measurements, coordinates, and inter-frame differences. The generated sheets have not been visually reviewed.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generator coding, with measured scene geometry and a 20-frame event plan. generate.py implements the continuation and writes all artifacts through the provided output-only artifact_io.py helper.

The first frame is preserved without pixel edits, and frames/00.png is also a byte-for-byte copy of the input PNG. The tool at the top edge, the projection screen, the concrete brick, and the lower surface retain their original source pixels outside the small mug reconstruction and debris/shadow footprints.

A sparse Laplace solve interpolates the screen hidden by the initial mug silhouette, using the surrounding source RGB values as boundary conditions. Very small deterministic noise restores local texture. A difference-based soft alpha matte extracts the original mug, including its left handle, gray opening, muted yellow glaze, and dark base. Premultiplied RGB sampling warps this texture with subpixel translation, a slight rotation, and a short three-sample shutter blur.

The mug descends with a projected gravitational acceleration of 850 pixels/s². Its shift in frame 01 is 26.5625 pixels. The base reaches the brick approximately 0.30525 seconds after frame 00, between displayed frames 01 and 02. This projected physics is an illustrative reconstruction, not a measured real-world scale or an inference of hidden video frames.

At impact, the intact mug is replaced by eight principal ceramic pieces (wall sections, base, rim sections, and detached handle) plus nine small chips. Their polygons and partial curved handle are drawn at four times the output resolution. Colors and fine texture sample the yellow part of the input mug; narrow pale fracture edges and modest curvature shading represent exposed ceramic. The pieces are rendered with individual rotations and changing projected thickness, then alpha-composited at 256 × 144. They are procedurally constructed fragments, not a recovered photograph of broken pieces.

Each piece has a deterministic trajectory integrated at 720 steps/s. The brick's oblique top polygon supplies the upper collision surface; the adjacent surface is seven projected pixels lower. Gravity, outward velocities, reduced rebound velocity, contact friction, and spin decay produce a short scatter and settling event. Soft local shadows track fragment height and their contact surfaces. No dust cloud, additional prop, person, or camera movement is added.

The initial plan allowed motion through frame 07. Numeric checks show that the implementation settles sooner: frame 05 has only 13 changed pixels, and frames 06–19 equal frame 05. This follows the simulated loss of energy and preserves a stationary final view for the remainder of the five-second playback.

Artifacts and validation

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, values encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: 20 RGB images at 256 × 144.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames assembled by the supplied helper.
  • transition_check.png: all adjacent-frame absolute RGB differences, magnified fourfold in brightness and annotated with changed-pixel counts. It is a diagnostic sheet, not animation content.
  • index.html: standalone player with all 20 frames embedded as an initial RGB frame and lossless delta packets; playback runs at 4 fps for five seconds.
  • .tmp/numeric_checks.json: numerical transition statistics, trajectory samples, and validation results.

The generator asserts exact first-frame RGB equality, tensor shape/type, agreement of every saved PNG with its tensor slice, and exact source equality outside the action region. An independent final check also decodes every player delta packet and compares it with the PNGs/tensor. All required output images remain RGB; alpha is used only internally while compositing.
