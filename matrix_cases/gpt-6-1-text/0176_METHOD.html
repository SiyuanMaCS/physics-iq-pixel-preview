Motion and construction plan

Model a slow constant-speed clockwise turn as seen from above: 8 degrees/second, 2 degrees/frame, total 38 degrees by the last sampled frame. The spout on the right moves modestly toward the camera and downward in projection, while the left handle moves away and upward. The painted body texture moves left across the curved visible surface. The vertical axis remains upright at the same contact point. No pouring, lid removal, translation, camera motion, or additional objects.

Use only local pixel operations: derive a foreground matte numerically, locally interpolate the background under the moving silhouette, apply a curved-surface inverse warp to the rotationally symmetric body/lid/foot, and use depth-aware projection for the handle/spout. Preserve source colors and measured silhouettes and use subpixel sampling for smooth motion. Newly exposed narrow surface regions must be approximated from neighboring source texture; one frame cannot establish their true appearance.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. The only scene inputs were that image and prompt.txt. Source inspection used numeric RGB samples, thresholded color statistics, and coordinate bounds. No image Read/View tool, visual review, network, external model, rendering engine, reference scene, or subagent was used.

Implemented construction

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy interpolation and distance transforms. It preserves the original 256 × 144 camera frame and constructs an upright axial turn about x=132. Frame t has clockwise yaw 2t degrees, corresponding to 8 degrees/second at 4 fps. Clockwise is defined from above: the initially right-hand spout turns toward the camera. The final sample at 4.75 seconds has 38 degrees of yaw; the 20 samples occupy 5 seconds of playback.

The measured vessel outline is represented by a row-wise left/right profile. The vessel, lid, knob and foot retain their photographed silhouettes. Their source texture is warped by inverse longitude mapping on curved cross-sections: target longitude is arcsin of normalized horizontal position, and source longitude is target longitude plus yaw. A small vertical correction accounts for the change in projected surface depth, using a camera elevation sine of 0.28. Texture lookup is constrained separately to knob, lid, bowl and foot height bands. A mild analytic shading ratio adjusts the inherited photographed light as the sampled surface turns. Source antialiasing is held near the body boundary to stabilize its silhouette.

External handle and spout coverage is obtained from RGB contrast against a local row-wise background estimate, limited to their measured coordinate regions outside the vessel. The estimated background follows the grey wall, mixed tabletop edge, and brown tabletop. Foreground RGB is recovered by solving the alpha compositing equation, and attachment edges are sampled as premultiplied color. Both attachments use an analytic yaw projection of their vertical midplanes: horizontal offset is multiplied by cos(yaw), and the screen y offset is 0.28 × sin(yaw) × the original horizontal offset. The handle is composited behind the vessel and the spout in front. Their source pixels are removed from the original external locations before compositing.

Bilinear subpixel interpolation gives continuous motion. The final implementation reconstructs the source exactly at zero yaw, which is explicitly asserted. The first implementation averaged sixteen subpixel samples for attachments; numeric zero-angle checking detected extra edge smoothing, so the final revision uses bilinear center sampling. Frame 00 is also copied directly from the source, and exact equality is verified.

Limits of the approximation

This is a layered, depth-informed single-image warp, not a recovered full three-dimensional teapot. It retains the existing body silhouette and approximates handle/spout thickness with projected image layers. Newly visible narrow side texture is inferred using a softened reflection of the photographed tangent strip, with nearest valid vessel pixels supplying texture outside the original lookup support. These regions cannot be established from the one supplied frame. The photographed platform, tabletop and surrounding shadows are retained; lighting and changing cast shadows are not fully simulated.

Validation

Running python generate.py verifies all twenty PNGs against the saved uint32 tensor, RGB dimensions, dtype, exact frame 00, zero-yaw reconstruction, and nonzero movement in every transition. Changed pixels lie within x=92–181, y=19–112; all rows y=113–143 and the frame sides outside the teapot stay exactly equal to the source. Consecutive whole-frame mean absolute RGB differences are approximately 0.72–0.81 out of 255, including 0.775 for the first transition. Numeric red-region tracking confirms the left handle moves up and inward and the right spout mouth moves down and inward. The standalone player's decoded frames are separately checked against every tensor frame. The contact sheets were created for review but were not viewed with an image tool.
