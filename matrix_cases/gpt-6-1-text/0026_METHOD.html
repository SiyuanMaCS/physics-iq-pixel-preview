This continuation was generated with local Python pixel operations using Pillow, NumPy, and SciPy. Scene inputs were only first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No image viewing tool, network, external reference, pretrained model, external renderer, or subagent was used. Scene inspection and output assessment were numeric RGB measurements. The contact sheets were generated as artifacts and were not visually reviewed.

The static scene is retained from the input. The initial ball and its nearby shadow are removed in a small local patch using a sparse Poisson solve, with texture gradients taken from unobstructed wood sixty pixels to the left at matching image rows. Boundary pixels come from the original frame. This is inferred background reconstruction; hidden source pixels are unavailable.

The approximately 23-pixel blue ball is an analytic sphere. A robust polynomial in its analytic normals models the source's fixed illumination. The remaining source color variations, including felt and the subdued seam, form an object-attached material map. Inverse Rodrigues rotation samples that map according to distance/radius, with a small deterministic felt modulation. Unseen surface material is completed with the same projected source disc, continuously mirrored across the front/back silhouette; this is an approximation rather than recovery of the ball's hidden surface. Four-by-four subpixel coverage retains soft source-like edges without resampling the whole scene.

The ball moves 9 pixels per frame toward the right, with a downward screen slope of 0.112. Its support/contact track stays within the measured glass boundaries after crossing x approximately 149. The transparent glass remains in its original position. Compact analytic shadows follow the ball; their strength changes continuously at the wood/glass boundary. A weak, vertically compressed sphere reflection is composited inside the measured pane. The ball exits the image to the right, where the pane's physical endpoint is not visible. No unsupported fall or additional interaction is introduced.

There are twenty RGB PNG frames at 256 × 144, played at 4 fps for five seconds. Sample times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds. Frame 00 equals the original RGB array exactly. pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 tensor of shape (144, 256, 20), packed as 0xRRGGBB. inspection.png is an eight-frame contact sheet; transition_check.png shows six pairs and amplified numeric RGB differences. index.html is a self-contained player containing the first packed frame and lossless subsequent pixel patches.

generate.py checks every saved PNG against the packed tensor and the generated arrays, exact source-frame equality, unchanged static pixels outside the reconstruction/motion footprints, all twenty browser packet decodings, and blue foreground measurements across time. Detailed numeric results are saved in .tmp/verification.json. These checks establish file consistency and the intended measured motion; they do not constitute visual review.
