Source observations and measurements

The source is an RGB image, 256 × 144 pixels. All observations here come from local Python array statistics and coordinate-indexed RGB samples. No image Read/View tool or visual review is used.

  • A narrow brown vertical mirror edge/support occupies approximately x = 106–129. A reflected gray field lies to its right. The gray-to-brown transition is around y = 76 at x = 132, and y = 69 at x = 188, consistent with perspective.
  • The main tennis ball is warm orange in this photograph; its bright pixels reach (246,146,70). Its silhouette occupies roughly x = 110–130, y = 63–82, with center (120.2,72.2), radius about 9.5 pixels. Its original felt/seam detail will be preserved.
  • The smaller mirror image occupies approximately x = 181–195, y = 51–63, centered at (187.5,56.5), radius about 6 pixels. It is darker than the main ball; measured bright reflected pixels are around (217,128,69).
  • The orange suspension cord appears near x = 118 in y = 0–25, with RGB around (183,110,52). Its reflection appears near x = 190 at the top and x = 189 near y = 25. Dark and silver attachment details continue downward to the balls: approximately y = 26–63 for the main assembly, y = 27–51 for its reflection. These details will move with each ball, while the visible cord length increases from above the frame.
  • Reflection-to-main ball diameter and attachment height ratios are approximately 0.64. Use that ratio for vertical motion to maintain the photographed perspective.

Intended motion

Acceleration and deceleration are gentle, with an approximately constant middle speed. Both cords remain continuous above the translated attachments.

Planned pixel method

Build local masks from measured silhouettes and RGB differences; reconstruct only the source areas exposed by motion using row interpolation and local inpainting. Extract the source ball and attachment textures into antialiased layers and translate them at fractional pixel positions. Extend each narrow orange cord using the measured source colors and its original top-to-bottom trajectory. Composite the synchronized reflection using its own original pixels. Preserve all other source pixels. Save the required artifacts and verify the packed uint32 tensor against each PNG, as well as source equality and smooth movement numerically.

METHOD.md

Method and result

Generated 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, played at 4 fps for five seconds. Frame 00 is pixel-exact to first_frame.png. The camera and scene remain fixed while the suspended tennis ball moves gradually downward by 24 pixels; its existing mirror image moves by 15.36 pixels. The cords lengthen from above the frame, and the dark/silver attachment details descend with their corresponding balls.

Actual generation method

This is a local, source-based 2D pixel animation, implemented in generate.py using NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. It does not use an external AI model, image reference, or rendering engine.

  1. Read only the supplied scene image and task text. Locate the ball, its reflection, orange cords, and attachment details through coordinate-indexed numeric RGB samples. Record the measurements and the 20-frame plan in OBSERVATION.md before writing the generator.
  2. Specify row-by-row subpixel silhouettes for both photographed balls and their attachments. Estimate edge coverage against a locally reconstructed background and extract premultiplied source RGB layers. The real felt/seam colors, lighting, and metal textures come from the supplied image. The reflection uses its own source texture, preserving its smaller size and darker appearance.
  3. Reconstruct exposed background only inside the source footprints. Use OpenCV's local Telea inpainting, horizontal interpolation through the thin suspension areas, and a measured brown-support gradient blending into the source tabletop near its lower edge. The newly exposed surface is an approximation because the source hides it.
  4. Apply fractional vertical translations with bilinear sampling. The displacement law is 24 × [0.55u + 0.45(3u² − 2u³)], with u = frame/19; the reflection uses 0.64 times that displacement. This preserves approximately constant middle speed and gently eases the ends. The reflection mapping approximates the perspective measured in the photograph; it is not a 3D mirror simulation.
  5. Extend each orange cord above the translated lower knot using its source trajectory and measured RGB. The new cord length has a small deterministic color variation. Composite the reflected object, then the foreground object, over the static background.
  6. Save the RGB PNGs, the uint32 tensor, a contact sheet, a successive-frame/difference sheet, and a self-contained HTML player using the supplied output helper. The source frame bypasses reconstruction and compositing entirely.

The final revision includes the cord-to-attachment junction pixels in the moving hardware layers. There is no added hand, camera motion, ball impact, or bounce.

Verification

  • pixels.npz contains key pixels, dtype uint32, shape (144, 256, 20). Each value is 0xRRGGBB.
  • All 20 PNGs were reloaded and compared pixel-for-pixel with their tensor planes. All comparisons passed.
  • Frame 00 was compared with the supplied image and matches exactly.
  • The HTML player's initial frame and all 19 delta packets were decoded locally and compared with the tensor. All 20 player frames match.
  • Numeric RGB selection confirms that both ball centroids descend monotonically. The measured foreground centroid moves approximately 24.00 pixels; the reflected centroid moves approximately 15.21 pixels. The small difference from the prescribed reflection displacement comes from threshold selection and fractional edge sampling.
  • Pixels outside the two assembly columns are unchanged. The mean absolute RGB difference between successive full frames ranges from about 0.224 to 0.400 on the 0–255 scale, without a large initial transition spike.
  • No image Read/View tool was used, and no visual review is claimed. inspection.png and transition_check.png are generated review artifacts; validation was numeric.
