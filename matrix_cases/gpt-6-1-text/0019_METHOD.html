Pixels

The source ball and its nearby shadow occlude a small tabletop area. An 866-pixel local mask covers that area. A discrete harmonic interpolation using neighboring source RGB values reconstructs the background. A small amount of high-frequency texture from adjacent source pixels restores photographic grain. All source pixels outside that reconstruction mask are preserved unless covered by the moving ball or its shadow.

The moving ball is a mathematically shaded sphere. A four-term linear RGB fit to 213 interior source pixels estimates illumination from the surface normal: a constant plus contributions from nx, ny, and nz. This preserves the source's subdued yellow-green color and fixed lighting. The synthesized starting ball's interior differs from the original by only about 1.1, 1.7, and 2.2 RGB levels per channel; the actual output frame 00 uses the original, so it has zero difference.

The source sphere's residual texture supplies its felt variation and visible seam detail. Texture moves with composed axis rotations derived from the projected rolling distance.
