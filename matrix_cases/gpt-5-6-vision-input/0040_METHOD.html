cut-orange · 生成方法

This sequence was generated entirely from first_frame.png with local Python pixel operations (NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV). No external images, models, engines, masks, or network resources were used.

The source frame was measured numerically to locate the tangerine and the two diagonal knife edges. I constructed two compact mattes: a gently irregular elliptical matte for the fruit and a measured polygonal matte for the blade. OpenCV's local Telea inpainting was used twice: first to restore fruit texture only where the blade occluded it, and second to make a stationary board/table plate beneath the foreground objects. Both inpaint regions are limited to the original knife/fruit neighborhood.

The restored fruit was divided at the measured blade centerline x = 114 + 0.82*y. The resulting pieces are transformed independently with small subpixel affine translations and rotations. Their separation uses smoothstep timing, starts only after the knife has descended, and ends with a subpixel damped settling term. A narrow, low-opacity brown-orange line appears only when the cut opens. Soft contact shadowing is analytically rendered beneath the moving pieces.

The original blade pixels are retained as their own top layer and moved through a short smooth stroke: approximately 1.9 px right and 6.2 px down at maximum cutting depth, followed by a gentle withdrawal up/left. Bilinear resampling and restrained subpixel alpha feathering avoid jagged edges. The camera, framing, glass board, table, illumination, and all pixels outside the compact action region remain fixed.

frame 00 is an exact array copy of first_frame.png. The other frames were generated at 256 x 144 RGB uint8. Numeric validation found that meaningful adjacent-frame changes are localized to x=106..203, y=0..127; no pixel outside the conservative action region x=98..210, y=0..129 changes at all. Adjacent mean absolute RGB changes range from 0.006 to 0.819 levels, and no adjacent transition changes more than 2,592 pixels by at least three levels.

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNG frames, the standalone player, the inspection contact sheet, and the compressed uint32 tensor. transition_check.png is a programmatically produced false-color adjacent-difference diagnostic; it was not visually reviewed. Finally, every saved PNG was decoded and repacked as 0xRRGGBB, then checked for exact equality with its corresponding pixels.npz slice.
