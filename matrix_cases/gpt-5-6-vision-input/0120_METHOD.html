I used only local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png; no external model, engine, reference image, mask, or network resource was used.

The camera and entire scene are held exactly fixed. The foreground ball was measured at approximately (102, 95) with radius 9.25 pixels, and the reflected ball at approximately (192, 76) with radius 6.65 pixels. A robust quadratic fit to the green/yellow foreground-ball pixels identifies the anomalously dark smiley printing. Only that small printed region is inpainted to form a clean, stationary felt-and-lighting layer.

For frames 01–19, the extracted print-strength map is reprojected through spherical longitude at 4.5 degrees per frame. The projection accounts for each scanline's reduced sphere cross-section, so the face compresses naturally as it approaches the limb. Its image-left travel corresponds to clockwise rotation as viewed from above. The source silhouette, fixed illumination, ball center, contact point, platform, mirror, cable, tabletop, walls, and all other scene pixels remain locked.

The mirror uses the same texture map at smaller scale and a rear-side view offset of -175 degrees. This makes frame 00 show the original plain side and brings only a small foreshortened part of the printed texture into the reflected view late in the rotation.
