OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB, locked-off camera, 4 fps target.
  • Background: softly lit neutral-gray wall above the tabletop. The wall/table boundary is near y=94 on the left and rises slightly toward the center/right because objects and perspective interrupt it.
  • Table: warm medium-brown wood with subtle horizontal/diagonal grain. It fills the lower third of the frame.
  • Turntable/platform: black low cylinder centered near (150, 123), approximately x=116..183 and y=105..143. Its top is mostly hidden by the cardstock; the visible front rim is an ellipse.
  • Cardstock: off-white rectangular sheet in perspective, approximately bounded by the quadrilateral (111, 51), (182, 47), (190, 112), (122, 127). It leans slightly and hides the central mechanism.
  • Grabber: black vertical shaft around x=114..120 enters through the top edge. A blue curved component is visible around x=100..108, y=51..92. Most of the lower grabber and any held object are initially hidden behind the cardstock.
  • Cable: thin dark line on the tabletop starts near the right side of the platform and continues toward x=256 around y=109..116.
  • Lighting: diffuse from upper left. The card is brighter on its upper-left half; objects cast soft, low-contrast shadows.
  • Frame 00 must be a byte-for-byte pixel copy of first_frame.png.

Motion interpretation

The setup is a real-time scientific demonstration. The cardstock is mounted on the turntable and rotates clockwise about a vertical axis, so its projected face gradually narrows and its right edge moves back while retaining the same bottom pivot region. The grabber lowers an orange tennis ball from above the sheet. The ball is in front of the wall while above the top edge, then becomes progressively occluded by the cardstock as it is lowered behind it. Near the end, only a small orange edge may remain visible beside the rotating sheet before it is fully hidden/released.

Generation constraints

  • Preserve the camera, wall, tabletop, platform, cable, lighting direction, and overall photographic color palette.
  • Use only local deterministic pixel operations. Do not use external assets, models, engines, or references.
  • Validate all later frames numerically (dimensions, tensor/PNG identity, motion masks, frame deltas, color-region trajectories, and temporal continuity) without opening or visually inspecting generated images.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

This continuation is a deterministic local pixel construction written in generate.py. It does not use external assets, external models, network access, a physics engine, or a rendering engine.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB NumPy array. Output frame 00 is assigned directly from that array without resampling or modification.
  2. A static clean plate is reconstructed only inside the original cardstock/grabber footprint. Wall and tabletop colors are interpolated from visible source-frame scanlines, with a small amount of visible source wood texture tiled into the hidden table region. The hidden center of the black platform is rebuilt with analytic ellipses and a soft cylindrical shading gradient.
  3. The original cardstock pixels are used as the cardstock texture. OpenCV perspective transforms map its measured source quadrilateral to a sequence of progressively narrower quadrilaterals, modeling a clockwise rotation about a vertical axis. A slight face-darkening term models the paper turning away from the upper-left light.
  4. The orange tennis ball is generated analytically as a shaded sphere. Its color, upper-left illumination, deterministic fuzz texture, and two curved pale seams are calculated per pixel. Hand-authored key poses lower it from above the frame, behind the sheet, then slightly right onto the platform/table setup.
  5. A slim blue-black grabber is drawn on a transparent 4x supersampled layer using anti-aliased local vector strokes. Its jaws flank the ball and open during release. The grabber and ball are composited before the cardstock, so the source-described behind-card occlusion is physically consistent.
  6. A restrained moving highlight on the platform makes the clockwise turn readable. The fixed background, table, cable, camera, and lighting remain unchanged outside the apparatus motion envelope.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the uint32 packed tensor, 20 PNG frames, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png from adjacent-frame numeric differences and stores transition metrics in .tmp/transition_metrics.json.

Validation method

Generated/later frames were not opened with image Read/View tools. Validation used local code and numeric checks only:

  • source image and frame 00 exact array equality;
  • all frame dimensions, channel count, and uint8 dtype;
  • exact unpacked pixels.npz versus every saved PNG;
  • 20/20 unique frame byte arrays;
  • adjacent-frame mean absolute error, 95th-percentile channel delta, and changed-pixel counts;
  • exact invariance outside the x=88..201 apparatus motion envelope;
  • thresholded orange-region count, centroid, and bounding-box trajectory to confirm entry, descent, cardstock occlusion, and final side/bottom glimpse;
  • expected perspective-card polygon area decrease and endpoint geometry;
  • standalone player placeholder replacement and presence of exactly 20 numbered PNGs.

The run is deterministic: rerunning python generate.py regenerates the same artifacts from first_frame.png.
