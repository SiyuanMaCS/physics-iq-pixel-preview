OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

First-frame measurements

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB PNG. The camera is locked off; the wall/table boundary runs near y=42 at the left and y=30 at the right because of perspective.
  • Table fills the lower part of frame. Its warm wood texture, mild lens blur, and the soft cast shadows are part of the source image and must remain fixed.
  • Yellow rubber duck silhouette occupies approximately x=122..166, y=35..91. Its body rests near y=87, its head is centered near (149,53), and its orange beak points right near (158,61).
  • Left-side domino run occupies approximately x=68..119, y=57..93. Visible upright pieces are centered near x=72 (white), 84 (black), 97 (white), 112 (black), and 120 (white/gray edge partly behind the duck). Perspective makes the rightmost pieces slightly taller.
  • Right-side domino/trigger group occupies approximately x=166..199, y=40..79. Visible upright pieces lie near x=169 (dark), 179 (white), 190 (black), and 196 (white side/highlight).
  • The black rotating platform is at the upper right: top ellipse approximately x=165..221, y=20..40, with a dark support beneath it. The wooden arm descends from a pivot near (193,31) toward the right-side domino group.
  • Dominant yellow pixels total about 1,659 and span x=122..166, y=35..91. Very-dark object pixels span x=77..220, y=26..89. These masks will be used as numeric continuity checks, not as later-frame visual review.
  • Illumination comes from upper left. Moving pieces need soft, offset shadows down/right and antialiased edges consistent with the source.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, real time)

  1. Exact untouched source frame.
  2. Wooden arm begins a small clockwise sweep; all dominoes upright.
  3. Arm advances toward the nearest right domino.
  4. Arm makes contact; the first (rightmost) domino starts leaning left.
  5. First domino falls left and contacts its neighbor.
  6. Second right-side domino tilts left; the arm continues past the contact point.
  7. Second domino is low; third domino begins to lean toward the duck.
  8. Third domino falls against the duck's right side; the duck begins to tip/slide left.
  9. Duck rocks left a few pixels while the right-side dominoes settle.
  10. Duck leans farther left and reaches the nearest left-side domino.
  11. The domino immediately left of the duck starts falling left.
  12. Next left-side domino begins to tilt as the previous one descends.
  13. Cascade advances one position left.
  14. Cascade advances to the next black domino.
  15. Leftmost white domino begins to fall.
  16. Leftmost domino reaches the tabletop; preceding pieces settle into an overlapping row.
  17. Duck rebounds slightly from its maximum lean; the rotating arm slows.
  18. Residual motion diminishes; fallen pieces settle.
  19. Only a subtle final rock remains.
  20. Stable final arrangement: trigger arm rotated clockwise, right-side dominoes down, duck displaced/tilted left, and the left domino chain fallen.

The motion will be implemented by source-patch restoration plus perspective-aware affine rotation/translation of segmented object sprites. Background holes are filled from nearby clean tabletop/wall texture, and soft analytic contact shadows are redrawn before the sprites.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic 2D pixel construction derived only from first_frame.png. No external images, models, masks, references, or rendering engines were used.

I measured the visible duck, dominoes, tabletop, and rotating-platform geometry in the source frame. Each domino is represented by a small antialiased polygon mask following its original perspective edges. The duck silhouette is extracted locally from its yellow/orange pixels, morphologically closed, and filled so its eye and other internal details remain part of the original sprite.

Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from the input. For subsequent frames, stationary scene pixels remain untouched. Once an object starts moving, its original footprint is restored from a single OpenCV Telea-inpainted clean plate and the original RGB sprite is rotated about its lower contact point with a cubic affine warp. The trigger is a shaded wooden arm analytically rotated clockwise beneath the unchanged black platform. Soft blurred masks provide contact shadows. The duck uses a small pivoted rotation and translation, followed by a damped partial rebound.

The domino timing is staggered to create contact propagation: platform arm, three right-side pieces, duck, then four left-side pieces. Motion is eased with smoothstep interpolation and settles by the last frames.

artifact_io.save writes the RGB PNG sequence, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png from per-transition absolute pixel differences and verifies every PNG exactly against the packed tensor. Later generated images were checked through array statistics and numeric transition tests only, not through image viewing tools.
