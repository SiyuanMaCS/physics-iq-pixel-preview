+def bilinear_sample(im: np.ndarray, sx: np.ndarray, sy: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
+    """Bilinearly sample an RGB image at float coordinate arrays."""
+    x0 = np.floor(sx).astype(np.int32)
+    y0 = np.floor(sy).astype(np.int32)
+    x1 = np.clip(x0 + 1, 0, W - 1)
+    y1 = np.clip(y0 + 1, 0, H - 1)
+    x0 = np.clip(x0, 0, W - 1)
+    y0 = np.clip(y0, 0, H - 1)
+    fx = (sx - x0)[..., None]
+    fy = (sy - y0)[..., None]
+    a = im[y0, x0].astype(np.float32)
+    b = im[y0, x1].astype(np.float32)
+    c = im[y1, x0].astype(np.float32)
+    d = im[y1, x1].astype(np.float32)
+    return (a * (1 - fx) + b * fx) * (1 - fy) + (c * (1 - fx) + d * fx) * fy

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Input is an RGB 256 x 144 image. The camera is locked off and the continuation must preserve the full original frame.
  • A dark baking tray occupies the lower portion of the frame (approximately x=23..255, y=58..143). It sits on a warm wooden tabletop; the pale wall and a woven dispenser stand remain static in the background.
  • The beverage dispenser is centered and partly cropped by the top edge. Its red-liquid region spans roughly x=114..189 at the top. The saturated blue tap/valve is centered around x=145..163, y=0..16.
  • The receiving glass is centered below the tap. Its visible red-filled body is approximately x=133..175, y=20..90, tapering to x=144..161 near its base. The dark tray begins behind it around y=58.
  • The pouring/impact zone is consequently very compact: the nozzle axis is x about 154; the gap and top of the glass are y about 14..35. The saturated-red mask in the full image contains 4,101 pixels and the blue tap mask contains about 258 pixels under the measured color thresholds.
  • The first frame already shows the glass substantially filled. The physically plausible continuation is therefore a narrow active pour with localized surface agitation and internal liquid shimmer, without moving the camera, tray, dispenser, stand, or table and without introducing hands or other objects.

Twenty-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00 (0.25 s): exact supplied first frame, byte-for-byte pixel identity.
  • Frames 01-03: the red stream establishes below the blue nozzle; the first small impact ripple appears at the upper liquid surface.
  • Frames 04-07: steady narrow pour. A slightly meandering stream, specular pulse, and two small impact ripples make the flow readable; subtle refracted shimmer is confined to the upper half of the filled glass.
  • Frames 08-11: continuous steady pour. The stream width breathes by about one pixel, the ripple phase advances, and a few tiny bright bubbles/caustics move through the red liquid.
  • Frames 12-15: sustained pour with a second gentle pulse. Surface highlights shift laterally while the glass silhouette and all background geometry stay fixed.
  • Frames 16-18: flow eases slightly but remains connected from nozzle to liquid; ripples damp and internal shimmer reduces.
  • Frame 19 (5.00 s): a narrow continuing thread and faint settled ripple end the shot, still clearly depicting the dispenser pouring into the glass.

Only local pixel compositing, analytic masks, deterministic shading, and small source-frame warps will be used. No later generated image will be inspected with an image Read/View tool; validation will be numerical and file-based.

METHOD.md

Method

Generation

The sequence is generated deterministically by generate.py from only first_frame.png.

  1. Frame 00 is copied directly from the RGB source array, with no resampling or recompression before it is passed to the output helper.
  2. A conservative liquid-interior mask is measured from red-channel dominance in the source and intersected with an analytic tapered-glass shape. This prevents motion from leaking onto the tray, tabletop, woven stand, wall, or glass silhouette.
  3. Existing photographed liquid texture is sampled with a smooth sub-pixel sinusoidal displacement field and blended at low strength. This supplies refractive liquid motion while retaining the source lighting and material appearance.
  4. The active pour is an analytic, continuous red thread beginning at the bottom edge of the measured blue fixture. Its center meanders by less than one pixel, its width varies by less than one pixel, and its shading contains a traveling translucent highlight. Source pixels classified as part of the blue fixture are protected.
  5. The impact zone uses damped elliptical ripple shading. Three faint analytic caustics and three tiny deterministic rising bubbles animate the filled glass interior. Flow and ripple strength ease gently in the final four frames but the stream remains present at frame 19.
  6. artifact_io.save packs the RGB frames to uint32 0xRRGGBB, writes pixels.npz, writes all PNG frames, builds inspection.png, and embeds lossless frame deltas in index.html.
  7. transition_check.png is generated in code from amplified maximum-channel differences for eight selected adjacent transitions. It is a diagnostic artifact, not an input to generation.

No external models, engines, masks, media, network resources, or image-view inspection of generated frames were used.

Numeric verification

  • Frame array: (20, 144, 256, 3), uint8, RGB.
  • Packed tensor: (144, 256, 20), uint32, with exact 0xRRGGBB decoding agreement against all 20 PNGs.
  • frames/00.png has exact decoded RGB equality with first_frame.png.
  • All generated changes are confined to x=137..172 and y=15..86; every pixel outside the conservative x=128..179, y=13..90 safety region remains identical to frame 00 across the sequence.
  • Adjacent transitions change 878..1,169 pixels after the initial transition, with maximum adjacent channel differences of 9..17. The first transition changes 977 pixels with maximum channel difference 28 because it establishes the initially absent active stream and impact response.
  • Required preview dimensions are inspection.png 1024 x 330 and transition_check.png 1024 x 330.

Run python3 generate.py from this directory to reproduce the generated artifacts.
