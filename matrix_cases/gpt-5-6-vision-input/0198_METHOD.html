+def soft_ellipse(cx, cy, rx, ry, inner=0.78):
+    """Return a geometrically feathered ellipse mask."""
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:H, :W]
+    d = np.sqrt(((xx - cx) / rx) ** 2 + ((yy - cy) / ry) ** 2)
+    return np.clip((1.0 - d) / (1.0 - inner), 0.0, 1.0).astype(np.float32)
+

+def affine_about(center, target, angle_deg=0.0, sx=1.0, sy=1.0):
+    """Forward affine transform: anisotropic scale/rotate about center to target."""
+    theta = np.deg2rad(angle_deg)
+    c, s = float(np.cos(theta)), float(np.sin(theta))
+    a = np.array([[c * sx, -s * sy], [s * sx, c * sy]], dtype=np.float32)
+    offset = np.asarray(target, np.float32) - a @ np.asarray(center, np.float32)
+    return np.column_stack([a, offset]).astype(np.float32)
+
+
+def warp(layer, matrix):
+    return cv2.warpAffine(
+        layer,
+        matrix,
+        (W, H),
+        flags=cv2.INTER_LINEAR,
+        borderMode=cv2.BORDER_CONSTANT,
+        borderValue=(0, 0, 0),

METHOD.md

Generation method

Inputs and constraints

The animation uses only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. It is a fixed-camera, 20-frame, 256 x 144 RGB sequence at 4 fps. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the decoded RGB pixel level from first_frame.png.

No external images, masks, models, engines, or network resources were used. No generated frame was opened with an image viewing tool; later-frame inspection was performed with local array calculations only.

Pixel construction

  1. The source ball was measured at approximately (61,52), with a compact body footprint around x=54..68, y=45..58. Its cast shadow extends farther to the right.
  2. Two analytic soft masks isolate the ball body and its elongated shadow. A deterministic OpenCV TELEA inpaint over that compact footprint reconstructs a clean tabletop plate from neighboring source pixels.
  3. The source RGB image minus the clean plate forms a signed residual. The residual is split into a body layer and a shadow layer. This preserves the original ball's real shading, color, edge softness, and light direction.
  4. For frames 01–19, affine warps translate the layers along a nearly horizontal path. The body residual rotates according to traveled distance, while the shadow translates without rotating. A short direction-aware three-sample filter provides mild high-speed exposure blur.
  5. Frames 07–10 apply a short deformation envelope. At frame 08, the ball is scaled to 70% horizontally and 118% vertically to represent tennis-ball compression against the kettlebell.
  6. A compact fixed foreground region containing the kettlebell is restored from the source after compositing. It acts as an occluder and ensures that the 30 lb kettlebell remains pixel-stationary while the ball and its shadow pass behind its silhouette.
  7. The ball reverses after impact and follows a decreasing leftward displacement sequence, ending near (64,53). The pipe, duck, table, sofa, frame borders, and all other scene pixels remain fixed.

Artifact construction

  • artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, the (144,256,20) uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone HTML player.
  • transition_check.png contains frames 01, 07, 08, and 09 above their 5x-amplified absolute predecessor differences. It is generated directly from arrays and was not visually opened.
  • .tmp/generation_diagnostics.npz stores the planned centers, inpaint mask, and clean plate for reproducible numeric validation; it is auxiliary and not required for playback.

  • Frame stack: (20, 144, 256, 3), uint8, all PNGs in RGB mode.
