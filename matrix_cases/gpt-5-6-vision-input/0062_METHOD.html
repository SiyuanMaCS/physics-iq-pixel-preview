OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • The supplied PNG is RGB, 256 x 144 pixels, and its encoded-file SHA-256 is 2d816526884d7745662b7d7745045b9cb83744be4f6c09ea6beeccf85b18e673.
  • The scene is a locked camera view of a yellow rubber duck resting on a light-brown wooden tabletop, with a plain light wall/background. The only permitted visual observation is the user-attached first frame.
  • A numeric yellow-color selection in the source occupies 1,389 pixels with bounds x=114..149 and y=50..113. This is only a coarse measurement of the duck, not a compositing mask.
  • The strongest image-wide horizontal luminance transition is at rows 43..45, consistent with the rear edge of the tabletop.
  • Source RGB channel means are (106.15, 88.11, 61.49); channel extrema are (23, 12, 0) to (196, 191, 138), and there are 8,132 unique RGB triplets.
  • The prompt explicitly says the duck sits stationary, while the common rules require a static locked-off shot. Therefore no object, camera, lighting, background, grain, or compression change is justified.

20-frame event plan

At 4 fps, frames 00 through 19 cover the requested five-second sample sequence. Every frame will be an exact pixel copy of frame 00:

Frames Sample times Planned event
00-03 0.25-1.00 s Duck remains fully stationary on the table; locked camera.
04-07 1.25-2.00 s No motion or lighting change.
08-11 2.25-3.00 s Continued static equilibrium.
12-15 3.25-4.00 s Continued static equilibrium.
16-19 4.25-5.00 s Duck and scene remain unchanged through the final frame.

This repeated-frame plan is intentional: inventing motion, flicker, or sensor noise would contradict “sits stationary,” “static locked-off,” and “fixed frame throughout.”

METHOD.md

Method

The prompt defines a static locked-off shot and says that the rubber duck sits stationary. I therefore used direct, lossless pixel repetition rather than synthesizing motion.

generate.py loads first_frame.png as an RGB uint8 array and repeats that array 20 times. artifact_io.save writes the required packed uint32 tensor, frame PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. The generator then copies the original PNG byte stream over frames/00.png, making frame 00 both pixel-identical and file-identical to the source.

transition_check.png is generated analytically. Its upper panels contain frame 00, frame 19, and a 16x-amplified absolute RGB difference. Its lower plot records the number of changed pixels for each of the 19 adjacent transitions. All counts are zero by design.

Verification in generate.py checks:

  • shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and uint8 RGB frame data;
  • exact equality of all 20 frames to the source pixels;
  • exact encoded-file SHA-256 equality between first_frame.png and frames/00.png;
  • exact equality of every decoded PNG to its corresponding generated array;
  • exact equality of the packed PNG-derived values to the (144, 256, 20) uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz.

No later output was visually reviewed with an image reader/viewer. Validation used local code, decoded pixel arrays, hashes, shapes, and numeric transition statistics only.
