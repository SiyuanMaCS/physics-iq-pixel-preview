paper-fall-water · 生成方法

The animation is a deterministic local pixel synthesis derived only from first_frame.png and the action prompt.

  1. The untouched source photograph is frame 00.
  2. For frames 01–19, a small mask around the original crumpled paper and lower grabber jaws is inpainted with OpenCV Telea inpainting to produce a locked clean plate. The stem, bowl, table, lighting, and all pixels outside that local mask come from the source photograph.
  3. The visible paper texture is cropped from the source. Orange/black pixels caused by jaw occlusion are locally healed, and an irregular feathered silhouette is applied. The sprite follows a hand-authored gravity trajectory with mild rotation. At water contact it flattens, darkens, loses a little color contrast, and settles with subpixel drift.
  4. The opened orange-black jaws are reconstructed as supersampled antialiased Bezier curves. Only jaw separation changes; the tool remains suspended at the same image location.
  5. The water response is deliberately small for a lightweight paper object: a brief low crown, several tiny droplets, and perspective-compressed elliptical ripples whose radius grows while opacity decays. A few muted glints suggest water on the wet folds.
  6. All overlays are rendered at 4x resolution and Lanczos-downsampled before RGB compositing. No external images, models, engines, masks, or network resources are used.

generate.py writes the uint32 packed RGB tensor through artifact_io.save, writes all 20 PNG frames and the player, produces inspection.png, and creates transition_check.png from numeric frame-to-frame absolute differences. It then reloads every artifact to verify shape, mode, dtype, exact tensor/PNG agreement, and byte-identical frame 00.

Generated frames after frame 00 were evaluated only with local numeric checks (change counts, bounding boxes, pixel statistics, tensor equality, and PNG reload equality), not with an image viewer or image-reading tool.
