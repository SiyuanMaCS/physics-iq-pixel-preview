OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Observed scene

  • The input is a locked-off, landscape 256×144 photograph of a white magnetic board, bounded by dark vertical frame/stand pieces near x=47–53 and x=203–210.
  • The white board has mild photographic shading: brighter toward the upper/right middle, cooler and darker toward the lower corners. This background and every fixed object must remain unchanged.
  • Four useful ramp elements form a descending zig-zag:
    1. a partly cropped green start/funnel at the top, roughly x=89–146, y=0–25, holding the small yellow marble around x=111–122, y=9–17;
    2. a blue patterned ramp around x=125–197, y=27–44, descending toward the left;
    3. a short purple deflector around x=102–128, y=44–64, turning the path toward the right;
    4. a long blue ramp around x=132–201, y=62–82, descending toward the left;
    5. a green patterned ramp around x=68–139, y=84–101, descending toward the right;
    6. a yellow final ramp around x=91–161, y=122–133, nearly horizontal with a slight rightward descent.
  • The requested moving object is a small yellow translucent glass marble. At this resolution it should read as a compact 7–9 px sphere with a dark amber rim, warm yellow body, bright upper-left specular highlight, and a faint contact shadow.
  • The implied route is a continuous gravity-driven zig-zag: release near the top, roll left along the upper blue ramp, turn on the purple piece, roll right on the middle blue ramp, fall to and roll left-to-right along the green ramp, then fall to and roll along the yellow bottom ramp before exiting below/right.

Measurements and continuity constraints

  • Canvas and output: 256×144 RGB, 20 frames, 4 fps, fixed camera.
  • Frame 00 must be an exact copy of first_frame.png.
  • Only a tight local patch around the marble and its soft shadow may change; no camera motion and no global relighting.
  • Drops use accelerating vertical displacement, while ramp travel uses smooth increasing motion with a small rolling highlight change. Contacts are kept close to the visible ramp surfaces.
  • To preserve the first frame exactly while still establishing release, frame 01 moves the marble only slightly at the upper start. Once it departs, the tiny revealed patch is reconstructed locally from adjacent board and green-ramp pixels.

METHOD.md

Method

I generated the continuation with deterministic local Python/Numpy pixel operations, using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs.

  • Frame 00 is copied exactly from the source RGB pixels.
  • The compact held-marble area at the top is reconstructed after release by feathered vertical interpolation from the immediately adjacent clean board and green-ramp pixels. Every later frame starts from this released-state plate, so the camera, whiteboard, ramps, stand, lighting, and noise otherwise remain fixed and the original marble is not duplicated.
  • I measured a hand-authored 2D path through the visible ramp levels. The path uses short ballistic/deflected spans between contact runs and keeps the ball close to ramp surfaces at landings.
  • The yellow glass marble is an analytic, supersampled 9-pixel sphere. It combines a translucent warm-yellow body, dense amber rim, fixed upper-left specular glint, rotating internal caustic, and a small Gaussian cast/contact shadow blended into the original photograph.
  • artifact_io.save packs each RGB frame as uint32 0xRRGGBB, writes pixels.npz, the 20 PNGs, inspection.png, and the standalone HTML player.
  • transition_check.png is generated from numeric consecutive-frame absolute differences. Each tile darkens the scene context and highlights changed pixels, with changed-pixel count and bounding box written above it.
  • The script finally reloads every PNG and pixels.npz, reconstructs the packed tensor, and asserts exact equality. It also asserts that frame 00 equals first_frame.png pixel-for-pixel.

No external model, engine, mask, reference image, or network resource was used. I did not visually inspect generated later frames; validation of those frames is performed through array statistics, coordinate checks, transition bounds, and exact file/tensor comparisons.
