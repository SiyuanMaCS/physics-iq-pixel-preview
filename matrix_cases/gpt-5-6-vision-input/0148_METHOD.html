+    # clean perimeter.  A soft inner blend prevents a synthetic ellipse edge.

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Source frame: RGB PNG, 256×144 pixels.
  • Camera: static, low oblique view across a light wood tabletop; no camera motion is planned.
  • Black tube: enters from the left image boundary and occupies approximately x=0..13, y=51..83, aimed horizontally to the right.
  • Brown tennis ball: already clear of the tube in frame 00, with its visible center approximately (93, 60) and a soft ball/shadow footprint of about 30×28 pixels. The solid-looking core is roughly 17–20 pixels across.
  • White lampshade: fixed near the upper center, approximately x=99..154, y=0..39. Its lower edge is well above the ball's horizontal path.
  • Table: fills nearly all of the foreground, with horizontal wood grain and a softly varying illumination field. The ball path is centered near y=60.
  • Frame 00 must remain byte-for-byte identical to first_frame.png.

Physical interpretation

The tube has just launched the ball. The ball travels straight to camera-right across the tabletop with mild frictional deceleration. Its center stays at essentially the same vertical image coordinate, while its surface rotates consistently with rightward rolling. A soft contact/cast shadow travels with it. The tube, shade, table, lighting, and camera remain locked.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual pixel-generation method

The continuation is generated entirely with local deterministic Python pixel operations (numpy, Pillow, and OpenCV). No external models, engines, masks, reference images, or network resources are used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is assigned directly from that array without resampling or editing.
  2. A measured elliptical mask around the source ball and its compact shadow is filled from its clean perimeter with OpenCV's Telea inpainting. Only the masked area is blended into the locked source image; all other background pixels remain unchanged.
  3. The source ball is represented as the RGB residual between the original image and the reconstructed tabletop. A darkness-derived smooth matte removes tiny wood/interpolation mismatches while retaining the ball's fuzzy edge and soft shadow. This is a lighting-relative residual rather than a rectangular crop, so destination wood texture remains visible and no crop boundary travels with the object.
  4. Ball center motion follows x(t) = 93 + 10.75t - 0.0525t², y(t) = 60, for integer frames t=1..19. The positive but gently declining frame-to-frame displacement models rolling friction and carries the ball completely past the right image boundary.
  5. The residual is split into a low-frequency spherical body/shadow and a high-frequency core texture. The body and shadow translate without changing shape. Only the internal texture rotates clockwise by the no-slip angle -(x-93)/10.5 radians. This avoids rotating the cast shadow or making the silhouette wobble.
  6. Each output frame averages three nearby subpixel shutter samples (-0.45, 0, +0.45 pixels) to retain the optically soft character of the source ball without creating a long streak.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the 20 PNG frames, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone HTML player. transition_check.png is a fixed-gain (3×) adjacent-frame absolute-difference sheet made for transition auditing.

Numeric revision and verification

The initial implementation rotated the entire ball/shadow footprint. Numeric bounding-box checks showed avoidable vertical footprint variation, so the compositor was revised to rotate only the core texture and to suppress low-amplitude background residuals.

Final checks performed by generate.py:

  • exactly 20 frames, each RGB uint8, 256×14;
  • frame 00 is pixel-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every saved PNG is pixel-identical to its in-memory frame;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and every entry equals the corresponding packed PNG pixel;
  • frames 01–18 have one monotonic right-moving localized residual, with measured energy stable until it is cropped by the right edge;
  • frame 19 equals the reconstructed static tabletop after the ball has fully exited;
  • 32,028 pixels outside the reconstruction/motion union remain exactly constant through frames 01–19.

No generated frame, inspection sheet, or transition sheet was visually reviewed with an image viewing tool; later-output assessment used local numeric checks only, as requested.
