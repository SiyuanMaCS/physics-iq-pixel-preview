+def ellipse_mask(center, radii, feather=2.0):
+    """Soft ellipse with value one in the interior and a cosine feather."""
+    yy, xx = np.mgrid[:H, :W]
+    cx, cy = center
+    rx, ry = radii
+    d = np.sqrt(((xx - cx) / rx) ** 2 + ((yy - cy) / ry) ** 2)
+    inner = 1.0 - feather / min(rx, ry)
+    out = np.zeros((H, W), np.float32)
+    out[d <= inner] = 1.0
+    band = (d > inner) & (d < 1.0)
+    u = (d[band] - inner) / (1.0 - inner)
+    out[band] = 0.5 + 0.5 * np.cos(np.pi * u)
+    return out

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured source frame

  • Source: first_frame.png, RGB, 256 x 144.
  • Camera: fixed, slightly oblique overhead view. The tabletop is a pale wood plane with horizontal grain and a mild illumination gradient; it fills most of the frame.
  • Fixed apparatus: a dark pipe/nozzle intrudes at the upper-left edge, and a second dark pipe intrudes from the right edge around y=69. Neither apparatus nor the camera should move.
  • Blue ball: approximately centered at (76, 50), visible radius about 8 px. Its saturated core is blue, with a dark lower/right side, a small cool highlight, and a soft tabletop contact shadow.
  • Yellow ball: approximately centered at (149, 58), visible radius about 9 px. Its core is yellow-green, with a bright upper-left highlight, darker lower/right shading, and a soft tabletop contact shadow.
  • The center-to-center line is slightly diagonal: from blue to yellow it changes by roughly (+73, +8), so motion should follow that same tabletop line rather than a perfectly horizontal screen line.
  • At frame 00 the balls are separated by about 73 px. Both are already clear of the pipes, so the supplied frame is treated as the observed instant shortly after emergence.

Physical interpretation

The balls have equal apparent size/mass and approach one another at equal constant screen speed. A near-elastic, head-on collision exchanges their velocities. Thus each ball reverses along the same line after contact, with no overlap or teleportation. Motion is sampled at a constant 4 fps; modest directional blur is applied only to moving ball texture, while the tabletop and pipes remain pixel-locked.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

The animation is generated locally by generate.py using NumPy, OpenCV, and Pillow. No external model, engine, reference image, mask, or network resource is used.

  1. first_frame.png is read as the sole visual source. Frame 00 is copied from it without any resampling or modification.
  2. The blue and yellow ball centers are measured from their color contrast at approximately (76.21, 50.01) and (148.91, 58.05). Their shared motion axis is the normalized line between these centers.
  3. Elliptical masks covering each ball and its soft contact shadow are inpainted with OpenCV's Telea interpolation to create a fixed clean tabletop plate. Fine wood-grain residual from a nearby strip is mixed into only the repaired areas at low strength.
  4. Each real ball is represented as the masked RGB residual between the source image and the clean plate. This preserves the source ball's highlight, fuzzy colored edge, body shading, and contact shadow instead of redrawing it as a synthetic disk.
  5. The ball residuals are translated with subpixel affine warps. Three short, weighted temporal samples form a restrained directional shutter blur. The contact frame uses a smaller shutter spread.
  6. Both centers advance at exactly 7 px/frame along the same axis in opposite directions. Their center separation therefore closes at 14 px/frame from 73.14 px to 17.14 px. At frame 04 the edges meet without center overlap. Equal-mass near-elastic collision is modeled by exchanging/reversing velocities; separation then grows at exactly 14 px/frame.
  7. The camera, clean tabletop, lighting, wood grain, and pipes remain locked. Near the final frames, a thresholded mask restricted to the known edge hardware regions reapplies source pipe pixels over the traveling balls, creating partial occlusion as they pass under the pipe mouths.
  8. artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. transition_check.png is a code-generated diagnostic contact strip with amplified adjacent-frame differences; it was not used as an image-viewing input.

Timing and event shape

  • 20 frames, 256 x 144 RGB, sampled at 4 fps for a 5-second presentation.
  • Frames 00-04: equal-speed approach.
  • Frame 04: head-on contact at one apparent ball diameter.
  • Frames 05-19: equal-speed elastic separation, ending with edge/pipe occlusion.

Verification performed

  • Asserted frame array shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and uint8 type.
  • Asserted frames/00.png is byte-for-byte pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • Repacked every RGB PNG as 0xRRGGBB and asserted exact equality with the uint32 tensor in pixels.npz (shape (144, 256, 20)).
  • Reloaded and compared all 20 saved PNGs against the generated frame array.
  • Asserted the analytic center separation changes by exactly 14 px/frame before and after contact and remains above 17 px at contact.
  • Numerically tracked color-weighted centers in the saved frames. Through contact they agree with the analytic centers within about 0.4 px; colored mass falls only when edge hardware deliberately occludes a ball.
  • Checked a conservative 23,322-pixel region outside the balls' swept corridor and edge apparatus: it remains exactly unchanged across generated frames.
  • Checked transition footprints: 624-1,405 pixels change per adjacent pair, localized to the balls, their repaired source regions on the first transition, and final pipe occlusion.

No later generated frame was inspected with an image Read/View tool. Later-output assessment used only local code and numeric pixel checks, as requested.
