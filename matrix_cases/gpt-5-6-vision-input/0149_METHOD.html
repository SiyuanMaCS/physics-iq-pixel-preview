OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

First-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB pixels. The camera is locked off and the tabletop fills the frame from approximately y=39 downward.
  • Lighting: soft, warm indoor light from above/front-left. The table is pale beige wood with low-contrast horizontal grain and a gentle left-to-right brightness variation.
  • Black tube: enters from the left edge, points horizontally right, and occupies approximately x=0..22, y=40..72. Its visible circular mouth is near (11, 56), with a dark interior and a narrow highlight on the upper rim.
  • White lampshade: fixed near the back of the table, approximately x=112..167, y=3..43. Its lower edge is horizontal around y=43, and it must remain fully stationary.
  • Brown tennis ball: approximately 20 x 20 pixels, centered near (108, 59) in frame 00. It is already clear of the tube and rests/rolls on a nearly horizontal path around y=60. The contact/shadow region falls near y=67..70.
  • Occlusion order: tabletop/background, then the ball's soft contact shadow, then the ball. The tube and lampshade are already baked into the static background and remain unchanged.
  • Intended motion: the ball continues rightward in a straight line. With a 20-pixel diameter and a center displacement of about 7 pixels per 0.25 s, the apparent no-slip rotation is roughly 0.7 radians per frame.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is generated entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations in generate.py. No external model, engine, reference image, mask, or network resource is used.

  1. The supplied first_frame.png is loaded as RGB and copied byte-for-byte to output frame 00.
  2. A clean static plate is reconstructed only beneath the initial ball. The reconstruction combines OpenCV Telea inpainting with a row-wise quadratic estimate from unobstructed table pixels on both sides, retaining a small amount of inpainted texture. A soft elliptical feather limits all reconstruction to the original ball/shadow area.
  3. The original ball is extracted with an antialiased elliptical matte. Edge colors are uncomposited against the clean plate to prevent a halo. Its appearance is split into a normalized Gaussian-blurred lighting/shading field and a fine texture residual.
  4. For frames 01–19, the ball center advances exactly 7 pixels per frame along the horizontal path from x=115.5 to x=241.5 at y=59.3. The fine texture rotates by displacement divided by the measured 10.7-pixel rolling radius, while the broad lighting field remains fixed in screen/world lighting. This conveys rolling rather than sliding. A compact analytic Gaussian contact shadow moves with the ball.
  5. The ball is alpha-composited over the unchanged plate; at the final frame it is naturally clipped by the right frame edge.
  6. artifact_io.py packs the exact RGB frame array into the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, PNG sequence, contact sheet, and standalone HTML player. transition_check.png contains 4x-gain absolute RGB differences for six adjacent-frame pairs.

Validation is performed numerically: dimensions/dtypes, exact frame-00 equality, PNG-to-tensor equality, changed-pixel locality, static-region invariance, motion centroids, and adjacent-frame continuity. The generated inspection images are not used as visual input during revision.
