OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

Measured scene

  • Source is an RGB 256 x 144 photograph. The camera is locked off and the entire source image must remain unchanged except for the animated marble and a very small local contact/drop shadow.
  • The usable whiteboard face occupies approximately x=38..165; a dark vertical board support/frame is visible at x=21..29, and the right board/frame boundary lies around x=163..175. The wall beyond it fills the right side.
  • Colored magnetic guides visible on the whiteboard, measured in source-pixel coordinates:
    • cropped teal curved entrance: about x=51..84, y=0..15;
    • olive curved guide: about x=80..115, y=18..32;
    • upper violet/blue straight guide: about x=110..167, y=33..49, with its working surface descending toward screen-left;
    • yellow straight guide: about x=61..123, y=48..67, descending toward screen-right;
    • blue straight guide: about x=113..164, y=67..82, descending toward screen-left;
    • green straight guide: about x=63..120, y=86..99, descending toward screen-right;
    • purple catch/terminal guide: about x=117..140, y=102..120.
  • A narrow pale vertical streak near x=132..135, y=55..70 is part of the photographed scene (a reflection/highlight), not something to erase or animate.
  • Lighting is dim and warm/neutral. The requested marble is not clearly present in the source frame, so frame 00 is treated as the moment immediately before it enters from the cropped top entrance.
  • The marble will be small (~7 px diameter), translucent amber/yellow rather than a flat disk. It will have a dark amber rim, golden body, a tiny warm specular highlight, transmitted light on the lower-right, and a faint offset shadow. Its size stays constant because all motion is on the board plane.

Numeric inspection targets

  • frames/00.png must match first_frame.png byte-for-byte at the RGB pixel-array level.
  • Exactly 20 RGB PNGs, all 256 x 144; pixels.npz must have shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32 with packed 0xRRGGBB values.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

I used local Python (NumPy and Pillow) for deterministic pixel compositing. No external model, network resource, physics engine, renderer, reference image, mask, or other case was used.

  1. first_frame.png is decoded to a 144 x 256 x 3 RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is a direct copy of that array.
  2. A 19-position, subpixel center path was authored from the ramp and catch coordinates measured in the source. It enters at the cropped teal guide, follows the upper guides, makes a short gravity-like launch to the right end of the violet guide, alternates across the yellow/blue/green ramps, and damps into the bottom purple catch.
  3. Every later frame starts independently from the untouched source array; nothing is accumulated from a preceding rendered frame. This keeps the camera, lighting, photograph grain, whiteboard, magnets, and background locked exactly.
  4. The yellow glass marble is analytic pixel art at photographic scale (radius 4.25 px):
    • a one-pixel antialiased coverage edge;
    • a semi-transparent golden body that retains some underlying photograph texture;
    • a darker amber glass rim;
    • a fixed upper-left warm-white reflection matching the source light direction;
    • a small rotating internal golden glint to suggest rolling;
    • a lower-right refracted caustic.
  5. A compact low-opacity warm-gray Gaussian shadow is placed slightly down/right of the marble. On explicitly unsupported handoffs it becomes marginally broader and is accompanied by two very faint, directionally aligned exposure samples. These are local photographic motion cues, not whole-frame blur.
  6. artifact_io.save packs RGB pixels as uint32 0xRRGGBB, transposes them to (144, 256, 20), writes pixels.npz, writes all 20 PNGs, creates inspection.png, and embeds the packed animation in index.html.
  7. transition_check.png is a programmatically generated diagnostic card containing per-frame change counts, change bounds, delta-weighted centers, maximum channel deltas, center steps, and a coordinate plot of the authored trajectory.

Revision and numeric inspection

Generated outputs were inspected only with local numeric/code checks, not with image Read/View tools. The initial pass showed that the upper handoff would read more cleanly if its intermediate point were above the violet ramp. I revised frame 05 from a ramp-height point to (132, 22), producing a clear short ballistic arc before contact near (156, 36).
