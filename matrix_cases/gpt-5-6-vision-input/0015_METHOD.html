OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured first-frame layout

  • Source frame: 256×144 RGB, landscape, fixed camera.
  • Orange inflatable basketball: approximately x=80–137 and y=7–66, about 58 px wide and 60 px high, centered near (109, 36). The brightest specular marks are on the upper-left and upper-middle surface; a dark vertical seam runs near the center. The lower edge overlaps the crate opening/rim.
  • Black plastic milk crate: approximately x=55–155 and y=55–133. Its top opening is a foreshortened dark quadrilateral around y=56–76, and its front face occupies roughly y=72–131. Diamond lattice openings must remain in front of the ball once it drops inside.
  • Wooden table: top edge slopes from about y=119 at the left border to y=104 near the crate and remains near y=105 on the right. The visible top fills the lower part of the frame; warm brown grain and the crate shadow remain fixed.
  • Background: pale gray wall with a left-to-right tonal falloff and mild photographic noise. A narrow dark wedge/background object touches the far left edge. Lighting, camera framing, and every non-ball object are static.
  • The ball is narrower than the crate opening, so the intended physically plausible action is a vertical drop into the crate rather than a collision with the rim or a lateral escape.

Planned construction and checks

  • Preserve frame 00 byte-for-byte.
  • Build a static clean plate only where the original ball is removed, using local wall gradients/noise above the crate and local crate texture/geometry around its rim.
  • Reuse the original photographed ball pixels as a softly antialiased sprite; translate it along an analytic gravity/bounce trajectory and apply very small contact-only squash/stretch transforms.
  • Restore the crate front from the untouched first frame after placing the dropped ball, using an aperture-derived occlusion mask so black lattice bars and the top/front rim remain fixed in front.
  • Numerically verify dimensions, RGB mode, exact frame-00 equality, tensor/PNG agreement, fixed-pixel stability, ball trajectory, bounded frame differences, and generated artifact presence. No later output will be inspected with image Read/View tools.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely from first_frame.png with deterministic local NumPy, OpenCV, and Pillow pixel operations. No external images, masks, models, engines, or network resources were used. The generated later frames were assessed only through code and numeric array checks, not through image viewing tools.

Image construction

  1. The supplied frame is loaded as 256×144 RGB and copied unchanged to frame 00.
  2. A static clean plate is made only under the initial ball:
    • the pale wall is reconstructed row-by-row from untouched samples on both sides, with a small amount of high-frequency texture borrowed from a nearby wall strip;
    • the wall/crate boundary is initially locally inpainted;
    • pixels hidden by the ball below the boundary are rebuilt from the neighboring dark crate opening and rim values.
  3. A 64×64 antialiased ball sprite is cut from the photographed orange ball. Its few unseen lower-cap pixels are completed by reflecting the corresponding photographed upper-cap surface and adding lower-hemisphere darkening. Thus the visible highlights, seam, texture, and color all originate in the supplied ball.
  4. The ball center follows a hand-sampled gravity-and-damped-bounce trajectory at 0.25-second intervals. It falls from y≈36 to the crate bottom at y≈99, rebounds twice with decreasing height, then settles near y≈98. Small horizontal subpixel drift and brief contact squash/stretch are included.
  5. A faint Gaussian wall shadow follows the ball only while it remains above the crate.
  6. The black crate top lip and front lattice are treated as a foreground layer. Dark structural pixels are detected from the first frame, expanded by one pixel to retain photographic edges, and combined with measured diamond-shaped lattice apertures. After the ball is composited, the fixed rim and lattice pixels are restored over it. This keeps the ball behind the crate and makes it visible only above the rim or through openings.
  7. Everything outside the ball/crate/shadow working region is copied from the fixed first frame with no camera motion.

Saved artifacts and validation

artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the 20 PNG frames, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py also writes an amplified adjacent-difference sheet as transition_check.png and performs these checks on every run:

  • exact array equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png;
  • shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype uint8;
  • stability of pixels outside the localized action region;
  • bounded and localized adjacent-frame changes;
  • exact equality of every saved PNG with the in-memory frame;
  • exact equality of pixels.npz with the same frames after 0xRRGGBB packing.

The numeric results from the final run are saved in .tmp/verification.json.
