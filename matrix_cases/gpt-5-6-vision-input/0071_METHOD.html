OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Observed first frame

  • Source is an RGB image at exactly 256 x 144. The camera is locked, front-facing, and slightly above table height.
  • A cylindrical ribbed glass beverage dispenser occupies roughly x=81..164, y=0..67. Opaque reddish grapefruit juice is visible inside it, mainly around y=35..59.
  • The dispenser rests on a round woven wicker riser occupying about x=65..190, y=42..111. The weave has strong horizontal brown/orange bands and must remain static.
  • A dark metal/plastic tap is centered at about x=124..130, with its body around y=27..60. Its outlet and the falling liquid are near the optical center.
  • A transparent drinking glass is centered below the tap. Its useful interior is approximately x=111..146, y=72..136, with the rim near y=72..80, rounded walls, and base near y=138.
  • The glass already contains muted grapefruit-colored liquid/water mixture. The prominent liquid body begins around y=91 and continues to the bottom, while reflections and the falling stream are visible above it.
  • The narrow pour path lies around x=125..130 from the spout near y=59 to the liquid surface. The source frame includes irregular dark orange highlights along this path, not a perfectly uniform line.
  • The backdrop is pale gray/lavender in the upper portion; the wooden tabletop begins around y=66 at the sides and fills the lower frame. Lighting and all objects other than the fluid must remain fixed.
  • Numeric source fingerprint: PNG SHA-256 0fa094b433043080c88cedf61da5e34ea7a51e423fc4635c89541267d32d604b.

Animation method and physical intent

The continuation will retain the photographed first frame as its immutable base. Only a compact mask covering the glass interior, its rim, and the falling stream will change. Existing photographed pixels from the glass/liquid will be sampled, shifted, blended, and shaded; analytic ellipses and thin antialiased stream strokes will provide the rising surface, ripples, bubbles, and specular variation. The liquid level rises monotonically as grapefruit juice displaces/mixes with the water, while its color becomes modestly more saturated. The stream remains continuous and flickers sub-pixel-wise under gravity.

20-frame event plan (4 fps)

  • Frame 00 (0.00 s): exact original first frame; pour already underway.
  • Frames 01-03 (0.25-0.75 s): stream continues; first surface ripple expands; level rises about 1 pixel.
  • Frames 04-07 (1.00-1.75 s): liquid level rises steadily another 3 pixels; impact dimple and small bright bubbles drift laterally.
  • Frames 08-11 (2.00-2.75 s): continuous pour; surface reaches the upper-middle glass; orange/pink grapefruit tint becomes slightly richer; alternating ripples remain subtle.
  • Frames 12-15 (3.00-3.75 s): level rises toward the shoulder of the glass, with slower apparent rise as the vessel widens; one or two bubbles ascend and fade.
  • Frames 16-18 (4.00-4.50 s): glass nears a safe full level below the rim; stream remains narrow and continuous; ripple amplitude damps.
  • Frame 19 (4.75 s): final level is stable just below the rim, still visibly pouring, with a small impact depression and no spill or camera/object motion.

Numeric validation targets

  • 20 RGB uint8 frames, each 256 x 144; frame 00 byte-identical to the source after RGB decoding.
  • All change bounds remain local to approximately x=109..148, y=58..137.
  • Measured liquid surface y-coordinate must be non-increasing across time (rising in the image), from about y=91 toward y=80, with no single-frame jump over 2 pixels.
  • Static control regions outside the local fluid mask must have zero changed pixels.
  • Adjacent frames must differ, but changes should stay temporally small and coherent; no global brightness or camera shift.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic local pixel continuation written in Python with NumPy and Pillow. No external model, engine, reference image, mask, score, network service, or other case was used.

first_frame.png is decoded as RGB and copied exactly into frame 00. Every later frame begins again from that same photograph, which guarantees a locked camera and a perfectly static background, dispenser, wicker stand, table, and outer glass. Changes are limited to a compact analytic mask around the falling liquid and the inside of the drinking glass.

The fluid continuation combines three techniques:

  1. Existing photographed liquid pixels are mildly blurred, vertically warped, and blended upward inside a measured glass silhouette to depict the rising level. The original photograph remains the dominant texture, including its reflections and grain.
  2. The free surface is an antialiased mathematical curve containing a small impact depression and damped capillary waves. A dark meniscus and broken warm highlights are composited along that curve. A few small analytic rings rise and fade as bubbles.
  3. The falling juice is a narrow Gaussian stream whose centerline, width, opacity, and warm highlight vary slightly over time. It runs continuously from the photographed spout to the moving surface.

The target surface rises smoothly from about y=91 toward y=79.4 over frames 01-19. Grapefruit warmth increases subtly as the stream mixes into the water already in the glass. The pour continues through the final frame without overflow.

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, all 20 PNG frames, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py also writes transition_check.png as fixed-scale absolute adjacent-frame difference maps. It then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz and asserts exact equality with the generated RGB array.

Later outputs were not inspected with an image Read/View tool. Revisions and validation use only local code and numeric measurements.
