juice-in-water · 生成方法

The animation is a deterministic local pixel continuation written in Python with NumPy and Pillow. No external model, engine, reference image, mask, score, network service, or other case was used.

first_frame.png is decoded as RGB and copied exactly into frame 00. Every later frame begins again from that same photograph, which guarantees a locked camera and a perfectly static background, dispenser, wicker stand, table, and outer glass. Changes are limited to a compact analytic mask around the falling liquid and the inside of the drinking glass.

The fluid continuation combines three techniques:

  1. Existing photographed liquid pixels are mildly blurred, vertically warped, and blended upward inside a measured glass silhouette to depict the rising level. The original photograph remains the dominant texture, including its reflections and grain.
  2. The free surface is an antialiased mathematical curve containing a small impact depression and damped capillary waves. A dark meniscus and broken warm highlights are composited along that curve. A few small analytic rings rise and fade as bubbles.
  3. The falling juice is a narrow Gaussian stream whose centerline, width, opacity, and warm highlight vary slightly over time. It runs continuously from the photographed spout to the moving surface.

The target surface rises smoothly from about y=91 toward y=79.4 over frames 01-19. Grapefruit warmth increases subtly as the stream mixes into the water already in the glass. The pour continues through the final frame without overflow.

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, all 20 PNG frames, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py also writes transition_check.png as fixed-scale absolute adjacent-frame difference maps. It then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz and asserts exact equality with the generated RGB array.

Later outputs were not inspected with an image Read/View tool. Revisions and validation use only local code and numeric measurements.
