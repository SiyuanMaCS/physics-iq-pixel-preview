domino-in-juice · 生成方法

This sequence was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations from first_frame.png. No external model, image, mask, reference, network service, or rendering engine was used.

Image construction

  1. The source RGB frame is loaded at its native 256 x 144 resolution. Frame 00 is assigned directly from that array with no resampling or modification.
  2. Hand-measured polygons isolate the white domino and the two blue-black grabber jaws. A lightly feathered alpha edge retains the source photograph's antialiasing.
  3. A static clean plate is formed by dilating the union of those masks and using OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. This only reconstructs the narrow area that the moving objects uncover; the mug, tabletop, background, lighting, and camera remain copied from the source.
  4. The original photographic pixels inside the masks are used as sprites. The domino receives small affine rotations and a vertical trajectory whose post-release increments increase to mimic gravity. The left and right grabber halves rotate/translate independently to open, then the grabber retracts out through the top edge. A short extension of the source top-edge texture keeps the lowered grabber connected to its off-screen handle.
  5. A curved analytic near-rim boundary clips the falling domino as it enters the mug. Pixels below y = 75 + 0.006(x - 119)^2 are hidden, making the near wall/rim correctly occlude the ceramic rather than letting it pass in front of the mug.
  6. A soft source-localized shadow tightens on the liquid as the domino approaches. At impact, a small depression, compact reddish splash crown, and two expanding elliptical ripple lines are alpha-blended only inside the measured liquid opening. Their amplitude decays through the final frames.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 tensor, frame PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone player. transition_check.png places selected previous/next frame pairs beside 4x absolute-difference maps for continuity diagnostics.

Timing

  • 20 frames, 256 x 144 RGB, 4 fps, 5 seconds.
  • Frames 01-05: subtle lowering and jaw opening.
  • Frames 06-10: accelerating release and rim entry.
  • Frames 10-13: impact, splash collapse, and expanding ripples.
  • Frames 11-15: grabber retracts fully beyond the top edge.
  • Frames 14-19: residual ripples decay to a settled mug.

Numeric verification

  • frames/00.png equals first_frame.png exactly (maximum channel error 0).
  • All 20 decoded PNG arrays have shape (144, 256, 3) and dtype uint8.
  • Every PNG matches its corresponding plane in pixels.npz after packing as 0xRRGGBB; the tensor has shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32.
  • Consecutive-frame changes are localized to approximately x=81..136, y=0..81. Pixels outside the broader action corridor x=80..159, y=0..129 remain identical to the source in every checked frame.
  • No new pure-black or near-black pixels were introduced in the action region, and late ripple transitions decay monotonically to a maximum per-channel step of only 3 levels at the last transition.

The supplied inspection.png and transition_check.png were generated as deliverables but were not visually opened or reviewed; later-output evaluation used only local decoding and numeric checks.
