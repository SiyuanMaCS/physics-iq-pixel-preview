OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB pixels. Camera is locked off and the scene is approximately bilaterally centered on x=128.
  • Background wall occupies roughly y=0..62 and is lavender-gray. The wooden tabletop begins near a shallow diagonal horizon from about (0,77) to (54,62), runs behind the apparatus, and fills the lower portion of the frame.
  • The glass dispenser body is centered near x=122 and occupies approximately x=82..161, y=0..65. Its lower glass edge and metal outlet assembly are directly above the drinking glass.
  • The dark tap/spigot is centered at about x=122. The outlet tip is near (122,67); the intended falling-water axis is x=122..123.
  • The wicker support is approximately x=68..180, y=40..105, with its central opening behind the receiving glass.
  • The receiving glass is approximately x=103..145, y=68..137. It has a bright rim/upper bowl at y=69..83, transparent side walls, and a rounded base. The existing grapefruit juice is orange-pink, with a visible liquid surface around y=88..91 and body extending to about y=134.
  • The water stream has a clear path from the outlet near y=67 to the existing liquid surface near y=89, a vertical travel of about 22 px. It should remain narrow (mostly 2 px, with subpixel-looking highlights) because the whole view is only 256 px wide.
  • Illumination is soft, from upper-left/front. Existing glass highlights are warm white/gray; believable added water therefore needs a pale neutral core, a slightly cool edge, and partial transparency rather than opaque white.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  1. Frame 00 (0.00 s): exact supplied still; no generated changes.
  2. Frame 01 (0.25 s): tap begins; a short, thin water tongue appears immediately under the outlet.
  3. Frame 02 (0.50 s): the falling tongue lengthens about halfway toward the juice.
  4. Frame 03 (0.75 s): stream nearly reaches the juice surface.
  5. Frame 04 (1.00 s): stream makes first contact; a small bright impact dimple/ring forms.
  6. Frame 05 (1.25 s): continuous narrow stream; surface ripple expands inside the glass.
  7. Frame 06 (1.50 s): steady pour; first small pale mixing plume descends below impact.
  8. Frame 07 (1.75 s): continuous stream with a slight natural width/brightness fluctuation; ripples soften.
  9. Frame 08 (2.00 s): juice level begins its subtle rise; mixing plume widens.
  10. Frame 09 (2.25 s): steady pour; brighter turbulent patch at the surface.
  11. Frame 10 (2.50 s): juice is visibly but modestly diluted near the center and surface.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic pixel-level continuation made only from first_frame.png.

  • Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from the supplied image.
  • Every later frame begins as a fresh copy of that same source, keeping the locked camera, lighting, dispenser, wicker stand, wall, tabletop, and glass outline fixed.
  • The water is an analytic, softly antialiased vertical alpha mask. Its length grows from the dispenser outlet for the first four frames, then remains connected to the moving liquid surface. Two nested masks create a dim refractive edge and a brighter transparent core; small deterministic sinusoidal offsets prevent it from looking perfectly rigid.
  • The juice region is bounded by an analytic tapered-glass interior mask. After impact, the fill surface rises by about 3.5 pixels. A shallow color layer fills the newly occupied band without replacing the original rim reflections.
  • Dilution uses depth-decaying Gaussian plume fields blended into the existing juice pixels. Their width grows with depth and their centerline varies slightly with time. Thin darker folds, tiny bubbles, a localized impact highlight, and low elliptical ripple arcs provide turbulence cues.
  • No external assets, masks, models, engines, or reference images were used. No later output was reviewed with an image-viewing tool; validation is performed by local numeric checks.

artifact_io.save packs RGB into uint32 0xRRGGBB values with tensor shape (144, 256, 20), writes the 20 PNG frames and inspection sheet, and creates the standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png from amplified numeric deltas and verifies that every saved PNG exactly equals its corresponding unpacked tensor frame.
