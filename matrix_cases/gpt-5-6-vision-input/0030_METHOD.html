OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Source measurements

  • Source is an RGB uint8 image at exactly 256×144.
  • Camera is locked. The coffee-table top occupies most of the lower image and recedes toward the upper right; the olive sofa is static across the upper background, with dark floor at the far right.
  • The black pipe enters from the upper-left edge and points diagonally toward the objects. Its visible dark silhouette is approximately x=0–59, y=12–45, with its outlet aimed down-right.
  • Grey ball: center approximately (85, 51), visible radius about 7 px. It is already outside the pipe, left of the target pair.
  • Brown ball: center approximately (121, 55), visible radius about 7 px.
  • Orange ball: center approximately (135, 56), visible radius about 7 px. Brown and orange are visually touching/back-to-back.
  • All three balls have soft, short contact shadows displaced slightly down-right. The grey-to-brown center gap is about 36 px; their surface gap is about 22 px.
  • The source has subdued indoor lighting and mild compression/optical softness. New frames must preserve that look, without camera movement or added objects.

20-frame event plan (4 fps)

  • Frame 00 (0.00 s): exact source image, byte-for-byte pixel equality after RGB decoding.
  • Frames 01–02 (0.25–0.50 s): grey ball begins rolling right and slightly downward along the table perspective, moving from x=85 toward x=92; target pair remains still.
  • Frames 03–05 (0.75–1.25 s): grey ball continues at nearly steady speed through x=97–104. Its source texture rotates subtly and its contact shadow follows it.
  • Frame 06 (1.50 s): grey reaches x≈107 and makes first visible contact with the brown ball at x≈121.
  • Frames 07–08 (1.75–2.00 s): collision response. Grey compresses visually by at most a fraction of a pixel, then loses most forward speed; brown is nudged right into the adjacent orange ball.
  • Frames 09–13 (2.25–3.25 s): momentum passes through the pair. Brown advances only modestly and slows, while orange rolls rightward more visibly; all motion stays on the tabletop.
  • Frames 14–17 (3.50–4.25 s): orange decelerates from table friction; grey and brown show only a tiny settling drift.
  • Frames 18–19 (4.50–4.75 s): all balls are at rest, leaving a stable final hold.

The implementation will use only local pixel operations: an inpainted static plate derived from the first frame, masked ball/shadow sprites cut from the first frame, subpixel affine placement/rotation, and analytic collision trajectories. Frame 00 will bypass compositing and retain the original pixels exactly.

METHOD.md

Generation method

Actual pixel method

The animation is deterministic and uses only local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png.

  1. The source is decoded as 256×144 RGB uint8. Frame 00 is assigned directly from that array, with no filtering or re-encoding operation before the output helper saves it.
  2. Two static plates are made with OpenCV Telea inpainting:
    • a plate with only the grey ball and its contact shadow removed, used before impact so the photographed brown/orange pair stays unchanged;
    • a plate with all three balls and shadows removed, used from the collision onward.
  3. Each ball is cut into a 21×21 floating-point sprite with a one-pixel feathered elliptical alpha mask. The tiny mutually occluded edges of the touching brown/orange balls are filled by mirroring pixels from the opposite clean edge of the same ball.
  4. Ball centers follow explicit subpixel trajectories. The grey ball rolls from (85, 51) to contact near (107, 53.5). At impact it loses nearly all forward velocity. The brown ball moves only about 2.8 px because it immediately transfers momentum into the touching orange ball. The orange ball then rolls to x=177 and decelerates to rest.
  5. Sprite displacement determines a small accumulated rotation (angle = -degrees(distance / 7)), applied with bilinear affine resampling. Soft analytic elliptical contact shadows move with each ball. The camera, pipe, table, sofa, floor, illumination, and every pixel outside the localized motion corridor remain fixed.
  6. Frames 18 and 19 are identical, creating a stable final rest hold.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 tensor, 20 PNG frames, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png from temporal difference magnitudes.

The generator performs these assertions after saving:

  • frame array shape is 20×144×256×3 RGB uint8;
  • frames/00.png equals the decoded first_frame.png exactly;
  • every saved PNG equals its in-memory frame exactly;
  • pixels.npz has shape 144×256×20, dtype uint32, and equals the 0xRRGGBB packing of all PNG pixels exactly;
  • the final two frames are a stable hold;
  • all temporal changes remain inside the ball-motion corridor (numeric bounds no wider than x=72–188, y=40–69).

No generated frame, inspection sheet, or transition sheet was opened with an image Read/View tool or visually reviewed. Later-output inspection was limited to local code and numeric checks, as requested.
