OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured composition

  • Source and output geometry: 256 x 144 RGB pixels; locked camera, single shot.
  • The tabletop begins near y=54 and fills the lower image. The softly lit gray wall fills the upper image.
  • Newton's-cradle base: black trapezoidal plinth spanning approximately x=64..174, y=119..135.
  • Chrome support frame: outer uprights near x=70 and x=168, top rails near y=8..20, and lower attachment points near y=126.
  • Five suspension pivots lie along the upper inner rail near y=18, spaced about 11 pixels apart. At rest the ball centers are near y=96, with a ball diameter of about 10 pixels.
  • Initial ball state: the two left balls are pulled to approximately x=92 and x=101 by the grabber; three touching balls remain near x=127, x=140, and x=153. The two pulled balls form the left-side amplitude specified in the prompt.
  • The grabber enters from the left. Its black/blue handle occupies roughly x=0..85, y=80..111; its metal jaws extend to about x=105 around y=96.
  • Lighting is warm from the upper left. Metal has dark lower rims, pale top-left highlights, and narrow specular streaks. Soft shadows fall onto the plinth/table.

Motion and continuity constraints

  • Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte from first_frame.png.
  • The camera, background, support frame, table, and base remain fixed.
  • Strings remain tied to fixed upper pivots and track the ball centers.
  • The tool opens slightly as it releases, then retracts smoothly to the left and is fully out of view.
  • Newton's-cradle transfer is represented as paired motion: two balls enter from the left, briefly compress/contact the resting row, then two balls leave on the right. On each return, the opposite pair departs. Swing amplitude and speed decay slightly after every impact.
  • Motion uses a pendulum-like eased arc rather than linear translation; balls rise slightly toward either amplitude and move fastest through the bottom.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a local pixel reconstruction driven entirely by first_frame.png and the prompt.

  1. I measured the cradle, ball centers, suspension points, base, table boundary, and grabber footprint from the supplied frame.
  2. I made a fixed clean plate by masking only the initially moving objects (grabber, balls, and their wires) and applying OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. The small support segment occluded by the tool is reconstructed as an anti-aliased chrome line. The plate is then held bit-stable outside moving-object coverage.
  3. Five balls are attached to fixed pivots with an 80-pixel pendulum length. The sampled horizontal displacements describe paired Newton's-cradle transfer: the left pair falls inward, the right pair departs, returns, and transfers decreasing energy back to the left pair. Vertical coordinates are computed from the circular pendulum constraint rather than translated linearly.
  4. Wires and balls are rendered at 4x resolution, then area-downsampled. Each chrome sphere uses analytic spherical coverage, a dark rim, wall/table reflection bands, and a small camera-space highlight. The original tool is isolated with a feathered hand-measured silhouette and translated smoothly out of frame after release.
  5. artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. transition_check.png is generated as a labeled grid around the impact transitions with per-frame numeric delta counts.

No network, external model, external engine, mask, score, or image-view tool was used. Generated frames were assessed only with local code and numeric checks.
