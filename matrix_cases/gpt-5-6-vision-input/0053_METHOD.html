double-cradle · 生成方法

The animation is a local pixel reconstruction driven entirely by first_frame.png and the prompt.

  1. I measured the cradle, ball centers, suspension points, base, table boundary, and grabber footprint from the supplied frame.
  2. I made a fixed clean plate by masking only the initially moving objects (grabber, balls, and their wires) and applying OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. The small support segment occluded by the tool is reconstructed as an anti-aliased chrome line. The plate is then held bit-stable outside moving-object coverage.
  3. Five balls are attached to fixed pivots with an 80-pixel pendulum length. The sampled horizontal displacements describe paired Newton's-cradle transfer: the left pair falls inward, the right pair departs, returns, and transfers decreasing energy back to the left pair. Vertical coordinates are computed from the circular pendulum constraint rather than translated linearly.
  4. Wires and balls are rendered at 4x resolution, then area-downsampled. Each chrome sphere uses analytic spherical coverage, a dark rim, wall/table reflection bands, and a small camera-space highlight. The original tool is isolated with a feathered hand-measured silhouette and translated smoothly out of frame after release.
  5. artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. transition_check.png is generated as a labeled grid around the impact transitions with per-frame numeric delta counts.

No network, external model, external engine, mask, score, or image-view tool was used. Generated frames were assessed only with local code and numeric checks.
