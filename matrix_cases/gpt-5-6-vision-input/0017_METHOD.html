OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

Source measurements

  • Source frame: first_frame.png, RGB, 256 x 144 pixels.
  • Camera: fixed, landscape, eye-level tabletop view; no camera motion or cut.
  • Table/wall boundary: soft diagonal/perspective boundary centered around y=69–72. The tabletop fills the lower half and has warm brown wood grain.
  • Green kinetic-sand mound: approximately x=93–181, y=79–119. Its apex is near y=79–82, its base near y=116–119, and its center is approximately (136, 101). It has an irregular granular silhouette and mottled green shading.
  • Blue tennis ball: approximately x=121–150, y=20–52, with center near (136, 36) and radius about 15 pixels. It is cyan-blue with a dark lower/right side, a small upper-left highlight, and a curved pale seam.
  • Blue-black grabber: visible from the top edge to about y=34 around x=112–155. Two curved dark-blue jaws surround the ball from above; the central upper body is cropped by the frame.
  • Alignment: grabber, ball, and mound are nearly collinear at x=136. Initial clearance from the ball bottom to mound apex is about 27 pixels.
  • Lighting: soft overhead/front-left illumination. Wall is cool gray, tabletop is warm brown, and contact/cast shadows are soft.
  • Locked elements: frame boundaries, wall, horizon, table texture, and all static illumination remain fixed.

The intended physics is a short gravitational drop followed by a highly damped impact: kinetic sand yields and spreads, absorbs most kinetic energy, then supports a partly embedded tennis ball.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations from first_frame.png. No external model, engine, reference image, mask, network source, or other case was used.

Construction

  1. The original 256 x 144 RGB image is loaded without resizing. Frame 00 is assigned as an exact byte-for-byte pixel copy.
  2. The blue tennis ball is isolated with a measured soft elliptical alpha matte centered at approximately (136, 36). Its original photographed texture, highlight, shading, and seam are retained.
  3. The vacated ball region is filled with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. After release, the two lower grabber tips are also isolated from their color/darkness within small measured regions, locally inpainted, and shifted a few pixels outward. The upper tool remains source imagery.
  4. The green kinetic sand is segmented only inside its measured tabletop bounds using green-channel dominance, followed by small morphological closing and a short feather. The original mound pixels supply all main sand color and texture.
  5. During impact, the mound texture and matte are affinely warped about the fixed base near y=117. Horizontal scaling represents spreading; vertical scaling represents compression. A few cohesive clumps follow short analytic ballistic paths during frames 04–07.
  6. The ball follows a hand-authored gravity/impact path based on the measured 27-pixel initial clearance: it drops, compresses the mound, makes one low damped rebound, and settles partly embedded. Source-ball pixels are rotated and mildly vertically squashed at contact.
  7. Soft analytic ellipses provide the falling/contact shadow. Textured left and right sand-rim masks are composited in front of the lower ball during penetration to establish occlusion and partial burial. Their texture is sampled from the original mound and receives low-amplitude deterministic grain.
  8. Background pixels outside the dynamic tool/ball/sand regions remain unchanged. The final pose is frozen from frame 14 through frame 19 so the resting state cannot flicker.

Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNG frames, the packed pixels.npz tensor, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. generate.py separately creates transition_check.png, whose panels are numeric max-channel difference heatmaps labeled with changed-pixel counts and mean/max deltas.

The generator then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz. It asserts:

  • exactly 20 frames of shape 144 x 256 x 3 and type uint8;
  • exact equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png;
  • tensor shape 144 x 256 x 20 and type uint32;
  • each tensor value is packed as 0xRRGGBB and exactly agrees with its PNG pixel.

No generated frame, contact sheet, or transition sheet was inspected with an image Read/View tool. Revision decisions were made using local numeric measurements only (centroids, masks, bounding boxes, changed-pixel counts, and pixel equality tests).
