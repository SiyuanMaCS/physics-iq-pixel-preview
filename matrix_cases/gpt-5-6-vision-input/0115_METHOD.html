Pixel-generation method

The sequence is generated deterministically by generate.py from the supplied first_frame.png. No network access, external model, external engine, other case, mask, or reference asset is used.

  1. The input is decoded as a 256 x 144 RGB array.
  2. Two compact masks cover the original real assembly and its mirror image below the fixed ceiling cords. OpenCV's local Telea inpainting reconstructs the gently varying wall/table pixels at those vacated source locations.
  3. The real and reflected moving assemblies are extracted from the first frame with feathered geometric mattes. The ball and magnet use ellipse/rounded-rectangle supports; the narrow hook and suspension use source-to-inpaint residual evidence within tightly bounded regions.
  4. A smoothstep timing curve translates the real assembly downward by 34 pixels over 20 frames. The reflected assembly follows a perspective-compressed 27-pixel descent and drifts two pixels left.
  5. Both source sprites are moved using bilinear affine resampling and alpha compositing. The ceiling segments of the cords stay fixed; local analytic antialiased lines extend the cords to the translated connectors.
  6. A low-opacity analytic Gaussian shadow strengthens slightly as each ball approaches the tabletop. This is multiplicative so the original wood texture remains intact.
  7. Frame 00 is assigned directly from the decoded input. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 tensor, RGB PNGs, inspection contact sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes a numerical transition diagnostic image.

Timing

  • 20 frames
  • 256 x 144 RGB
  • 4 fps
  • 5 seconds
  • Locked camera and unchanged static scene

Verification

Verification is performed in code, without opening later frames in an image viewer:

  • all 20 PNGs decode as RGB at 256 x 144;
  • frame 00 is pixel-identical to first_frame.png (and, with the current encoder, byte-identical as well);
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32;
  • every packed 0xRRGGBB tensor value equals the corresponding exported PNG pixel;
  • every consecutive transition is nonempty and has a low global mean RGB change;
  • measured orange-object centroids descend monotonically along the planned real and reflected paths;
  • changes remain confined to the two swept assembly/cord/shadow regions, apart from one-level rounding at the tail of the analytic shadow.

inspection.png and transition_check.png are generated artifacts only. They were not visually reviewed; later-frame inspection was strictly numerical, as requested.
