20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 s)

The cloth is warm ivory silk, sampled chromatically from the existing satin background and rendered opaque with fine weave, moving folds, soft edge antialiasing, and a small contact shadow. Motion uses eased lowering and damped settling rather than constant-speed translation.

Validation targets

  • Every frame must be RGB uint8 at 256×144; the tensor must be (144,256,20) uint32 packed as 0xRRGGBB.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

This animation was generated locally with deterministic Python, NumPy, and Pillow pixel operations. No external model, engine, mask, reference image, network resource, or other case was used.

  1. first_frame.png is decoded to a 256×144 RGB uint8 array. It is copied directly into frame 00 without modification.
  2. A finite warm-ivory silk sheet is represented by a time-varying upper profile, lower hem, and two side boundaries:
    • Frames 01–05 lower a suspended panel from above the frame with a shallow curved hem.
    • Frames 06–14 interpolate the panel into a measured teapot-conforming silhouette. The final upper profile follows the handle, lid knob, body shoulders, and spout; the lower profile reaches and pools along the tabletop.
    • Frames 15–19 apply small damped profile displacements and a damped specular phase, producing residual settling rather than a sudden stop.
  3. The sheet is rasterized at 4× spatial resolution. Its mask is then Lanczos-downsampled for antialiased fabric edges.
  4. Silk appearance is analytic and deterministic: a warm foreground palette derived from the source satin, broad and narrow sinusoidal fold lobes, localized highlight/shadow ridges, vertical gravity darkening, and fine two-frequency fiber grain. The fabric is opaque inside its mask.
  5. A narrow blurred cast shadow is placed below the moving hem. A compact contact shadow is introduced only as the cloth approaches the table. Sub-visible blur tails are removed so distant and border pixels remain locked.
  6. Every frame is composited independently over the original first frame. This prevents accumulated resampling, background drift, or camera motion.

Output construction

artifact_io.save packs RGB pixels as uint32 0xRRGGBB, transposes them to (144, 256, 20), and writes pixels.npz. It also writes the 20 PNG frames, inspection.png, and the standalone delta-coded index.html player. generate.py separately creates transition_check.png from computed RGB differences.

Numeric validation performed

  • Frame 00 exactly equals the RGB decoding of first_frame.png.
  • All frames have shape (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype uint8.
  • Every frames/NN.png decodes exactly to its in-memory frame.
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and exactly equals independently repacked frame pixels.
  • All 19 consecutive transitions contain changed pixels.
  • Motion remains localized; the peak transition changes 16,824 of 36,864 pixels.
  • Mean absolute RGB transition delta falls during final settling from 3.616 at frame 13→14 to 0.161 at frame 18→19.
  • After frame 11, no transition changes any outer-border pixel.
  • In the final frame, zero of 1,068 strictly classified red ceramic pixels remain unchanged, while a distant safe-region sample remains 100% identical to frame 00.

No later generated frame or contact sheet was inspected with an image Read/View tool; revision decisions were based on local array measurements and numeric checks.
