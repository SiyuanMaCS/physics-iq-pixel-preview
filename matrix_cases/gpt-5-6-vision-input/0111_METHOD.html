OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

First-frame measurements

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB, landscape, fixed camera.
  • Background: pale gray wall above a warm medium-brown wooden tabletop. The wall/table boundary is a soft diagonal running approximately from (0, 36) to (91, 0); the rest of the frame is tabletop.
  • Lighting: soft key from upper left. The glass casts a broad, low-contrast shadow toward the lower right; no camera or lighting motion is expected.
  • Glass: centered slightly left of the image midpoint. Its outer bounds are approximately x=95..150, y=35..118. The elliptical mouth is about x=96..149, y=35..63; the body tapers from roughly 49 px wide under the rim to 35 px at the base. The water surface/rim area is around y=49..56 and the transparent water-filled body extends to the thick base near y=104..115.
  • Match: a thin tan wooden shaft enters from the top near (79, 0) and runs diagonally down-right to the head near (124, 43). The lit head/flame is a compact orange-yellow feature around x=120..132, y=36..49, already over the glass opening.
  • Motion constraints: glass, table, wall, shadows, and camera remain completely fixed. Only the match, flame, and a very small amount of extinction smoke/ripple motion change.

20-frame plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  1. Frame 00: exact source frame, lit match at the mouth.
  2. Frames 01-03: match rotates/lower-translates gently; head moves a few pixels down into the opening. Flame stays lit but leans and flickers subtly.
  3. Frames 04-06: head crosses the water surface. Flame rapidly contracts from orange-yellow to a tiny dim ember. A small bright surface disturbance appears at the contact point.
  4. Frames 07-09: flame is fully extinguished. The darkened match head continues downward just inside the water; two or three tiny ripples expand across the water surface and fade.
  5. Frames 10-14: match is lowered deeper through the upper half of the glass. Its submerged segment is mildly refracted, dimmer, cooler, and slightly displaced relative to the dry segment.
  6. Frames 15-17: descent slows; the blackened head reaches roughly the middle/lower-middle of the visible water column. A very faint pale smoke wisp above the water dissipates.
  7. Frames 18-19: motion settles with the match held underwater, no flame and no remaining ripple beyond a subtle residual highlight.

The action is continuous and real-time, with easing at initial contact and at the final held position. No cuts, zooms, added objects, hands, labels, or camera movement.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic local pixel synthesis built from first_frame.png; no external images, masks, models, engines, or network resources are used.

  1. The input is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is copied directly and is never redrawn.
  2. A clean locked-camera plate is made for frames 01-19 with a narrow OpenCV Telea inpaint mask covering only the original 2-pixel match shaft and compact flame/head. The photographed glass, wall, table, lighting, shadow, and wood grain remain the source pixels.
  3. The moving match is analytically rasterized along an eased path. Its top remains out of frame while the head descends from the mouth to the middle/lower-middle of the water. The shaft is anti-aliased tan wood above the surface. Below y=53, the apparent segment bends slightly toward vertical, darkens, and becomes more translucent to represent water refraction and absorption.
  4. A small oriented dark-brown head follows the shaft. The flame uses layered orange and pale-yellow teardrop masks plus a weak blurred glow. It flickers for the early frames, shrinks sharply at contact, and is gone after frame 05. The head transitions to a cool charred color.
  5. Two clipped elliptical surface ripples appear at contact and fade. A deterministic, blurred pale-gray smoke curve rises briefly and disperses. These effects remain inside or immediately above the glass.
  6. Fixed pixels from the photographed front rim, glass edges, and base glints are composited over the synthesized match, restoring the correct foreground occlusion.
  7. artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, 20 PNG frames, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py also writes transition_check.png, then reopens every PNG and pixels.npz to compare them exactly with the in-memory frames.

Numeric validation

  • Shape and type: exactly (20, 144, 256, 3) RGB uint8.
  • Frame 00: exact array equality with first_frame.png.
  • Tensor: exactly (144, 256, 20) uint32, with every value equal to the corresponding packed PNG pixel.
  • Motion is constrained to the measured match/glass corridor; protected background pixels are unchanged across frames 01-19.
  • Every adjacent pair changes, and the generator rejects transitions with fewer than 31 or more than 7,999 changed pixels.

No generated frame, inspection sheet, or transition sheet was reviewed with an image viewing tool. Revisions were based only on local pixel statistics, change bounds, hot-pixel counts, and exact array comparisons.
