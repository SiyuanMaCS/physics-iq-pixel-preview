OBSERVATION.md

Initial-frame observations and event plan

Scene measurements

  • Source is RGB, 256 x 144. The camera is locked off and the frame contains a gray wall/mirror background above a warm wooden tabletop.
  • The real decorated teapot is centered at approximately (98, 80). Its colorful rotating portion occupies about x=80..116, y=50..106; its black circular platform occupies about x=79..121, y=101..119 and remains stationary.
  • A vertical mirror boundary/support is visible around x=113..132. The reflected teapot lies approximately within x=169..219, y=43..95; the reflected pedestal/platform is below it around x=175..215, y=87..104 and remains stationary.
  • A thin dark power lead starts near the foreground platform and crosses the tabletop toward the right. It is static and must not be warped.
  • The teapot bodies contain saturated blue, red, and gold details against largely low-saturation gray surroundings. That color separation, combined with hand-measured elliptical body/lid regions, can isolate the rotating painted surfaces without moving the platforms, tabletop, mirror, or cable.
  • The real and reflected bodies have different apparent sizes and viewpoints. Their surface phases must therefore be treated independently, with the reflection advancing in the mirrored horizontal direction.

Motion design

  • Duration: 20 frames at 4 fps (5 seconds), with frame 00 copied exactly from first_frame.png.
  • Motion is a smooth, constant-speed clockwise revolution: phase 0 at frame 00 and phase 2*pi*19/20 at frame 19. The implied loop step from frame 19 back to frame 00 completes the remaining twentieth of the revolution.
  • Rotation is represented as a cylindrical surface remap: colorful painted details travel around the curved body while the body outline, fixed lighting envelope, base contact, and platform stay locked. The unseen half of the vessel is synthesized deterministically from reflected/recolored variants of observed surface samples, so a full turn has no blank areas.
  • The reflection receives the same physical angular phase but the horizontally mirrored apparent phase. Small specular highlights remain fixed in screen space while painted motifs travel underneath them, reinforcing three-dimensional rotation under stationary illumination.

METHOD.md

Method

The continuation is generated entirely from first_frame.png with deterministic NumPy, OpenCV, and Pillow pixel operations. No additional imagery, masks, models, engines, or network resources are used.

The foreground teapot body and the smaller mirror image were measured as row-wise curved silhouettes. Within each silhouette, color saturation separates the blue/red/gold enamel from the neutral wall and mirror. Every output pose maps each visible horizontal body coordinate to a cylindrical longitude, advances that longitude by a constant 18 degrees per frame, samples the recorded surface with linear interpolation, and reapplies a fixed screen-space luminance envelope. This makes painted details travel around the vessel while illumination and the outer contact geometry remain anchored. The initially unseen half-turn is synthesized from a deterministic mirrored/channel-mixed version of observed enamel samples, blended continuously from the observed front at the silhouette limbs.

The reflected teapot's surface phase advances in the mirrored horizontal direction. Its protruding handle/spout pixels are isolated outside the central body, locally removed with OpenCV inpainting, and horizontally projected by cos(angle) about the reflected body's axis. They compress toward the axis at quarter turns and swap sides after a half turn; the remapped body is then placed in front to provide simple occlusion. The real platform, reflected pedestal, power cable, table, mirror structure, and all pixels outside two measured teapot zones stay bit-identical to the first frame.

Frame 00 is a direct RGB copy of the source array. artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally creates a three-transition numeric heatmap and verifies source equality, static-region equality, temporal change bounds, every decoded PNG, and exact agreement between all PNG pixels and pixels.npz.
