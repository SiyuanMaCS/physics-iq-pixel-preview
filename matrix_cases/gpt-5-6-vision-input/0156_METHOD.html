OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and animation plan

Measured source scene

  • Source: the supplied first_frame.png, 256 x 144 RGB.
  • Camera: locked, wide view of a wooden tabletop. Nothing except the tennis ball is intended to move.
  • Tennis ball: chroma segmentation gives a visible bounding box of x=67..102, y=86..122 (952 strongly yellow-green pixels), with an estimated center of (85, 104) and radius about 18 px.
  • Target kettlebell: the real, stationary bell occupies approximately x=104..143, y=0..56; its body center is near (123, 37). Its handle is clipped by the upper frame edge.
  • Mirror image: the reflected bell occupies approximately x=174..220, y=0..49. The mirror/table lower edge runs diagonally through the right-rear part of the table.
  • A separate dark foreground object intrudes at the lower-left edge. It remains unchanged and is not the impact target.
  • Planned contact point: ball center about (110, 68), where a perspective-reduced ball touches the lower-left side of the real 30 lb kettlebell.
  • Lighting: soft light from upper-left; the ball has an upper-left highlight and a soft, slightly down-right contact shadow.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations using Pillow, NumPy, and OpenCV. No external images, models, masks, engines, or network resources were used.

Construction

  1. frames/00.png is a direct byte-for-byte RGB copy of first_frame.png.
  2. The tennis ball was isolated from frame 00 using its measured yellow-green hue, saturation, connected location, and a soft circular edge model.
  3. The original ball and its small contact shadow were removed for subsequent frames with an expanded elliptical mask. OpenCV inpainting supplies the low-frequency wooden tabletop continuation; high-frequency grain residual copied from adjacent clean table pixels restores local texture. The unrelated dark foreground object at lower-left is explicitly protected.
  4. For frames 01-17, the extracted ball is transformed along a measured diagonal path from approximately (85, 104) to (110, 68). Its radius decreases by about 17% with depth. The source texture rotates in proportion to distance traveled.
  5. A soft analytical contact shadow follows the ball. Its offset and falloff remain consistent with the source lighting.
  6. Near the mirror, a horizontally reflected, slightly dimmed/cooler ball copy is clipped to the measured diagonal lower boundary of the mirror pane. The existing kettlebell reflection remains untouched.
  7. Frames 16-17 use a small deformation along the travel direction to communicate contact. Frames 18-19 recoil by only a few pixels and settle. The 30 lb kettlebell stays stationary, as expected for a tennis-ball impact.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNGs, the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone player. generate.py also writes transition_check.png with frames 14-19 and amplified consecutive-frame differences.

The generator verifies:

  • tensor shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32;
  • exact equality of frame 00 and first_frame.png;
  • exact RGB equality between every PNG and the corresponding unpacked tensor frame;
  • finite values and correct 20 x 144 x 256 x 3 frame structure;
  • numeric ball-component centroids, areas, inter-frame step sizes, mirror-reflection area, changed-pixel counts, and the reconstructed-region replacement delta.

The supplied first frame was observed visually. Generated later frames and diagnostic sheets were not opened with an image viewing tool; they were evaluated only by local code and numeric pixel/component checks, per the task instruction.
