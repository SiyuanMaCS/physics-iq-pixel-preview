+def smoothstep(u):
+    """C1 interpolation for short physical handoffs."""
+    u = max(0.0, min(1.0, float(u)))
+    return u * u * (3.0 - 2.0 * u)
+
+
+def ramp_position(x0, x1, surface_y, u):
+    """Accelerating roll on a measured ramp line."""
+    # Distance under constant acceleration, with a little initial velocity.
+    q = 0.18 * u + 0.82 * u * u
+    x = x0 + (x1 - x0) * q
+    return x, surface_y(x) - 3.65
+
+
+def airborne(p0, p1, u, arc=-1.4):
+    """Short continuous handoff with a gravity-like quadratic sag."""
+    q = smoothstep(u)
+    x = p0[0] + (p1[0] - p0[0]) * q
+    y = p0[1] + (p1[1] - p0[1]) * (u * u)
+    y += arc * math.sin(math.pi * u)
+    return x, y
+

OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Source-frame measurements

  • The source is an RGB, 256 x 144 frame from a locked-off camera. The whiteboard occupies roughly x=27..161; dark frame/stand elements run near x=14..29 and x=157..173; a pale wall fills the right side.
  • The useful marble-run geometry is entirely on the whiteboard. Hough-line and HSV measurements give these sloping ramp surfaces (pixel coordinates, with y increasing downward):
    • upper purple ramp: approximately (87,36) to (149,28), descending toward the left;
    • yellow ramp: approximately (43,43) to (104,55), descending toward the right;
    • blue ramp: approximately (91,69) to (152,64), descending toward the left;
    • green ramp: approximately (39,84) to (100,94), descending toward the right;
    • lower purple catch: a small U-shaped receiver spanning about x=90..117, y=99..117.
  • The yellow ramp is the first long, unobstructed downhill run visible in the shot. Its left end is the release area. The lower ramps alternate direction and overlap the preceding ramp ends, producing short transfers and a final drop into the purple receiver.
  • The background already contains realistic low-resolution lighting, reflections, compression texture, and translucent ramp shadows. It will remain unchanged. Only a small glass-marble footprint and its local contact shadow/highlight will be composited over it.
  • The marble is modeled as a roughly 7-pixel yellow glass sphere: amber rim, translucent golden body, a darker lower/contact edge, and a small moving white specular glint. Its apparent size stays constant because the board plane is nearly front-facing and the camera is static.

Twenty-frame event plan (4 fps)

  • Frame 00: exact source image, preserving the required initial condition.
  • Frames 01-07: the released yellow marble accelerates from the left/top of the yellow ramp and rolls rightward along its measured slope. Rotation is conveyed by the changing internal highlight, not by changing the background.
  • Frames 08-09: it leaves the yellow ramp's right lip and makes a short gravity-driven drop onto the blue ramp.
  • Frames 10-13: it rolls leftward down the blue ramp, accelerating toward its left end.
  • Frame 14: short downward transfer from the blue ramp to the green ramp.
  • Frames 15-17: it rolls rightward along the green ramp.
  • Frames 18-19: it falls from the green lip into the lower purple U-shaped catch; the last frame shows it seated/settling inside the receiver.

The marble center path is continuous at each handoff and is constrained to the observed ramp surfaces. Motion easing uses constant tangential acceleration on a ramp and quadratic vertical motion during airborne transfers.

METHOD.md

Method

I used the supplied photograph directly as the immutable background. The ramp endpoints were measured from color regions and straight edges in first_frame.png; the four visible ramp surfaces were approximated with line equations in image coordinates.

The continuation is generated by generate.py with local Python, NumPy, and Pillow operations only. Frame 00 is a byte-for-byte pixel copy of the supplied first frame. For frames 01-19, a small antialiased RGBA marble layer is composited over a fresh copy of that same source frame. The marble uses nested translucent amber circles, a darker glass rim, a tiny contact shadow, and a rotating white specular highlight. The changing highlight phase suggests rolling while keeping the sphere's apparent size stable.

Ramp motion uses an accelerating distance curve along each measured line. Short transfers use continuous horizontal interpolation and quadratic vertical displacement to suggest gravity. In the last frame, the photographed front edge of the lower purple receiver is restored over the marble so it appears seated in the catch.

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, all 20 PNGs, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. The generator also creates transition_check.png from key-frame difference maps and verifies that every saved PNG exactly matches its corresponding tensor plane.

No later output was examined with an image viewing tool. Validation was performed using array equality, shapes, dtypes, pixel bounds, changed-pixel masks, connected-component measurements, centroid/path checks, and temporal difference statistics.
