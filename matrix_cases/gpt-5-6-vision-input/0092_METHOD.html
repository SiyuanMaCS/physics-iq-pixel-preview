lit-candle · 生成方法

This continuation was made entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations. No external model, engine, reference image, mask, network service, or other case was used.

Generation

  1. first_frame.png is decoded as RGB and retained verbatim as frame 00.
  2. A fixed half-open region (x=76..111, y=5..31) encloses the photographed left flame and its soft halo. No later frame changes pixels outside this region.
  3. For each row, the gray wall behind the flame is estimated with a linear fit to clean wall samples on both sides (x=64..76 and x=110..122). The positive luminance residual between the photograph and that estimate isolates a soft source-derived emissive layer. A radial confidence taper and top/bottom fades suppress wall texture and exclude the candle shaft.
  4. For frames 01-19, the measured emissive layer is bilinearly inverse-warped around the fixed wick anchor (93, 29). Small deterministic height, width, tip-lean, and intensity curves produce continuous flame flicker while preserving the source photograph's real colors, gradation, and halo.
  5. The warp is exactly locked on and below the wick-anchor row, and on all four edit-region boundaries. Thus the candle body, wick/base boundary, wall seam, table, both brass holders, and unlit right candle remain static.
  6. artifact_io.save packs the RGB frames into the required uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, writes the 20 PNGs, creates inspection.png, and embeds the lossless delta stream in index.html. transition_check.png is a separately generated sheet of enlarged flame crops, adjacent-difference heat maps, and numeric transition statistics.

Validation

generate.py asserts all of the following after saving:

  • shape is exactly (20, 144, 256, 3) and dtype is uint8;
  • frame 00 exactly equals the decoded first_frame.png;
  • all pixels outside the measured flame region are identical to frame 00;
  • every boundary pixel of the edit region is identical to frame 00;
  • each adjacent transition contains restrained local motion and all 20 frames are distinct;
  • each saved PNG exactly equals its in-memory frame;
  • pixels.npz is uint32, has the expected (144, 256, 20) layout, and exactly equals the packed RGB values.

Review of later outputs was performed through these local numeric checks only; no image Read/View tool was used on generated frames or contact sheets.
