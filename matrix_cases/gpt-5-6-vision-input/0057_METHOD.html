OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene layout

  • Source is 256 x 144 RGB. Camera and crop remain locked for all 20 frames.
  • The back edge of the warm wooden tabletop runs diagonally from about (0, 51) through (109, 24) to (255, 30). The full lower field is the tabletop; the upper field is a dark background.
  • The yellow rubber duck occupies approximately x=104..141, y=47..91. Its head is centered near (120, 59), bill points left, and its body rests near (123, 77).
  • The right-side domino run, in impact order from the platform toward the duck, has four upright pieces with planted bases near (179, 97), (167, 95), (157, 93), and (148, 91). Their tops lie near y=65..74 and their height differences follow the table perspective. Colors alternate black, white, black, white.
  • A black circular drive platform occupies approximately x=207..255, y=57..91, with its pivot near (231, 68). A tan wooden arm extends from that pivot toward (179, 75), already close to the first black domino.
  • Left of the duck are three visible upright dominoes centered near (101, 61), (93, 59), and (87, 58), alternating black/white/black. The duck interrupts the otherwise straight chain.
  • Lighting comes mainly from upper-left. Contact shadows fall subtly down/right on the tabletop. Generated objects retain that direction and the source image grain.

20-frame event plan (4 fps)

  1. Exact source frame, unchanged.
  2. The drive arm begins a small clockwise sweep; everything else remains upright.
  3. Arm reaches and loads the first (rightmost black) domino.
  4. First domino visibly tilts left; its base stays planted and its shadow broadens.
  5. First domino falls farther and contacts the adjacent white domino.
  6. Second domino begins falling left as the first approaches the tabletop.
  7. Second contacts the third black domino; the first is nearly flat.
  8. Third domino begins falling left; arm has completed its short powered sweep.
  9. Third contacts the fourth white domino.
  10. Fourth domino starts falling toward the duck; earlier pieces settle.
  11. Fourth domino leans into the duck's right side.
  12. Duck compresses/nudges slightly left and begins a gentle counterclockwise rock.
  13. Duck rocks left a little farther while the last right domino drops.
  14. Duck reaches its maximum small displacement; left-side dominoes remain upright because the soft duck absorbs most of the impulse.
  15. Duck begins elastic recovery and rocks back right.
  16. Duck passes close to its original pose with a smaller overshoot.
  17. Duck returns left by a sub-pixel-scale damped amount; fallen dominoes stop moving.
  18. Duck nearly settles; the arm and platform are stationary.
  19. Only the last tiny damped duck motion remains.
  20. Static final state: four right-side dominoes lie in a staggered pile against the duck; left-side dominoes remain standing.

The intended physics is a powered clockwise arm sweep followed by a sequential, base-pivoting domino cascade. The rubber duck behaves as a compliant, highly damped interruption rather than transmitting enough impulse to continue the chain on its far side.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

The continuation is a deterministic local Python pixel animation built from first_frame.png; no external model, engine, reference image, or additional scene input was used.

  1. The camera image is held at its original 256 x 144 crop. Frame 00 is copied directly from the source array.
  2. The four right-side domino silhouettes were measured from source pixels and stored as compact polygon masks. Each masked RGB cutout is moved with a 2D affine rotation about its photographed table-contact point. The fall is a cubic smoothstep over four frames, staggered by two frames per domino, ending at a 77-degree lean toward the duck.
  3. The original movable regions and their contact shadows are removed from a clean plate with OpenCV Telea inpainting. This affects only the arm/domino/duck working region. Vacated pixels therefore reveal a locally reconstructed version of the same photographed wooden tabletop.
  4. The wooden arm is drawn as a supersampled transparent overlay. Its endpoint follows an 11-degree clockwise arc around the platform pivot. Rendering on a transparent overlay is important: the photograph itself is never resampled, so untouched pixels remain bit-exact.
  5. New soft contact shadows are analytic blurred polygons aligned with each transformed domino. Their low opacity, down-right offset, and blur match the source's upper-left illumination.
  6. The duck mask is derived from the dominant saturated-yellow connected component within a tightly bounded region. Its outer contour is filled so the original eye, bill shading, highlights, and texture remain in the cutout. After the last domino reaches it, the duck receives a maximum two-pixel translation and three-degree rock, followed by alternating, rapidly damped recovery offsets. The left-side dominoes are deliberately left upright: the rubber body absorbs the small remaining impulse.
  7. The final frames are packed as uint32 0xRRGGBB, transposed to (144, 256, 20), and written through the supplied artifact helper. The PNG sequence is written from the same uint8 RGB tensor.

Timing

  • Frames 00–02: exact start, then clockwise arm engagement.
  • Frames 03–11: staggered right-to-left domino cascade.
  • Frames 11–18: small damped duck response.
  • Frame 19: settled arm, fallen right-side dominoes, upright left-side dominoes.

Verification and revision

The first render exposed whole-frame sub-pixel drift because an early arm-rendering path resampled the photograph. It was revised to use a transparent overlay; the corrected output has zero changed pixels outside the bounded action region.

Final programmatic checks confirm:

  • 20 frames, each RGB uint8 at 256 x 144;
  • frames/00.png exactly equals first_frame.png pixel-for-pixel;
  • all saved PNGs exactly equal the in-memory frame tensor;
  • pixels.npz has uint32 shape (144, 256, 20) and exactly matches every packed PNG pixel;
  • motion is local to x=90..238, y=38..109, with no camera or background drift;
  • first-to-last global mean RGB drift is below one code value per channel;
  • inspection.png is the helper-generated frame contact sheet;
  • transition_check.png is a code-generated set of absolute-difference heatmaps labeled with changed-pixel counts and peak deltas.

Generated later frames were not visually inspected with an image-viewing tool. Revision and acceptance used source measurements, array statistics, masks, bounding boxes, temporal pixel deltas, and exact serialization comparisons, as requested.
