OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Scene measurements from first_frame.png

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 RGB, fixed camera, low-contrast indoor illumination.
  • The wooden tabletop occupies the lower portion and rises toward the right in perspective. A thin black cable remains fixed across the table from the foreground platform toward the lower-right edge.
  • Foreground tennis ball: yellow/green sphere centered approximately at (117.2, 101.5), visible diameter about 19 px, with bounds x=108..126 and y=93..111. The black smiley is on the camera-facing surface. Its black circular platform is centered near (117, 117), approximately x=97..138 and y=103..129.
  • Reflected ball: centered approximately at (190.0, 80.5), visible diameter about 15 px, with yellow bounds x=183..197 and y=75..86. Its reflected surface initially has no smiley. The reflected platform is approximately x=176..204 and y=83..98.
  • Mirror/background geometry: the strong vertical boundary near x=135..137 and the dark right-side structure stay unchanged. The reflection is dimmer, smaller, and warmer than the foreground ball.
  • All background, table, cable, platforms, lighting, exposure, and camera framing remain static. Only the spherical surface appearance of the two ball images changes.

Motion model

The ball spins in place about its vertical axis, clockwise when viewed from above. In the camera view this carries the front smiley progressively toward the ball's right limb; perspective compression makes the marks narrower near the limb. The same marked longitude subsequently begins to appear in the right-side mirror view, entering its reflected ball from the appropriate limb. The sphere centers, silhouettes, cast shadows, and platforms do not translate.

The angular phase uses smooth ease-in/ease-out motion over 5 seconds, reaching about 78 degrees: visibly slow and continuous, with no looping reset or sudden jump.

20-frame event plan (4 fps)

  • Frame 00 (0.00 s): exact supplied first frame; smile centered on foreground ball; reflected side unmarked.
  • Frames 01-03 (0.25-0.75 s): rotation begins gently; smile shifts subtly right, reflection remains effectively unmarked.
  • Frames 04-07 (1.00-1.75 s): steady slow spin; facial marks move across the right half and narrow with spherical foreshortening.
  • Frames 08-11 (2.00-2.75 s): smile approaches the foreground limb; its outer portions progressively self-occlude; a faint edge of the marked longitude begins entering the reflection.
  • Frames 12-15 (3.00-3.75 s): most of the foreground smile passes around the right limb while the corresponding marks become increasingly visible on the reflected ball.
  • Frames 16-18 (4.00-4.50 s): clockwise rotation slows gently; foreground face is mostly side-on, reflected face is clearer but still oblique.
  • Frame 19 (4.75 s): final held phase near 78 degrees, with continuous physical orientation and no change to object position or scene geometry.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

The continuation is produced entirely by local Python pixel operations in generate.py; no external engine, model, image source, mask, or network service is used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as an RGB uint8 array. Frame 00 is copied directly from that array without modification.
  2. Two fixed sphere masks are defined from numeric scene measurements:
    • foreground ball: center (117.2, 101.5), radii (9.8, 9.3)
    • reflected ball: center (190.0, 80.5), radii (7.8, 6.4)
  3. A small mask isolates the dark printed smile in the foreground ball. Its covered background is reconstructed by normalized Gaussian interpolation from nearby unmarked pixels on the same ball. The source remains byte-identical everywhere outside that repair patch.
  4. The smile is represented in spherical texture coordinates by two compact eye marks and a curved mouth. For every output pixel, the code inverts the sphere projection with longitude = asin(normalized_x), subtracts the current rotation phase, and tests the resulting object-space coordinate against those marks. Five-by-five subpixel sampling plus a slight Gaussian softening produces stable antialiasing at the ball's small size.
  5. The phase advances clockwise through 78 degrees using cubic ease-in/ease-out across the 20 frames. This moves the foreground marking toward the right limb and compresses it naturally as the sphere turns.
  6. The mirror is rendered from the same physical phase with an 87-degree side-view yaw. Thus the marked longitude is absent initially, then enters the reflected ball from its left limb. Its ink strength is reduced to match the dimmer reflection.
  7. Ink is composited multiplicatively over the source shading so the existing highlight and tonal variation remain intact. A one-second residual calibration blend transitions from the exact supplied raster smile to the analytic texture without a first-frame redraw jump.
  8. Every pixel outside the two ball surfaces—including platforms, mirror, table, cord, shadows, and background—is copied unchanged from the first frame.

Timing

  • 20 frames
  • 256 x 144 RGB
  • 4 fps
  • 5 seconds total
  • Frame 00 exactly matches first_frame.png

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the PNG sequence, packed uint32 tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png, containing selected two-ball crops and amplified adjacent-frame differences.

The generator verifies all of the following on every run:

  • exact frame count, shape, and uint8 RGB type;
  • exact source equality for frame 00;
  • zero changes outside the allowed ball surfaces;
  • non-static adjacent transitions and bounded changed-pixel area;
  • monotonic rightward movement of the foreground printed texture;
  • exact equality among every saved PNG, the generated RGB array, and the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz.

The outputs were assessed through these local numeric checks only; no visual review of generated frames was performed.
