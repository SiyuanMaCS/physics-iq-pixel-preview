OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Source is an RGB 256 x 144 locked-off frame.
  • The light wooden tabletop fills most of the frame. Its grain is predominantly horizontal and must stay fixed.
  • The black pipe enters from the left; its visible outlet is near x=14, y=50 and points across the table toward the ramp.
  • The tennis ball occupies approximately x=78..95, y=45..62, with its chromatic green core measured at x=80..92, y=47..58; measured center is about (86.4, 53.4). The visible radius is about 9 px, including its soft edge.
  • The cardboard ramp begins at a near vertical edge around x=145..153, y=35..75. Its low/near contact corner is about (153, 75), and its visible lower edge climbs toward the right.
  • A blue support block occupies approximately x=218..253, y=40..72; it remains static and is partly occluded by the cardboard.
  • Illumination is soft and dim, with the ball casting a diffuse shadow mainly down/right. Nothing except the ball and its shadow should move.
  • Intended motion line from the pipe/initial ball position to the low ramp edge is diagonal down/right. Once on the ramp, the projected path turns up/right along the cardboard surface.

METHOD.md

Method

Generation

The animation is generated entirely from first_frame.png with deterministic local NumPy, OpenCV, and Pillow operations in generate.py.

  1. Frame 00 is copied directly from the supplied PNG without modification.
  2. For frames 01–19, a small elliptical region covering the original tennis ball and its diffuse shadow is filled with OpenCV's Telea inpainting, using only neighboring pixels from the source photograph. This produces a fixed clean plate while retaining the photographed pipe, wood, ramp, and blue support.
  3. The photographed ball is extracted from its original location with a feathered circular alpha matte. Edge foreground colors are estimated from the original image and the local clean plate, so the sprite can be composited onto both wood and cardboard without carrying a rectangular table patch.
  4. Hand-measured centers define a continuous path from the pipe, diagonally across the table to the low ramp edge, then up/right along the cardboard. Projected step length decreases monotonically after the ball is fully on the incline. The radius decreases from about 9.4 px to 8.2 px for mild perspective.
  5. The extracted ball texture is bilinearly rotated by accumulated travel divided by ball radius, implementing rolling without slipping in screen space.
  6. A blurred analytic ellipse supplies the cast/contact shadow. Its offset, aspect ratio, softness, and opacity interpolate over the ramp-edge crossing from a broad tabletop shadow to a tighter cardboard-plane shadow.
  7. Only the ball and its shadow are recomposited. The camera and all scene geometry remain locked.

Output and checks

artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the 20 RGB PNGs, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. generate.py also makes transition_check.png, a chronological 5-by-4 sheet containing every generated frame.

Validation was performed with local numeric checks rather than opening generated imagery in an image viewer:

  • all 20 PNGs are 256 x 144 RGB uint8 images;
  • frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every PNG unpacks pixel-identically from pixels.npz;
  • green-object centroids follow the planned path and the detected green footprint shrinks gradually on the ramp;
  • changed-pixel bounding boxes follow only the moving ball/shadow region;
  • the pipe, upper table band, and lower table remain exactly static after frame 01;
  • the reconstructed initial ball region has no residual green pixels in late frames, and its inpaint-boundary gradient is below the ordinary local wood-gradient distribution.

No external assets, image models, engines, masks, cases, or network resources were used. Generated outputs were inspected only through code and numeric measurements.
