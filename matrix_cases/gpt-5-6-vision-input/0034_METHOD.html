Pixel construction

The animation is made entirely with local Python pixel operations using Pillow, NumPy, and OpenCV. No external images, masks, models, engines, or network resources are used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as a 256 x 144 RGB array. Frame 00 is assigned directly from that array without resampling or compositing.
  2. The six visible blocks are measured as quadrilaterals. Each quadrilateral becomes a 4x supersampled, Lanczos-downsampled alpha mask, retaining the actual photographed RGB texture inside the block.
  3. A later-frame clean plate is made only for the pixels originally hidden by the blocks. The combined masks are slightly dilated, reconstructed with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting, and lightly edge-blended. An upright block remains the exact source pixels; its clean-plate footprint is revealed only after that specific block begins moving.
  4. Each moving block is premultiplied by its alpha and transformed with a cubic affine rotation around its measured foot contact. Premultiplication avoids black interpolation fringes. The rotation follows a smoothstep curve to about 84 degrees, followed by a sub-degree damped settling motion.
  5. The rightmost purple block, nearest the platform, moves first. Starts are offset by two frames in the order purple, blue, green, pink, red-orange, yellow, producing the right-to-left domino transfer.
  6. The platform arm is rendered as a narrow shaded brown wooden radial piece with a dark mounting hub. In screen coordinates its angle strictly increases from 18 to 149 degrees, which is visually clockwise because image y increases downward. It reaches the purple block at frames 03-04. The arm is drawn behind any still-upright block.
  7. Soft shadows are computed from each transformed alpha silhouette, blurred, shifted down/right, and darkened over the reconstructed plate before the textured blocks are composited. The camera, source wall, platform, table, and lighting remain locked.

Output and checks

artifact_io.save writes the required packed tensor, PNG sequence, inspection sheet, and standalone player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png from all 19 adjacent-frame absolute differences.

The final numeric checks performed after generation were:

  • array shape: (20, 144, 256, 3), dtype uint8;
  • frame 00 equals first_frame.png exactly;
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and every decoded 0xRRGGBB value equals its corresponding PNG pixel;
  • all 20 frame byte hashes are unique;
  • adjacent changed-pixel counts range from 437 to 5,219, with no empty transition;
  • the far wall and lower-right table remain pixel-identical through the sequence;
  • final frame differs from frame 00 at 10,760 of 36,864 pixels, localized to the arm, platform-front overlap, blocks, and their shadows;
  • arm angles are monotonically clockwise and the measured block trajectories activate in the planned right-to-left order.

Later generated outputs were not visually reviewed with an image viewing tool; they were inspected only through local code, tensor decoding, pixel statistics, difference bounds, and analytic sprite trajectories, as requested.
