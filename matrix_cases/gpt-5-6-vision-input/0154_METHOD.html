OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Observed geometry

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB, locked camera, landscape framing.
  • A warm wooden tabletop fills the lower portion of the image. Its rear edge is near y=50 on the right and the left wall/table boundary runs diagonally to the lower-left corner, establishing strong depth perspective.
  • The real 30 lb black kettlebell occupies approximately x=86..136, y=47..105. Its rounded body is centered near (112, 82), with the handle above it.
  • A vertical mirror beside/behind the bell produces a second, smaller-looking dark kettlebell image around x=127..174, y=15..71. Both bell and reflection are static.
  • The bright chartreuse tennis ball begins partly outside the lower-right frame. Its visible extent is approximately x=194..234, y=117..143, implying a center near (213, 137) and radius about 19 px.
  • The intended table-plane route is from roughly (213.7, 138.7) toward a final center near (139.5, 91), where a roughly 9.5 px ball reaches the kettlebell's measured right edge near x=130. Because the ball moves away from the camera, its apparent radius shrinks from about 20 px to about 9.5 px.
  • Illumination is soft and warm. The ball is brightest on its upper-left, darker on its lower-right, and casts a small soft contact shadow on the tabletop.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00: exact source image, including the partly cropped ball at the lower-right edge.
  • Frames 01-03: the ball begins a slow roll up-left, initially moving only a few pixels per frame; the original ball area is restored as wood tabletop.
  • Frames 04-07: steady diagonal roll; perspective makes the ball gradually smaller. The curved tennis-ball seam rotates consistently with traveled distance.
  • Frames 08-11: ball crosses the open right-center tabletop, continuing toward the bell. Motion remains smooth with modest physical acceleration/deceleration rather than frame-to-frame jumps.
  • Frames 12-15: ball approaches the kettlebell, now noticeably smaller because it is farther from the camera. Its soft shadow remains attached to the table plane.
  • Frames 16-18: final slow approach to the kettlebell's lower-right edge. The bell and reflected bell remain perfectly fixed.
  • Frame 19: the ball reaches first contact with the heavy kettlebell near its right edge around (130, 91) and stops; there is no bounce or displacement of the 30 lb kettlebell within the shot.

Only frame 00 is the supplied photograph. Later frames will be synthesized locally by restoring the source ball area from nearby tabletop texture and compositing a perspective-scaled, shaded, rotating tennis ball plus a soft contact shadow. Later outputs will be checked through arrays, pixel statistics, masks, and generated diagnostic sheets only—not image viewing tools.

METHOD.md

Method

The supplied first_frame.png is copied without modification into frame 00.

For frames 01–19, I used only local Python pixel operations with NumPy, OpenCV, and Pillow:

  1. I detected the initial chartreuse ball by its channel ratios and expanded that footprint to include its antialiased edge and small contact shadow.
  2. I restored the covered tabletop using real same-row wood texture sampled 48 pixels to the left, with a Gaussian-feathered boundary. This preserves the tabletop's depth-dependent brightness and horizontal grain.
  3. I generated a spherical tennis-ball sprite with fixed upper-right lighting, edge shading, subtle deterministic felt texture, and color samples remapped from the visible portion of the photographed ball. The unseen lower cap is filled analytically.
  4. I animated the sprite from approximately (213.7, 138.7) to (139.5, 91.0) using a smooth start/stop trajectory. Its radius falls from 20.0 to 9.5 pixels to match the table perspective. A pale curved seam rotates by about one revolution according to traveled progress, and a soft elliptical contact shadow follows the ball.
  5. The final ball edge reaches the measured right edge of the kettlebell near x=130. The 30 lb kettlebell and its mirror image are never transformed or redrawn, and the clip ends at first contact without moving the bell.

inspection.png is produced by the provided output helper. transition_check.png is a code-generated sheet of amplified absolute frame-to-frame pixel differences. No later generated frame was inspected with an image Read/View tool; validation used array geometry, chroma centroids, monotonic motion checks, static-region equality, and exact PNG/tensor comparisons.
