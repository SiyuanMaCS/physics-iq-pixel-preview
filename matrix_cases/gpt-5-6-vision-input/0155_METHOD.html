OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB. The camera is locked off and looks slightly downward across a wooden tabletop.
  • The wall/table boundary runs diagonally from about (0, 112) to (104, 76), then continues behind the objects toward the right.
  • The physical 30 lb kettlebell is the dark connected component at approximately x=103..141, y=23..86. Its body/base is approximately x=103..141, y=45..86; the lower-right impact side is near (132, 82).
  • The reflected kettlebell is the dark component at approximately x=155..201, y=19..71. It remains static because the heavy physical kettlebell does not visibly move when struck by a tennis ball.
  • The tennis ball starts partly outside the lower frame, approximately x=132..168, y=123..143. Its inferred full-disc center is about (150, 141) with radius about 18 px.
  • The tennis ball travels away from the camera over the table toward the kettlebell. Perspective therefore makes it decrease from about 18 px radius to about 9 px radius.
  • Lighting comes broadly from the upper-left/front. The ball has a bright upper-left crown, a darker lower-right edge, and a small soft contact shadow on the tabletop.

METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is a deterministic local Python pixel animation; no external model, renderer, reference image, mask, score, network resource, or physics engine was used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as RGB and copied byte-for-byte to frame 00.
  2. The partly cropped source tennis ball was measured at an inferred center near (150, 142) and radius near 19.5 px. Its initial footprint is removed for subsequent frames with OpenCV TELEA inpainting restricted to a small elliptical tabletop region (x=128..172, y=120..143).
  3. A complete 45 x 45 RGBA tennis-ball sprite is built from the photographed visible ball pixels. The hidden lower hemisphere is reconstructed by mirroring visible surface samples, applying a fixed lower falloff, and filling low-confidence edge samples with a matching analytic lime/yellow surface. A subtle curved pale seam and deterministic low-amplitude nap provide a visible rotation cue. The edge uses a one-pixel alpha falloff.
  4. For frames 01–15, the sprite follows the measured tabletop path from the lower foreground toward the kettlebell. Its radius decreases from 17.7 to 10.2 px to express table perspective. Rotation is accumulated from frame-to-frame travel divided by apparent radius.
  5. At frame 15 the ball becomes tangent to the lower-right edge of the kettlebell. Frame 16 compresses its horizontal scale to 0.82 and expands its vertical scale to 1.08. Frames 17–19 rebound down/right with decreasing displacement. The 30 lb kettlebell and its reflection remain unchanged.
  6. A small blurred contact shadow is composited beneath the ball at every generated position. All pixels outside the ball/shadow/table-restoration corridor are copied from the static scene.
  7. artifact_io.save writes 20 RGB PNGs, the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, the inspection sheet, and the standalone player. transition_check.png contains 4x-amplified absolute differences for selected adjacent frames.

Verification

  • Array shape and type: (20, 144, 256, 3), uint8.
  • Frame 00 is exactly equal to first_frame.png.
  • Every saved PNG is decoded and compared exactly against its source frame array.
  • pixels.npz has shape (144, 256, 20), type uint32, and exactly matches packed RGB values for every pixel and frame.
  • Tennis-color connected-component measurements confirm a continuous approach, perspective-size decrease, single compressed impact, and down/right rebound.
  • Collision-mask measurement finds no ball/kettlebell overlap during approach, one antialiased overlap pixel at peak compression, and none after rebound.
  • Pixel-change bounding boxes remain confined to the intended motion corridor; later generated outputs were inspected through local code and numeric checks, not image viewing tools.
