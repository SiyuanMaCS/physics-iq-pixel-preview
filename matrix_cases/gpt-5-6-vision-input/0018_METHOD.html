ball-in-sand · 生成方法

Actual generation method

The sequence is a deterministic local pixel animation written in Python with NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. It does not call a renderer, external model, network service, or external asset.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as the source RGB array. Frame 00 is copied directly from this array without resampling or recomposition.
  2. A small elliptical mask around the source ball is repaired with OpenCV Telea inpainting to make the wall plate exposed after release. A separate green-HSV mask isolates the kinetic-sand texture; a second inpainted plate supplies only the wood pixels exposed when the mound crown compresses.
  3. The tennis ball is source-derived rather than redrawn. Source pixels near center (118, 61) are inverse-warped to a prescribed vertical center, with a feathered elliptical alpha and a small, gradually stopping rotation. A difference-from-clean-plate term suppresses source-wall contamination at its edge.
  4. The ball center follows a hand-authored gravity/damping path: 61 px initially, then 72, 83, 92, 98, and 100 px through maximum penetration, followed by a 1.4 px recovery and asymptotic settling to 100 px. This is intentionally strongly damped because kinetic sand yields plastically rather than producing a large bounce.
  5. From contact onward, the source mound texture is inverse-warped. Its crown moves downward by up to 8 px near the impact center, while its sides spread horizontally by up to 11.5%. The deformation ramps to full compression, recovers slightly with the ball, and then converges back to the final compressed state.
  6. The ball is composited after the rear mound, then the foreground mound texture is composited again below a smooth contact-depth threshold. That ordering buries the ball's lower part. Four soft, irregular source-textured rim clumps form at the contact shoulders.
  7. Six deterministic, one-pixel-scale source-colored sand clumps follow short ballistic arcs during frames 03–11 and stop on a curved mound/table floor. A compact approach/contact shadow is analytically shaded beneath the ball. Gaussian support is explicitly clipped, preventing distant pixels from changing.
  8. The camera, wall, table, and suspended grabber are never transformed. The detected blue/dark grabber pixels in y<43 are identical to frame 00 in all 19 later frames.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes the 20 RGB PNG frames, the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor in pixels.npz, the inspection contact sheet, and the self-contained HTML player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png from contrast-boosted adjacent-frame absolute differences.

The generator then reloads every PNG and pixels.npz and asserts exact equality with the in-memory frames. Verified properties include:

  • shape: 20 x 144 x 256 x 3, RGB uint8;
  • tensor shape: 144 x 256 x 20, uint32 packed as 0xRRGGBB;
  • frame 00 exactly equals first_frame.png;
  • all 20 PNGs exactly equal their corresponding tensor planes;
  • all meaningful motion is local to the original ball/sand setup;
  • the final adjacent transition contains only eight changed pixels, consistent with motion coming to rest.

No generated/later frame was inspected with an image Read/View tool. Evaluation and revision used local code, array statistics, connected components, color-mask centroids, changed-pixel bounds, and exact byte comparisons only.
