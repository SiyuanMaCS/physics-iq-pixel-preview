OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Scene measurements

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB, landscape, fixed camera.
  • The wall occupies approximately y=0..88 and the wood tabletop/floor y=89..143. The wall is a smooth gray vignette; the floor has low-contrast horizontal/diagonal grain and a strong contact-shadow region beneath the structure.
  • The gate-like structure is centered near x=133. Its blue lintel spans approximately x=113..153, y=63..72. The left red/orange leg occupies about x=114..121, y=70..109; the right yellow leg occupies about x=144..152, y=70..109.
  • The placed pink rectangular block occupies approximately x=134..143, y=30..65. Its bottom already intersects/seats on the top face of the blue lintel, so the first frame is the end of placement rather than an approach pose.
  • A narrow blue-black grabber enters through the top edge around x=130..145 and terminates at about y=30, touching the center of the pink block. It has a black core/shadow and saturated blue edge/highlight.
  • Lighting is soft and overhead/front-left. Shadows, wall vignette, floor perspective, and all structure geometry must remain locked.

Interpretation

The supplied first frame shows the pink block already carefully placed and still contacted by the grabber. The visible continuation is therefore a short release/hold followed by a smooth upward retraction of the tool. The pink block remains supported and does not follow the tool. No new objects enter.

METHOD.md

Method

Actual generation method

The animation is a deterministic local Python pixel edit built from first_frame.png; no external model, engine, reference image, mask, or network source was used.

  1. The first frame was measured to locate the top-entering grabber at approximately x=129..145, y=0..31. Its inclusive silhouette width was recorded row by row so the antialiased taper at the bottom would be retained.
  2. A clean wall plate was estimated independently for each row and RGB channel. A quadratic was fit to wall-only samples on both sides of the grabber (x=116..128 and x=146..158), then a small amount of deterministic local residual texture was added back. This preserves the existing wall vignette and avoids a flat painted patch.
  3. The grabber's final two rows overlap the top of the pink block. Those occluded pink pixels were reconstructed from the first fully visible face row with progressively darker top/contact shading. No visible gate, floor, or shadow pixels were altered.
  4. The grabber was represented as its signed, premultiplied RGB residual relative to the clean plate. That residual was translated upward by integer offsets [0, 0, 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 20, 25, 32], followed by ten frames at the clean endpoint. This produces an eased, monotonic retraction while preserving the original soft boundary. Frame 00 and the brief frame 01 hold are exact copies of the input.
  5. artifact_io.save wrote the canonical packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, all RGB PNG frames, the inspection contact sheet, and the standalone delta-compressed HTML player. transition_check.png contains selected frames plus 4x absolute-difference maps from frame 00.

Verification

  • Shape and type: 20 frames, each 256 x 144 RGB uint8, at the required 4 fps / 5 seconds.
  • Source preservation: frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png.
  • Spatial confinement: every changed pixel is inside x=129..145, y=0..31; the pink block below, gate, floor, shadows, and camera framing remain fixed.
  • Motion check: the changed-tool footprint contracts monotonically upward and is absent from frame 09 onward; frames 09..19 are identical.
  • Clean-plate check: the two wall patch boundaries have mean per-channel discontinuities of approximately 1.2 and 0.8 RGB levels over y=0..28.
  • Storage parity: every PNG was reloaded and compared with the in-memory frame array, and pixels.npz was reconstructed and compared exactly against the packed 0xRRGGBB tensor.

The supplied first frame was observed visually through the user's attachment. Later/generated frames were not reviewed with an image Read/View tool; they were inspected only by local numeric checks (pixel equality, masks, bounding boxes, centroids, RGB discontinuities, and tensor parity).
