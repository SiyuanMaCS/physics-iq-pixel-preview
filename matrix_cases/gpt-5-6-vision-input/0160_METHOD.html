OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene layout

  • Canvas: 256 x 144 pixels, fixed camera, warm wooden tabletop and softly lit gray wall.
  • Newton's cradle base: approximately x=109..209, y=126..143 (the bottom edge is cropped by frame).
  • Front support frame: left post around x=109, top rail y=18..22, right post around x=205, with shiny dark/chrome tubing.
  • Rear support frame: left post around x=85, top rail y=9..13, rear/right post around x=168.
  • Resting ball row: five small chrome balls centered approximately at (130,105), (140,103), (150,101), (160,100), and (169,102), each about 7-9 pixels across. Their suspension wires rise toward the rails.
  • Held/released left ball: centered approximately at (78,112), displaced far to the left and attached to a suspension point near (108,19). It is partly adjacent to the black jaw of the blue grabber.
  • Grabber: blue handles enter from the left edge and occupy roughly x=0..70, y=88..126; its narrow black jaws reach the ball around x=78.
  • Lighting comes from upper left. Metal balls have bright white highlights, dark lower arcs, and small soft shadows/reflections on the black base.

Animation method and physical intent

Use the supplied photograph as the locked background. Reconstruct only small regions exposed by the moving grabber, wires, and balls using local texture/color interpolation from the same image. Render moving wires as antialiased translucent gray lines and moving balls from image-derived chrome ball sprites with highlight/shadow preserved. Use a damped, piecewise Newton's-cradle motion: the released left ball accelerates into the row, transfers momentum to the rightmost ball, the right ball rises and returns, then the left ball rebounds with reduced amplitude. The supports, base, table, wall, and camera remain fixed.

METHOD.md

Generation method

The result is a deterministic 20-frame, 4 fps continuation built locally from first_frame.png. No external images, models, engines, masks, or reference cases were used.

Image construction

  1. The supplied RGB photograph is loaded as the locked camera plate. Frame 00 is kept untouched and, after artifact serialization, the original PNG is copied directly to frames/00.png so it is both pixel-identical and file-identical to the input.
  2. A conservative color-and-shape matte isolates the blue/black grabber. The newly exposed tabletop is reconstructed from a polynomial fit to unobstructed pixels on the same photographed table, plus deterministic correlated grain and subtle directional wood variation derived procedurally.
  3. The initially displaced ball, its two old suspension strands, and the far end ball are removed only within small masks using OpenCV Telea inpainting. The fixed support frame, base, wall, and remaining cradle balls stay on the original plate.
  4. Chrome ball sprites are copied from the two corresponding balls in the source photograph with feathered circular mattes, preserving the photographed highlights and dark reflections.
  5. Motion follows explicit piecewise positions. The released left ball accelerates toward the row over frames 01-05. Momentum is transferred to the far-right ball, which swings outward and returns over frames 06-13. The left ball then makes a smaller damped rebound over frames 14-19. Thin antialiased suspension strands connect each moving ball to fixed measured suspension points. A faint base reflection and a very light two-sample exposure trail on the fastest incoming steps help integrate the motion.
  6. The grabber translates left after release and is entirely outside the frame by frame 06. Every other scene element remains locked.

Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz, the 20 PNG frames, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. transition_check.png is a 5-by-4 sheet of amplified consecutive-frame difference maps annotated with changed-pixel counts and mean RGB deltas.

The generator then verifies:

  • tensor shape (144, 256, 20) and uint32 packed 0xRRGGBB representation;
  • exact equality of frame 00 to first_frame.png;
  • exact RGB equality between every PNG, the in-memory frames, and the decoded tensor;
  • non-frozen consecutive frames;
  • localized motion (each transition changes fewer than 15,500 of 36,864 pixels);
  • restrained temporal deltas (mean absolute RGB change below 24 per transition).

Later generated outputs were not inspected with an image Read/View tool. Validation was performed through local array decoding, pixel equality assertions, difference bounding boxes, fixed-region checks, transition counts, and file/dimension checks.
