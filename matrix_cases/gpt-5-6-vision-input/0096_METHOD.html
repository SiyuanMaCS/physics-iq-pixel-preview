magnet-domino · 生成方法

The animation is a deterministic local pixel transformation of first_frame.png; no external images, models, masks, references, or engines are used.

  1. The first source frame is loaded as RGB and retained unchanged as frame 00.
  2. A hand-measured convex quadrilateral isolates the photographed pale domino at x=101–126, y=40–54. Its real source pixels, including the original surface shading, become the moving color layer. A subpixel-feathered version of the same shape supplies alpha.
  3. The object's original footprint and immediate edge fringe are removed with a small OpenCV Telea inpaint constrained to that local footprint. Everything outside the footprint remains bit-identical to the source.
  4. For frames 01–19, the domino layer is transformed about (113, 47.5) with bicubic interpolation. Its relative rotation follows a smoothstep-eased 0–170 degree clockwise curve over five seconds, plus a closed drift of at most 1.35 px horizontally and 0.70 px vertically. The rotation is conjugated through a 0.58 vertical tabletop-foreshortening transform, so it represents rotation on the photographed surface instead of a flat billboard spinning in the image plane.
  5. A blurred, offset copy of the moving alpha creates a restrained contact shadow before the real object pixels are composited. The camera, magnet, platform, cable, table, and background remain fixed.
  6. artifact_io.save writes all PNG frames, the uint32 packed pixel tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py also writes transition_check.png and performs exact array checks between the RGB frames, every saved PNG, and pixels.npz.

The generated diagnostic images were not viewed with an image viewing tool; validation is performed by code using dimensions, equality checks, pixel-change bounds, per-frame change counts, and motion statistics.
