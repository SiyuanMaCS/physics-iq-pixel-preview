+def make_domino_layers(first: np.ndarray):
+    """Return a clean plate and the domino color/alpha layers.
+
+    The quadrilateral follows the pale object's antialiased outside edge.  A
+    slightly larger mask is used for inpainting so no stationary pale fringe
+    remains when the object turns.
+    """
+    # Source-frame polygon, measured in the supplied 256x144 frame.
+    polygon = np.array([[105, 39], [127, 46], [121, 55], [99, 48]], np.int32)
+
+    hard = np.zeros((H, W), np.uint8)
+    cv2.fillConvexPoly(hard, polygon, 255, lineType=cv2.LINE_AA)
+
+    # Preserve the real photographed color and edge shading, with a gently
+    # feathered silhouette for stable subpixel transforms.
+    alpha = cv2.GaussianBlur(hard, (0, 0), 0.45).astype(np.float32) / 255.0
+    color = first.astype(np.float32)
+
+    # Remove the original domino plus its immediate contact-edge remnants.
+    clean_mask = cv2.dilate((hard > 20).astype(np.uint8) * 255,
+                            cv2.getStructuringElement(cv2.MORPH_ELLIPSE, (5, 5)))
+    clean = cv2.inpaint(first, clean_mask, 4.0, cv2.INPAINT_TELEA)
+
+    # Restrict any inpaint change to the explicit footprint. The fixed scene
+    # is therefore bit-identical everywhere else.
+    plate = first.copy()
+    plate[clean_mask > 0] = clean[clean_mask > 0]
+    return plate, color, alpha
+

+def warp_about(color: np.ndarray, alpha: np.ndarray, angle_deg: float,
+               dx: float, dy: float):

+    center = (113.0, 47.5)
+    matrix = cv2.getRotationMatrix2D(center, -angle_deg, 1.0)
+    matrix[:, 2] += (dx, dy)

+        borderMode=cv2.BORDER_REFLECT_101,

OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Measured scene

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB, landscape, fixed camera.
  • The tabletop fills most of the image; the pale wall/backdrop occupies the upper band.
  • The black circular platform occupies approximately x=105–181, y=40–100. Its visible top is an ellipse centered near (143, 62), with a dark cylindrical side below it.
  • A circular silver/gray magnet assembly is at approximately x=78–101, y=30–60, immediately left of the platform. Purple strips/supports extend down-left from it.
  • The small pale domino is the rectangular piece at approximately x=100–125, y=41–53. It begins almost horizontal, with a slight clockwise/down-right slant, and lies over the platform's upper-left top surface.
  • A thin black cable leaves the right side of the platform and continues to the right frame edge.
  • Lighting is soft and constant, with no camera movement. All non-domino scene elements must remain pixel-stationary.

20-frame event plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

  • Frame 00: exact copy of first_frame.png.
  • Frames 01–03: the domino begins a subtle clockwise turn on the platform; motion starts gently.
  • Frames 04–08: it rotates clockwise at a clear, steady rate while remaining near its initial center and fully supported by the platform.
  • Frames 09–14: clockwise rotation continues through a near-vertical orientation and beyond, with constant lighting and a small contact shadow.
  • Frames 15–18: the domino approaches a roughly 170-degree total clockwise rotation and eases down.
  • Frame 19: the domino comes to rest; the camera, magnet, platform, cable, tabletop, and background remain unchanged.

The trajectory is a smooth eased 0–170 degree clockwise in-plane rotation about the domino's center near (112, 47). A very small elliptical center drift (under 2 px) prevents a mechanically perfect pivot while keeping the object near the magnet and safely on the platform.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic local pixel transformation of first_frame.png; no external images, models, masks, references, or engines are used.

  1. The first source frame is loaded as RGB and retained unchanged as frame 00.
  2. A hand-measured convex quadrilateral isolates the photographed pale domino at x=101–126, y=40–54. Its real source pixels, including the original surface shading, become the moving color layer. A subpixel-feathered version of the same shape supplies alpha.
  3. The object's original footprint and immediate edge fringe are removed with a small OpenCV Telea inpaint constrained to that local footprint. Everything outside the footprint remains bit-identical to the source.
  4. For frames 01–19, the domino layer is transformed about (113, 47.5) with bicubic interpolation. Its relative rotation follows a smoothstep-eased 0–170 degree clockwise curve over five seconds, plus a closed drift of at most 1.35 px horizontally and 0.70 px vertically. The rotation is conjugated through a 0.58 vertical tabletop-foreshortening transform, so it represents rotation on the photographed surface instead of a flat billboard spinning in the image plane.
  5. A blurred, offset copy of the moving alpha creates a restrained contact shadow before the real object pixels are composited. The camera, magnet, platform, cable, table, and background remain fixed.
  6. artifact_io.save writes all PNG frames, the uint32 packed pixel tensor, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. generate.py also writes transition_check.png and performs exact array checks between the RGB frames, every saved PNG, and pixels.npz.

The generated diagnostic images were not viewed with an image viewing tool; validation is performed by code using dimensions, equality checks, pixel-change bounds, per-frame change counts, and motion statistics.
