OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

First-frame measurements

  • Source: first_frame.png, 256 x 144 RGB. The camera is locked off and the entire five-second continuation will keep this framing.
  • A pale beige wood-grain tabletop fills the foreground and middle of frame. Its rear edge rises slightly toward the right, from roughly y=32 at the left to y=17 at the right. A mustard sofa occupies the upper background.
  • The black pipe enters from the left. Its visible mouth/body occupies approximately x=0..20, y=40..68 and points horizontally to the right.
  • The brown ball is already clear of the pipe, centered at approximately (84, 51), with a visible radius of about 9 px. It has a dark lower-left rim and a soft highlight toward its upper-right.
  • The yellow rubber duck occupies approximately x=201..223, y=29..51, centered near (212, 41). Its orange beak points right. The table contact is near (212, 50).
  • The ball-to-duck travel direction is therefore a straight, shallow up-right screen-space line, from (84, 51) toward contact at about (194, 44). This also follows the tabletop's perspective.
  • The scene contains mild image noise, JPEG-like texture, and soft object edges. New pixels should retain that photographic character rather than use hard vector outlines.

METHOD.md

Method

This continuation is a deterministic local pixel animation built only from first_frame.png and the written prompt.

  1. The decoded RGB source is measured at 256 x 144. Frame 00 is inserted directly from that array with no resampling, filtering, or color conversion after decoding.
  2. Soft silhouettes for the photographed brown ball and yellow duck are constructed around their measured first-frame bounds. Their RGB pixels are retained as premultiplied object layers.
  3. Tight masks over the original ball, duck, and their contact shadows are filled from nearby tabletop pixels with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. Before impact, only the ball footprint is reconstructed so the original duck pixels remain untouched. From impact onward, both footprints are reconstructed.
  4. The ball follows a shallow straight line from (84, 51) toward the duck with slight frictional deceleration. Its extracted texture rotates according to traveled distance divided by radius. At contact it receives one frame of horizontal squash and vertical expansion, then slows sharply.
  5. Starting at frame 13, the duck is affine-transformed about its table-contact pivot. It translates right, tips clockwise, and then undergoes a small damped settling motion. Ball and duck receive soft, locally rendered contact shadows and very low deterministic sensor-like noise only within moved-object mattes.
  6. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, all PNG frames, the inspection sheet, and the standalone HTML player. transition_check.png contains contact-adjacent source frames beside 4x amplified adjacent-frame differences.
  7. The generator reloads pixels.npz and every written PNG, repacks the in-memory RGB frames, and requires exact equality. It separately requires frame 00 to equal the decoded first_frame.png pixel-for-pixel.

No external images, masks, models, rendering engines, or network resources are used. The later generated images were not assessed with an image Read/View tool; validation is performed through code, pixel equality, motion bounds, and numeric transition statistics.
