OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

Locked-shot scene measurements

  • Source frame: 256 x 144 RGB, landscape, fixed camera.
  • Whiteboard / pale backdrop occupies roughly x=0..169. A narrow dark vertical frame is near x=21..28, and the board's right frame is near x=164..174.
  • The right background is a muted olive wall (about x=174..255). A pale blue-gray board/reflection polygon occupies approximately x=172..204, y=20..143.
  • The run is a staggered, descending zig-zag of magnetic ramps:
    • dark green curved entry at about x=48..84, y=0..16;
    • short mustard top ramp at about x=79..115, y=18..32, descending to the right;
    • long violet ramp at about x=110..164, y=38..53, rising to the right, hence rolling left;
    • short magenta transfer guide at about x=94..117, y=51..68;
    • long dark-blue ramp at about x=112..166, y=69..85, rising to the right, hence rolling left;
    • long green ramp at about x=56..120, y=86..100, descending to the right;
    • long mustard lower ramp at about x=77..136, y=115..132, rising to the right, hence rolling left.
  • The yellow-green glass marble is initially around (99, 20), approximately 6 px across. It has a dark olive edge, a translucent yellow-green body, and a small bright highlight.
  • Illumination is soft and subdued. The board has low-frequency shadows; the ramps have dark contact shadows. The moving marble should retain a small directional shadow/highlight without changing the rest of the locked frame.

Motion and physics approach

  • Preserve the camera and all apparatus exactly. Reconstruct only the tiny initial-marble patch by local texture-aware inpainting, then composite an analytically shaded glass marble and a faint contact/drop shadow.
  • Motion follows a piecewise path with gravity-like acceleration on free-fall arcs and rolling motion along each ramp. At impacts the marble is compressed by one pixel vertically for one frame, then continues along the new ramp. Because the output is only 4 fps, positions prioritize an unambiguous continuous route over sub-frame contact detail.

METHOD.md

Method

The animation is a deterministic local Python pixel construction. No external model, renderer, engine, reference image, mask, or network resource is used.

  1. first_frame.png is loaded as the sole scene input. Frame 00 is copied from it exactly.
  2. For frames 01–19, the original marble's tiny footprint is removed with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting over a 5-pixel-radius ellipse. This propagates only immediate neighboring board/ramp texture; the camera and apparatus remain locked.
  3. The marble follows an explicitly authored 20-sample piecewise route across the measured ramp surfaces. Free-flight samples bridge ramp ends under gravity-like increasing vertical displacement; impact samples receive a subtle one-pixel squash.
  4. The new marble is drawn with NumPy analytic pixel shading: an antialiased elliptical silhouette, translucent yellow-green body, darker glass rim, upper-left specular highlight, lower caustic, and a small soft contact/drop shadow. Only fast airborne samples receive a restrained two-sample exposure trace.
  5. artifact_io.save writes the packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, 20 RGB PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone player. The script also creates a representative transition/difference sheet and then reloads every saved PNG and asserts exact equality against the matching tensor plane.

python generate.py
