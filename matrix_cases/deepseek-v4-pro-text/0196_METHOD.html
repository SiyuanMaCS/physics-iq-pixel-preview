METHOD.md

METHOD.md

Approach

All frames are generated via pure Python pixel operations on NumPy arrays. No external rendering engines, AI models, or network access is used.

Scene Analysis

The first frame (first_frame.png) was analyzed through numeric RGB inspection to identify object locations:

  • Black pipe: Located at x:2–19, y:41–59 on the left side of the frame
  • Tennis ball before motion: Not visible in the first frame (presumed inside the pipe)
  • Kettlebell: Identified by its specular highlight at approximately rows 108–116, cols 130–160, with body extending from (133,105) to (160,140)
  • Yellow rubber duck: Located at x:222–233, y:30–45 in the top-right area
  • Table surface: Covers most of the lower portion of the image (y:15–143)

Frame Generation Method

Frame 00

Exact pixel copy of first_frame.png. Verified via np.array_equal.

Frames 01–19

Each frame starts as a full copy of first_frame.png, then modifications are applied:

  1. Tennis ball drawing: A precomputed 11×11 pixel sprite representing a shaded grey sphere (radius 5 pixels) is overlaid onto the frame at the computed position. The sprite uses:

    • Center color: RGB(95, 95, 95) — neutral dark grey
    • Edge color: RGB(55, 55, 55) — darker edge for contrast against the light table
    • Specular highlight: RGB(175, 175, 175) — small bright spot at upper-left for 3D appearance

  2. Kettlebell wobble (frames 11–13 only): The kettlebell region (approx. x:130–165, y:100–142) is horizontally shifted by ±1 pixel to simulate the subtle impact response of a 30lb kettlebell. Frame 11 shifts right, frame 12 shifts left, frame 13 settles.

Ball Trajectory

Pre-impact (frames 1–10):

  • Ball emerges from pipe opening at (19, 50)
  • Moves horizontally right at constant speed (~12.9 px/frame)
  • Reaches kettlebell left edge at x=135 in frame 10

Post-impact (frames 11–19):

  • Ball bounces off the kettlebell surface
  • Trajectory follows: x(t) = 135 + 9t, y(t) = 50 − 6t + 0.5t² (parabolic arc)
  • The ball rises initially, then gravity pulls it down slightly
  • t is frames since impact (1 through 9)

Physics Rationale

  • The kettlebell (30lb ≈ 13.6 kg) is ~240× more massive than a tennis ball (~57g)
  • The kettlebell barely moves on impact — only a ±1 pixel wobble is simulated
  • The tennis ball deflects with most of its incident velocity preserved
  • The parabolic post-impact trajectory represents the ball bouncing upward off the curved kettlebell surface
