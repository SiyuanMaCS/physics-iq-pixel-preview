METHOD.md

METHOD.md — Case 0023

Approach

Pixel compositing with procedural ball rendering. Every frame is built by copying first_frame.png and compositing the tennis ball at its animated position. Frame 00 is bit-exact the original.

Scene Understanding (from numeric RGB inspection only)

  • Wall/background: y=0–38, grey-green tones
  • Table surface: y=38–144, warm wood tones (red > green > blue, blue channel near 0–30)
  • Black pipe: dark horizontal band at y=76–100, most prominent on left side (x≈0–120)
  • Table foreground (ball path): y≈105–120, lighter grey-brown surface
  • Ball: not visible in frame 00; emerges from pipe opening and rolls right

Ball Rendering Method

  • Shape: Circular mask with soft anti-aliased edge (1.5 px linear falloff)
  • Colour: Warm grey base (158, 152, 140) — approximates a used tennis ball
  • Texture: Procedural uniform noise (±8 per channel) to simulate fuzzy felt surface
  • Shading:
    • Radial darkening toward edges (spherical shading)
    • Top-left highlight (exponential falloff) simulating light from upper-left
  • Shadow: Soft elliptical shadow beneath the ball (offset down-right, 35% alpha, darkened multiplicatively)

Animation

  • Keyframe interpolation: 20 keyframes define (y, x) ball centre positions
  • Trajectory:
    • Frame 1: ball emerges from pipe at y=98, x=50
    • Frames 2–3: drops from pipe height to table surface (y=102→108)
    • Frames 4–16: rolls horizontally across table foreground (y≈114–118, x=100→233)
    • Frame 17: ball exits frame right
    • Frames 18–19: empty scene (identical to frame 00)
  • No external models: All rendering is pure NumPy pixel math

Output Verification

  • Frame 00: byte-exact match with first_frame.png (verified by np.array_equal)
  • All 20 frames: 256×144 RGB uint8
  • Tensor: pixels.npz stores packed uint32 0xRRGGBB values, verified frame-by-frame against PNGs
  • transition_check.png: 1×20 strip of amplified frame diffs vs frame 00
  • index.html: standalone player via artifact_io.save()
