Approach: Layer-Based Pixel Compositing

The animation is generated entirely through direct pixel operations in Python/NumPy. No external AI models, physics engines, or renderers are used.

1. Scene Analysis (OBSERVATION.md)

The first frame was analyzed numerically:

2. Mask Computation

Boolean masks are computed using vectorized NumPy operations:

  • Kettlebell mask: brightness < 45, spatially constrained to x∈[85,190], dilated 3× to capture edge/transition pixels (brightness < 70 after dilation).
  • Cup body mask: y∈[74,98], brightness > 135, and brighter than per-row background interpolation by 15+ units.
  • Cup rim mask: y∈[26,35], x∈[60,200], brighter than per-row background by 8+ units, excluding kettlebell pixels.

3. Background Fill

Per-row linear interpolation between left edge (x=5, always unobstructed) and right edge (x=250) provides the fill color for areas where objects move away. This preserves the wall's vertical gradient and table colors without needing a full background model.

4. Frame Generation

For each frame t (1-19) with displacement D:

  1. Start: Copy the original frame 00.
  2. Erase: Set all moving-object pixels (KB + cup rim + cup body) to their per-row background fill value.
  3. Draw compressed cup body: Vertically squish the original cup body from height H to H−D (minimum 2px), bilinear interpolation between source rows.
  4. Draw shifted objects: Move kettlebell + cup rim down by D pixels.
  5. Bulge effect (D>4): Horizontally expand cup edges by D/3 pixels with alpha falloff, simulating Poisson expansion under compression.
  6. Crush texture (D>3): Add per-pixel noise (±4) and slight brightening to the compressed cup to simulate densification of crushed styrofoam.
