METHOD.md

METHOD.md — Generation Method

Overview

20-frame animation (256×144, 4 fps, 5 s) of a lit match being lowered into a glass of water. Frame 00 is an exact copy of first_frame.png. All generation is done with local Python (NumPy/Pillow) pixel operations — no external models, engines, or network access.

Extraction & Analysis

  1. Loaded first_frame.png as a uint8 (144, 256, 3) NumPy array.
  2. Identified the match region by inspecting pixel values in the center-right area:
    • Match sprite bounding box: x=138–152 (15 px), y=44–79 (36 px).
    • Flame sub-region: rows 48–53 (rel y=4–9), centered at x≈145.
    • Match stick: dark thin vertical region from y=54 to y≈70, x=142–148.
  3. Measured water surface at approximately y=60, judged from bright reflection pixels inside the glass.
  4. Scene components (unchanged across frames):
    • Background wall gradient (y=0–28).
    • Wooden table surface (y=28+).
    • Glass beaker with water (x≈125–158, y≈48–112).

Motion Plan

  • Match descends at ~2–2.5 px/frame for frames 01–05, reaching the water surface.
  • Flame dims and extinguishes on contact (frames 05–07).
  • Smoke/steam puff rises and dissipates (frames 06–11).
  • Match continues slight descent then stops (frames 12–19).
  • Cumulative displacements: [0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 20, …, 20].

Per-Frame Generation

Each frame t (1–19) is built by modifying first_frame.png in-place:

Step 1: Erase match from original position

  • Alpha mask: For each pixel in the match sprite, compute color distance from a horizontal left→right interpolated clean-plate estimate. Pixels with distance > 15 are marked as match (alpha 0–1). Flame and match-head regions are forced to alpha ≥ 0.90.
  • Nearest-neighbor inpainting: For each match pixel (alpha > 0.3), search the same row leftward then rightward for the nearest non-match pixel (alpha < 0.3 or outside the match bounding box) and copy its color from first_frame.png. This fills the old match position with plausible background.
  • Pixels with alpha ≤ 0.3 are left unchanged (preserving surrounding detail).

Step 2: Composite match at displaced position

  • The match sprite is copied from its frame-00 position.
  • Flame dimming: For frames 05–07, flame region pixels are darkened by a factor (0.4 → 0.15 → 0.05) and blended toward dark brown. After frame 07, flame pixels are replaced with charred match-head color (~RGB 45,32,22).
  • Water refraction: Sprite rows below the water surface (y ≥ 60) are darkened (×0.60) with a blue tint (+[8,14,25]).
  • Alpha blending: Match pixels are composited over the background using the alpha mask. Within the glass region (y=48–112, x=125–158), alpha is reduced to 72% to simulate partial transparency through the glass.

Step 3: Post-effects

  • Smoke: Semi-transparent gray Gaussian blobs (RGB ~175,170,165) drawn above the flame's current position. Intensity peaks at frame 07 (0.9), fading to zero by frame 12. Smoke rises ~28 px as it dissipates. Secondary wisps at ±4 px offset for frames with intensity > 0.3.
  • Water ripples: Subtle brightness modulation (sine-wave rings, ±7% amplitude) on the water surface when the flame contacts/extinguishes.

Output Verification

  • Frame 00 byte-identical to first_frame.png (verified via np.array_equal).
  • All 20 frames verified against pixels.npz uint32-packed tensor.
  • Outputs: pixels.npz, frames/00.png–19.png, inspection.png, transition_check.png, index.html.

Limitations

  • The background inpainting uses nearest-neighbor row search; the filled area behind the erased match may show subtle horizontal consistency artifacts compared to the original glass texture.
  • Smoke is rendered as additive gray blobs rather than physically simulated particle dispersion.
  • Water refraction is approximated with a uniform darkening + blue tint rather than ray-traced optical effects.
