Physics Event Plan

A 30lb (13.6 kg) cast iron kettlebell is placed on top of a white styrofoam cup. Styrofoam (expanded polystyrene) has compressive strength of approximately 0.1–0.3 MPa. The kettlebell's weight concentrated on the cup rim far exceeds this, causing rapid structural failure.

Expected behavior: The cup initially resists compression elastically, then buckles and collapses. The kettlebell accelerates downward as the cup gives way, contacts the table, and settles. The cup fragments scatter as white debris.

Frame Time (s) Kettlebell descent Cup state
00 0.00 0 px Intact. Exact copy of first_frame.png.
01 0.25 1 px Cup compresses: 7 rows × 1.05× width.
02 0.50 2 px Cup compresses: 6 rows × 1.10× width.

METHOD.md

Method

Overview

This is a local pixel-compositing animation produced entirely from first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No external AI models, physics engines, or network resources were used. All operations are numpy/PIL/scipy pixel manipulations.

Approach

1. Scene Analysis (OBSERVATION.md)

The first frame was inspected numerically at the RGB pixel level to identify:

  • Kettlebell (dark gray/black): rows 0–80, cols 101–175. The handle extends to the top of the frame.
  • White styrofoam cup: rows 85–91, cols 128–163. A narrow white band visible below the kettlebell.
  • Wooden table: rows ~81–144, brown (R~105, G~76, B~46).
  • Background wall: rows 0–80 outside the kettlebell zone, gray gradient.

2. Clean Background Plate

To animate the kettlebell moving downward, a "clean plate" (scene without kettlebell or cup) was constructed:

  • Kettlebell region fill: For each row in the kettlebell zone (rows 0–80, cols 95–181 with 6px buffer), background wall colors were estimated by:

    1. Computing the horizontal color gradient from safe background regions (cols 50–90 left, cols 190–230 right)
    2. Extending the gradient linearly across the fill zone
    3. Adding textured noise matching the source statistics (per-channel standard deviation scaled by 0.6)
    4. Cross-fading the filled region with the original image at the boundaries (6px fade) to eliminate visible seams

  • Cup region fill: The cup area (rows 84–93, cols 120–175) was filled with table surface pixels randomly sampled from adjacent same-row table regions.

3. Object Extraction

Kettlebell sprite: Pixels in the kettlebell zone that differed significantly from the clean background (sum of per-channel absolute differences > 40) were identified. The mask was hole-filled and dilated by 1 pixel. Specular highlights on the kettlebell surface were included by dilating an additional 2 iterations and filtering out pixels brighter than 200. This produced an RGBA sprite (81×74) with alpha channel for seamless compositing.

Cup sprite: White-ish pixels (max channel brightness > 180) in the cup region were identified, with hole-filling and 2-iteration dilation to capture edge transitions. Produced an RGBA sprite (9×55).

4. Frame Generation (20 frames, 4 fps, 5 seconds)

Each frame (1–19) was composited on the clean plate in this order:

  1. Crushed cup: The cup sprite is resized (nearest-neighbor) according to the physics schedule — compressed vertically, spread horizontally — and alpha-blended onto the frame.
  2. Cup fragments: White debris particles (1–2px radius circles) are scattered randomly around the cup zone in increasing numbers as the cup crushes.
  3. Kettlebell: The kettlebell sprite is translated downward by dy[t] pixels and alpha-blended.
  4. Shadow: A soft dark shadow (rows × 4, alpha up to 0.3) is drawn below the kettlebell once it's close to the table (dy ≥ 15).

5. Physics Animation Schedule

The kettlebell descent follows a physically-motivated curve:

  • 0.0–1.0s (frames 0–4): Slow initial compression as the cup resists. Descent: 0→6 px.
  • 1.0–2.5s (frames 5–10): Accelerating collapse as the cup structure fails. Descent: 9→24 px.
  • 2.5–3.0s (frame 11–12): Contact with table. Descent: 25 px.
  • 3.0–3.25s (frame 13): Slight bounce (recoil to 24 px).
  • 3.25–5.0s (frames 14–19): Settling at rest (25 px descent).

The cup compresses from 9 rows tall to 1 row, spreads from 1.0× to 2.05× width, and produces 0→26 fragments.

6. Output Verification

  • Frame 00: Asserted identical to first_frame.png pixel-by-pixel.
  • pixels.npz: Verified uint32-packed values match frame RGBA data.
  • All 20 frames: Verified 256×144 RGB dimensions.
  • transition_check.png: Shows original (top) vs final frame (bottom) with changed pixels in red.
  • inspection.png: 8-panel contact sheet generated by artifact_io.py.
