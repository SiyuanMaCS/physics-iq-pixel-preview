METHOD.md

Method — Case 0087

Summary

Pixel-level liquid-pouring simulation using numpy arrays and PIL. All 20 frames are generated from first_frame.png through local Python pixel operations only. No external renderers, physics engines, or AI models are used.

Scene Analysis

The input frame was inspected numerically. Key objects were located by colour thresholding:

  • Glass dispenser (x:105–215, y:0–55): identified via high-brightness glass reflections and saturated red pixels of the contained liquid.
  • Red liquid region (x:108–195, y:0–35): sampled to obtain the base liquid colour RGB ~(200, 74, 58).
  • Spout/valve (y:58–65, x:136–158): dark pixels at the bottom of the dispenser glass define the pour exit.
  • Duck mask (x:100–220, y:48–104): built from (G−B > 12) ∧ (R−B > 8) ∧ (G > 55), capturing the yellow-green duck body. ~4097 mask pixels.
  • Tray surface: below y≈105, dark metallic colour ~(55, 52, 50).

Generation Technique — Five-Layer Compositing

Each frame (1–19) starts from a clean copy of first_frame.png and applies five layered effects:

1. Duck Coating (alpha blending)

Duck mask pixels are sorted by flow-distance from the pour impact point (top-center of duck). A growing fraction of these pixels receives red liquid colour per frame:

  • Coverage fraction ramps from 0.03 (F01) to 0.95 (F19).
  • Per-pixel alpha varies with distance from impact: near pixels are opaque (~0.95α), farther pixels are thinner (~0.35α), simulating liquid spreading and sheeting.
  • Colour is slightly noised (±12 per channel) to avoid synthetic uniformity.

2. Liquid Stream

A vertical column of red pixels is drawn from the spout exit (y=59) down to the duck surface:

  • Stream thickness varies per frame (2–10 px), peaking at full flow (F10–F12), then thinning (F17–F19).
  • Sinusoidal horizontal wobble (±1–5 px) adds turbulence.
  • Stream edges are transparent (α falls off with distance from centre), producing a soft cylindrical appearance.
  • Near the impact point, the stream spreads laterally over the duck surface.

3. Splash Particles

When the stream first hits the duck (F03–F12), small red droplet pixels are scattered upward and outward from the impact point:

  • Random angle (30°–70° from horizontal) and speed determines each particle trajectory.
  • Count varies from 8 (F03) to 24 (F05–F07), tapering off as flow stabilises.

4. Drips

Vertical drip lines extend downward from the duck's bottom edge:

  • 1–9 drip sources spaced evenly along the duck's underside.
  • Each drip grows: thin tail (1 px wide, α≈0.5) ending in a thicker drop head (3 px wide, α≈0.85).
  • Drip length increases per frame (2→30 px), with some reaching the tray.
  • When a drip contacts the tray, a small splash splotch is drawn.

5. Tray Pooling

An elliptical puddle grows below the pour zone on the tray surface:

  • Radius expands from 0 to 54 px.
  • Alpha increases from 0 to 0.80, with edge falloff (1 − dist/radius).
  • Pool colour is darker (0.55R, 0.65G, 0.75B of base liquid) for realism.
  • Two smaller secondary puddles appear on either side once the main pool is large enough, simulating runoff.

Output Verification

  • Frame 00: bit-exact match with first_frame.png (asserted).
  • Pixel tensor (uint32 0xRRGGBB) verified round-trip against all 20 PNGs.
  • All frames: 256×144, uint8 RGB, no pure-black artifacts.
  • transition_check.png: 4×5 visual grid of all frames for rapid inspection.
  • inspection.png: key-frame sheet generated by artifact_io.save().

Limitations

  • No 3D geometry: duck coating follows a 2D mask sorted by approximate flow distance, not true surface normals.
  • The duck mask may include some non-duck fringe pixels near the dispenser boundary.
  • Splashes are stylised point particles, not fluid-dynamic droplets.
  • Alpha blending over dark tray areas can produce muted reds; this is physically plausible for shallow liquid on dark metal.
