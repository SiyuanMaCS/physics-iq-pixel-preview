Physical Scenario

A 30lb (≈13.6kg) cast-iron kettlebell rests on top of a ceramic mug on a wooden table. The mug is structurally incapable of supporting this load. The ceramic will fracture and collapse, causing the kettlebell to fall onto the table. Mug fragments will scatter outward from the impact zone.

Physics Notes

  • Mug material: ceramic, brittle failure mode. Cracks propagate rapidly (~100–500 m/s), at this scale essentially instantaneous across frames 01–05.
  • Kettlebell mass: 30lb falls under gravity g≈9.8 m/s². At 4fps, each frame ≈0.25s. Free-fall distance per frame: d = 0.5 × g × t².
    • After 0.25s: d ≈ 0.31m (scale-dependent)
    • After 0.50s: d ≈ 1.23m
    • At our pixel scale (~1px ≈ 1mm estimated), the kettlebell would cross the mug height in ~2 frames.
  • Impact: kettlebell hits wooden table, brief bounce (< 2px) due to wood's slight elasticity.
  • Mug fragments: ejected outward at ~30–60° from horizontal, initial velocity ~3–5 px/frame, decelerate due to air resistance and gravity.

METHOD.md

Method

Overview

This simulation shows a 30lb cast-iron kettlebell crushing a yellow ceramic mug on a wooden table. The 20-frame animation (5 seconds, 4 fps) depicts crack formation, mug shattering, kettlebell free-fall, table impact, and settling. All generation is done with local Python pixel operations — no external models, engines, or network access.

Pipeline

1. Scene Analysis (numeric RGB inspection)

  • first_frame.png is loaded as a (144, 256, 3) uint8 array.
  • Regions are identified by color thresholding:
    • Kettlebell: dark pixels (R,G,B < 80) in rows 0–71 — forms a teardrop-shaped cast-iron body with thin handle arcs in rows 0–24.
    • Mug: yellow-orange pixels (R > 130, G > 80, B < 60) in rows 71–103 — warm ceramic with lighting variation.
    • Table: brown pixels (R > 80, G 50–130, B < 90) from row 70 downward — rich wood grain.
    • Wall: light gray gradient filling the remainder.
  • Object masks are refined with scipy.ndimage morphological operations: binary closing to fill gaps, binary fill holes to create solid masks, and binary dilation for soft edges.

2. Background Plate Construction

  • A clean background is built by filling removed-object regions with interpolated colors from surrounding pixels.
  • For each row in the filled zone, left and right sample windows (20px each) provide endpoint colors, and smoothstep interpolation fills the gap horizontally. Per-pixel noise (±3) is added for texture.
  • The kettlebell area (rows 0–71) is filled with wall gradient; the mug area (rows 68–114) is filled with table wood tones.

3. Sprite Extraction

  • RGBA sprites are extracted for the kettlebell and mug using their boolean masks padded by 2–3 pixels.
  • Alpha channels are feathered with a 2-pass box blur for soft compositing edges.

4. Frame Generation (5 stages)

Stage Frames Description
1: Cracking 01–04 Mug develops 7 crack lines (main vertical, branches, horizontal, lower fractures) with increasing density. Mug compresses vertically by 1–3px under load. Kettlebell descends 1–2px. Cracks restricted to mug mask to prevent table bleeding. Non-mug pixels restored from first_frame.png.
2: Shattering 05–07 Mug fragments (20–40 yellow ceramic pieces) fly outward with randomized velocities and gravity. Kettlebell accelerates downward (initial velocity from compression + gravity).
3: Impact 08–10 Kettlebell contacts table surface. Shadow drawn beneath kettlebell as darkening of table wood. Impact dust spray (30–50 light particles). Mug fragments settle.
4: Settling 11–14 Kettlebell bounces 2px upward then settles. Fragment positions stabilize. Dust fades over 3 frames.
5: Rest 15–19 Static final state: kettlebell on table, 28 mug ceramic fragments scattered around impact zone. Subtle shadow under kettlebell.

5. Crack Drawing

  • Cracks are drawn as anti-aliased jagged lines using random midpoint perturbation for natural appearance.
  • Line thickness grows from 1.0 to ~1.8px as gaps widen (gap parameter 0→1.4).
  • Colors darken from (25,18,12) to (55,35,18) as cracks deepen.
  • Random seed 42 ensures reproducible patterns across runs.

6. Fragment Generation

  • Mug fragments are simulated as small colored blobs (1–3px radius) with ceramic-yellow RGB values.
  • Initial positions are randomized within the mug bounds.
  • Velocities are assigned with random angles (mostly upward/outward) and speeds (1.5–5.5 px/frame).
  • Physics: gravity (1.0 px/frame² downward), velocity decay (none explicit; positions computed via kinematic equations).
  • Final rest positions scattered around the impact zone on the table.

7. Sprite Compositing

  • Sprites are blitted with alpha blending: out = sprite * alpha + background * (1 - alpha).
  • Frame 00 is set directly from first_frame.png (exact equality verified).
  • Frames 01–04 start from first_frame.png (preserving kettlebell and wall pixel-perfect).
  • Frames 05–19 start from the background plate (kettlebell and mug removed).
