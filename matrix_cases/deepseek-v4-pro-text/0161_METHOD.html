Scene Observation & 20-Frame Event Plan

Scene Measurements

Image Dimensions

  • 256×144 pixels, RGB uint8

Spatial Layout (from top to bottom)

Region Y Range Description
Ceiling/upper wall 0-11 Bright whitish area
Background wall 12-55 Gray-beige wall with Newton's cradle frame visible
Cradle apparatus 55-100 Newton's cradle frame, strings, and balls
Table surface 100-143 Dark brown wooden table

Newton's Cradle Structure

  • Frame: Dark vertical support lines at x≈68-69 (left) and x≈173-174 (right), spanning y≈16-70
  • Crossbar: Around y≈16-20, horizontal
  • String length: ~45 pixels (from crossbar y≈30 to ball center y≈75)

Five Metal Balls

  • Ball diameter: ~19 pixels
  • Held ball (pulled to left amplitude by grabber): center ~(73, 75)
  • 4 resting balls (touching, at center): centers at approximately x=104, 123, 142, 161, all at y≈75
  • Ball colors: specular silver/bronze metallic - bright center highlight (R>170), warm edges (R~120-150, G~80-110, B~50-70)

Blue-Black Grabber

  • Position: x≈0-61, y≈67-94 (left side)
  • Blue pixels (B > R+40, B > G+30) concentrated in roughly triangular region
  • Holds the left ball at its amplitude position
  • Ball is partially obscured behind/within grabber gripper

Background Behind Balls

  • Gap region x≈83-93, y≈55-90: warm brown gradient, approximately RGB(124,93,66) transitioning darker lower down
  • This is the wall/table area behind the moving balls

Physics Parameters

  • String length L ≈ 45 px
  • Max angular displacement ≈ 43.6° (amplitude from x=73 to x=104, horizontal displacement ≈ 31 px)
  • Pendulum period T ≈ 2π√(L/g) → for animation: half-swing ≈ 7 frames at 4 fps

20-Frame Event Plan (4 fps, 5 seconds)

Phase 1: Left Ball Release and Swing (Frames 0-4)

Frame Time (s) Event

02 0.50 Ball swings further right (~30% of arc), gravity accelerates it downward.
03 0.75 Ball continues swing (~60% of arc), approaching resting balls.
04 1.00 Ball nearly at impact position (~90% of arc).

Phase 2: Impact and Right Ball Swing-Out (Frames 5-10)

Frame Time (s) Event

06 1.50 Rightmost ball just begins separating from the group (~20% of right arc). Left ball now at rest with others.
07 1.75 Right ball swinging outward (~50% of right arc).
08 2.00 Right ball continues outward (~80% of right arc).

Phase 3: Right Ball Return and Impact (Frames 11-15)

Frame Time (s) Event

Implementation Method

Approach: Pixel-level compositing with background fill and ball sprite translation.

  1. Background plate: Create a clean background by sampling static pixels adjacent to ball regions and filling ball-occupied areas with smooth gradients matching the surrounding background.

  2. Ball extraction: Isolate ball pixels from frame 0 using circular masks centered on each ball position.

  3. Pendulum physics: Compute ball positions using θ(t) = θ₀ · cos(ωt) with ω = π/(7 frames) for a half-swing period of 7 frames (~1.75s).

  4. Frame rendering: For each frame:

    • Start from clean background plate
    • Composite static frame elements (frame, strings, grabber after release)
    • Draw balls at computed pendulum positions
    • Handle the grabber separately (present in frame 0, retracted in later frames)

  5. Grabber handling: Frame 0 shows grabber holding the ball. After release (frame 1+), the grabber retracts or remains stationary while the ball moves away. The grabber tip area is filled with background in later frames.

METHOD.md

Method: Newton's Cradle Animation

Approach Summary

Pixel-level compositing with subpixel ball rendering and pendulum physics. The method uses frame 0 as the reference, extracts ball sprites, creates a clean background plate via per-row gap sampling, and composites balls at physically-correct positions for each frame.

Steps

1. Scene Analysis (first_frame.png pixel inspection)

  • Resolution: 256×144, uint8 RGB
  • Newton's cradle: 5 metal balls, ~19px diameter each, hanging from strings ~45px long
  • Held ball (ball 0): pulled to left amplitude by blue-black grabber, center at ~x=73, y=75
  • 4 resting balls: centers at x=104, 123, 142, 161, y=75 (19px spacing, touching)
  • Grabber: blue-black tool at x=0-61, y=67-94, holding the left ball
  • Support structure: metal-colored frame visible on right at x=174+
  • Background: wall gradient transitioning to dark table below y=100
  • Max horizontal displacement: 12 pixels (from rest x=85 to amplitude x=73)

2. Background Plate Creation

  • Sample per-row background color from the gap region (x=84-93) between held ball and resting balls, where background is visible in frame 0
  • Fill two regions with per-row average colors:
    • Left (x=55-84): covers held ball positions from amplitude to rest, plus grabber tip area
    • Center (x=94-172): covers 4 resting balls at rest
  • 3-pixel edge blending at region boundaries to avoid hard seams
  • Gap region (x=84-93) and support structure (x=174+) are preserved from original image

3. Ball Sprite Extraction

  • Circular masks (radius=10px) centered at each ball's position in frame 0
  • Ball 0 (held) uses ball 1's clean sprite (center x=104) since the held ball is partially occluded by the grabber in frame 0
  • Balls 1-4 use their own positions from frame 0
  • Sprites are full-frame arrays (144×256×3) with non-zero values only within circular mask

4. Pendulum Physics

  • Half-swing period: 6 frames = 1.5 seconds (ω = π/3.0 rad/s)
  • 4-phase animation over 20 frames:
Phase Frames Time (s) Event
1 0-6 0.00-1.50 Left ball swings from amplitude (d=12) to center (d=0)
2 6-12 1.50-3.00 Right ball swings out (d: 0→12)
3 12-18 3.00-4.50 Right ball returns (d: 12→0)
4 18-19 4.50-4.75 Left ball begins swinging out (d: 0→3.1)
  • Displacement functions:
    • d_left(t) = 12·cos(ωt) for t∈[0,1.5], 12·sin(ω(t-4.5)) for t∈[4.5,5.0]
    • d_right(t) = 12·sin(ω(t-1.5)) for t∈[1.5,3.0], 12·cos(ω(t-3.0)) for t∈[3.0,4.5]
  • Vertical rise from pendulum arc: dy = L·(1 − cos(arcsin(d/L))) where L=45px

5. Subpixel Compositing

  • Ball positions computed as floating-point coordinates
  • Bilinear interpolation: render at 4 integer-offset positions and blend by fractional weights
  • np.roll for integer-pixel shifts with wrap-edge clearing
  • Balls composited right-to-left for correct overlap ordering

6. Frame Generation

  • Frame 0: exact copy of first_frame.png
  • Frames 1-19: background plate + composited balls at computed positions
  • Grabber tip area automatically filled by background plate (simulating retraction after release)
