ball-ramp · 生成方法

Approach

This animation uses sprite extraction and compositing — the yellow tennis ball is identified as a connected component in frame 00, extracted as a sprite, and redrawn at new positions across frames 01–19 against an inpainted background.

Ball Detection

Tennis-ball pixels are selected by the colour predicate:

G > R + 25  &&  G > 150  &&  R > 110  &&  B < 110

The largest 8-connected component (via scipy.ndimage.label) is retained, yielding a 224-pixel blob spanning x=[95,111], y=[65,80]. The sprite is 17×16 px with a centre of (103.0, 72.5) and a half-height (effective radius) of 8 px.

Background Inpainting

For each row that contains ball pixels, the background colour at those pixels is estimated by horizontal interpolation: the nearest non-ball colour from the left and right in the same row is averaged. This produces a plausible continuation of the cardboard ramp and table surface behind where the ball was.

Ground Surface

The ball's bottom in frame 00 sits at y=80. This is the local ground plane (a cardboard/table mixture). A very gentle slope is modelled: the surface is 2 px higher at x<95 (ramp behind pipe) and levels to y=80 at x≥135 (flat table). The surface is smoothed with an 11-pixel uniform filter.

Trajectory

Horizontal position follows a simple physics model:

  • Frames 0–7 (ramp phase): constant acceleration a_ramp = +0.75 px/frame², initial velocity v0 = 2.5 px/frame. Models gravity pulling the ball down an inclined cardboard ramp.
  • Frames 8–19 (table phase): constant deceleration a_table = −0.07 px/frame², modelling rolling friction on the wooden table.

Vertical position: the ball centre is placed so the ball bottom rests on the ground surface. A 4-frame blend transitions from the original frame-00 centre to the surface-following position.

Compositing

For frames 01–19:

  1. Start with the inpainted background (bg).
  2. Copy the ball sprite pixel-by-pixel at the new centre position.
  3. Only sprite pixels marked in the binary mask are drawn (full opacity).

Frame 00 is the unmodified first_frame.png (verified: np.array_equal passes).
