OBSERVATION.md

Scene Observations — first_frame.png

Image dimensions

256×144 RGB, uint8, static locked-off single-shot.

Scene elements

1. Background wall

  • Vertical gradient from top (R≈160,G≈155,B≈162) downward, darkening slightly.
  • Slight horizontal gradient: left side ~2-6 units darker per channel than right side.
  • Visible at x=0–55 and x=160–255, y=0–~115.

2. Table surface

  • Dark wood/brown: R≈85–100, G≈62–70, B≈35–43.
  • Covers y≈115–143 across the full width.
  • Smooth slight horizontal variation; mostly uniform.

3. Woven basket

  • Large woven object occupying x≈55–160, y≈55–115.
  • Warm brown/tan weave texture: R≈90–150, G≈60–115, B≈30–90.
  • Basket interior darker region at x≈85–130, y≈70–110.
  • Basket bottom edge around y≈115, with a slight curve/taper.
  • The left and right edges curve inward near the bottom.

4. Magnet

  • Dark metallic rectangular-ish shape at x≈95–106, y≈4–43.
  • Very dark pixels (R,G,B < 30 in the core).
  • Connected to the orange cord above and the basket below.

5. Orange cord

  • Thin orange line visible only at top of frame: y=0–1, x≈99–101.
  • Bright orange: R≈150–180, G≈65–95, B≈10–30.
  • The cord connects the magnet to the top edge of the frame.

6. Orange tennis ball

  • Round cluster at y≈115–128, x≈85–125.
  • Center approximately at (x=105, y=121), radius ≈10–12 px.
  • Orange coloration: R≈100–140 in a round pattern.
  • Partially visible below the basket in frame 00.
  • The ball sits on the table surface.

Rig (movable assembly)

The cord, magnet, and basket move together as one unit:

  • Rig mask includes: cord pixels (y=0–1, x=99–101), magnet (y=4–43, x=95–106), basket (y≈55–115, x≈55–160).
  • Rig top: y=0 (cord attachment point).
  • Rig bottom: y≈115 (basket bottom).

Positive rig_dy = downward movement (basket descends). Negative rig_dy = upward movement (basket lifts).

When rig_dy > 8 the ball is fully hidden behind the basket (ball at y≈115, basket bottom at y≈130).

Technical approach

  • Rig extraction: Color-difference mask (warmer-than-wall = basket/magnet/cord).
  • Background inpainting: Bilinear interpolation of wall gradient from left/right visible walls, plus table surface.
  • Ball extraction: Round orange cluster from original frame, placed statically on table.
  • Compositing per frame: Background → Ball (if visible) → Rig (shifted by rig_dy).
  • Cord extension: When rig moves down, extend cord pixels from top attachment to magnet top.
  • Edge blending: Soft blending at rig edges to avoid hard seams.

Verification

  • Frame 00 saved from generate.py must be bit-identical to first_frame.png.
  • All 20 frames verified as 256×144×3 uint8 before saving.

METHOD.md

Method

Approach

Block-shift compositing with bilinear background inpainting.

  1. Clean background: For each row, the wall/table region behind the rig is estimated by bilinear interpolation between left (x=15–45) and right (x=185–215) reference strips. This provides a seamless backdrop when the rig moves.

  2. Rig block: A rectangular region (x=50–165, y=0–118) containing the basket, magnet, and cord attachment is extracted as a single block. Edge feathering (4–5 px alpha ramp) reduces visible seams.

  3. Ball: Extracted from the original frame using a circular mask centered at (105,121) with radius 11. The ball is composited onto the clean background as a static element; the rig block covers it when positioned over it.

  4. Cord: A thin vertical orange line is drawn from the top attachment point (y=0, x=100) to the magnet top, extending/retracting with the rig movement.

  5. Animation: 20 frames at 4 fps over 5 seconds. The rig descends by up to 15 pixels to cover the ball, pauses briefly, then lifts 55 pixels to the top of the frame.

  6. Frame 00: Returned as an exact copy of first_frame.png (bit-identical).

Tools

Pure Python with NumPy and PIL/Pillow. No external AI models, physics engines, or network resources used. All pixel operations are local array manipulations.
