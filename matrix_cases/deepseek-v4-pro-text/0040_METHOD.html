METHOD.md

Method

Approach

This animation simulates a knife slicing through a tangerine half on a glass cutting board. The scene is a static locked-off shot with a fixed frame throughout, filmed at constant 4 fps over 5 seconds (20 frames).

Technical Method

1. Scene Analysis

  • Tangerine segmentation: Bright orange flesh pixels detected via RGB thresholds (R>150, G 60–160, B<90, R−G>25, R−B>45). The flesh region is dilated 8 iterations (5×5 kernel) and hole-filled to capture the full tangerine including peel. Result: 5,584 pixels, bounding box x=[128,206], y=[29,107].
  • Knife original detection: Dark handle (R+G+B<25) plus a geometrically-defined blade polygon based on the handle position. Used purely for inpainting the knife out of the background.
  • Kinematic parameters: Blade left-edge slope and base positions computed per row from the geometric knife model.

2. Background Preparation

  • The original photograph's knife is removed via nearest-neighbor inpainting (search radius 35px).
  • The inpainted knife region is smoothed (3×3 uniform filter) to reduce artifacts.
  • Non-knife areas are restored from the original frame to avoid global smoothing.

3. Knife Rendering

  • A simplified drawn knife is rendered each frame rather than reusing the photographic knife. This avoids transparency/blending issues.
  • Handle: dark brown rectangle at the original handle position, translated per frame.
  • Blade: thin (3px wide) gray trapezoid extending upward from the handle at the same angle as the original blade.
  • The knife is composited on top of all scene elements with hard edges (no alpha blending).

4. Cut Animation (Per-Frame Generation)

Each frame is built independently from the clean background (no cumulative error):

  1. Tangerine pixel placement: Left-half pixels (cx < blade_left_edge) are shifted left by sep pixels, where sep grows from 0 to 6 as the cut progresses. Right-half pixels stay at original positions. All sourced from the original first_frame.png.

  2. Cut line: A 2px dark line is drawn at the blade's current cutting edge (blade_left_edge ± 1px).

  3. Gap fill: The visible gap between the separated halves is filled with inner-flesh color (light orange: RGB~215,165,105) plus per-pixel noise for texture. The gap extends from blx − sep to blx + 4 to show the cut surface on both sides.

  4. Knife composited on top: The drawn knife is rendered last, covering the cut line exactly at the blade edge but leaving the gap and inner flesh visible to the left.

5. Motion Curve

  • Knife translation: −72px horizontal (left), +36px vertical (down) over 20 frames.
  • Easing: Hermite smoothstep (t²(3−2t)) for natural acceleration and deceleration.
  • Blade model: Linear interpolation per row along the blade's left edge from top (hx0−22) to handle junction (hx0−10).

6. Output

  • 20 PNG frames (256×144 RGB) saved as frames/00.png–19.png.
  • Packed uint32 tensor (pixels.npz) where each pixel is 0xRRGGBB.
  • Inspection sheet (inspection.png) with 8 keyframes.
  • Transition check (transition_check.png): horizontal strip through tangerine center for all 20 frames.
  • Standalone player (index.html) for frame-by-frame viewing.

Limitations

  • The original photographic knife is replaced with a drawn knife for clean compositing; this sacrifices photorealism for clarity of the cutting action.
  • Per-frame generation means cut effects are recomputed each frame rather than accumulating; this ensures no drift but prevents very subtle progressive effects.
  • The 3px blade width means some cut surface pixels are occluded directly at the blade edge.
