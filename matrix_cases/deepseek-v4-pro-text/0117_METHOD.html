METHOD.md — Case 0117

Method: Rectangle Sliding with Wall Fill and Cord Gap Restoration

Overview

The tennis ball, magnet, and orange cord assembly is shifted downward by 1 pixel per frame (4 px/s over 5 seconds at 4 fps) while the background is restored from interpolated wall colors. The mirror reflection is maintained via horizontal mirroring of the real scene side.

Algorithm

  1. Rectangle Definition: A bounding rectangle [x=93..135, y=5..96] is defined to enclose the entire moving assembly (cord, magnet, ball) and their mirror reflections in frame 0.

  2. Wall Color Reference: For each row, two reference wall columns are sampled at x=85 (left of object) and x=145 (right of object). These provide the gradient wall colors behind the objects.

  3. Per-Frame Generation (for each frame t, displacement dy = t):

    a. Start from frame 0 — ensures frame 00 is exact.

    b. Wall Fill: The original rectangle area [y=5..96, x=93..135] is filled with interpolated wall colors:

    • For each pixel at (x, y), linearly interpolate between left_ref[y] and right_ref[y] based on horizontal position x.
    • This removes all object content from the original position.

    c. Rectangle Shift: Copy the original rectangle content from frame0[y, x] to frame[y+dy, x] for all y in [5, 96], x in [93, 135]. This shifts the entire assembly down by dy pixels.

    d. Cord Gap Restoration: The cord attachment point remains at y=8 (top of frame). After shifting, there is a gap from y=8 to y=8+dy where the cord was. This gap is restored by copying original non-wall cord pixels from frame0[y, x] for y in [8, 8+dy], x in [105, 125].

    e. Edge Blending: The left and right edges of the shifted rectangle (x=93..96 and x=132..135) are blended with the wall fill colors over 3 pixels to avoid sharp seams.

    f. Mirror Reflection: For rows y >= 88 (floor/mirror area), the real scene side x in [26, 113] is mirrored to the reflection side x in [113, 200] with 0.82× brightness dimming. A gradual blend factor (0.3 at y=88 to 1.0 at y=98) smooths the transition.

Justification

  • Rectangle sliding avoids complex per-pixel masking while correctly moving the entire object assembly.
  • Wall interpolation provides a natural-looking background behind the objects since the wall has a smooth horizontal gradient.
  • Gap restoration preserves the cord attachment at the top while the assembly descends.
  • Mirror dimming maintains the illusion of a mirror reflection.
  • No external models or engines — purely local pixel operations.
