balls-collide · 生成方法

Overview

Generated 20 frames (256×144, 4 fps) showing a physics demonstration: blue and yellow tennis balls roll out of pipes and collide on a tabletop.

Scene Analysis (Frame 0)

  • Yellow tennis ball: Center at (188.0, 40.5), emerging from green pipe on right side. Diameter ~15px, bounding box rows [33,49], cols [182,195].
  • Blue tennis ball: Center at (121.1, 71.7), already on table after emerging from dark pipe on left side. Diameter ~17px, bounding box rows [63,81], cols [113,130].
  • Left pipe (dark): Diagonal tube at rows 30–56, columns 0–55. Ball exits rightward.
  • Right pipe (green): Green tube at rows 33–50, columns 190–255. Ball exits leftward.
  • Table: Light-colored wood surface, rows ~50–143.

Approach

Background Inpainting

Horizontal linear interpolation per row: for each row within the ball bounding box, the background is estimated by linearly interpolating between the average of pixels just left of the ball and the average of pixels just right of the ball. This produces a smooth fill without visible seams.

Ball Sprite Extraction

For each ball, a soft alpha mask is computed from the difference between the original frame and the inpainted background. The alpha is a clamped linear ramp: alpha = clamp((max_channel_diff - 15) / 25, 0, 1). This preserves soft edges for natural compositing at new positions.

Physics Model

  • Pre-collision (frames 0–10): Yellow ball moves left at 3.35 px/frame and down at 3.12 px/frame (dropping from pipe level to table). Blue ball moves right at 3.35 px/frame on the table surface.
  • Collision (frame 10): Centers meet at approximately (154.5, 71.7). Equal-mass elastic collision: velocities are swapped.
  • Post-collision (frames 11–19): Yellow ball moves right at 3.35 px/frame. Blue ball moves left at 3.35 px/frame and down at 3.12 px/frame.

Rendering

For each frame 1–19:

  1. Start from the clean inpainted background
  2. Place yellow ball sprite at computed position using alpha compositing
  3. Place blue ball sprite on top at computed position using alpha compositing
  4. Clip values to [0, 255] and convert to uint8

Frame 0 is an exact byte-for-byte copy of first_frame.png.

Tools

  • PIL/Pillow for image I/O
  • NumPy for all pixel operations
  • No external AI models, physics engines, or network access used

  • transition_check.png: 5×4 grid of all frames
