METHOD.md — Glass Jar Lowering Animation

Method Summary

All pixel generation is done with local NumPy/PIL operations — no external models, engines, or networks. The animation simulates a transparent glass jar being lowered over lit matchsticks in a bowl of red liquid.

Scene Analysis (from first_frame.png)

The 256×144 reference frame shows:

  • Top (rows 0–59): Transparent glass jar held above, with specular highlights on the left side (cols 75–95) and a dark bottom rim (rows 44–58).
  • Center (rows 60–79): Match bundle extending upward from the bowl, with smoldering match tips at rows 63–68, cols 125–145 (warm glow, R≈150, G≈82, B≈75).
  • Bottom (rows 72–94): Red liquid in the bowl, curved meniscus from cols 80–188 narrowing to cols 112–159.
  • Background: Gray-blue gradient, darker on left (R≈80) and brighter on right (R≈160).

Pipeline

Step 1 — Background Estimation

For each row in the jar region (rows 0–59), the background behind the jar is estimated by horizontal linear interpolation between the known background pixels to the left (cols 35–50) and right (cols 200–215) of the jar.

Step 2 — Overlay Decomposition

The full overlay (jar pixels minus estimated background) is separated into:

  • Glass features (move with jar): specular highlights (overlay > 6 in ≥2 channels), left/right edge dark bands, and the bottom rim (rows 44–58, cols 65–120). These are identified by a spatial mask that isolates the jar's visible structural features.
  • Scene content (stays fixed): the match bundle and other content visible through the transparent jar center, which should not move.

Step 3 — Scene Reconstruction

The "scene without jar" is built as: background + scene_content_overlay for rows 0–59, plus the original bowl for rows 60–143.

Step 4 — Frame Generation (20 frames, 4 fps)

For each frame:

  1. Jar translation: Glass features are composited at their new vertical position using smooth ease-in-out cubic descent over 85 pixels (jar rim reaches match tips around frame 6–7, fully covers bowl by frame 16).
  2. Vacated area fill: Rows above the jar that it vacated are filled with the estimated background.
  3. Flame glow: A Gaussian glow mask centered on the match tips adds warm orange-yellow flame color. Glow intensity fades linearly as the jar rim passes over the match tips (jar_rim_y passes glow_center_y).
  4. Smoke/steam: Four elliptical smoke wisps drift upward from the match tip area, intensifying after the jar has descended 15+ pixels. Smoke color is gray-white with partial alpha blending.
  5. Fractional-pixel interpolation: Sub-pixel jar position is handled by blending adjacent overlay rows.

Step 5 — Output

  • 20 PNG frames (256×144 RGB)
  • pixels.npz: uint32 tensor 0xRRGGBB packed, shape (144, 256, 20)
  • inspection.png: contact sheet of 8 frames
  • transition_check.png: 4-frame comparison
  • index.html: standalone player with delta compression

Physics Basis

The animation models the classic science demonstration where a jar lowered over burning matches in colored water causes the matches to extinguish (oxygen depletion). The flame glow peaks before the jar seals the match tips, then fades. Smoke wisps appear inside the enclosed jar volume.

Limitations

  • Glass transparency is approximated by additive overlay; true refraction is not computed.
  • Smoke is procedural (sinusoidal wisps) rather than fluid-simulated.
  • The red liquid level does not visibly rise (pressure effect is very subtle at this scale).
