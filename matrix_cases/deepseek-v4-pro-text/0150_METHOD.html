Method — case_0150

Approach

Pure Python pixel manipulation using NumPy and PIL. No external AI models, physics engines, or network access.

Scene Analysis

  • Loaded first_frame.png and inspected pixel values numerically using NumPy
  • Identified region boundaries through color thresholding and spatial analysis:
    • Black tube: dark pixels (R<20) in upper-left, x∈[0,39], y∈[0,55]
    • White lampshade: bluish-white pixels (B≥R, R>115) at x∈[110,162], y∈[3,36]
    • Table: warm wood tones (R≈110-140) covering lower ~60% of frame
  • Computed tube right-edge profile to locate ball exit point at (x≈30, y≈42)

Ball Rendering

  • Shape: Perfect circle of radius 7 pixels, with 1.5px anti-aliased edge blending
  • 3D Shading: Per-pixel analytic sphere normals with Lambertian diffuse, Phong specular highlight, and Fresnel-like edge darkening
  • Lighting: Directional light from lampshade position (upper-center, direction ≈(-0.3, -0.5, 0.81))
  • Color: Brown tennis ball — base RGB(155, 95, 35), highlight RGB(200, 150, 90), shadow RGB(75, 40, 12)
  • Texture: Subtle sinusoidal variation (±4%) for visual interest
  • Shadow: Elliptical contact shadow beneath the ball (rx=1.2r, ry=0.4r), alpha=0.45, offset (2,3) pixels from ball center

Occlusion Handling

  • Built a lampshade mask from the base frame using color criteria (B ≥ R with bluish tint)
  • Ball and shadow pixels are skipped where the lampshade mask is active
  • This correctly places the ball behind the lampshade (the ball passes under the lampshade on the table)

Motion

  • 20 frames, 4 fps, 5 seconds total
  • Ball starts inside the tube at frame 00 (not drawn, frame equals first_frame.png)
  • Ball emerges from tube at frame 01 (x=35) and rolls at constant velocity rightward
