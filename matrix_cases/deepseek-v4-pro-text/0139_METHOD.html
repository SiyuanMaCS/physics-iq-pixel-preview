METHOD.md

METHOD.md — Generation Approach

Overview

20-frame animation (256×144, 4 fps, 5 s) of a folded paper burning on a glass cutting board with white smoke. Frame 00 is a pixel-perfect copy of first_frame.png. Frames 01–19 add flame, smoke, and paper charring via pure NumPy/PIL operations.

Scene Understanding

From pixel analysis of first_frame.png:

  • Background wall (rows 0–18): blue-gray gradient, brighter on left, darker on right
  • Glass cutting board (rows ~55–90): transparent surface with bright specular highlight at (170–192, 72–82)
  • Folded paper (rows 36–82, cols 98–168): warm beige/brown tones (R:80–120, G:50–90, B:25–70)
  • Dark right area (cols 185–255): deep shadow environment
  • Table surface (rows 90–143): warm wooden tones

The first frame shows the paper already burning with smoke rising, though the flame itself is not clearly visible (likely at a flicker minimum or obscured). We synthesize a flame above the paper top and animate smoke, flame, and charring.

Methods

1. Flame Animation (draw_flame)

  • The flame is placed as a small hollow ellipse centered just above the paper top edge (paper_top - 2 px vertically, centered horizontally on the paper).
  • Ellipse dimensions: 5px vertical semi-axis, 8px horizontal semi-axis, with a smaller inner cutout (3.5×6px) to create a hollow/flame-tip shape.
  • Each frame: generate per-pixel HSL values in the orange-yellow range (H:0.07–0.14, S:0.4–0.85, L:0.30–0.75).
  • Temporal variation: lightness modulated by a phase term sin(t * 5.5 + x * 0.3 + y * 0.4) to create organic flickering. Additional random HSL perturbations per pixel per frame.
  • The flame pixels are written directly (opaque replacement) over the paper/background.

2. Smoke System (draw_smoke)

  • Particle-based: 500–1640 particles per frame, emitted from the flame center with random offsets (±4px vertical, ±25px horizontal from flame center).
  • Advection: Each particle rises at 5.0 px/frame-of-age. Horizontal drift uses Gaussian noise with spread proportional to age (σ = age × 0.30).
  • Kernel: Each particle deposits a 2D Gaussian kernel with radius growing with age (r = 1.2 + age × 0.08).
  • Fading: Particle intensity fades linearly with age: alpha = (1 - age/100) × random(0.08, 0.25).
  • Color: Warm white (R:0.94, G:0.91, B:0.85 of white) to give smoke a slight warm tint from the fire.
  • Blending: Smoke is alpha-blended over the existing frame: new = old × (1 - smoke_alpha) + 235 × smoke_alpha, where smoke_alpha = smoke_buf × smoke_color, clamped to [0, 0.55].
  • Particles that leave the frame bounds are clipped. This produces a realistic rising smoke column that widens and fades with altitude.

3. Paper Charring (char_paper)

  • The paper mask (rows 36–82, cols 98–168) identifies the paper body.
  • Each frame t, the top 1.2 × t pixel rows of the paper are progressively charred.
  • Charring replaces each pixel with dark charcoal tones (R:30–58, G:18–38, B:12–28), blended with the original pixel color.
  • Blend factor: 0.5 at the charring front, increasing to 0.9 at the deepest charred area (linear gradient).
  • 88% of pixels in the char zone are affected (12% left uncharred for irregular edge effect).
  • The charring zone expands 3px wider than the paper mask left/right edges for a burned-edge look.

4. Static Region Preservation (fix_static)

  • Glass specular highlight (rows 70–85, cols 165–200, pixels with R,G,B > 200): copied exactly from frame 0 each frame (reflection doesn't change).
  • Dark right area (cols ≥ 185, pixels with R < 85, G < 65, B < 55): preserved from frame 0.
  • Table bottom (rows 95–143): preserved from frame 0 (far from the action).

5. Composition Order

For each frame t (1–19):

  1. Start with frame 0 as base (all static pixels)
  2. Char paper top edge
  3. Draw flame over the paper top
  4. Alpha-blend smoke over everything
  5. Restore truly static regions

Verification

  • Frame 00 is confirmed pixel-identical to first_frame.png via np.array_equal.
  • pixels.npz contains a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor encoding 0xRRGGBB values. All 20 frames match between .npz and individual PNGs.
  • Frame-to-frame pixel change ranges from 1.9% (frame 00→01) to 10.6% (frame 18→19), concentrated in the smoke column and flame area.
