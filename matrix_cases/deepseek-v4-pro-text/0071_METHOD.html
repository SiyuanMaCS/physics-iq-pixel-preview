Physics of the Pour

  • Gravity: Stream accelerates slightly as it falls; negligible change over 20px drop.
  • Stream dynamics: Continuous laminar flow with minor turbulence. Slight horizontal wavering (±2px) from spigot vibrations and air resistance.
  • Impact: Stream hits the liquid surface causing ripples, small splashes, and air bubbles.
  • Mixing: Grapefruit juice (denser, red-orange) mixes with water (clear). The mixture gradually becomes more intensely red-orange as juice concentration increases.
  • Volume addition: As juice pours in, the total liquid volume increases, causing the liquid level to rise.
  • Rise rate: Over 5 seconds at a typical pour rate, the liquid level rises from approximately y=77 to the glass rim at y=74 (≈3–4px rise). This is a slow, steady rise since the glass is already mostly full.

METHOD.md

Method

Approach

All 20 frames (256×144 RGB, 4fps, 5s) generated with pure NumPy pixel ops. No external AI, physics engines, or rendering libraries.

Strategy: Frame-0 Baseline Modification

Each frame (1–19) starts as a copy of first_frame.png. Only dynamically-changing regions are modified:

  1. Juice stream (y=53–80, x=108–142): Procedurally redrawn per frame with wobble, width variation, and edge softness.
  2. Liquid surface (top 10px of liquid body): Ripple patterns from dual-frequency sinusoidal waves.
  3. New liquid zone: Area between original surface (y≈77) and rising surface — filled with liquid color as level rises.
  4. Deep liquid: Frame-0 pixels preserved with ±1.5px micro-variation for subtle fluid motion.
  5. Splash droplets: Small particle clusters near impact point.

All static areas (wall, table, dispenser body, spigot, glass structure) remain pixel-identical to frame 0.

Animation Parameters

  • Liquid level: Ease-in-out from y=77 to y=73 (≈4px rise over 5s)
  • Flow taper: Exponential decay from t=13–19 (full pour → nearly stopped)
  • Wobble: Dual sine components for natural stream variation
  • Color: Liquid deepens from (130,78,48) to (150,72,36) as juice concentrates
  • Splashes: 0–10 droplets/frame proportional to flow rate
