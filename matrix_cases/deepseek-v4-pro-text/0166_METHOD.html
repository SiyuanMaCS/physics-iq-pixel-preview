Platform occlusion: The platform remains static. Ball pixels behind the platform stay hidden. As the ball rotates, the face features shift position on the visible surface.

Method

  1. Define ball circle: center (76, 107), radius 48
  2. Extract ball texture from frame 0 within the circle
  3. Fill top-edge gaps (where wall merges with dark ball edge) using estimated ball colors
  4. For each frame t (1-19), apply polar coordinate rotation: each pixel (y,x) in the ball circle samples from the original ball texture at angle offset -θ_t (where θ_t = t × 2.25° clockwise)
  5. Composite: static background outside ball circle + rotated ball inside circle
  6. Platform pixels remain unchanged (render order: background → ball → platform)

METHOD.md

Method: Tennis Ball Clockwise Rotation

Approach

Polar-coordinate texture remapping: each pixel inside the ball circle samples from the original frame-0 ball texture at a counter-rotated angular position, simulating clockwise rotation about the ball centre.

Ball Geometry

  • Centre: row=76, col=107
  • Radius: 48 pixels
  • Determined by fitting a circle to the visible ball edge transitions and verifying that >99% of smiley-face feature pixels fall within the circle boundary.

Rotation

  • Direction: clockwise (positive angle in screen coordinates)
  • Rate: 2.37° per frame
  • Total: 45.0° over 19 frames (5 seconds at 4 fps)
  • Interpolation: bilinear (scipy.ndimage.map_coordinates, order=1)

Static Elements

  • Background wall: All pixels outside the ball circle are copied unchanged from frame 0.
  • Black platform: Dark pixels (R+G+B < 80) in the lower-right portion of the ball circle are treated as platform occlusion and remain static. The rotating ball renders behind them.
  • Dark right edge: The thin dark strip at the far right (cols 238-244, rows 55-85) is also preserved as a static scene element.

Texture Repair

  • Top-edge wall intrusion: The ball's dark top edge (rows 28-55) in frame 0 appears wall-coloured because the dark sphere shading blends with the background. These pixels are replaced using radially-scaled samples from the visible ball equator.
  • Bottom platform occlusion: Pixels behind the platform that would rotate into view are filled by mirroring the visible upper ball texture across the equator.

Libraries

  • NumPy for array operations
  • SciPy (ndimage.map_coordinates) for bilinear sampling
  • PIL/Pillow for image I/O
  • artifact_io (provided helper) for saving outputs
