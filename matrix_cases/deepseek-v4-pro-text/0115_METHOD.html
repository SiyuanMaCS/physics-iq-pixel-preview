OBSERVATION.md

Scene Observations

Frame dimensions

  • 256×144 pixels, 4 fps, 5 seconds = 20 frames
  • Frame 00 must exactly equal first_frame.png

Scene layout (left to right)

  • x=0–104: Mirror surface — smooth gray-white gradient (brightest at far left RGB(143,143,145), transitioning to medium gray RGB(145,143,143) at right edge). Reflects a uniformly lit background. No visible tennis-ball reflection in original frame 0.
  • x=104–110: Dark mirror border/frame — dark gray-brown, RGB(80–100, 70–90, 65–85).
  • x=110+: Background wall/floor — brownish-gray, warm tones, darkening toward right edge and bottom.

Key elements

Orange cord

  • Vertical at x=117–119, bright orange RGB(182–187, 107–117, 50–67) from y=0–24 at top.
  • Gap from y=25–63 where the magnet sits between cord segments (the cord passes through/behind the magnet area).
  • Cord resumes as darker orange/brown from y=64 downward, merging into the tennis ball and continuing below.

Tennis ball

  • Bright orange rounded cluster centered at approximately (x=122, y=71).
  • Core bright region: x=117–127, y=66–74, RGB(190–246, 100–146, 20–72) (warm orange-yellow).
  • Full extent including edges: x=112–130, y=64–78 (~18×14 px oval).
  • The tennis ball partially overlaps the right edge of the mirror border.

Magnet (inferred)

  • Dark metallic region at approximately x=111–116, y=30–60, between the upper cord segment and the tennis ball.
  • Appears as dark gray pixels (RGB(60–90, 50–80, 40–70)) adjacent to the cord line.

Mirror reflection

  • The mirror surface (x=0–104) shows a smooth gradient with no visible tennis ball reflection in the original frame.
  • By physics, a reflection should appear at mirror-plane-symmetric position: ball center at x≈122 → reflected at x≈90 (2×106−122=90).
  • For the animation, a dimmed, horizontally flipped tennis-ball reflection will be composited into the mirror at the corresponding position.

  4. Mirror reflection: Composite horizontally flipped, dimmed (50% opacity) tennis ball into mirror at reflected x position, moving in sync with the real ball.

METHOD.md

Method

Approach

Local Python pixel operations: warm-colour sprite copy-paste compositing, no external models.

Scene elements

  • Mirror: x=0–104, smooth gray-white gradient.
  • Mirror border: x=105–110, dark strip.
  • Cord: x=117–119 (3 px wide), bright orange at top (y=0–24), passes through magnet (y=25–63, dark), resumes at y=64+ through the tennis ball.
  • Magnet: dark region y≈35–60, x=108–120, stays fixed.
  • Tennis ball: warm-orange rounded cluster, x≈112–131, y≈63–80, centred ~(122, 71).

Animation technique

  1. Ball mask: Warm-colour threshold (R>85, R>G>B) within bounding box x=109–134, y=61–82. Captures only actual ball pixels — excludes dark magnet shadow and mirror border.
  2. Movement: 0–24 px linear downward displacement (4.8 px/s, 20 frames, 4 fps).
  3. Background fill: Vacated area filled column-wise from row 61, matching the dark magnet-shadow region.
  4. Cord extension: 3-px-wide cord from y=64 to ball top, colours averaged from y=15–20 top-cord segment.
  5. Mirror reflection: Horizontally flipped ball blended at 35% opacity into mirror at symmetric x-position, moving in sync.
  6. Frame 0: Exact pixel copy (array-equality assertion).

Output verification

  • Frame 00 equals first_frame.png (np.array_equal).
  • All 20 frames: 256×144×3 uint8.
  • pixels.npz: (144, 256, 20) uint32 0xRRGGBB, verified against PNGs.
