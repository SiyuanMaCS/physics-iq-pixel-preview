METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0017

Blue-black grabber releases a blue ball onto a pile of green kinetic sand. All 20 frames are produced by generate.py from the single input first_frame.png, using only local NumPy/SciPy/PIL pixel operations — no physics engine, renderer, network access or external assets. Frame 00 is written byte-identical to first_frame.png; frames 01–19 are synthesised by compositing locally extracted layers.

1. Scene analysis (see OBSERVATION.md)

Measurements were taken directly from the 256×144 first frame:

  • Ball: circle fit to the blue disk gives centre (134.57, 35.20) and solid radius 15.0 px (thresholded rim ≈ 15.5). The ball falls straight down, so cx ≡ 134.57.
  • Grabber: static blue/black arched arms entering at the top edge, tips around (112, 24) and (145, 19).
  • Sand: green blob bbox x 93–181, y 79–119; dome top y ≈ 80 at the centre column, so the ball bottom (y = 50.2) starts 30 px above the pile.
  • Wall: near-neutral grey with a soft affine intensity gradient; table: warm brown wood below the horizon (y ≈ 73–74).

2. Layer model

The first frame is treated as four overlapping layers:

  1. grey wall / wooden table background,
  2. green sand pile,
  3. blue ball (moving after release),
  4. blue-black grabber arms (static, in front of the ball at release).

Every generated frame is rebuilt as: clean background → deformed sand → contact shadow → ejected grains → moving ball → grabber tips → front sand mound. Because the grabber never moves, its pixels can simply be re-composited from frame 0; only the ball changes between background and foreground layers.

3. Background: wall model and vacated-ball inpainting

Wall model. A per-channel affine plane a·x + b·y + c is least-squares fitted to clean, near-neutral grey wall pixels (|R−G|<6, |G−B|<6, 140<R<230, y < 70, x outside 100–166). This plane is used to separate the grabber from the wall (below) and served during development as a fill source.

Erase mask. The frame-0 ball occupies the disk together with a small vertical motion-blur fringe. This region is modelled as a vertical capsule: segment from (CX, 30.7) to (CX, 38.7), radius 18.8, with anti-aliased coverage clip((18.8+0.7−d)/1.4, 0, 1). The hard interior (coverage > 0.55) defines the hole.

Real-pixel inpainting. Synthetic wall left a visible flat patch behind the moving ball, so the final fill is cloned from real frame-0 pixels:

  • candidate sources are all frame-0 pixels except the hole and the blue/black grabber region above it (clawish pixels in y < 33, x 100–164);
  • scipy.ndimage.distance_transform_edt(not_source, return_indices=True) maps every hole pixel to its nearest valid wall pixel;
  • the deep interior of the hole (2-pixel erosion) is lightly Gaussian smoothed (σ 1.3) so repeated wall texture does not freeze into a hard contour, while the boundary stays pixel-exact;
  • faint Gaussian noise (σ 1.1, fixed RNG seed 17) is added inside the hole to match the wall's natural grain.

The capsule lies entirely above the table horizon (bottom ≈ y 58), so no table fill is required.

4. Ball sprite

The sharp sphere texture is extracted once from frame 0:

  • a 35×35 sprite grid is centred on the fitted centre; every pixel with dist < R_INNER = 13.7 copies its true frame-0 RGB (the inner skin is free of background fringe);
  • unknown cells inside the stamp disk (R_STAMP = 15.4) are filled from the nearest known sample by EDT index mapping, then Gaussian-smoothed (σ 0.6) only where they were filled; the original known samples are always restored unchanged, preserving the two pale curved seams and the upper-right/lower-left shading gradient;
  • the synthetic outer rim (14.5–15.4 px) receives a 1.0→0.90 radial shade so the stamped silhouette keeps the photographic sphere's dark edge.

The sprite is bilinearly sampled at stamp time. There is no analytic "re-shading": the sphere is the photographed ball itself.

5. Motion-blurred ball stamping

For each frame the ball is composited by averaging multiple anti-aliased stamps over the camera exposure:

  • nm = clip(round(|Δy|·2.2), 3, 20) positions along the travel segment from the previous frame's centre to the current one, each with 3 vertical sub-offsets (−0.75, 0, +0.75 px);
  • each sample contributes coverage clip(R_STAMP − d + 0.5, 0, 1) weighted by the bilinear sprite colour; accumulated colour is divided by accumulated coverage and alpha-composited over the background.

This produces a short blur even at low speed and a growing vertical smear as the ball accelerates (up to ~7 px/frame near impact), without ghosts.

6. Grabber extraction and re-compositing

The static claw layer is recovered by un-compositing against the fitted wall plane:

  • deviation |F0 − WALL| plus a colour test (B−R > 35 and B > 95) or luminance < 78 identifies saturated blue plastic and black rim, while excluding the neutral blue-grey fringe of the ball;
  • the alpha is further restricted to dist_from_ball_centre > 16.8, y < 30, x ∈ (102, 162), and Gaussian-softened (σ 0.5);
  • claw RGB is solved from F0 = a·claw + (1−a)·WALL.

draw_claw simply alpha-composites this layer on top of every frame, so the arms are identical in all 20 frames and the ball visibly drops out from between the tips after release.

7. Sand deformation

Mask and profiles. The sand is segmented by G > 95, G > R+18, G > B+12; the largest connected component is hole-filled and binary-closed (2 iterations), giving per-column top/bottom profiles over x 93–181. A 1-px dilation of the mask (SMF) supplies anti-aliased coverage so shifted ragged edges never reveal table pixels.

Height-field warp (draw_sand). Each column is shifted vertically as a sum of analytic Gaussians:

  • central depression D·exp(−(x−cx)²/(2·8.5²)) — the crater;
  • two raised lips −lip·[G(x, cx−11, 3.3) + G(x, cx+11, 3.3)];
  • pile squash Q·(0.35·G(x, cx, 30) + 0.8·G(|x−cx|, 42, 6)) — a ~1 px flattening/widening at peak impact that eases back as the pile relaxes.

Destination pixels are filled by inverse-mapping into the frame-0 column with a column-compression term ys = (yd − shift·b0/h)/(1 − shift/h), bilinearly sampling the original sand texture so the granular pattern stretches and compresses rather than smearing. Pixels pushed above the original crest are mirrored into a sunlit lip (+8 % brightening, fading with crest height); newly exposed inner crater walls are shaded dark green toward the bottom of the depression.

Contact shadow. From f08 on, a darkened multiply ellipse (half-width 13, half-height 2.6) is applied only inside the sand mask, strength fading 0.9 → 0.2 as the ball sinks and the mound closes over it.

Front mound (draw_front_mound). To show the ball embedding, sand is piled against its lower arc in every column |dx| < 15.8. The upper contact line follows the ball circle, y_line = cy + k·sqrt(R²−dx²) with a per-frame factor k (0.55 at first contact, curling to −0.08 at rest — a "smile" that hugs the sphere). Inside the ball disk coverage is forced to 1 (the pile fully occludes the sphere); outside it uses the ragged mask coverage. The 1.8 px band at the contact is darkened (0.52→0.85) as ambient occlusion; the lit mound face is +6 %.

Ejected grains. Six grains launch on impact from offsets ±3–9 px around the ball's flanks with outward horizontal velocities (2.2–7.4 px/frame). For each grain the initial vertical velocity is solved analytically so the ballistic arc gy = 83 + vy·t + ½·5.1·t² lands on its target pile surface at t = 4 (f13); grains are 2–3 px blocks in sand green, drawn behind the ball (f09–f13) and alpha-faded through the last 2 px of descent.

8. Motion schedule and time scaling

Centre positions (cy_tab) use constant-acceleration kinematics cy = 35.20 + ½·0.931·t² for t = 0…8 (frames 00–08): contact at f08 (cy = 65, ball bottom at the y ≈ 80 dome top). Impact is then key-framed by hand to match a soft, inelastic material: sink 72 → 78.5 → 84 → 89 over f09–f12, a small rebound to 87.4 at f13, and damped settle 88.3 → 88.7 for f14–f19. Crater depth, lips, squash and mound-contact factor each follow their own per-frame tables (D_tab, LIP_tab, Q_tab, KL_tab, SH_tab) that peak at f12 and relax toward a permanent embedded state.

Disclosed pacing choice. At the image scale (~2.3 mm/px) a real 7 cm drop lasts ≈ 0.12 s — under one frame at 4 fps. To keep the release–fall– impact–burial event legible across the required 5 s clip, gravity is time-scaled so contact occurs at t = 2.0 s and burial completes near t = 3 s. The motion is still computed from the same kinematic equations (smooth ease-in acceleration, deceleration on entry, damped rebound); only the gravitational constant is non-physical. All frame timestamps correspond to real 4 fps clock time.

9. Per-frame build order

For f = 1…19, starting from a copy of frame 0:

  1. alpha-erase the capsule with the inpainted wall fill;
  2. draw_sand (crater/lips/squash for that frame);
  3. draw_contact_shadow;
  4. draw_grains (behind the ball);
  5. stamp_ball at cy_tab[f] with displacement cy_tab[f] − cy_tab[f−1];
  6. draw_claw (static tips in front);
  7. draw_front_mound.

Frame 00 is the untouched decoded first_frame.png (asserted pixel-equal).

10. Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save() writes pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, shape 144×256×20), frames/00.png–19.png, inspection.png (8 key frames) and a standalone index.html delta player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png (selected frames, consecutive |Δ| maps, and cumulative |f − f00| maps).

Verified after the final run:

  • frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png and the in-memory frame 00 equals the decoded input array (assertion in the script);
  • all 20 PNGs decode to exactly the uint32 tensor packed in pixels.npz;
  • per-frame changed-pixel counts vs frame 00 grow monotonically through the fall (1812 at f01 → 2591 at f08), jump with the sand deformation (≈ 4.9 k f09–f11), then converge to a constant 4236 for f16–f19 (zero inter-frame change: stable rest state);
  • visual review used full-frame and enlarged crop montages (fall, contact, burial, ejected grains) rendered into .tmp/.

11. Known limitations

  • Impact response (sink curve, crater/lip/squash tables, rebound) is artist-keyframed to look physically plausible, not simulated from material constitutive equations.
  • The ball texture is a 2D sprite of the photographed sphere; rotation is not modelled (no visible spin cue in the source frame).
  • Ejected grains are six simple 2–3 px blocks rather than a full particle system.
  • The grabber is assumed perfectly static, matching the locked-off prompt.
