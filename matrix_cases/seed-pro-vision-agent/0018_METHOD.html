METHOD.md

METHOD

Everything was generated locally in Python (generate.py, numpy + PIL + scipy.ndimage only): polynomial fitting, alpha compositing, analytic heightfields and ballistic clumps. No external models, engines, images or network access were used. artifact_io.py only packs the finished frames.

Pipeline

  1. Measurements (OBSERVATION.md). Colour masks segment frame 0 into wall, wood table, grabber, ball and sand. The sand top is stored as a per-column height map; the ball centre (117.5, 58.5), radius (14.5) and the 17 px fall gap are measured directly.

  2. Clean background plate. Degree-3 polynomial surfaces (8 terms for the wall including cross terms; 8 terms for the wood) are least-squares fitted to clean frame-0 pixels, excluding every object and the ball rectangle (x 94–142, y 36–82). Residuals sampled on clean strips just outside the rectangle are bilinearly interpolated across it so the inpaint meets the real wall/wood brightness on all four edges; a contact-shadow darkening is added along the table back edge. The plate replaces the rectangle with a distance-transform feather whose outer 3 rows stay byte-exact, plus a weak low-pass noise field matched to the image's grain. This plate is static: wall, table and grabber never move.

  3. Ball sprite. Inside an elliptical window centred on the measured ball, an RGBA sprite is un-premultiplied against the clean plate. Coverage is a sharp core with a ~1 px anti-aliased rim (the frame-0 ball's own softness is motion blur, not surface softness); a blue-minus-red chroma gate on the rim discards grey wall bloom, and noisy fringe colours are pulled toward the median cyan of the core.

  4. Animation. The ball drops vertically (g = 60 px/s²) and first touches the crest at frame 3, then follows a damped penetration schedule to rest (cy = 81.5, 6.0 px into the pile). Each frame composites a 5-tap temporal average of translated sprite instances, with tap spread proportional to instantaneous speed (0–9.4 px), reproducing 4 fps exposure motion blur while leaving the resting ball sharp and halo-free. Coverage of the averaged taps is normalised so the fully covered core stays opaque.

  5. Sand deformation. After the ball is drawn, the pile is resampled column by column under an analytic crater/rim heightfield D·exp(−(u/8)²) − R·exp(−((|u|−15)/6)²) (D up to 6.0, R up to 3.2), with the column bottom anchored so undeformed columns map to themselves. The resampling respects the original per-pixel sand occupancy: ragged air gaps and lumps in the frame-0 silhouette are never filled, and pixels outside a |u| ≈ 34 px smoothstep window remain byte-identical. Subtle darkening on the crater floor/wall and a slight lightening on the rim crest give the depression form; a thin shadowed inner wall fills the crescent between ball and surface, strictly inside the original pile envelope (nothing is drawn in the air above it). The sand is composited over the ball, so the raised rim naturally occludes the ball's lower edges.

  6. Ejected clumps. Four fixed 2–4 px lumps in the measured sand palette launch at frame 4 from the rim on ballistic arcs (g = 30 px/s²) and freeze the frame their centre meets the (deforming) surface, remaining as resting grains. Trajectories are precomputed so settled frames never twinkle.

  7. Output. Frames 12–19 are the identical settled frame. Frame 00 is the untouched input PNG; every frame is 256×144 RGB. artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB, shape 144×256×20), the 20 PNGs, inspection.png and index.html; transition_check.png shows frame pairs and ×3 amplified deltas for release, contact, impact, settling and rest.

Verification performed

  • frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png; all 20 PNGs decode to exactly the packed uint32 tensor in pixels.npz.
  • Frames 12–19 are byte-identical; every pixel outside the inpaint rectangle that is not part of the ball, the deformed crater footprint or a flying clump is unchanged from frame 0 (static locked-off scene). All saturated grabber pixels above the ball are byte-static in every frame.
  • inspection.png, transition_check.png and zoomed contact sheets were viewed with the image Read tool across revisions; the final revisions removed an inpaint seam, a resting-ball halo, wall-coloured ghost columns and over-raised/silhouette-filling crater rims.

Known simplifications

  • The ball is a translated copy of the frame-0 sprite (its shading and seam rotate with it only by translation); no perspective or scale change is modelled because the fall is vertical and short.
  • The crater is an analytic heightfield resampling the original texture rather than a granular simulation; the ejecta are four hand-placed lumps.
