METHOD.md

METHOD

Task

20 frames (256×144, 4 fps, 5 s), locked camera. Frame 00 is the byte-exact first_frame.png. Event: the grey/bluish tennis ball rolls out of the black pipe on the left along axis u = (0.995, 0.095), strikes the brown ball, which is initially touching the orange ball; the orange ball rolls away to the right.

Everything was produced with local Python pixel math (NumPy + OpenCV), no external models or assets.

1. Scene measurement

Ball centers and radius were found by color thresholding and perimeter-circle fits on first_frame.png: grey (85.8, 50.7), brown (120.3, 54.0), orange (135.4, 56.5), geometric radius R = 7.6 px (brown↔orange center distance 15.1 px, i.e. touching). Grey is 34.55 px from brown, so its travel to contact is 19.46 px. Lighting/shadow directions were read off the still (soft contact ellipse under each ball plus a faint multiply-dark cast streak to the right).

2. Clean table plate

  • All three ball discs (radius ≈ 9.9), their soft shadow supports, and one static motion-smear patch left of the grey ball are masked out.
  • Masked pixels are filled independently per row/channel from robust smoothed profiles of clean table pixels in x ∈ [18, 228] (Gaussian-smoothed row samples, linear interpolation across gaps, vertical smoothing), preserving the table's horizontal color banding; faint tiled table grain is added in repaired areas.

3. Ball sprites (23×23 RGBA tiles)

  • Analytic soft-disc alpha on a 1.5-px antialiased rim.
  • Felt layer: original disc pixels are kept verbatim for the inner core; the outer rim and seam band are rebuilt with OpenCV TELEA inpainting from core pixels (neighbor-ball pixels excluded from inpaint sources at the brown/orange contact), then a subtle ambient-occlusion darkening is applied toward the rim. Sphere shading therefore stays fixed to the light source.
  • Seam layer: an analytic soft sinusoidal band y = y0 + A·sin(k·x + φ) with per-ball measured color/width (bluish-grey arc on grey; lavender band on brown; cream band on orange). It is a separate alpha layer so it can rotate while the felt stays fixed.

4. Shadow stamps

For each ball, the still-frame darkening ratio (frame0/plate) inside its elliptical support is extracted as a soft multiply stamp (alpha remapped to a faint, soft range), and re-positioned with the ball. Shadows are composited onto the table before any balls are drawn.

5. Physics (1-D, equal tennis balls)

  • Grey: constant v = 19.46/6 ≈ 3.24 px/frame; contact with brown exactly at frame 6; +0.7 px post-impact creep, then rest.
  • Chain impact between frames 6→7 through the pre-contacted brown: brown settles at +2.0 px by frame 8; orange launches at 3.2 px/frame with rolling resistance −0.13 px/frame², ending +31.46 px at frame 19 (center ≈ (166.7, 59.5)).
  • Rolling cue: only the seam layer rotates, θ = traveled_distance/R (clockwise); felt shading stays fixed.
  • Motion cue: moving ball tiles get a short anisotropic Gaussian blur aligned with u (sigma proportional to per-frame speed, premultiplied alpha), reproducing the slight exposure smear of the original photo.

6. Compositing

Per frame: plate → (a) any ball that has not started moving is pasted back as the verbatim first-frame patch (its disc + shadow support with a soft feather), keeping all stationary pixels essentially identical to the still; (b) moving balls' shadow stamps (multiply); (c) moving ball sprites far→near (felt + rotated seam, motion-blurred) with sub-pixel affine placement. Frame 00 is copied unchanged from first_frame.png.

  • frames/00.png–19.png, pixels.npz (H×W×20 uint32 0xRRGGBB), inspection.png (8 keyframes), transition_check.png (5 frame pairs with ×3 absolute-difference panels), index.html (standalone player built by artifact_io.save), plus this file and OBSERVATION.md.
  • Verified in Python: frames/00.png is byte-exact to first_frame.png; every PNG unpacks to the matching pixels.npz tensor slice; frame-to-frame changed-pixel bounding boxes stay on the moving balls/shadows only.
