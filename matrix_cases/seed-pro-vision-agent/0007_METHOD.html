METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0007

Task: 20 RGB frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s). A brown tennis ball rolls straight out of the black pipe on the left and hits the yellow rubber duck. Frame 0 is first_frame.png, reproduced byte-for-byte. All work is local Python pixel math (NumPy, OpenCV, Pillow); no external models, assets, or references.

1. Scene measurement (from frame 0)

Measured directly in native pixels (see OBSERVATION.md for the table):

  • black static pipe x 0–20.5, y 41–67;
  • ball: center (84, 51.5), core r≈9, soft fuzz to r≈11, dark maroon (~RGB 47,26,19) with a faint lighter seam band across the upper hemisphere;
  • duck faces left: body x 203–222, y 30–51, orange beak x 205–210 / y 38–41;
  • key light comes from upper right: both objects cast soft shadows leftward (ball shadow visible to x≈64, duck shadow x≈182–203), shadow multiply tint ≈ (0.85, 0.78, 0.74);
  • the ground plane rises toward the back: the ball→duck center-line slopes −0.0773 px/px and the ball's apparent radius shrinks 9.5→8.8 px along the roll (perspective).

2. Clean-table reconstruction (wood_fill)

Footprint masks are built from the measured silhouettes (ball: soft disc r 11–14.2 plus an ellipse covering its cast shadow; duck: a per-row hand- traced body polygon, dilated for the fuzz halo, plus the shadow ellipse). Each masked interval is filled along the wood grain direction (x):

  • tone = per-row linear interpolation between medians of the horizontally smoothed source in clean bands 5–14 px on either side of the interval (bands are kept away from the pipe/fireplace and other holes);
  • fine grain = residual frame − Gaussian-blurred frame, mirrored from intact table immediately beside the interval (right side for the ball hole, left for the duck hole), damped ×0.85;
  • 3 px distance-feathered blend at the boundary.

This gives BG_BALL (only the ball removed; duck untouched pixel-for-pixel for frames 0–14) and BG_BOTH (both objects removed, used frames 15–19).

3. Object sprites

  • Ball color: the frame-0 image itself is warped each frame with an affine map (rotation about the frame-0 ball center + scale + translation), so the captured fuzz/seam shading moves with the ball. The silhouette is a freshly rendered soft disc alpha at the target center/radius (r−0.9 → r+1.5 smoothstep), i.e. a perfect sphere outline with a fuzzy rim.
  • Duck alpha: residual of frame 0 vs BG_BOTH, restricted to the body polygon and feathered, so only duck/beak pixels are carried (no shadow).

4. Animation

  • Pre-impact (frames 0–14): constant 7.86 px/frame (≈31 px/s ≈ 1.6 ball diameters/s) along the measured sloped center-line; no-slip roll angle θ = ∫ v/r dt accumulated with the varying radius (≈12.1 rad, just under two clockwise turns, at contact); radius interpolated with position.
  • Frame 14 (3.50 s): the ball right edge meets the duck's chest (x≈203); the duck is still the untouched original.
  • Frames 15–19: near-inelastic contact — the ball stays pressed to the chest and both execute a damped rightward slide (2.0→5.8 px), while the duck rocks clockwise about body pivot (213,48), peaking at 12° on frame 17 and settling toward 8° by frame 19. Sprite warps are cv2 affine rotations (negative angle = screen clockwise, verified empirically).
  • Shadows are blurred ellipse masks with multiply blending; they scale with the ball and follow the duck's post-impact transform. Frames 12–14 also apply a softer (×0.55) multiply on the portion of the ball entering the duck's cast shadow. Frames 0/15–19 re-composite the moved duck, preserving the original duck shadow on frames where it does not move.
  • Frame 0 is then overwritten with the exact input PNG as a hard guarantee of identity.
