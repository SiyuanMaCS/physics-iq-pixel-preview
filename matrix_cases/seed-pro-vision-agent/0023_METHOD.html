METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0023

Task: 20 RGB frames, 256×144, 4 fps. A grey-blue tennis ball rolls rightward out of a black pipe, across a light wood coffee table. Locked-off camera. Only inputs: first_frame.png, prompt.txt. All processing is local Python (numpy + Pillow) pixel math; no external models, no network.

Frame 00 is the input PNG, written byte-unmodified (frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png, asserted in generate.py). Frames 01–19 are composites built from layers extracted out of frame 0 and translated.

1. Clean table plate

Only the ball-and-shadow region needs reconstruction. A soft defect mask is the union of three blurred ellipses (ball [140.5,106.5,163.5,126.5], contact crescent [131,111,169,128], cast [158,113,192,128], Gaussian σ≈1.5).

Table tone under the mask is modeled per row as a quadratic in x fitted by least squares to clean columns x∈[36,125]∪[194,251], with 3 robust passes (residuals above median+8 replaced by the prediction). Quadratic coefficients are then vertically smoothed (5-tap [1,2,3,2,1]). Fine grain is borrowed from a horizontally rolled copy (roll +58 px, a region the ball never occupies): high-pass = donor − Gaussian(σ=4) of donor, clamped to ±3.5 levels. Plate = original outside the mask, tone+grain inside it. Everything else in the room (sofa, pipe, lamp, nightstand, table edge diagonal) is untouched original pixels, so static parts stay exactly static. The soft tan table streak around x120–145 is preserved: it lies in clean columns and is part of the plate.

2. Geometry

Disc: center (151.5, 116.5), radii (11.0, 8.7) in x,y. These were refined from an initial visual estimate (152, 116.5; 10.5, 8.6) by thresholding the observed color difference against the modeled shadowed table and matching the soft fringe extent at every row; the dark mauve lower-left flank extends the silhouette ~1 px farther than the first visual estimate. Radial coordinate d = sqrt(((x−151.5)/11)² + ((y−116.5)/8.7)²).

3. Ground shadow layer

Physical shadow darkens all channels about equally, so a single luminance field is used (Rec.601 luma), avoiding the color fringes that a per-channel factor picked up from the bluish ball rim:

  • support = union of ellipses [130,116,171,129] (contact/AO crescent at the base, wrapping lower-left) and [157,115,193,128] (long cast to the right), Gaussian σ=1.1. An earlier larger support falsely captured table above-left of the ball and dragged a smudge with the sprite; it was tightened to y≥115/116.
  • darkening k = clip((L_plate − L_orig)/L_plate, 0, 1), suppressed inside the ball by (1−clip(1.06−d,0,1)), blurred σ=0.7.
  • stored factor 1−k (broadcast to RGB); measured cast-zone RGB ratios are equal across channels (0.959/0.959/0.959).

4. Ball layer (coverage + decontaminated color)

Background reference at the home position is bg = plate * factor.

Coverage alpha combines geometry and observation:

  • support gate G = clip((1.10−d)/0.12, 0,1) (σ=0.3 blur) — the soft fringe extends ~10% beyond the nominal ellipse;
  • interior prior I = clip((1.00−d)/0.12, 0,1) forces full coverage deep inside the silhouette even where the dark mauve flank has low contrast against the contact shadow;
  • observed coverage clip(max_c|orig−bg| / 7, 0,1);
  • initial a = max(I, G·obs), then 4 alpha-matting iterations: given the current intrinsic ball color C, a least-squares coverage a = Σ(orig−bg)(C−bg) / Σ(C−bg)² is computed per pixel; where C and bg are too close (denominator <14-ish) it blends back to the observed coverage. Each iteration is gated by G, maxed with I, and σ=0.4 blurred. Alpha is exactly 0 where G<0.03, so no coverage leaks into the pure cast shadow (a previous version ghosted an annulus over the dark nightstand).

Intrinsic ball RGB (the key halo fix): soft pixels are a premultiplied mix of ball and shadowed table, and translating the raw sprite drags the home table color into rim pixels — visible as a light halo when the ball crosses the dark nightstand. For coverage band 0.04≤a<0.985 the intrinsic color is estimated by unpremultiplying: C = clip((orig − (1−a)·bg)/a, 40, 240); per-channel numerators and weights are Gaussian-smoothed (σ=1.0) and divided as sums (denominator guard 0.05), which stabilizes near-transparent pixels. Core pixels (a≥0.85) keep exact original RGB. Frame-0 recomposition bg·(1−a)+C·a matches the original with mean abs error ≈1.4 levels, p95 ≈7.4, max ≈16 over the disc (the original frame itself is never replaced, so this only characterizes the translated sprite's fidelity).

5. Motion and compositing (frames 01–19)

Constant-speed horizontal translation, fixed screen y: x(t) = 151.5 + 4.921·t, t=0..19 (frame 19 center 245.0; the right rim reaches the frame edge). 4.921 px/frame = 19.68 px/s. Rolling-without- slipping spin θ = travel/8.7 ≈ 32.4°/frame (≈1.71 turns over 5 s). Layers are translated on a 128-px-padded canvas with a Pillow bilinear affine and cropped back; padding 128 (not 64) is required because the largest shift is ~93 px — with small padding the affine wraps edge columns to black.

Per frame: comp = plate · factor(t) (shadow moved with the ball; fill factor 1.0 outside its support), then alpha-over comp = comp·(1−a(t)) + C(t)·a(t). The ball is not rotated: its shading is fixed in world space, and uniform felt under that fixed shading looks identical while rolling. To give the spin a visible cue without contradicting the fixed shading, a very subtle scrolling mottle is multiplied on the disc interior only: 1 + 0.014·sin(6λ + 2.5·nz)·clip(0.7+nz,0,1) with λ = arcsin(nx) − θ (body-frame longitude), gated to nx²+nz²<0.9 and a>0.4. Peak amplitude 1.4%.

At the far-right frames the upper disc passes over the nightstand behind the table-edge diagonal; the composited rim blends correctly against that dark background (verified: in the annulus 1.08<d<1.5 around translated centers, brightening over dark background is 0 levels except ≤1-level rounding, and 0 over the table).

7. Honest limitations

  • Plate fill under the home ball/shadow is a smooth quadratic + borrowed grain, not the true occluded table; differences are sub-grain (~few levels) and only visible in the vacated zone during early frames.
  • Ball appearance is a rigidly translated, matte-extracted sprite (the actual rendering is soft; rim RGB is estimated intrinsic color), plus a synthesized 1.4% scrolling mottle; there is no real felt texture or seam in the source to rotate.
  • The ground shadow is a simple 2D darkening field rigidly attached to the ball (AO crescent + right cast); it is neutral-colored and slightly simplified where it crosses the table/nightstand edge at the far right.
  • Constant speed assumes a flat table with no deceleration cue in the single still; frame 19 deliberately ends at the frame edge with the ball still partly visible (exit implied after the clip).
