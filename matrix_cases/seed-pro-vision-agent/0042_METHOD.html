METHOD.md

Method — case_0042

Continuation of a locked-off close-up: a granton chef knife finishes a rocking cut through one half of a tangerine on a frosted-glass cutting board. 20 frames, 256×144, 4 fps (5 s). Everything was produced with local Python pixel operations (numpy / OpenCV / Pillow) on first_frame.png; no external images, no network, no generative models. generate.py is fully deterministic (fixed RNG seed) and regenerates every artifact from scratch.

1. Layer extraction from frame 0

  • Blade silhouette (blade_poly): a hand-traced polygon over the steel, tip (72,108) → spine → top edge at x≈186–204 → cutting edge/belly → tip.
  • Tangerine pieces: HSV threshold on orange (H 3–26, S>65, V>85, red-dominant red–green difference), connected components, interior-hole flood fill, plus a one-pixel dilation pass to absorb pale pith/flesh pixels. The two surviving components are the crescent (centroid 102.7,68.2) and the main lobe (centroid 120.7,85.9). Each piece mask is clipped to its physical side of the blade band (x+y<179 / x+y>194) so the dark cut-slot pixels are not carried along as "fruit".
  • The blade mask loses a dilated ring of fruit pixels (5 px) so that orange flesh seen in front of the steel travels with the fruit sprites, never with the knife.

2. Static background plate

A single clean plate of the set without knife/fruit is built once:

  1. Navier–Stokes inpaint (cv2.INPAINT_NS, radius 4) only under a compact ellipse beneath the fruit, where the board texture is isotropic and directional cloning would smear.
  2. Directional clone-fill through the blade corridor (silhouette dilated 17 px): 14 iterative averaging passes from neighbours at ~14–18 px offsets, accepting only samples that are known and on the same side of the board rim (table vs. board), so wood is never pulled onto glass and vice versa.
  3. Table-side row interpolation: corridor pixels above the rim are repaired by horizontal interpolation between good pixels on the same scanline, preserving the horizontal oak grain.
  4. Rim restoration: the hidden segment of the frosted board's bright back rim (measured line y = 0.55x − 51.5) is rebuilt by sampling the real, never-occluded rim segment over x≈112–142 and sliding it along the rim axis (a gentle sinusoidal along-axis jitter breaks the periodicity), blended at 0.72 through a feathered strip that is wide on the board side and thin on the table side (so wood features are not tiled).
  5. Static contact darkening under the fruit's resting footprint.
  6. Matched, static grain: Gaussian noise clouds + small mottle + per-channel fine noise on the glass; two-scale horizontal streaks on the table; feathered over several pixels at every zone edge so the added texture dissolves into the untouched photo. Grain is baked into the plate once, so consecutive frames never shimmer.

3. Sprites

  • Blade sprite on an oversized canvas (screen range x∈[−60,320), y∈[−50,180)): real frame-0 pixels inside the blade mask, plus a short procedural steel continuation beyond the top/right frame borders (per-band profile colors sampled as medians from the real blade, gaussian noise, and continued granton slits with bright rims). Alpha is the hard mask feathered with σ≈1.1 px.
  • Fruit sprites: tight RGBA crops of the two piece masks carrying real pixels, α feathered σ≈0.9 px.

4. Per-frame animation (frames 1–19)

Motion is driven by a smoothstep on t/12 (frames 0–12), followed by a tiny sinusoidal settle (frames 13–16) and a static hold (17–19):

  • blade: +8° rigid rotation (cv2 positive angle = visually clockwise) about the tip pivot (74,107);
  • crescent: translate (−3.2,−1.0), rotate −3.5° about its centroid;
  • lobe: translate (+4.2,+1.6), rotate +3.0° about its centroid;
  • three translucent amber juice beads (soft halo + blurred body + glossy core) fade in at the base of the lobe from frame 8 onward.

Compositing order per frame, starting from a copy of frame 0:

  1. Reveal-only repaint: the plate is painted only where a frame-0 object (blade polygon or fruit pieces) used to be but is no longer covered by the transformed objects (dilated old coverage minus dilated new coverage, with a ~1 px feather). This is the key anti-flicker measure: surroundings that no object crossed stay byte-identical to the photo, and the very first frame is not re-rendered wholesale.
  2. Moving per-piece contact shadows (their alpha ramps in with displacement so they cannot "pop" against the baked-in shadows of frame 0).
  3. Warped fruit sprites; juice beads; a soft two-sided blade cast shadow that follows the rotation; the warped blade sprite last.

Frame 0 is never passed through the compositor and is asserted byte-identical to first_frame.png.

5. Outputs and checks

  • artifact_io.save writes the 20 PNGs (frames/00.png…19.png), the pixels.npz uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player.
  • The script additionally writes transition_check.png (key frames plus amplified differences against frame 0).
  • Verification performed: frame-00 byte equality; tensor/PNG round-trip equality for every frame; ROI-wise frame-to-frame change energies (monotonic ease-in/out, zero change outside the swept corridor, static hold at the end); visual inspection of enlarged crops and contact sheets drove the iteration on rim cloning, grain matching, juice appearance and temporal pops.

Known approximations: the off-frame steel/handle continuation is procedural; the knife is modeled as a rigid 2-D rotation (no perspective or flex); the tangerine pieces are rigid bodies with small planar tilts only; and a few juice beads stand in for pulp/juice released by the cut.
