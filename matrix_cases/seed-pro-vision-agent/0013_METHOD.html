METHOD — case_0013_attempt_01

Task: 20 RGB frames (256×144, 4 fps, 5 s, locked-off static camera) of an orange basketball released over a black plastic milk crate, dropping in, bouncing and settling. Everything was produced locally with generate.py (NumPy + PIL only); no external models, engines, references or masks were used. Inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt alone.

Pipeline overview

  1. frames[0] is a verbatim copy of first_frame.png (verified byte-identical after re-encoding path: same uint8 RGB array).
  2. Build one static ball-less plate from frame 0 by inpainting only the pixels the ball occupies (wall + the top strip of the crate rim).
  3. Derive a static fractional occlusion mask of the black plastic bars from the plate's luminance.
  4. Every later frame: analytically render the shaded sphere at the sampled bounce position, darken it as a function of depth inside the crate, and composite it behind the occlusion mask over the plate.
  5. Save via the provided artifact_io.save: pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB, shape (144,256,20)), the 20 PNGs, an inspection sheet and index.html. A separate transition_check.png montage is also written.

Scene measurements

From frame 0 (see OBSERVATION.md for the full list): ball circle center (144.39, 25.13) px, radius 27.62 px; rim back edge y = 48 at x = 103 rising to 51 at x = 178 and 53.5 at x = 198 (measured in unobstructed strips); front lip y ≈ 52–55; crate interior floor projects to ball-bottom y ≈ 110, i.e. rest center cy = 82.4. Scale ≈ 2.31 px/cm from a 23.9 cm ball diameter, so g = 9.81 m/s² ≈ 2260 px/s².

Static plate (build_plate)

The rebuild mask is the union of (a) thresholded orange ball pixels, (b) a generous analytic disk of radius 1.10·R around the fitted center (to catch the anti-aliased fringe and the cropped top), and (c) a bounded warm-fringe box at the ball/rim junction, dilated by 1 px. Everything outside that mask is untouched frame-0 data.

  • Wall fill. A per-row horizontal linear bridge between the nearest known neutral-gray wall anchors left and right of the footprint. Anchors must be neutral (|R−G|, |G−B| ≤ 22), light (95 < R < 225, luminance > 140) and lie above the measured sloped rim edge, so neither orange fringe nor dark rim anti-aliasing can bias the bridge. Rows with no anchor pair copy the nearest good row; two passes of a strict 3-tap vertical smoothing (applied only where all three rows are wall fill) finish the gradient.
  • Rim fill (rows y = 46…58 inside the rim zone). For each channel, known dark plastic pixels below the edge (luminance < 130) are linearly detrended along x; the residual is cloned by the best integer horizontal period searched among 4–7 px (the crate grid repeats roughly every 5 px), using per-phase medians, and the trend is added back. This synthesizes the dark bar/hole texture where the ball had hidden it. This strip is almost entirely hidden in the final video — by the ball cap on frames 1–2 and by the opaque plastic (occlusion mask ≈ 1) at rest — so only its mean tone and edge position matter.

Plastic occlusion mask (build_occluder)

Inside the side-face and front-face polygons (100,47)-(134,52)-(128,122)-(100,104) and (133,52)-(199,55)-(195,114)-(127,122) (dilated 1 px), alpha is clip((85 − plate_luminance)/30, 0, 1): dark bars → 1, open holes (handle slot, square-hole band, diamond lattice) → 0, with soft edges. One 3×3-weighted box pass ((4·center + 4 neighbours)/8) anti-aliases bar edges. The mask is static; only the ball moves behind it.

Ball rendering (render_ball)

Analytic sphere, rendered at 4× supersampling and 4×4 box-filtered to native resolution.

  • Color at a surface point with unit normal (nx, ny, nz toward viewer) is a least-squares affine model fitted to the frame-0 ball: [R,G,B] = [1,nz,nx,ny] · SHADE with SHADE = [[177.4,75.6,21.6],[16.2,-1.8,-21.3],[-2.05,-2.9,-5.6],[-0.98,-1.08,1.33]], reproducing the warm center, blue limb rim-light and vertical gradient.
  • Two dark meridian seams at longitudes −18° and +58° (soft |sine of longitude from the meridian plane| bands, half-width 0.030, channel darkening 0.40/0.42/0.85). Seams rotate with the ball's spin; the observed normal is rotated about the screen-y axis into the ball frame before testing.
  • Two view-fixed near-white Gaussian specular streaks at local (−8.9, +0.9) σ=(0.75,2.4) and (+19.8, +0.8) σ=(0.95,2.8), amplitude 0.95, added (×235) before clipping — the streaks stay attached to the room light, not to the spinning surface.
  • Optional vertical squash: within ±0.02 s of a sampled impact the ball gets up to 6% vertical compression with 0.6× compensatory horizontal stretch about the contact point (only frame 3, ~3%, is actually affected).

Physics (bounce_centers)

Free fall from rest at cy = 25.13 with g = 2260 px/s²; first impact on the crate floor (cy = 82.4) at t₁ = √(2·(82.4−25.13)/2260) = 0.225 s. Instantaneous impacts with coefficient of restitution e = 0.68; bounce segments are exact parabolas, stopping when launch velocity < 30 px/s. Impact times: 0.225, 0.531, 0.739, 0.881, 0.977, 1.043, 1.087 s. Sampled centers (cy):

Spin about the screen-y axis follows θ(t) = 32°·(1 − e^(−t/0.45 s)) during the bounce and is frozen at the frame-5 value for frames 5–19, so the settled tail is perfectly static.

Compositing and lighting

For each frame the ball RGBA is rendered at (cx, cy, θ). Ball pixels below the front rim line 52 + (x−133)·3/66 and inside the crate polygon are darkened by 0.80 − 0.24·depth (depth 0→1 over 55 px below the lip), with an extra ×0.85 on the side face, modeling the reduced interior light. Final alpha is ball_alpha · (1 − occluder_alpha); frame = ball·α + plate·(1−α) over the plate only where α > 1/255. Above the rim the ball is unoccluded and shows as a small orange cap on frames 1–2; at and below the rim it is seen only through the lattice/handle openings. Nothing else in the scene changes.

Known approximations

  • The hidden rim strip is periodic texture synthesis from neighboring rows, not a recovered true background; it is masked by plastic in every output frame except the few cap-edge pixels of frames 1–2, where only its darkness matters.
  • Contact dynamics are instantaneous-point impacts with constant e; the crate flex, ball-crate friction, and the off-center horizontal rattle are approximated rather than simulated as rigid bodies.
  • The ball's interior shading darkening is a simple depth ramp inferred from the hole brightnesses, and the tiny ±1 px rattle/spin parameters are hand-tuned for plausibility at 4 fps.
