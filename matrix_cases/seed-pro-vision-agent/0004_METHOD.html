ball-behind-rotating-paper · 生成方法

Everything is generated with local Python only (numpy, opencv-python, Pillow) from the two provided inputs, first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external images/models, or other cases are used. All temporary diagnostic images live in .tmp/. The single entry point is:

python3 generate.py

which builds all 20 frames in memory and writes them through artifact_io.save.

1. Scene model

The shot is static and locked off. Measurements from first_frame.png (see OBSERVATION.md) give:

  • a black rotating platform (puck) centred at image (150, 111.5), rx ≈ 35, ry ≈ 8.5;
  • a cream cardstock tent: a front panel A (lit cream quad SRC_A = [[115,51],[183,49],[191,108],[123,121]], order Rt, At, Ab, Rb) and a shorter rear flap B in shadow;
  • a blue-black grabber with a fixed upper shaft/grip entering from the top and a moving lower claw;
  • grey-lavender wall behind and a warm wooden table below.

A weak-perspective 3D model was least-squares fitted to the six visible card corners (the fit script lived in .tmp/fit.py during development; the resulting constants are in generate.py): focal length f = 227.60, D0 = 301.58, principal x cx = 123.93, horizon py gy = 120.85, pitch λ = 0.2687, image shear 0.1144. The vertical rotation axis passes through world (Xis, Zis) = (39.1, −54.4), the disc centre. Projected corner error at θ = 0 is about 1 px. The card rotates clockwise (viewed from above) at constant speed, θ(t) = 22°·t/19, over the 20 frames.

2. Clean plate

The card, flap, claw and blue tip are removed with geometric masks (exact quads/polygons, dilated 5 px for the removal mask and 9 px for the donor exclusion so anti-aliased fringes cannot leak into the fill). The background is reconstructed in layers:

  • Wood. Known table pixels (colour-filtered to exclude black puck/cable) are propagated along the −5° grain direction in both senses by iterative affine warps; the two fills are blended by propagation distance, lightly blurred (σ 0.9), and modulated with long horizontal grain streaks plus noise.
  • Wall. Two-stage normalised diffusion. Stage 1 iteratively normalised Gaussian blurs (σ 22, 6 passes) grow the known wall across the hole. Stage 2 fixes the donors and refines every target pixel (σ 6, 40 passes) into a smooth harmonic field, which removes the coarse mottle of the coverage pass. The fill is alpha-blended with a 1.1 px feather so it meets the original wall seamlessly; the wall/table seam fringe is forced to wall colour after the wood pass.
  • Platform puck. Fully procedural: an ellipse (top) of near-black satin (46,44,45) with a faint Gaussian sheen near the far rim and gentle edge darkening, plus a cylindrical front face filled with the measured vertical gradient (≈(45,43,44) → (5,4,2)) and a faint warm tint on the outer right rim.
  • Final per-region Gaussian grain matches the measured camera noise (σ ≈ 1.0 on the defocused wall, 1.8 on the sharper wood).

3. Card rendering (render_card)

For each frame the six corners are rotated about the disc-centre axis and projected.

  • Panel A: the real cream texture inside SRC_A is taken from frame 00 after a small Telea inpaint of dark occlusions, then perspective-warped (cv2.warpPerspective) onto the rotated quad, with a ±5 % brightness term from the analytic normal (panel tilt 54°).
  • Flap B: the rear flap curls outward (piecewise-linear bulge, max 8.4 px) so it is rendered as 16 vertical strips, each independently warped from a 120×170 procedural dark-tan texture (corner-gradient base, a few faint diagonal fibres, grain), shaded by the rotated normal and slightly darkened toward the curled edge, with a thin paper-thickness highlight.
  • Skirt: the panel bottom edge visibly dips over the puck rim by 5.6 px at the ridge foot, decaying linearly toward the far corner. A seam-free per-pixel cv2.remap pastes a narrow strip whose top row is sampled from the real panel 2 px inside its bottom edge (with a 3.5 % downward shade), so the droop matches the panel texture and lighting at every frame.
  • Fixed crescent: the near-black platform sliver at the ridge foot (x ≈ 112–133, y ≈ 103+, segmented by max-channel ≤ 52, with a half-alpha dark ring) is stationary platform geometry, so the exact frame-00 pixels are alpha-composited between the rotating flap and panel. The panel then occludes it naturally where it passes in front; the puck's near rim occludes the flap only where the flap is absent.
  • A dark ridge crease line is drawn just on the flap side.
  • The function also returns an accumulated coverage mask used for the behind-card occlusion.

4. Grabber and ball

  • Frames 0–2: the original lower claw pixels from frame 00 are segmented inside a hand polygon as near-neutral dark (max channel < 80, R−B < 28) or blue — deliberately excluding the tan card visible through the open tongs — and pasted shifted by (3,−8) at frame 1 and (6,−17) at frame 2 (retracting out of frame). Frame 0 is copied byte-for-byte from first_frame.png.
  • Frames 3–19: a procedural arm is drawn on the clean plate: a quadratic-Bézier black rod from the fixed elbow (121,45) to the wrist, two prongs, a small blue grip highlight while holding, and a grey wrist joint. When empty the prongs open (spread grows, tips turn upward).
  • The static silver shaft/black grip at the top (segmented from frame 00) is composited on top of every frame.
  • Ball: an orange tennis ball of true radius 9 px is rendered at 3× supersampling — radial sphere coverage with a Gaussian-soft edge (σ 2 at supersampled resolution), base (226,116,26), a warm upper-left highlight and lower-right shading, and two pale curved seam arcs — then downscaled bilinearly. Keyframed centres descend from (143,2) at frame 3 to (150,101) at frame 14; the ball is released between frames 14 and 15 and remains at rest at (150,101), hidden behind the card, through frame 19.

5. Compositing and keyframes

Per frame t ≥ 1: the card is rendered over the clean plate; separately the arm + ball are drawn on another copy of the clean plate ("behind" layer); behind-layer pixels that differ from the plate are shown only where the card coverage mask is below 0.5, giving correct occlusion at the rotating top edge; frames 1–2 additionally paste the retracting original claw in front; the static grip is always restored on top.

Keyframes (ball/claw positions, θ) are tabulated in OBSERVATION.md. Intermediate motion follows the hand-set keyframe coordinates (one entry per frame), and the card angle is exactly linear in t.

6. Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz (uint32 0xRRGGBB, shape (144,256,20)), frames/00.png–19.png, inspection.png, and a standalone index.html delta player. transition_check.png (built by .tmp/make_transition_check.py) is a labelled 4-row montage of the entry, behind-edge descent, release, and withdrawal transitions.

Verification (all run after the final regeneration):

  • frames/00.png is byte-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every PNG decodes to exactly the corresponding slice of the uint32 tensor in pixels.npz;
  • all 20 frames are 256×144 RGB;
  • frames were visually reviewed with the image Read tool: the inspection.png sheet, the transition_check.png montage, and pixel-zoomed crops of the claw entry (f1–3), ball crossing the card top edge (f6–9), the release (f13–16), and withdrawal (f17–19).

Known limitations: at 256×144 the warped card edges and thin claw are only softened by light anti-aliasing (some pixel stepping is intrinsic to the resolution); the wall fill deliberately reproduces the smooth defocused wall and therefore lacks the faint large-scale mottling of real plaster; the released ball and the lowered claw below the card top are occluded by design and are not visible in frames 15–19.
