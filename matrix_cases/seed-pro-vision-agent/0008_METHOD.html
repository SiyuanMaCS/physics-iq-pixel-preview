ball-hits-duck · 生成方法

Event: the brown tennis ball rolls straight right out of the black pipe and hits the yellow rubber duck, which slides a little, tips clockwise and rocks into a near-rest against the ball.

Everything was produced with local Python pixel operations only (NumPy + Pillow, generate.py): clean-plate inpainting, analytic geometry/shading, sprite unmatting and 4× supersampled transforms. No external data or models.

1. Clean table plates

Two background plates are built from first_frame.png:

  • plate_static (frames 1–14): the original ball disk, its soft contact darkening and rightward cast-shadow ellipse are filled. The hole is the disk + two analytic ellipses, dilated once (3×3) to include the soft rim.
  • plate_post (frames 15–19): additionally fills the duck footprint (colour key mask dilated to cover its feathered edge) so the transformed duck can be re-composited.

Filling is per-row linear interpolation between the nearest clean neighbours on that row, which preserves the table's horizontal banding exactly; a small, horizontally smoothed grain residual from the clean x=100–160 strip is added (0.3 weight) plus σ≈1.1 seeded noise, and the fill is blended over a ~1.5 px Gaussian rim so no seam is visible. The pre-existing warm brown mark left of the duck is treated as a static table feature: it stays untouched while the duck is static and is physically covered by the ball and duck at and after contact (checked frame by frame).

Mask holes inside the duck (the black eye) are filled with a border-flood background labeller implemented in NumPy (Pillow 11.3's ImageDraw.floodfill was found to be a no-op in this environment).

2. Ball

  • Sphere shading is fixed in screen space (correct for a rotating sphere: shading depends on the surface normal, which is invariant under rotation). The base sprite is the original 26×26 patch with the seam band replaced by a smooth reference (per-radius ring medians + Gaussian blur).
  • The seam is an analytic light arc (5-point polyline, half-width ≈1.15 px) with alpha faded toward the silhouette by the modelled n_z. Per frame the seam mask is rotated about the ball centre by the pure-roll angle θ = Δx / 8.4 (clockwise for rightward motion) and re-lit at each destination from the local base shading (0.45·base + 0.55·(126,118,110)), so the seam never darkens the rim as it turns.
  • Disk alpha has a 1 px anti-aliased edge; sprite rotation/downsampling uses a 4× canvas with sub-pixel centring (Lanczos).
  • Shadow: two analytic Gaussian ellipses evaluated directly at 1× on small crops (a 4×-grid/Pillow mode-F resampling path was tried first but produced stray artefacts far from the shadow, so it was removed) — a contact ellipse (cx, cy+5.5), (8.0, 2.8), peak .15 and a rightward cast ellipse (cx+10.5, cy+1.5), (10.5, 4.3), peak .12, applied as neutral multiply. Before contact the shadow is masked out over the static duck footprint.

3. Motion

  • Frames 1–14: x(t) = 66 + 110.6·(t/15)^0.92 (gentle ease-out), constant y = 52.
  • Frame 15 (3.75 s): contact, ball centre 176.6 touching the beak; the duck gets a brief 4% vertical squash / 6% widen about its base pivot.
  • Frames 16–19: duck pose keyframes (translate, clockwise tip about base pivot 195,67): (4 px, 12°), (7 px, 24°), (9 px, 19°), (9 px, 13°) — impact, maximum tip, rock-back, settle. The ball centre each frame is measured from the transformed duck alpha (leftmost pixel in y=44–62 band − radius), so contact is maintained even as the beak swings; it ends at cx ≈ 184.8.
  • Frames 15–19 add a soft under-duck ground shadow; draw order is plate → shadows → ball → duck (duck hides the small overlap).

4. Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz (packed 0xRRGGBB uint32 tensor, H×W×20), frames/00..19.png, inspection.png and index.html. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png (9 key frames over amplified |prev→frame| diff maps with changed-pixel counts) and asserts:

  • frame 00 is byte-identical to first_frame.png;
  • every PNG equals the in-memory frame;
  • the packed uint32 tensor in pixels.npz equals all 20 frames.

Visual review was actually performed with image tools (enlarged sprite/plate debugs, key-frame stacks, full frames, inspection and transition sheets). Change footprints per transition stay inside the tabletop action corridor (x ≈ 51–241, y ≈ 40–82); the couch, pipe, table edges and everything else are static. Known simplifications: the seam is a single short analytic arc matched to the (blurry) original rather than a full tennis-ball seam curve, and the duck's post-impact motion is hand-tuned keyframes rather than a rigid-body solve.
