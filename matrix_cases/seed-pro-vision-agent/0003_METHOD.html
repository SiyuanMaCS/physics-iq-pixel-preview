METHOD.md

METHOD — case_0003

Two overhead grabbers release a brown tennis ball and an orange block, which fall onto two soft pillows. Locked-off camera, so every frame is a local pixel edit of frame 00. All work is local Python (numpy/scipy/PIL): no network, no external models or engines, no reference material beyond first_frame.png and prompt.txt. Frame 00 is emitted byte-for-byte unchanged; frames 01–19 are composited from the layers below.

1. Layer decomposition of frame 00

  • Wall fill behind moving elements. Vacated regions (both objects' tracks, the left blue finger's swept track, the right arch-tip splay) must be replaced by clean wall. Iterative normalized convolution was tried first but diverged (low-frequency snap-back over large holes blew out to white). The final fill (wall_plane_fill) fits, per unknown pixel, a weighted quadratic surface [1, x, y, x², y², xy] (6×3 least squares, gaussian weights, σ≈10 px, radius 22) to nearby clean wall samples only. Clean-wall samples are defined as bright (>95), nearly-neutral pixels, excluding the ceiling band (y<15), pillow band (y≥86), and boxes around both arms. Because dark claws/rail/objects never enter the fit, the fill is stable and preserves the wall's slight vignette curvature. A small gaussian grain field (σ 0.7, amplitude 1.3) is added inside filled zones so they are not smoother than the real wall. The removal mask is feathered (gaussian 0.6–1.4 px), so F0 blends into the fill instead of leaving a seam.
  • Ball sprite. Soft alpha = analytic disc coverage at measured center (52, 36), r = 10.6, gated by color distance from the filled wall, cut off at r+1.6 px.
  • Block sprite. Alpha from redness (R−G)+0.5(R−B) within a dilated support hull of the largest connected red core, smoothed 0.7 px; RGB crop bbox x165–183, y32–68, center (174, 49).
  • Pillows, rail, housings stay as pixels from frame 00; only a thin horizontal band of the pillow is ever warped/shaded.

2. Reconstructed object appearance

  • Analytic ball. In frame 00 the upper hemisphere is occluded by the dark/blue jaw, so the extracted pixels alone would show a bitten ball once it falls clear. The sphere is rebuilt analytically at the measured center/radius: surface normals from the disc, a dark core color A=(23,9,5), a Lambert term (key light upper-front) and a Fresnel rim term (light from lower-right). The two per-channel shading coefficients are fitted by least squares to the visible brown pixels of the real ball, so the analytic hemisphere matches its actual tone; a tangent-normal continuation of the same shading fills the anti-aliased fringe.
  • Block at rest. The extracted sprite carries a pinkish motion/AA halo and is slightly soft; while falling it is used as-is with speed-proportional vertical motion blur (multiple shifted copies, spread = |v|/26, capped at 4.5 px). On landing it crossfades over two frames into a crisp 8×-supersampled rounded 9×28 bar tilted +7.5° (sign chosen to match the measured lean: bottom edge left of the top), with an inset lighter face and a dim right-flank strip matching the measured sprite colors (104,15,3) / (137,24,4).
  • Grabber jaws are redrawn as 4×-supersampled quadratic-bezier strokes. The left arm to the rail and the dark lower prong are static; the blue upper finger lifts and retracts from its position over the ball toward the housing. The two orange right-arch tips splay outward ~3 px. Opening follows a smoothedstep over frames 01–06. The dark lower prong was deliberately not wall-erased (it tucks into the black housing); only the blue finger's swept bezier track (x<63, y16–40) is removed from the wall.

3. Physics

Screen y points down. Each object is ballistic with constant gravity (ball g=46, block g=48 px/s²), then meets a unilateral damped spring at its pillow surface: while the contact point is below the undeformed surface, force −K(y−eq) − 2ζ√K·v with K=180, ζ=0.35, equilibrium eq = surface − half-height + settle-indent (ball 3 px, block 2.5 px); the spring switches off when the object rebounds above the surface. Integration uses 8000 substeps over 5 s, sampled/interpolated at the 20 frame instants (dt=0.25 s). Results: block contacts at frame 6 (y 86.6→93.3, impact v≈60 px/s), ball at frame 7 (y 87.8→94.1, v≈69 px/s); each makes one ~2 px rebound and is effectively at rest by frame 10–11.

4. Pillow reaction

For each contact, pillow_layer adds, restricted to a gaussian footprint at the contact x (σx≈13 px) around the measured surface level:

  • Dent: the frame-00 pillow band is vertically remapped (map_coordinates) by a gaussian downward displacement plus a raised-rim term, depth driven by the spring penetration (0–8 px). Warp/shade is applied only at displaced pixels, so untouched pillow pixels remain bit-identical to frame 00.
  • Contact shadow: an elliptical dark gaussian (σx 11, σy 5) whose opacity fades in over the last 9 px of the object's fall (proximity fade) and is full once touching, plus a thin crease darkening inside the dent.
  • Ripple: one expanding elliptical ring (radius 6→34 px) with amplitude 0.10·exp(−age/2.2), gone by age 9.
  • After age 6 the dent depth is quantized to 0.25 px and after age 9 ripples are forced to zero, so frames 16–19 are exactly static.

5. Compositing and outputs

Per frame f≥1: filled-wall base → warped/shaded pillow band (only where the pillow lives) → ball layer (extracted alpha + analytic colors, translated, motion-blurred) → block layer (sprite/crisp crossfade, translated, blurred) → jaw layers on top (fingers overlay objects while close). Compositing is unpremultiplied float alpha; results clip to uint8. Frame 00 is the untouched input.

artifact_io.save writes the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor (144×256×20) to pixels.npz, frames/00–19.png, the 8-frame inspection.png sheet, and the standalone index.html player. transition_check.png is a separate diagnostic montage of selected frame pairs with their absolute-difference masks. Temporary crops/montages live in .tmp/.

6. Honest limitations

  • This is 2.5-D illusion, not simulated optics: shading is a fitted analytic model and the pillow is a displaced 2-D image, so contact silhouettes are plausible rather than physically derived.
  • The tennis ball has no visible fuzz texture or seams; the original disc in frame 00 is a nearly uniform dark brown rim-lit sphere and the rebuild matches that appearance.
  • Horizontal positions are fixed (straight vertical drops, as prompted); only jaws rotate.
  • Wall texture in filled zones is synthesized grain; long stares at the vacated tracks can show it differs subtly from the original sensor noise.
