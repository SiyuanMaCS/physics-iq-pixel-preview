METHOD — case_0043

Two blue-black grabbers pull a twisted green paper strip from opposite corners until it tears. Everything is generated by local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png only (NumPy / OpenCV / SciPy / Pillow); no external models, inpainting engines, network access, or other frames are used. python3 generate.py (~20 s, one CPU core) writes all 20 frames, pixels.npz, inspection.png, transition_check.png and index.html through the supplied artifact_io.save.

1. Segmentation from frame 0

Colour thresholds on the 256×144 photo define:

  • green paper G>110, G-R>40, G-B>40;
  • wall (pale blue-white upper-left triangle) B>R+12, R>100;
  • tools per ROI (tool_classify): confident blue plastic, near-black housing, and silver screws; connected mid-grey pixels that touch a confident component are grown in (recovers anti-aliased edges). The green mask is subtracted so the paper stays visible through each pinch. The table-coloured interior of the jaw loop fails all tests, so the oval handle hole stays transparent.

ROIs: left tool x0–82, y20–64; right tool x178–256, y0–38.

2. Static table plate (revealed background)

When objects move away, real table has to be painted in behind them.

  1. A rough σ30 normalized convolution over pixels ≥4 px from objects gives a smooth local colour estimate; pixels within 20 px of an object that are

    7/255 darker than it are labelled contact shadow.

  2. A trust mask excludes wall and a 2 px dilation of objects+shadows. The plate colour base is a cascade of normalized convolutions with σ = 9, 14, 22, 30 (each stage reintroduces trusted raw pixels), which propagates the slowly varying wood tone under the occluded footprint without the halo of a single large blur.
  3. Grain: on clean table pixels the residual of frame 0 against a gaussian(5) has per-channel std ≈ (3.8, 3.4, 3.3). Three layers of channel-correlated gaussian noise are anisotropically blurred ((.75,1.2), .5·(1.4,2.2), .18·(2.0,7.0) px — fine near-vertical wood streaks plus a faint long tail), whitened on the clean region, coloured by the residual Cholesky covariance, and rescaled so their gaussian(5) residual matches the measured stds exactly.
  4. The plate is valid only inside a 4 px dilation of the object+shadow footprint (softened σ2.2); outside that it is irrelevant because frame-0 pixels are kept there.

Reveal alpha (flicker control). The plate is never shown under currently present objects. Per frame, covered is a dilation of the warped paper mask (4 px) and tool masks (3 px); the reveal alpha ra = blur(plate_zone & ~covered, σ1.3). The canvas starts as f0·(1−ra) + PLATE·ra, so untouched background is the byte-identical photographic pixel (the lower-right table region changes by exactly 0 between frames 00 and 01), and plate texture appears only where an object has actually left.

3. Grabber sprites

Each tool's frame-0 RGB crop is stored with a soft alpha (gaussian blur σ1 of the hard mask, zeroed outside its 1 px dilation, which keeps edges crisp while anti-aliasing them). Per frame the sprite is translated sub-pixel with warpAffine by the same grip delta used for the paper mesh, so the pinched paper end and the jaw never separate. Source anchor/pinch points: grip L = (73,47), grip R = (203,4).

4. Paper source mesh

From the green mask, per-column top/bottom silhouette edges are extracted, gap-filled, median- and gaussian-filtered, and resampled by arc length into a ribbon parameterization (u along the strip 0→1, v ∈ [−1,1] across it), NU×NV = 222×45 stations, plus two zero-width grip stations exactly at the pinch points (u just outside 0 and 1). The latter anchor the mesh at (73,47)/(203,4) even though the green silhouette ends a few pixels inside the jaw sprites; texture at those stations lands on jaw-coloured pixels with alpha 0, so only the jaw sprite is visible there.

Source coordinates (SX,SY) point at the frame-0 texture; frame 00 itself is never warped (see §8).

5. Animation (20 frames @ 4 fps)

Hand-tuned keyframes in generate.py encode one continuous event:

  • Grip tracks pull along the measured handle axes (left toward lower-left, right toward upper-right); the left jaw ends at (26.5,39), the right at (240.5,7).
  • Tension τ ramps 0 → 1 over frames 01–11. sheet_target(τ) builds the pre-tear target mesh by linear interpolation of each station toward the moving grip chord: the sagging diamond's two bulges (gaussian bumps at u≈.35 and .72) collapse onto the chord, the face-on diamond narrows (×0.38 at u≈.35), and the edge-on twist waist widens (×1.70 at u≈.69) — the untwist. Cross-station y coordinates scale by the same local factor, so the warp is fold-free and exactly reproduces the source mesh at τ=0.
  • Tear: a jagged cut line in paper coordinates, u_tear(v) = .685 + .020·sin(5.5v+1.2) + .010·sin(13v+.4). A slit opens from the lower edge: progress 10 % at f09 (notch), 45 % f10, 80 % f11 (pixels straddling the line are alpha-cut, width growing slightly with progress), and full separation at f12.
  • Post-tear recoil (f12–f19). Each half is its own mesh from grip station to the jagged tear edge, blending the taut f11 shape with the frame-0 grip-relative rest shape by rel (.60→.90), then: torn-edge contraction sc (.97→.93, grip end fixed), small rotation about the grip (left half −5°,−8°,−4°,+1°,+3°,+4°,+3°,+2°; right half −2°,−4°,−2°,−1° then 0), and a released-twist flutter maximal on the torn edge that decays 1.6→0 px. This reads as an elastic snap that settles while both pieces remain clamped in their jaws.

6. Warping and shading

warp_paper fits three LinearNDInterpolators (target xy → source x, source y, u) on the mesh stations and uses them with cv2.remap (bilinear) to sample the frame-0 paper texture at every output pixel. Output alpha is the same inverse-map applied to the σ0.8-blurred green mask (cutoff .12), preserving the original photographic edge anti-aliasing and silhouette irregularities instead of a geometric outline.

Tension shading: dark fold/underside greens (G<125, B<85) are brightened as τ rises, concentrated at the waist (u≈.70) and the crease (u≈.44), up to +30 %, modelling the twist opening toward the light; it fades back out as the torn halves relax (shade_tau = 1−rel).

Torn edges (the v-row nearest the cut, edge weight >.96) receive a thin darkened rim (up to 16 %) and ~0.2 % of those pixels get a pale-fibre tint for a ragged paper edge.

7. Compositing per frame

  1. tool sprites positioned (needed for coverage);
  2. paper target mesh built — one warped sheet with optional slit (f00–11) or two half-sheets (f12–19);
  3. reveal alpha from current coverage → plate blended in;
  4. analytic paper shadow: mask shifted (4,7) px, blurred σ5, multiply 1−.62·(1−.70)·m, applied only through the reveal alpha, so the photographic frame-0 shadows survive wherever objects have not moved;
  5. paper composite (left half, then right half over it);
  6. tool contact shadow (shift (3,4), σ3.5, strength .45), likewise reveal-masked;
  7. tool sprites over the pinched paper ends.

  • frames[0] = F0 by assignment after rendering, so frames/00.png is a byte-exact copy of first_frame.png (verified with cmp).

Known limitations

  • Physics is hand-keyed kinematics (tracks, τ, relaxation, flutter), not a simulation; timing and amplitudes were chosen by eye against the prompt.
  • The revealed table colour and grain are statistical matches to the visible table, not recovered true occluded texture; only one small knot region and slowly varying tone are modelled.
  • Paper texture is reused from frame 0 via the warp, so surface markings deform with the mesh; tear-edge fibres are procedural sparse pixels.
  • Shadows are analytic offset-blur multipliers rather than geometry-derived, and appear only on revealed plate regions.
