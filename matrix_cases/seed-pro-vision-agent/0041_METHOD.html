METHOD.md

METHOD

How generate.py produces the 20-frame clip (256×144, 4 fps) from the single input first_frame.png. Everything is deterministic local pixel maths (NumPy / OpenCV / Pillow; RNG seed 41) — no external assets, models or network.

1. Scene interpretation

Frame 00 shows one tangerine half, cut face up, on a frosted-glass board over a warm wood table. A narrow paring blade already runs through the centre of the fruit, tip on the board below it. The prompt ("the knife slices through the tangerine") is realised as one continuous cut on a locked-off camera:

  1. press (f00→f08, 0–2.0 s): the blade advances ~16 px along its own axis, finishing the stroke while the two quarters are pushed apart;
  2. hold (f08→f12, 2.0–3.0 s): blade fully through, quarters splayed;
  3. withdraw (f12→f19, 3.0–4.75 s): blade pulled straight back up out of the fruit with a short motion-blur trail, quarters relax ~10% (spring-back) and stay separated by a visible kerf.

2. Geometry measured from frame 00

  • Blade centre (bright steel face): line x(y) = 133.0 − 0.120·y (CX0, SLOPE); it leans ~7° with its top to the right.
  • Blade screen motion for parameter d is (−SLOPE·d, +d), i.e. along the line itself, so the projected blade line is invariant: the columns through the fruit never slide sideways, only the tip advances/retreats (tip y = 118 + d).
  • Cut pivot PIVOT = (123, 85) — the pointed bottom of the heart-shaped half.
  • Dynamic pixels are confined to ROI (88, 0, 168, 122); outside it every generated frame keeps the exact frame-00 pixels.

3. Fruit segmentation

orange is a colour predicate on the frame-00 RGB (R>145, G 55–200, B<120, R−B>70, R−G>25). Connected components in the expected size/position range are selected, and each component row is filled between its extents so that pale pith and the dark cut slit inside the silhouette belong to the fruit mask. A 1 px warm-peel rim ((R−B)>30, dilate-minus-mask) is added around the edge.

The mask is split on the blade line into two quarters (xx < cxline−1.0 left, xx > cxline+0.5 right). Orange flesh within ~2 px of the kerf is darkened 14–20% (piece_rgb wall tint) so each piece carries its own brownish cut-wall tissue; because the quarters (not a fixed overlay) carry this tint, the tissue edges move with the pieces as the kerf opens.

4. Background plate

A mask covering the fruit (7 px dilation) and the blade corridor (|x − cxline| < 2.4, widened above the fruit) is reconstructed with cv2.inpaint(..., INPAINT_TELEA), giving a clean frosted-board plate. To keep the repair from looking smooth, two seeded noise fields are added only inside the repaired mask: fine Gaussian speckle matched to measured per-channel std (~[3.2, 4.0, 5.4]) and weak vertical-streak grain (~[2.0, 2.4, 3.0]). A soft blurred ellipse under the fruit (alpha 0.10) restores a faint contact shadow, and a thin blurred dark line along the blade path (gap_shade, alpha 0.16) is painted on the plate so that, once the blade leaves a row, a slit-like shadow remains between the quarters instead of bare flat board.

5. Quarter layers

Each quarter is rendered as an RGBA layer (RGB = tinted frame-00 fruit, alpha = 3×3-Gaussian-blurred mask, for a 1 px anti-aliased edge). The layer is warped with a single affine built analytically as rotate about PIVOT, then translate (inverse map for warpAffine):

  • left: angle −3.0°·e, translation (−1.7·e, 0);
  • right: angle +3.0°·e, translation (+1.7·e, 0);

with e = smoothstep press progress (0 at f00, 1 at f08), held at 1 to f15, then relaxed to 0.90 by f19 (spring-back). The kerf opens to ~8–9 px at the top of the fruit and ~3 px at the bottom point.

6. Analytic blade

The blade is not a cut-out of frame 00 (its tip is half-buried in the fruit and it needs to translate); it is rebuilt as an analytic coverage strip. For every row, perpendicular coordinate u around the centre line is sampled at the 5 relevant columns:

  • dark cutting edge for u ∈ [−2.05, −0.7] (coverage ramps 0.4 px wide),
  • bluish steel face for u ∈ [−0.3, +0.95], with a short dark→steel blend;
  • below y = 102 the strip width scales linearly to zero by y = 118 (tip taper), collapsing toward the cutting edge.

Colours are piecewise constants sampled from the frame-00 blade (dark edge ~[80,82,100]→[98,104,126] near the tip; steel ~[142,134,140]→[160,161,174], plus a faint specular bump around y 94–110). The strip is stored as RGB + coverage and translated by the same warpAffine used for the quarters.

7. Motion, blur and the frame-00 seam

blade_shift(t): d = 16·smoothstep(t/8) for t ≤ 8; 16 through t = 12; then 16 − 93·smoothstep((t−12)/7), reaching d = −77 at f19 (tip y ≈ 41).

Per-frame compositing order is:

  1. plate + contact shadow + gap shade;
  2. left and right quarter layers (their cut walls travel with them);
  3. withdrawal ghost: when |Δd| > 2.5 px, one copy of the blade from 5 px behind composited at alpha 0.22 — a single faint exposure, not a broad translucent streak;
  4. the crisp blade at its current position.

For f01–f02 the composite is smoothstep-blended with the untouched frame-00 image (f01 29.6%, f02 70.4% composite weight) so the first sub-pixel/kerf changes do not pop. Frame 00 itself is never rendered: it is the decoded first_frame.png, and the script asserts byte/array equality.

8. Outputs and verification

artifact_io.save asserts uint8 (20,144,256,3), packs each pixel to 0xRRGGBB in a (144,256,20) uint32 tensor (pixels.npz), writes frames/00.png…19.png, inspection.png and index.html. A separate reload check (pixels vs every decoded PNG, and frames/00.png bytes vs first_frame.png) passes for all 20 frames. Changed-pixel counts per transition match the intended rhythm: ramp through the press, zero changed pixels f08→f12 (hold), a burst at the fast withdrawal, tapering to 316 px at f18→f19. Frames were reviewed at 6× zoom around the blade/fruit corridor while tuning strip width, the ghost and the seam.

9. Known simplifications

  • The board inpainting is statistical (matched grain), not a copy of hidden real texture; lighting/highlights elsewhere are static (locked-off shot).
  • The blade is rebuilt from sampled colours rather than reusing frame-00 pixels, so its exact sub-pixel shading is approximate.
  • Quarters are rigid pieces; only the small spring-back pose change is modelled — no juice, deformation or peel flex beyond rotation/translation.
