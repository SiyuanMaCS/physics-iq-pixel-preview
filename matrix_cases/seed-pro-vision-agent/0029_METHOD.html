Rolling is rendered by rotating each disk by the no-slip angle θ = Δx / 7.5 rad; the table-residual layers are not rotated (they live on the table).

4. Compositing

  • Shadows first, then disks. A translated table layer is added to the canvas as canvas += a·L (multiplied by the table-edge gate and, for brown/orange near contact, by a wedge mask at floor coordinates x153–161, y52–63 that fades out as the pair separates). Disks are alpha-over composited with bilinear sub-pixel sampling at integer/sub-pixel centre positions.
  • Rolling motion blur. A moving disk leaves a short trail: disk samples at 0.4 px steps behind the centre, each rotated by its own no-slip angle, are accumulated front-to-back into a single coverage/colour pair and composited once. Accumulation (rather than stamping separate ghosts) makes overlapping samples merge into one smooth smear. Trail length is proportional to speed (grey max 2.5 px at 22 px/s — frame 0 shows the felt edge is soft but carries no long motion smear, so the trail stays well under one ball radius) and fades to zero over 0.5 s after impact.
  • Pre-impact frames (1–5). These start from a copy of frame 0 so the static brown and orange balls stay pixel-identical. Only the union of the grey ball's old/new blurred footprint, plus the corridor it vacates (y 43–66, x 104–136), is erased to the table model; the brown streak is then repainted (with its left taper), the grey table layer translated to the new centre, and the moving grey disk with its trail composited. The static brown/orange disks are explicitly excluded from the erase mask.
  • Post-impact frames (6–19) start from the inpainted base and composite all three translated table layers and rotated disks.

5. Outputs and verification

generate.py writes pixels.npz, frames/00.png–19.png, inspection.png, transition_check.png, and index.html through artifact_io.save; the tensor is uint32 0xRRGGBB, shape (144, 256, 20). Checks performed:

  • frames/00.png is byte-equal to first_frame.png; tensor frame 0 equals the packed RGB of frame 0.
  • Pre-impact frames preserve the untouched static balls outside the grey corridor.
  • Visual review used the image reader on transition_check.png, inspection.png, and zoomed diagnostics of the pre-impact roll, the impact frames, and the late frames near the table edge; iterative fixes included reattaching grey's contact knot to its sprite, switching shadows from multiplicative darkening to additive residual layers (to keep the faint warm streak), feathering brown's revealed streak end, and replacing discrete ghost disks with a single accumulated motion-blur layer.
