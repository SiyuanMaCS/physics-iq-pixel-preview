ball-hits-duck · 生成方法

Task: given only first_frame.png (256×144) and prompt.txt, generate 20 locked-off frames at 4 fps (5.0 s) of the brown tennis ball rolling out of the left-hand black pipe and knocking over the yellow rubber duck. Everything below is deterministic local Python pixel math (NumPy / SciPy / Pillow) in generate.py; no network, external models, engines, references, or subagents were used. Measurements and keyframes are documented in OBSERVATION.md.

1. Clean table background B

Two rectangular "holes" mark everything that must be removable:

  • ball + rightward motion smear: x38–74, y39–55;
  • duck + its up-left cast/contact shadow: x146–202, y46–82.

A table polygon (piecewise-linear back/front/right edges, with the black muzzle region x<37/y<56 excluded) defines pixels that are certainly clean table. All other pixels (couch, floor, pipe) are never used as fill samples.

The fill is built in two parts:

  1. Slow trend — for each row, a quadratic in x is least-squares fitted to the RGB of clean table pixels, capturing the gentle brightness/colour ramp across the tabletop.
  2. Real grain — for each contiguous hole run on a row, the nearest same-width interval of fully clean pixels on the same row is found by search (the wood grain is horizontal), and the hole is filled with trend(x) + [f0(x+shift) − trend(x+shift)]: a genuine strip of table texture corrected for the local trend. The outer 5 px of every run are blended toward the mirrored real neighbours (weight 1.0 at the edge) so the seam is continuous, and the compositing stage adds a ~1.15 px Gaussian feather on top. Where a mirrored neighbour is the pipe, that side is left unanchored.

B equals f0 everywhere outside the holes (asserted in code), and all values are asserted finite. Residual seam gradients across the hole edges were measured afterwards and are at or below the table's natural grain gradient (.tmp/verify-style numeric checks during development).

2. Layers extracted from frame 0

Ball. Coverage alpha inside an inbox (x36–78, y37–57) is estimated as clip((luminance(B) − luminance(f0)) / (0.60·luminance(B))), i.e. how much the frame-0 optical blend darkens the clean table; blurred σ0.45 and hard-cropped to x39–72/y≤54 to keep pipe and table pixels out. The stamped RGB is the raw frame-0 pixel, unpremultiplied by its alpha at stamp time, so the moving ball is optically identical to the frame-0 ball (including its fuzzy rim and the embedded shadow/smear tones).

Duck. A yellow colour score min(R−B, G−B) gives a soft alpha, restricted to the duck's bounding box; dark eye/shaded-edge pixels are re-admitted next to strong body pixels, the map is blurred σ0.6, and only the largest connected component is kept. The coverage is then made a crisp binary interior (morphological closing of alpha>0.22) surrounded by one anti-aliased ring (Gaussian σ0.55, capped at 0.9). Ring colour is not taken from the photo edge (which is optically blended with table/shadow); instead each fringe pixel gets a σ1.0 Gaussian average of nearby interior colours, so rigid rotation produces a proper yellow→table anti-alias edge instead of dragging a pale table-coloured rim around the toppling sprite.

Erase mask per frame. A hard mask is the union of dilated alphas of everything that must disappear from frame 0 (frame-0 ball, and — only after impact — frame-0 duck, its extracted shadow, and the rotated duck/shadow ellipse footprints), Gaussian-feathered σ1.15. The canvas starts as f0·(1−erase) + B·erase, so untouched areas remain byte-identical to frame 0.

3. Animation (20 keyframes, table in generate.py)

  • Frames 00–10 (roll): the ball layer is sub-pixel shifted with bilinear interpolation along the measured straight path, +10.4 px/frame in x and +1.34 px/frame in y (the projected pipe axis, slope 0.129). Frame 00 is the untouched first_frame.png (asserted pixel-identical). The duck is not re-stamped at all before impact, so it stays exactly the frame-0 photograph; frame 10 is first contact (ball right edge reaches the beak).
  • Frames 11–16 (impact and topple): the ball slows from +10.4 px to stopped over frames 11–15 and its rightward motion-smear alpha fades (0.4 at f11, gone at f12). The duck is a rigid 2D screen-clockwise rotation of the premultiplied duck layer about pivot (174,72): 7°→24°→45°→66°→88°→100°, with a 0→7 px rightward slide of the base. Rotation is one shared bilinear kernel (scipy.ndimage.affine_transform) on float premultiplied RGBA about the explicit pivot; premultiplication keeps transparent pixels from fringing the sprite, and float math avoids the >255 edge-colour inflation that an 8-bit PIL rotation produced in an earlier revision.
  • Frames 17–19 (settle): slight rotational overshoot settles 100°→96°→95°→94°, ball and base otherwise stationary.
  • Shadows after impact: two soft Gaussian-edged ellipses, applied as warm 0.80 multiply: the up-left blob inherited from frame 0 fades out as the duck tips (strength 0.80→0.06), while a compact contact shadow under the pivot grows (0.06→0.32) and lengthens as the duck lies flat.

Layering order per post-impact frame: cleaned table → contact shadows → ball → rotated duck.

4. Output and verification

artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz (uint32 (R<<16)|(G<<8)|B, shape 144×256×20), frames/00.png–19.png, the 8-frame inspection.png sheet, and index.html (standalone player built from frame 0 + run-length frame deltas). generate.py also writes transition_check.png (amplified frame-to-frame differences for seven slot pairs).

Independent verification (.tmp/verify.py) asserts:

  • tensor is 144×256×20 uint32 within 0x000000–0xFFFFFF;
  • every PNG is 256×144 RGB and pixel-identical to its tensor slice (0 mismatching pixels across all 20 frames);
  • frames/00.png is pixel-identical to first_frame.png;
  • the base64 payload embedded in index.html reconstructs all 20 frames identical to the tensor;
  • every inter-frame transition changes some pixels (no frozen frames).

Visual review used the image Read tool on: labeled-grid/sample crops during measurement, per-frame zoom strips of the ball roll and the duck topple, amplified difference maps (local vs full-frame), the clean-background fill crops, the inspection sheet, and the transition sheet. Revisions replaced successive background fills (row interpolation → residual streaming → trend + same-row real-texture strip copy), shrank the ball hole to the real coverage, recoloured the duck anti-alias fringe from interior pixels, and replaced PIL's premultiplied rotation with a float affine rotation.

Honest limitations

  • The topple is a 2D rigid rotation/slide of a sprite cut from the one photograph, not a simulated 3D deformation: perspective change, the wing/tail motion a real rubber duck would show, and squash are absent.
  • The ball is a translated copy of the frame-0 ball pixels; its surface pattern does not spin and the frame-0 smear fades rather than re-forming.
  • Shadows during the topple are hand-shaped soft ellipses matched to the frame-0 shadow's colour and direction, not re-solved physical lighting.
