METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is a deterministic local Python pixel animation based on the supplied photograph. It uses NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy interpolation, with no network, external references, AI model, external rendering engine, or subagents. The source photograph supplies the camera view, objects, shadows, reflections, and liquid texture. It is an analytic approximation of pouring rather than a full fluid simulation.

The liquid region is described by a tilted elliptical surface and the slightly tapered sides of the drinking glass. Donor pixels from the existing grapefruit juice move upward as the surface rises; the bottom stays anchored. A mild RGB adjustment approximates dilution, and a soft, time-varying plume below the impact point suggests mixing. The surface rises by 0.5 pixel per sampled interval, totaling 9.5 pixels. A very small donor-pixel warp lowers the estimated dispenser waterline by 1.7 pixels, using an approximate tank-to-glass area ratio of 5.6. Measurements and volume ratios are image-based estimates, not calibrated quantities.

A narrow, nearly vertical stream starts at the faucet outlet. Its width decreases gently with fall distance. Local source-pixel refraction, a dark edge, and fractional-pixel specular highlights make the stream translucent. Analytic elliptical ripples, a small impact depression, and a few tiny bubbles move continuously with time. Foreground glass reflections and the near rim remain fixed and occlude the liquid and jet appropriately. The scene has no added people, captions, camera motion, or overflow.

Rendering uses a 4-times sampling grid for subpixel liquid boundaries and stream highlights. Signed changes relative to the supersampled source are averaged over each pixel, then added to the untouched original RGB values. This preserves the original photographic texture in all unaffected pixels. Frame 00 bypasses rendering and copies the source RGB array exactly. Frames 01–19 sample t=0.25 through 4.75 seconds. The 20-frame sequence plays for five seconds at four frames per second.

artifact_io.py writes the PNG sequence, the packed pixel tensor, an inspection contact sheet, and a standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally writes transition_check.png and verification.json. The tensor has shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, and values encoded as 0xRRGGBB. Every PNG is decoded and compared exactly with its corresponding tensor slice. The HTML's first frame and delta packets are independently decoded and compared with all 20 tensor frames.
