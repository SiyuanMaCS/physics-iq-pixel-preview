METHOD.md

Method and delivered artifacts

This animation was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations using the supplied first_frame.png and the action in prompt.txt. No network, other scene, external model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. The initial image was provided visually by the user. Generated outputs were inspected only through local code and numeric checks; no image Read/View tool was used and no visual review of generated frames is claimed.

Pixel construction

generate.py preserves the exact source image as frame 00. It reconstructs the small wall area behind the starting enclosure and hook with per-row quadratic interpolation of nearby source wall pixels. A faint texture residual from another part of the same source wall is added to avoid a completely flat reconstruction.

The original dark ring, connecting stem, and top plate are extracted into a premultiplied alpha layer. The acrylic is extracted as a per-channel transmission gain relative to the reconstructed wall. This optical layer darkens and tints the actual scene at the new enclosure position, letting the original ball, smiley, and wooden grain show through the moving enclosure. The original wall itself is not pasted into the enclosure at its destination. Small analytic corner highlights make the acrylic edges readable where they cross the darker tabletop.

The hook, plate, and enclosure translate down by the same displacement, without horizontal movement or resizing. A dark orange-brown cord extends between the original fixed upper cord and the moving hook; the source cord is nearly black under the original lighting. NumPy and OpenCV perform compositing and subpixel bilinear translations. Pillow writes the PNG artifacts, and SciPy is used only for local pixel texture filtering.

The event schedule was written in OBSERVATION.md before generator code. Motion accelerates gently, descends at about 2.4 pixels per frame in the middle, and slows to a 31-pixel final displacement. The final top plate starts at y=75, above the ball's y=91 top. The lower front edge reaches y=113, around the ball's y=112 bottom. A faint, bounded analytic shadow strengthens on the tabletop as the enclosure approaches its resting height. The original ball and its contact shadow remain in place. Frames 18 and 19 are identical.

This is a source-image, two-dimensional pixel-compositing method. It approximates transparency and reflections without a reconstructed three-dimensional scene or ray tracing.

Numeric verification and revision

The first numeric run detected a two-level RGB change outside the intended setup from the tail of the analytic shadow. The shadow was bounded to the setup region and the final artifacts were regenerated. The completed verification establishes:

  • Exactly 20 RGB frames, each 256×144, played at 4 fps for five seconds.
  • Frame 00 equals the input RGB pixels exactly.
  • Every PNG matches its packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor plane. pixels.npz contains the pixels array with axes (height, width, time) and shape (144,256,20).
  • All twenty independently decoded standalone-player frames match the tensor; its timer interval is 250 milliseconds.
  • Pixels outside the localized setup region are unchanged, with maximum absolute channel error zero.

  • Adjacent-frame mean absolute RGB differences range from 0 to 0.874 on the 0–255 scale, with the expected acceleration, steady descent, deceleration, and final hold.
