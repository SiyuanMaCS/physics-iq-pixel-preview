cut-paper · 生成方法

This is a deterministic animation made with local Python pixel operations on the supplied photograph. It uses NumPy, OpenCV, SciPy, and Pillow. No network, external images, pretrained models, simulation engines, or subagents were used. The supplied first frame was visible in the task. Later outputs were inspected only with code and numeric checks; no visual review of generated frames is claimed.

Construction

The original green paper is identified by green chroma, producing one 4,788-pixel component. Spatial envelopes and dark/blue color tests identify the two grabbers. Fractional edge alpha is estimated from foreground/background color contrast. Interior photograph colors are retained, and edge colors are unmixed to reduce background fringes during movement.

Newly exposed tabletop is reconstructed from this same photograph. A quadratic color field estimates the table's lighting from visible, unoccluded wood. Fine grain is transported from the clear lower tabletop along the approximate grain direction. Local Telea inpainting provides boundary continuity, blending into the transported texture in the deeper hidden areas. Reconstructed regions therefore approximate the wood hidden in the input.

Both grabbers undergo rigid, opposing translations with eased acceleration. Each grip moves about 12.25 pixels by the final frame, with a small vertical component along the initial paper span. The photographed green sheet undergoes an analytic, spatially varying deformation: initial slack flattens, then an irregular material-space crack advances from its exposed lower edge through the narrow curved section. Two complementary ownership masks use exactly the same crack boundary. They share displacement ahead of the crack and relax toward their respective grips behind it. This keeps the remaining ligament connected until the complete break at frame 12.

The tear edge has subpixel irregularities and a thin pale green fiber highlight. The separated pieces retain their original surface texture and remain at their respective clamps. Damped torsion gives a small recoil after rupture. This is an authored analytic deformation, rather than a full material fracture simulation.

Moving paper and tools are inverse-warped and composited at four times output resolution, with soft, translated shadows. Only moving layers are supersampled; stationary background pixels are replicated so reduction preserves them exactly. Frame 00 is replaced with the original RGB pixels and its original PNG file bytes.

Outputs and playback

pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 tensor of shape (144, 256, 20). Every element stores 0xRRGGBB; the top eight bits are zero. The 20 RGB PNG frames are frames/00.png through frames/19.png, each 256 × 144. Playback is 4 fps, giving a five-second sequence. index.html is standalone and embeds the pixel data, with play, pause, replay, seek, and loop controls. inspection.png contains eight selected frames. transition_check.png contains six adjacent-frame comparisons and numeric difference heatmaps. Both sheets were generated without opening them in an image viewer.

Numeric validation

  • All 20 PNG arrays exactly match both the generated RGB arrays and their uint32 tensor slices.
  • The first frame exactly matches the input RGB array; the PNG also preserves the input file bytes.
  • Independently decoding every embedded player delta exactly reproduces all 20 tensor frames.
  • Paper alpha has one substantial connected component through frame 11 and two from frame 12 onward.
  • The final green pieces have a minimum pixel-center separation of 21.84 pixels.
  • Distant tabletop regions have a maximum RGB change of zero across the full sequence.
  • Adjacent-frame mean absolute RGB differences range from 0.077 to 1.178 on the 0–255 channel scale. The final motion decays smoothly.
  • Additional numeric deformation checks at frames 1, 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, and 19 found positive Jacobian determinants for both paper pieces, with the lowest about 0.84; no inverted material patches were found in those checks.

The per-frame component sizes, centers, crack progress, tool displacement, and image differences are recorded in verification.json. Numeric checks establish artifact consistency, fixed framing, connectivity, and separation; they do not establish a visual realism assessment.
