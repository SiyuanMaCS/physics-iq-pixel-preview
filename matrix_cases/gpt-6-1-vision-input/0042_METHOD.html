This is a deterministic, source-image-based 2D pixel animation made in local Python with NumPy, Pillow, SciPy and OpenCV. It uses the supplied first_frame.png and prompt.txt as its scene inputs. No network, external reference, pretrained model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. The supplied helper only packages the generated pixels and builds the player.

The knife and fruit silhouettes were authored from the attached initial image and numeric RGB boundary measurements. The knife keeps the real steel colors, reflections and indentations. Subpixel affine translations move it approximately 4.6 pixels toward the tip and 10.6 pixels downward during the stroke, followed by a 1.5-pixel horizontal and 3.1-pixel upward release. Its initially cropped upper portion is extended from the source's top steel row along the blade axis; this keeps the blade entering from the top frame boundary throughout its motion.

The fruit is split into back and foreground layers along the curved boundary already occluding the blade. The foreground part gradually translates 4.8 pixels right and 3.7 pixels downward and rotates 1.6 degrees; the back part shifts less than one pixel. Small temporary vertical compression models yielding under the blade. Smooth cubic timing ramps control the cut, separation and release. The last three samples hold the completed cut. This is a prescribed, physically motivated motion, rather than a 3D simulation.

Visible fruit pixels are copied from the photograph. Initially hidden fruit pixels use local radial pulp shading, fine deterministic grain, pale pith and thin segment membranes. The hidden blade beneath the foreground fruit uses steel sampled along the source blade's axis. Newly uncovered board beneath the fruit is locally inpainted. Board and tabletop beneath the long knife use a source copy displaced parallel to the glass board's upper edge, preserving the boundary's direction. Low, soft contact shadows are composited under the fruit. A short initial correction removes matte rounding discrepancies; frame 00 is explicitly assigned the original RGB pixels. Pixels outside a fixed local action region are restored exactly from the source.
