The animation uses only the supplied first frame and the prompt. All generation is local Python with NumPy, SciPy, OpenCV pixel processing, and Pillow. No network, external references, AI models, external engines, or subagents were used. The attached initial frame provided the visual observation. Generated frames and the two contact sheets were inspected only through local code and numeric measurements; no image Read/View tool was used for later outputs.

The static scene is retained from the photograph. A hand-measured polygon and luminance segmentation isolate the porcelain figure, including its internal texture and soft edges. Missing wall pixels are recovered with row-wise quadratic interpolation of the fixed spotlight from unobstructed wall pixels on either side, followed by slight smoothing. Tabletop pixels obscured by the initial statue are recovered with local OpenCV Telea inpainting. The initial wall-shadow matte is estimated from the difference between photographed wall luminance and recovered wall illumination; statue pixels are treated as occluders while estimating that shadow. Source colors preserve its dark brown-black appearance and soft penumbra.

The rotation is a 2.5D image-based approximation. Smoothed silhouette center and radius profiles define a shallow analytic relief for each horizontal row. Texture coordinates turn about the fixed x=146 vertical axis. The apparent cross-section width is computed as sqrt(cos²(angle) + 0.64² sin²(angle)), and a parabolic front relief provides differential motion of surface details. The maps remain monotonic to avoid folded pixels. Premultiplied-alpha resampling prevents dark background colors from bleeding into porcelain edges. A small continuous shading adjustment represents changing surface orientation under the fixed spotlight. The source-calibrated shadow relief follows the same rotation angle and remains clipped to the wall, with reduced displacement near its lower attachment. This approximates the cast-shadow response; it is not a reconstructed full 3D statue or an independently ray-traced shadow. Unseen side detail comes from transformed source pixels.

The circular top of the black disk is represented by a perspective ellipse centered at (146, 131), with radii 27 and 8.5 pixels. Its reconstructed local texture turns in normalized tabletop coordinates. Positive angles are clockwise when viewed from above: near-side material moves toward screen left, and far-side material moves toward screen right. The circular boundary and lower cylindrical rim stay fixed in the locked camera. The porcelain remains within the platform's support footprint.

Frame 00 is the original PNG copied byte-for-byte. Frames 01–19 sample a constant 12 degrees/second turn at angles 3° through 57°. The scene reconstruction is calibrated at angle zero. Small residual reconstruction corrections are carried with the moving mattes and fade over the first four samples; corrections for fixed pixels remain fixed. This smooths the handoff from the exact original without changing the prescribed angular phase. There are no camera movements, cuts, added objects, or moving light sources.

The embedded HTML delta packets reconstruct every tensor frame exactly. Twenty frames at 4 fps give five seconds of playback; sample timestamps run from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds.

Every pixel outside x=95–195 is unchanged across the full sequence. The bottom four image rows, including the platform's lowest rim, remain exact. The largest bright connected porcelain region remains within x=127–166 initially and x=130–161 in the last sample, with its lowest support pixels remaining at y=135. Thresholded connected-component measurements are not a visual judgment: thin, shaded upper details can belong to a separate component in an oblique sample.

The wall shadow changes continuously while the fixed spotlight and exterior scene remain unchanged.

The method is limited by having one observed view. It gives coherent shallow parallax, silhouette deformation, platform rotation, and a synchronized shadow over a partial turn; it cannot establish the statue's true hidden geometry. No later visual review is claimed.
