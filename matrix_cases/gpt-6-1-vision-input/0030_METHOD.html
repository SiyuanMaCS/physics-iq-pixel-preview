This continuation was generated deterministically with local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. It uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy array sampling/filtering, and OpenCV's local inpainting operation. No network, external images, trained model, rendering engine, or subagent was used. The attached initial image supplied the scene appearance; every later output was checked through local code and numeric measurements, without an image Read/View tool.

Scene preservation and pixels

Frame 00 is an exact RGB pixel copy of the supplied frame. The camera, pipe, table perimeter, sofa, floor, and all pixels outside the original ball cleanup areas and the moving ball/shadow footprints stay fixed.

The three balls were measured at screen centers (86.0, 51.3), (121.0, 55.0), and (135.5, 56.4). Their image silhouettes are described with manually measured polygon mattes. The mattes are rasterized at eight times the image resolution, reduced with antialiasing, and thresholded to eliminate small distant filter ringing.

The original silhouettes and cast shadows are removed from a background copy with local Navier–Stokes inpainting. Weak real wood grain from pixels 24 rows below the affected area is added only inside the repaired regions. Surrounding pixels are copied from the source exactly. The untouched brown/orange pair and its original shadows are preserved until the grey ball makes contact.

Ball RGB textures are extracted from the photograph, with edge color corrected against the repaired background. Colors from valid interior pixels extend to the silhouette rather than borrowing surrounding table pixels. To depict rolling, the projected texture is sampled through a mathematical sphere rotation. The rotation axis lies perpendicular to the direction of travel; angle is travelled distance divided by radius. The unobserved rear surface is approximated by reflection of the supplied front texture. This is a texture inference from one photograph, not a reconstruction of the actual hidden tennis-ball markings.

A smooth illumination residual is suppressed in the extracted texture so that rotating the surface does not rotate broad baked-in lighting. A fixed upper-left light is then applied through analytic sphere normals. A short two-and-a-half-pixel blend joins the exact extracted appearance to the rotating surface approximation. Antialiased translated silhouettes preserve the photographed shape. Broad soft cast shadows, tight contact shadows, and a faint orange color bounce follow the balls on the table. There is no camera motion or exposure change.

Dynamics and timing

The grey ball starts already outside the clipped black pipe, as in the input. It moves along the measured grey-to-brown direction, approximately (0.99446, 0.10513) in image coordinates. The balls are treated as equal masses constrained to a straight tabletop path. This is a screen-space physical approximation; the single photograph does not provide calibrated world dimensions.

The incoming speed is 42 screen pixels/second. Rolling resistance reduces speed by 7.3 pixels/second², clamped at zero so resting balls do not start moving backward. Pairwise compliant contact uses a spring stiffness of 22000 s⁻² and damping derived from a nominal restitution of 0.68. Integration steps are 1/4000 second. Contact forces transfer the impulse from grey to brown and then to the touching orange ball, dissipating energy through contact damping and rolling resistance.
