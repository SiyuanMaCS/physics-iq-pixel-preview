METHOD.md

Method and validation

The continuation contains 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144 pixels, displayed at 4 fps for 5 seconds. The left red candle keeps burning with gentle flame flutter. The right candle stays unlit, and the camera and physical setup stay fixed.

Actual pixel generation

generate.py uses the supplied first_frame.png and local NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy pixel operations. It does not use a generative model, external imagery, a rendering engine, or other scene inputs. This is an image-based continuation with prescribed small flame deformations; it is not a combustion or fluid simulation.

  1. Preserve the original RGB frame and convert a working copy from sRGB to linear light.
  2. Estimate the wall radiance beneath the flame by interpolating between two narrow bands beside it. Smooth those scanline estimates vertically. Extract the positive excess radiance of the original flame and its nearest halo with soft spatial support.
  3. For each later frame, resample this radiance with a deformation anchored around the original wick at (94, 30.5). Height changes range from −0.65 to +0.85 pixels, upper-tip lean from −0.78 to +0.82 pixels, and width scales from 0.96 to 1.04. A small curvature term avoids a perfectly rigid taper. Radiance scales range from 0.984 to 1.020.
  4. Subtract the original isolated flame radiance and add the deformed radiance over the original image. This retains the source's photographic flame colors and the fixed wall texture. A very weak correlated warm halo term is also evaluated; much of it falls below one RGB quantization step. The changing nearby halo predominantly comes from the resampled source radiance.
  5. Convert back to RGB uint8. Explicitly restore every pixel at y≥32, x<57, or x≥130 from the input. This preserves the candle bodies, brass holders, tabletop, and right candle exactly. Frame 00 bypasses all processing and is copied exactly from the input.
  6. Use the supplied output helper to save the tensor, RGB PNGs, contact sheet, and standalone HTML player. Separately create a transition diagnostic with scene frames, amplified differences, and enlarged flame crops.

The final pose is close to the opening pose, producing a mild replay transition. No visible wax consumption, smoke, or second ignition is introduced; none is supported by the described setup during this short interval.

Numeric checks performed

  • Exactly 20 RGB PNGs at 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 tensor of shape (144, 256, 20). Values use 0xRRGGBB packing with no alpha channel.
  • Frame 00 is pixel-for-pixel identical to first_frame.png.
  • Every PNG is pixel-for-pixel identical to the corresponding tensor slice.
  • The actual HTML payload independently reconstructs all 20 tensor frames exactly.
  • All changed pixels lie within x=82–105 and y=6–31. All pixels below y=31 and the entire right side from x=130 are identical to the input in every frame.

  • The largest adjacent-frame channel change is 59/255 and occurs in the narrow flame region. The last-to-first replay transition has a maximum channel change of 5/255 across 44 pixels.
