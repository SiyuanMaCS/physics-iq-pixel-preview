METHOD.md

Method

This continuation was generated with local Python, NumPy, SciPy, and Pillow pixel operations. The only scene sources were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external references, models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Pixel construction

The background uses the source photograph. Narrow measured corridors around the initial smoke strands are reconstructed by feathered horizontal interpolation between neighboring background samples. The original signed RGB difference from this reconstructed background initializes the moving smoke, including its cool lighting tint. The small curled filament immediately above the right fold is included in this reconstruction.

Smoke uses a two-dimensional semi-Lagrangian transport approximation at 32 integration steps per second, with upward velocity, smooth spatial and temporal lateral variation, diffusion, and gradual decay. Two small Gaussian sources follow the burning paper edges. New smoke is composited as translucent cool white from optical density; the original smoke residual is transported and attenuated as it leaves the frame. This is a local image-space simulation, not a full three-dimensional fluid or chemical combustion solver.

The paper keeps the source facets and crease texture. A subpixel warp makes its burned margins curl slightly inward. Deterministic spatial texture perturbs burn fronts traveling inward from the right and lower left edges. A brown transition precedes mottled charcoal; a narrow ember lies along part of the right burn front. Small antialiased flame silhouettes change height and lean with continuous functions of elapsed time. Their white-yellow core, orange edge, and restrained reflection are composited near the paper. The tabletop, glass board outline, sunlight, and camera remain fixed.

Frame 00 is copied exactly from the supplied RGB image. Subsequent frames use deterministic calculations and random seed 140. All modifications are confined to a feathered region around the paper and smoke; 26,889 background pixels per frame lie wholly outside it and remain exactly equal to the source.

Artifacts and timing

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32; each value is 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: standalone embedded pixel player with play, pause, seek, replay, and loop, at 250 ms per frame (4 fps). Twenty displayed frames occupy five seconds; their sample times run from 0 to 4.75 seconds.
  • inspection.png: eight-frame contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: adjacent frame pairs and four-times-amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • OBSERVATION.md: initial measurements and the event plan written before implementation.
  • generate.py: reproducible generation and primary validation.
  • validation.json: numeric verification and per-frame combustion/smoke measurements.

Run python generate.py inside this workspace to regenerate the output. The optional additional verification script is .tmp/check_artifacts.py.

Verification and review limits

All 20 saved PNGs were compared exactly with the uint32 tensor, and frame 00 was compared exactly with the input. An independent decoder reconstructed every frame from the standalone player's embedded first frame and delta packets and matched them with the tensor. Artifact presence, RGB dimensions, 4 fps player timing, the static region, a monotonically expanding burn mask, and upward movement of newly emitted smoke were checked numerically.

Adjacent-frame mean absolute RGB changes range from approximately 0.20 to 0.73 on the 0–255 scale across the full image. The opacity-weighted centroid of new smoke rises approximately 31 pixels during the sequence, and smoke reaches the upper region while continuing to emit from the paper. These checks establish encoding integrity and intended numerical progression; they do not establish photorealism by visual assessment.

Only the user-attached initial image was available as a visual reference. Later images and the contact sheets were not opened with image Read/View tools. Output inspection was performed exclusively through local code and numeric checks. No visual review of generated frames is claimed.
