METHOD.md

Generation method

This is a local photographic pixel continuation of first_frame.png, guided only by prompt.txt. It uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV pixel operations, and SciPy numerical integration and sparse linear algebra. No external reference, network access, AI model, rendering engine, or subagent was used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator and records measurements, the held-ball occlusion assumption, and the twenty-frame plan. The held ball is not clearly exposed in the photograph. Its initial center is inferred near (44,74), its equilibrium center near (101,97.2), and its projected pendulum length is 82 pixels. The four photographed resting balls remain the reference for scale and appearance. Frame 00 copies the original PNG without re-encoding, so both file bytes and decoded RGB pixels are identical to the input.

Pixel construction

The background begins as the original photograph. Manually measured polygons and a blue-pixel threshold identify the grabber. A discrete harmonic fill reconstructs the small region it uncovers; high-frequency wood grain is sampled from another clear tabletop region of the same image and blended into the hole. The diagonal wall/table boundary is preserved. The right ball's original position is cleared using actual pixels from the vacant left socket and neighboring wood. Its stationary wires are removed with short horizontal interpolations. Its original base reflection is filled from an empty portion of the photographed base.

The two moving end balls use the original right sphere's RGB texture, resampled at subpixel positions with a circular antialiasing matte. The three middle balls retain their source pixels. Paired suspension wires are drawn with analytic line-distance coverage; their endpoints follow the bob. Two photographed front upright strips are restored over the moving objects to provide occlusion. The upper and lower grabber jaws use the original RGB pixels and a color/brightness matte. They open slightly and translate left, leaving the view by 0.75 seconds. No hand or additional object is introduced.

Moving shadows use low-contrast Gaussian coverage. Ball reflections sample the photographed right reflection, move on the base, and are clipped to its reflective region. The reflection and lighting approximations are image-based and are not a full optical simulation. Each later frame averages three poses across a 10 ms shutter window, preserving static pixels while allowing slight motion blur.

Motion and timing

The scalar angle follows q'' = -(9.81/0.28) sin(q) - 0.018 q', with an assumed physical pendulum length of 0.28 m. This scale is inferred, not measured from a known object. The initial angular displacement is approximately −0.769 rad. The nonlinear period is about 1.1 seconds.

For negative angle, the left end swings and the right end rests. For positive angle, the right end swings and the left end rests. At each zero crossing, momentum is transferred through the middle row and angular speed is multiplied by 0.975. The middle row is idealized as stationary during the brief contact impulse. This gives alternating end-ball motion with weak damping. Nine contacts occur from approximately 0.276 to 4.644 seconds. Every bob position lies on its 82-pixel pendulum circle.

Twenty RGB frames are sampled at n/4 seconds, n=0…19. Each displays for 0.25 seconds, giving a five-second clip at 4 fps. Motion continues at the end of the clip.
