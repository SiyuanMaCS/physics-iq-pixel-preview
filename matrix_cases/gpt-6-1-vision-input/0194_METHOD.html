METHOD.md

PixelWorld-Vision-Input generation method

This continuation was constructed with local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. The attached first frame was the only visual reference. Neither generated frames nor either generated inspection sheet were viewed with image Read/View tools. Validation of later outputs used local code and numeric checks exclusively. No network, external references, learned models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Source reconstruction and animation

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy array filters, and OpenCV's local image processing functions. Source RGB thresholds inside measured polygon boundaries isolate the black kettlebell, green sheet, and two holding elements. The source photographic object textures are retained. Small vacated regions are filled with Telea pixel inpainting. A quadratic RGB surface fitted to unobstructed source wall pixels continues the gray wall into the strip revealed above the compressed pillow. Its faint texture comes from a source wall patch.

Both objects begin lowering on the first interval. The kettlebell stays rigid and translates downward by about 26.9 pixels. Over the first 1.5 seconds, the left pillow's crown moves downward about 28 pixels under a smooth placement curve. The original pillow pixels are compressed through a monotone inverse map, with the deformation concentrated under x=96 and fading toward the seam and edges. The lower seam moves much less than the crown. Subtle multiplicative shading and two narrow folds strengthen the indentation. A small exponentially damped response follows placement. This is an illustrative mathematical deformation, not a calibrated finite-element simulation of a 30-lb load.

The paper's lower edge moves downward just 1.8 pixels and remains supported. A projective warp rotates its source texture toward the camera, from the initial upright sheet to a shallow footprint approximately x=164..205, y=64..88. Its projected area briefly narrows during the edge-on part of the rotation. The sheet is green on both sides; the top clip's occluded texture is locally filled with surrounding green pixels before the rotation. Orientation-based brightness and a tiny damped near-edge bend accompany settling. The right pillow receives no load deformation. Soft local shadows and a weak green reflection tie both objects to their surfaces.

The two existing holding elements follow the objects until release around 1.35 seconds, then translate upward. Both are completely outside the image by frame 08 (2.00 seconds). The final frames hold the loaded kettlebell in the compressed pillow and the light sheet on the uncompressed right pillow.

All pixels outside the local action region are copied directly from the source. In particular, the wooden support at y=132..143 is identical in every frame. The frame-00 override copies the entire source RGB array exactly.

Numeric checks and revision

numeric_checks.json records geometry, mask areas, transition statistics, and PNG-array SHA-256 hashes. The script asserts:

  • Correct frame count, RGB dimensions, tensor dtype, and 24-bit value range.
  • Exact source equality for frame 00.
  • Exact equality between every PNG, its tensor slice, and its in-memory frame.
  • Exact equality between all 20 frames reconstructed from the actual HTML packets and the tensor.
  • Exact preservation of the table and all pixels outside the action region.
  • A monotone pillow map with inverse residual below 0.01 pixel.
  • Constant kettlebell mask area under translation, and complete removal of both holding elements from frame 08 onward.
