METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This continuation uses local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png, plus a mathematical Newton's cradle motion model. No external image, reference, neural model, network, subagent, or external rendering engine was used. The scene prompt and the supplied initial photograph are the only scene inputs.

The camera, wall, tabletop, cradle base, and three middle balls are retained from the photograph. The moving elements are composed with masks measured from the supplied frame. Blue source pixels and their connected regions refine the grabber silhouettes. The tool's upper and lower photographed parts separate slightly, then translate out to the left during the first second. The initially held sphere is removed from the old tool region.

Newly exposed tabletop pixels are reconstructed by a quadratic local illumination fit to 2,675 unobstructed wood pixels, clean wood texture sampled from the same photograph, and narrow-edge OpenCV pixel inpainting. The right end ball, its old fine suspension threads, and its old reflection are removed with small masks and local inpainting. A short section of rear support previously covered by that ball is interpolated from its neighbouring source pixels. This reconstruction is a pixel approximation of the hidden scene.

Both end spheres use chrome texture sampled from the original right sphere, transformed into antialiased circular silhouettes. A small size change follows the receding swing direction. Paired suspension threads are drawn between the fixed rail attachments and moving spheres. Subdued, vertically mirrored sphere textures form reflections clipped to the base, with weak diffuse shadows. Original foreground uprights occlude balls as they cross the frame. No new object or camera motion is introduced.

The end motion follows the nonlinear equation θ″ = −(9.81/0.16) sin θ − 0.025 θ′, integrated locally with scipy.integrate.solve_ivp. The initial angle is −0.505 rad. The projected suspension length is 86.4 px, the row direction is (1, −0.285), and the projected rise is 86.4(1 − cos θ). On reaching θ=0, the moving end stops; its angular velocity, multiplied by 0.985, transfers to the opposite end. This is an idealized equal-mass, instantaneous collision model; the three central balls stay fixed. The first impact is at 0.20406 s, followed by impacts approximately every 0.407 s. At most one end has nonzero angular displacement at any instant. Small exposure integration at t−1/240, t, and t+1/240 s gives fast-moving surfaces subtle motion blur.

Frame 00 is copied directly from the source array without compositing. Frames 01–19 are sampled every 0.25 s. They are 256 × 144 RGB uint8 arrays. The tensor in pixels.npz is named pixels, shaped (144, 256, 20), and stores each pixel as uint32 0xRRGGBB. The standalone index.html decodes a full first frame plus exact delta packets and plays the sequence at 4 fps for a five-second cycle. Both inspection sheets are generated locally from the saved arrays.
