METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is a deterministic local Python pixel synthesis, using only first_frame.png and the text prompt as scene inputs. It is not a learned video generation model, a reconstructed 3D scene, or a physically simulated fluid. The supplied initial frame was visually available in the user message. All later evaluation used local Python and numeric checks; no generated frame, contact sheet or transition sheet was opened with an image Read/View tool.

Scene treatment

The photographed table, glass board, wall, window lighting, and shadows are retained. The paper stays in its initial location. The animation introduces white smoke moving upward, a few small orange/gold flame tongues at the lower paper fold, gradual edge browning and char, and a very faint local warm reflection underneath. The paper remains mostly folded and attached over the five-second interval.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generation code. It records initial scene measurements and an explicit plan for frames 00–19.

Pixel operations

  1. Five narrow interpolated ribbon masks follow the existing smoke above the paper. OpenCV's local Telea pixel inpainting estimates the underlying photographic background within those ribbons. Only the positive RGB residual is retained as a smoke layer. Negative inpaint residuals are discarded. This estimate is approximate, especially where bright smoke overlaps the textured glass.
  2. The initial residual is bilinearly sampled with SciPy map_coordinates to transport it upward at approximately 13–16 pixels per second, with mild horizontal shear and exponential attenuation. Subtracting the same residual from the original gives a background that reconstructs the source at t=0. Frame 00 is additionally copied directly from the input PNG, ensuring byte equality as well as pixel equality.
  3. Fresh smoke is continuously introduced at the paper. Gaussian density profiles, smooth sinusoidal filaments, broad low-opacity vapor, a denser close vapor strand, and analytic upward phase transport approximate buoyant curls and diffusion. A rising front moves at 23 pixels per second. The new plume smoothly enters over the first 0.7 seconds, widens with height, and remains translucent. It is composited with pale white RGB=(229,234,239).
  4. A manually specified polygon follows the lower paper edge. Smooth expanding distance fields produce brown margins and a textured dark char patch. The texture is taken from the supplied photograph. The large pale upper fold is preserved; no collapse or new debris is synthesized.
  5. Two tapered analytic flame lobes, a gold edge and pale inner core are rasterized at 4× resolution and downsampled by area averaging. Continuous-time oscillations vary their height and lean. A Gaussian glow changes only a few pixels directly beneath the fire.
  6. Every pixel outside the fixed local smoke/combustion support is restored exactly from the input. All compositing is clipped and rounded to uint8 RGB. There is no camera motion, reframing, scene cut, or external visual asset.

All animation functions depend on continuous time t=i/4, without independent random frame noise. The first sample is at 0.00 seconds, and the final sample is at 4.75 seconds. Each of the 20 frames is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving 5.00 seconds of playback.

The first numeric inspection showed that the fresh plume was weaker than the dense photographed smoke near the source. A revision increased the replenished filament density and added close vapor so the source keeps emitting smoke after the original plume exits. Final late-frame smoke density sums range from 221.030 to 227.572 equivalent opacity pixels. These values describe the procedural field, not a measured smoke mass.
