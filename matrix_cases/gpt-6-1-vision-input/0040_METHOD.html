METHOD.md

PixelWorld continuation method

This is a deterministic 2D pixel animation generated from first_frame.png with local Python, NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. The only scene reference is the supplied first frame and prompt. No network, external assets, AI models, other cases, subagents, physics engines, or rendering engines were used.

Source reconstruction

The scene was measured from the attached initial image and its RGB scanlines. Four-times supersampled polygons isolate the visible knife and tangerine. High-confidence orange pixels supplement the fruit outline so that the initial continuation retains its thin rind. Frame 00 bypasses reconstruction and is copied exactly from the input.

OpenCV Telea inpainting reconstructs the background behind the original silhouettes. Source-derived high-frequency texture restores glass grain. The table area uses per-row interpolation between visible neighboring pixels. Board samples shifted along its projected diagonal edge restore the boundary hidden by the blade. Existing table, glass, and reflections outside the local reconstruction and motion region retain their source pixels.

The hidden part of the tangerine combines mirrored visible flesh texture with an analytic radial field for flesh, segment membranes, pith, and rind. The visible rear peel is reused from the original image. These hidden surfaces are approximations inferred from the single supplied frame. The apparent fresh cut is a narrow extrusion along the same projected cutting plane, textured with the local flesh field.

Motion and compositing

The knife rocks by up to 10.4 degrees about its tip at approximately (199.5, 104.8). A small draw motion accompanies the press. Its tip stays at board contact height during frames 00–10. Frames 11–16 lift the blade along its length. The source image clips the upper blade; a source-colored extension outside the initial image prevents the rocking motion from exposing an artificial endpoint. The orange reflection on the blade fades as it leaves the fruit, while the existing neutral bevel highlights remain.

The tangerine compresses by at most 1.2 percent during the stroke. A diagonal plane aligned with the lowered blade divides its mask into two portions. After the cut, the front portion moves 1.65 pixels left and 0.55 pixels down and tilts 1.2 degrees. The rear portion moves 4.5 pixels right and 0.25 pixels up and turns 3 degrees. Smoothstep trajectories govern the press, lift, and separation. Soft contact shadows move with the portions. Premultiplied-alpha affine warping avoids color contamination at moving edges. Frames 16–19 hold the settled state.

This method uses prescribed geometric motion, local deformation, and approximate contact shading rather than a full volumetric simulation.
