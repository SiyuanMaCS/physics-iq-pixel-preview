METHOD.md

Method

This continuation was produced entirely with local Python pixel operations using NumPy, Pillow, SciPy and OpenCV. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The supplied initial visual and numerical sampling informed OBSERVATION.md, which was written before the generator. No network, other cases, external models, external rendering engines, references or subagents were used.

The background is the supplied photograph. A small polygon covers the original domino and its immediate contact shadow. OpenCV local inpainting continues the concealed tabletop texture. A sheared ellipse fitted to unobstructed platform boundary pixels separates wood from platform inside that polygon. A quadratic fit to nearby source platform pixels supplies the concealed dark surface shading. Fine texture is taken from the same photograph. No preexisting mask is used; the generator constructs the removal polygon from the recorded coordinates.

The moving domino is an analytically projected shallow rectangular prism. Its contact center stays at (112.25, 48.8), its plane dimensions are 22.8 × 8.6 local pixel units, its projected thickness is 3.15 px, and the tabletop depth scale is 0.56. The top texture is sampled from the source domino and restricted to its measured cool-white plastic color range. Visible side faces receive directional shading calibrated to the source. A short, blurred contact/cast shadow follows the rotated footprint.

The prism starts at 28.1° yaw and turns clockwise by 180° over samples t = 0 to 4.75 seconds, at a constant 37.895°/s. Geometry is rasterized at six times the output resolution and averaged down. Three closely spaced exposure samples smooth subpixel motion. This is a prescribed kinematic rotation with image-based reconstruction; magnetic forces are not simulated. There is no camera or platform motion.

Frame 00 is copied from the original RGB pixels. Every later frame preserves the source outside the old footprint, current object and local shadow. Columns 0–99, containing the magnet, are protected exactly. The output consists of 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, presented at 4 fps for 5 seconds. The last sample occurs at 4.75 seconds and is displayed for the final quarter second.
