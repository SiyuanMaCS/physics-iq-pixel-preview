METHOD.md

Method

This is a deterministic local photographic pixel continuation, implemented by generate.py. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external reference, learned model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. NumPy, SciPy, OpenCV, and Pillow perform local pixel operations and mathematical motion. artifact_io.py packages the resulting pixels and standalone player.

The camera, black tube, white lampshade, tabletop geometry, and lighting are fixed. The single ball is already outside the tube in the supplied first image. It rolls to the right along a nearly horizontal straight projected line, with mild continuous slowing. Its path passes behind the foreground lampshade. The ball is fully hidden in frames 08 and 09; it is nearly fully hidden in frame 10, partly visible again in frame 11, and fully visible to the right in frame 12. It crosses the right image boundary in frames 16–17 and is outside the view by frame 18. The final two frames hold the empty table.

A small elliptical region containing the original ball and its shadow is reconstructed with OpenCV's local Telea inpainting. Fine wood texture is restored from a patch of the same first frame after subtracting the donor's low frequency illumination. A feathered boundary joins this patch to the unchanged photograph. This reconstruction estimates the unseen wood behind the ball.

The ball is extracted using a soft ellipse centred at (51.0, 65.7), with radii (9.65, 9.30). A shadow transmission field is measured from the input and reconstructed tabletop. Two Gaussian terms are fitted to the portion outside the ball, then blended with the measured transmission; this estimates the shadow hidden beneath the ball. The original photographic sphere colour is recovered by removing its background contribution through the soft matte. The sphere's low frequency lighting remains fixed, while a small image-derived high frequency texture residual is transported through a mathematical sphere rotation. This approximates rolling texture without rotating the illumination. There is no newly drawn tennis-ball seam.

The trajectory is x(t)=51+50t−0.30t², y(t)=65.7−0.015[x(t)−51]. The scale is 1−0.00035[x(t)−51]. Time samples are t=i/4 for i=0…19, each displayed for 0.25 seconds, yielding 20 frames at 4 fps over five seconds. Rolling phase follows travel divided by the current horizontal sphere radius. Three subpixel samples approximate a 1/60-second exposure. The sphere and its shadow are composited over the reconstructed wood and behind the fixed lampshade silhouette. All pixels are rounded and clipped to 8-bit RGB. Frame 00 is assigned the untouched input array rather than a reconstructed rendering.

The hidden table texture, ball depth, exact shadow beneath the ball, and unseen surface texture are single-image estimates. This method is an image-based physical approximation, not a full three-dimensional simulation.

Verification

Only local code and numeric checks were used to inspect generated outputs. Neither the output frames nor the diagnostic sheets were opened with an image Read/View tool. The initial attached image supplied the visual scene observation.

verification.json records the measurements and checks. The generation script verifies:

  • Exactly 20 RGB PNGs at 256×144, and an unsigned 32-bit tensor of shape (144,256,20).
  • Tensor values encode 0xRRGGBB. Every PNG matches its tensor slice exactly.
  • Frame 00 matches first_frame.png pixel for pixel.
  • Every reconstructed delta packet in index.html matches the same tensor.
  • The tube and fully covered shade pixels remain identical to the input; fixed regions remain unchanged.
  • Changed pixels stay within the original removal region or the current moving ball/shadow region.
  • The ball moves continuously rightward, with foreground occlusion and boundary exit, and frames 18 and 19 are equal.

Additional numeric inspection measured source-location sphere reconstruction error of 0.058 channel levels in the opaque core and 0.380 channel levels in the feathered rim, on a 0–255 scale. The recovered core has no colour values clipped at the surface bounds. The generated visible ball core retains the source's cool grey colour. These are numeric measurements, not claims of visual output review.

inspection.png is the required eight-frame contact sheet. transition_check.png contains the first four frames, amplified transition differences, and a graph of visible sphere area. Both are written for inspection by the user. index.html is a self-contained player using the exact packed pixels and a 250 ms playback interval.
