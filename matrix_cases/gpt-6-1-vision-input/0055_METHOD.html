duck-and-dominos · 生成方法

The sequence is generated entirely in local Python from first_frame.png. It uses NumPy, OpenCV, SciPy and Pillow pixel operations. No network access, external images, AI models, simulation engines, rendering engines or subagents are used. The supplied initial image was available visually in the request; generated images were never opened with image Read/View tools or visually reviewed. All subsequent inspection used local code and numeric checks.

The camera, wood table, wall, duck and four left-hand dominoes retain the original source pixels. Frame 00 is the exact decoded RGB source image. Subsequent frames apply a locally rendered change to the original image, so supersampling does not filter the fixed photograph. This was corrected after an initial numeric check detected unintended resampling differences.

Three right-hand dominoes use measured source quadrilaterals for their top, broad side and narrow side. Their actual photographic textures are mapped into new quadrilaterals with projective transforms. Geometry rotates each domino around its lower leading edge, including the thickness lifting away from the table. Continuous orientation-dependent shading brightens surfaces that turn toward the light. The renderer uses four times the output resolution and averages the local change down to 256 × 144.

A small-step angular integrator drives tipping with gravity-like acceleration. Neighbor contact limits an upstream block's angle to the downstream angle plus a gap/height contact term. A contact impulse releases the next block. The final block stops at a 25-degree lean against the duck; the preceding blocks then rest against their neighbors. The measured chain gaps give final angles of approximately 63.66, 45.66 and 25 degrees. The duck is treated as a stationary obstruction, and its source silhouette occludes the leaning block. All four left-hand dominoes remain exactly unchanged. This is an approximate model in projected coordinates, rather than a calibrated three-dimensional material simulation.

The wooden arm rotates clockwise at 42 degrees per second about the fixed motor pivot. Its circular tabletop path projects as an ellipse with the same approximate depth foreshortening as the motor disk. Its wood color, narrow edge shading and pivot are drawn locally. The arm shadow is split between the raised platform and the lower tabletop. Domino contact shadows follow their projected geometry and the original upper-left light direction.

Revealed surfaces use local inpainting on the motor and source-only wood reconstruction on the table. A low-order fit describes the source table's broad color variation; residual grain comes from a clear strip in the same source image. Soft analytic shading retains the motor's shadow. No object is removed until it begins moving. Static and foreground source pixels are composited back afterward.

artifact_io.py saves the 20 RGB PNG frames, the packed tensor and the standalone player. pixels.npz contains a uint32 array named pixels with shape (144, 256, 20), using 0xRRGGBB packing. inspection.png is an eight-frame contact sheet and transition_check.png shows amplified frame differences. These inspection artifacts are saved for the user and were not viewed during generation.

Validation checks frame count, resolution, RGB mode, tensor dimensions and dtype, exact equality of frame 00 with the source, and pixel-for-pixel agreement of every PNG with the decoded tensor. Numeric checks also cover the clockwise angular rate, contact order, monotonic tipping, stationary left dominoes and the bounded region of changed pixels. The player's embedded first frame and all 19 delta packets are independently reconstructed and compared against the saved tensor. Results are recorded in verification.json and delivery_check.json.

To reproduce the required animation artifacts and generation checks, run python generate.py from this workspace. The code is deterministic. delivery_check.json records the additional independent handoff verification.
