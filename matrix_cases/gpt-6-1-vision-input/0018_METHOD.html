ball-in-sand · 生成方法

This sequence was made entirely with local Python, NumPy, SciPy array operations, and Pillow. It uses the supplied first frame as its only pixel source. It is a source-image composite with analytic motion and deformation, not a learned video generator or a full three-dimensional simulation. No network, external models, rendering engines, other cases, reference images, or subagents were used.

The initial frame is copied exactly. Later frames reconstruct the small patch formerly covered by the falling ball by interpolating source wall pixels parallel to the table's sloping rear edge. Nearby source tabletop pixels supply the grain at the bottom of that patch. The interpolation samples avoid the grabber, which remains fixed and pixel-exact. The distant wall and tabletop also remain pixel-exact.

The ball surface comes from the original ball, retaining its blue color, lighting, and curved seam. A radial transparency estimate removes the gray background from its soft original edge. An analytic sphere shader finishes the circular silhouette; deterministic fine grain supplies a subdued fuzzy surface. The final ball radius is 13.8 pixels. A small rotation carries its source seam with the surface. Sharper later outlines represent a stationary ball after the motion-blurred initial exposure.

The motion starts at the measured blue-mask center, approximately (118.09, 61.39). A constant screen-space acceleration of 4,000 pixels/s² and initial downward speed of 100 pixels/s bring the bottom of the ball to the sand after approximately 0.0687 seconds. This is a chosen scale consistent with a tennis-sized ball in this close view, not a recovered camera calibration. A heavily dissipative exponential penetration response then approaches a final center of (118.69, 100.00); compliant recovery stays below one pixel. The early impact happens between the first two samples at four frames per second.

The sand is segmented from source green chroma, with enclosed dark details retained. A smooth inverse coordinate warp compresses the mound, shifts its side texture outward, raises a small side rim, and spreads its front slightly. Existing granular texture moves with the deformation. Local concave shading adds contact depth. The foreground sand ridge is composited over the bottom of the ball with an irregular, antialiased edge, so the ball is partially embedded. Seven very small source-colored cohesive clumps follow short ballistic paths and stop near the mound. There is no dust cloud. Motion decays quickly, and frames 07–19 are identical settled observations.

The implementation and reproducible artifact export are in generate.py. Run python generate.py from this directory. The generator reads first_frame.png, prompt.txt, artifact_io.py, and player_template.html; all output stays in this workspace.

Required outputs

  • OBSERVATION.md: initial scene measurements and the 20-frame plan, written before generator coding and refined using source-pixel measurements.
  • generate.py: complete deterministic generation and numeric verification.
  • pixels.npz: the pixels array, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, with each value packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • inspection.png: an eight-frame contact sheet generated for inspection.
  • transition_check.png: adjacent-frame pairs and numeric RGB-difference heatmaps for five selected transitions.
  • index.html: a self-contained player with play, pause, replay, seeking, and a 250 ms frame interval, giving a five-second clip.
  • METHOD.md: this description of the actual method.
  • VALIDATION.json: additional per-frame motion measurements and verification results.

Verification and review limits

Every PNG is decoded locally and compared pixel-for-pixel with its tensor slice. Frame 00 is compared with the supplied image. The standalone HTML's base frame and all delta packets are independently decoded and compared with the same tensor. Checks also verify RGB mode, dimensions, packing range, stationary grabber and distant background, and the identical final hold.

Numeric color masks check the ball's final position, its visible cap, and the absence of blue remnants at its original location. Transition statistics record changed-pixel counts, bounds, and channel differences; all changes stay within the ball/sand interaction region. Later outputs, including both diagnostic sheets, were inspected only through local code and numeric checks. No generated image was opened with an image Read/View tool, and no visual review of the generated sequence is claimed.
