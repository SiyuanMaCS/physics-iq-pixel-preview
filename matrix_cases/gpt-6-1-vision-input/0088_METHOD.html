OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The supplied first frame is 256 × 144 RGB. Coordinates below are image pixels, with (0,0) at the upper left. The shot is fixed; the background, dispenser, basket, glass, and tray stay in place.

Initial scene measurements

  • Pale grey wall in the upper background; wooden counter underneath. A wicker basket occupies approximately x=63–170, y=9–84.
  • Cropped dispenser reservoir at x=107–182, y=0–25, with bright orange-red liquid and vertical glass texture.
  • Blue faucet at approximately x=166–195, y=0–18; its downward outlet is near (190,16.5).
  • Tall receiving glass at x=163–211, y=18–104. Its mouth is an ellipse centered near (186.8,24), about 41 pixels wide and 9 pixels high. Its walls taper toward x=166–201 at the bottom.
  • The receiving glass already appears almost full of bright red liquid. At x=190, the red channel is 238 at y=30, 218 at y=60, and 197 at y=80, while green remains 57–69. The image therefore cannot honestly start with an empty glass or a low rising fill level.
  • The short gap from faucet to liquid surface is only about 6 pixels. Pouring should produce a narrow continuous red jet, small surface ripples, and eventually overflow as a direct consequence of the nearly full initial state.
  • The dark metal baking tray occupies most of the lower/right frame, beginning near (81,90) at its left rim and reaching the right image edge. The glass contacts it around y=101–104. Overflow can collect in a shallow red puddle around its base without leaving the tray.
  • The glass has strong original pale specular highlights around x=182–187, y=36–44, thin edge highlights, and a bright base rim. Preserve these photographic cues and all static surroundings.

METHOD.md

Method

This continuation uses the supplied photograph and local Python pixel operations. It does not use a generative model, external imagery, an external renderer, network access, or other cases. The code is deterministic with seed 88.

The original receiving glass is already nearly full. A red stream therefore produces gentle surface agitation followed by a narrow overflow down the right exterior of the glass. The shallow spill collects within the baking tray while pouring continues. The dispenser, glass, basket, counter, tray, lighting, and camera remain stationary.

generate.py composites analytically defined liquid over the photograph on a 4× supersampled pixel grid. It draws a short shaded red jet from the existing blue nozzle, damped elliptical surface ripples, a thin wet run following measured glass contours, and a shallow puddle. The moving wet front follows a constant effective acceleration with a small initial velocity; this is an approximation to gravity-driven liquid slowed by adhesion to the wall. The puddle begins only when the wet front reaches the tray. Its elliptical area grows with elapsed inflow time, approximating constant flow into a shallow pool. Surface shading, narrow reflections, subdued capillary waves, and transmitted photographic texture make the added liquid consistent with the original material cues. All added masks are calculated locally from coordinates; none were supplied externally.

The liquid flow is an illustrative analytic approximation, not a computed Navier–Stokes solution or a calibrated fluid simulation. The unseen reservoir level and faucet control are held as in the original photograph. No hand or new apparatus is introduced. Original highlights on the glass body are retained. Supersampled pixel changes are reduced and added to the original RGB image, so the untouched scene is preserved without resampling its texture.

The sequence contains 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, played at 4 fps for 5 seconds. Frame 00 is the unmodified input. Frame sample times run from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds, with each sample displayed for 0.25 seconds. pixels.npz contains the pixels tensor with shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and values packed as 0xRRGGBB. frames/00.png through frames/19.png are the corresponding lossless RGB images. index.html is a self-contained player with embedded initial pixels and lossless frame patches. inspection.png is the required contact sheet; transition_check.png shows magnified adjacent-frame absolute RGB differences.

Review used local code and numeric checks only. No generated frame, contact sheet, or transition image was opened with an image Read/View tool, and no visual review is claimed. numeric_checks.json records frame equality, tensor/PNG correspondence, changes confined to the liquid region, adjacent-frame change statistics, wet-front position, pool area, and arrival timing. The generator asserts that every PNG matches its tensor slice, that frame 00 equals the input, that the background outside the liquid region stays exact, and that both the wet-front extent and puddle area are monotonic.
