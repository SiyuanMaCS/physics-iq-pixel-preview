METHOD.md

Method and delivery

Generated 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, displayed at a constant 4 fps for five seconds. Frame 00 is a direct pixel copy of first_frame.png. Frame k samples time k/4 seconds, so the last sampled instant is 4.75 seconds and its display interval completes the five-second sequence.

The only scene sources are the provided initial image and prompt.txt. This is deterministic local Python pixel synthesis, using NumPy, Pillow, SciPy array filtering/interpolation, and OpenCV's classical pixel inpainting and affine warping. No network, external assets, AI models, external scene engines, or subagents were used.

Appearance

The original photograph supplies the stationary tabletop, pipes, lighting, and background. Two softly feathered regions cover the balls and their original cast shadows. Classical Telea inpainting reconstructs the tabletop within those regions; a small high-frequency grain contribution comes from nearby unobstructed pixels of the same input photograph. All pixels outside the cleanup regions remain exact in the clean plate.

For each ball, a soft elliptical silhouette is constrained by the observed color values to recover a foreground matte without negative or excessive color values. Per-channel logarithmic source-to-table ratios recover the observed shadows; local scalar-field inpainting estimates the hidden part beneath each ball. Shadows move with their ball and are composited before the solid foregrounds, preserving the bodies during contact. The source's dark blue and lime-yellow appearance, mild difference in apparent size, soft focus, and fixed lighting are retained.

Small material texture variations are extracted from the source foregrounds, inverse-mapped using analytic sphere normals and a rotation around the transverse motion axis, then added back while the low-frequency illumination stays fixed. The unseen hemisphere uses a mirrored approximation of the supplied texture. The input contains little visible tennis-ball seam detail; the rolling cue is therefore subtle. Spin remains continuous at impact and relaxes toward the reversed rolling direction over a short 0.14-second interval. This is an appearance approximation, rather than a complete 3D reconstruction.

Ball and shadow layers are translated at subpixel positions. Each generated frame averages five pixel renderings across a short 1/80-second exposure. Frame 00 bypasses all reconstruction and exposure processing to preserve the supplied pixels exactly.

Motion

The initial centers are (76.35, 49.80) and (149.30, 58.35). Both are already outside the pipe openings, so the continuation begins with their ongoing approach. Their common motion axis follows the line between these centers and is approximately aligned with the two openings.

Both incoming speeds are exactly 23.2597345121 pixels/second in this screen-space model. They collide head-on at t = 1.25 seconds, frame 05, with center separation 15.30 pixels. An equal-mass one-dimensional collision with restitution 0.90 reverses the two velocities symmetrically. After contact, resistance of 0.90 pixels/second² gradually slows both balls as they roll toward their respective pipes. They remain visible on the tabletop throughout. The background and camera remain fixed.

These are measured image coordinates and an analytic planar collision approximation; they do not claim recovered real-world length, mass, or camera calibration.
