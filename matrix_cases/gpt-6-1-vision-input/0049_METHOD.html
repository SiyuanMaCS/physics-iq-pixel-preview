METHOD.md

Actual generation method

The continuation is a local Python construction from the supplied image and prompt. It uses NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV pixel operations, and SciPy Gaussian filtering. No network, external model, reference image, scene engine, or subagent was used.

The camera/background stays in source coordinates. A 1134-pixel region beneath the original three motor-side dominoes and stick is reconstructed from nearby source wood texture, local inpainting, and a small mathematical fill for source-occluded black cylinder pixels. The photographed wall, tabletop outside moving objects/shadows, motor silhouette, handle, and four left dominoes remain registered. Unmoved right dominoes retain their original masked photo pixels until they tip.

Three rectangular solids use a calibrated oblique projection. Their widths, heights, positions, source face color, and source-derived residual surface texture are measured/approximated from frame 00. Each falls about its leading lower edge under a numerical hinged gravity curve, sampled internally every millisecond. Initial contact times are calculated using convex rectangular cross sections; sequential block angles are capped with a separating-axis collision check and bisection. This is a constrained analytic animation, not a general rigid-body simulation. Adjacent solids settle on one another without positive-area intersection. A 1.2-degree damped table-contact rebound is allowed for the last block.

Motor-side fall onsets (near, middle, far) are 0.727, 0.369, 0.120 seconds. Final fall angles from upright are 90.00, 76.79, 71.11 degrees. Source-projected geometry leaves at least 2.61 pixels of clearance before the left-row right edge. The gap stops the cascade, and all four left domino solids retain their source pixels in every frame.

The wooden stick is a thin projected rectangular bar rotating clockwise as viewed from above at a constant 38 degrees/second. Its green source mounting detail rotates with it. A side face supplies thickness, and projected blurred shadows move on the fixed platform and tabletop. Block face shading changes with orientation, while surface residuals stay attached to their faces. Object polygons are rasterized at 4× resolution, area-filtered as premultiplied color/alpha, and composited into source-resolution RGB pixels. The animation is a static single shot with no cuts, camera motion, captions over the scene, extra props, or printed domino pips.
