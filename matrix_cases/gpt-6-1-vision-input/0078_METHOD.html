OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The attached initial image is a 256 × 144 locked camera view of a yellow mug on a small black turntable. A fixed spotlight illuminates the mug and the gray wall behind it. The left side of the image is nearly black. A wood tabletop crosses the lower image, with its rear edge sloping down toward the right. The mug body is approximately x=116–150, y=87–131, its dark opening centered near (133, 90). The yellow handle extends left to about x=104, over y=96–120. The turntable is approximately x=104–161, y=125–142. A broad, dark mug shadow occupies roughly x=145–194, y=44–104, with a loop-shaped handle shadow at x=132–154, y=51–88. The spotlight and camera remain stationary.

The visible mug cylinder and opening are nearly rotationally symmetric. Its handle is the clearest cue for rotation. A clockwise turn viewed from above will carry the initially leftward handle toward the wall, hide it behind the cylinder, bring it out on the right, then carry it toward the camera. The wall shadow must follow the same angular progression. The physical mug stays upright at the same position, with a fixed contact footprint and no lateral translation.

Plan: 20 frames at 4 fps, corresponding to sample times 0.00–4.75 seconds, held for a total playback duration of 5 seconds. Use a uniform 15-degree step, totaling 285 degrees of clockwise rotation. Preserve frame 00 exactly. Frame events:

METHOD.md

Method

The continuation is made entirely with local Python pixel operations. It uses NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV image inpainting/resampling, and SciPy filtering. No external models, reference assets, network services, or rendering engines were used.

The attached first frame supplied the scene geometry and photographic texture. Frame 00 is copied exactly. The cylindrical mug, its opening, the black turntable, tabletop, large cylindrical wall shadow, and fixed spotlight illumination are retained from that photograph. The turntable is treated as an axisymmetric surface; its stationary illumination and reflections are retained while the mug's handle provides the visible rotation cue.

The old leftward handle is isolated using color and position, then removed from the static background with bounded local inpainting. The old handle shadow is separated from the larger cylinder shadow by estimating the lit wall from adjacent unshadowed pixels. This gives a clean background and a source opacity texture for the shadow loop.

The mug turns clockwise as viewed from above at a constant 60 degrees per second. Its initially leftward handle moves toward the wall, behind the cylinder, out to the right, and then toward the camera. Each frame advances 15 degrees. The 20 samples cover times 0.00 through 4.75 seconds, with each frame displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving 5 seconds of playback.

The handle is predominantly a projected source texture on a rotating radial plane. A locally coded NumPy triangle rasterizer supplies a rounded rectangular ceramic tube when that plane approaches an edge-on view. The tube uses analytic normals, a fixed illumination direction, and specular highlights. Source-textured portions receive an angular illumination adjustment. An orthographic camera model and cylinder depth comparison hide the handle behind the mug and allow it to overlap the front when appropriate. Rendering at four times the output resolution provides antialiased edges.

The wall's handle shadow is projected from its source opacity texture using a calibrated point-light perspective transform. It changes position, width, skew, and magnification with the same rotation angle as the handle. Its edges are softened locally, and the opaque foreground mug occludes it. This is a calibrated pixel reconstruction with a mathematical motion model, rather than a complete physical reconstruction of the original camera and lamp.

All generated-frame inspection was performed through local code and numeric checks. Generated images were not opened in an image Read/View tool, and no visual review of generated outputs is claimed. The contact sheet and transition sheet are supplied as artifacts for subsequent review.

generate.py produces the complete deliverables and verifies that every decoded PNG matches its corresponding packed uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor slice. verification.json records dimensions, data types, exact preservation of frame 00, adjacent-frame differences, and projected handle/shadow measurements. The final checks also independently decode all 20 frames from the standalone player, verify the dark mug opening is preserved exactly, and verify that platform rows 129–143 stay bit-identical to the source. index.html contains the complete animation data and runs locally at 4 fps without fetching dependencies.
