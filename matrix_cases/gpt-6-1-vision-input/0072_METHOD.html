juice-in-water · 生成方法

This is a deterministic local Python pixel synthesis from first_frame.png. It uses NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV for array operations, image remapping, compositing, and output encoding. It does not use an external AI model, a renderer or physics engine, network access, other scene inputs, references, masks, scores, or subagents.

The attached initial frame was available visually. All generated outputs were inspected only by local code and numerical checks. inspection.png and transition_check.png were generated as deliverables and were not opened with an image Read/View tool.

Image construction

generate.py preserves frame 00 exactly and synthesizes frames 01–19 at times 0.25–4.75 s. Playback holds each of the 20 frames for 250 ms, giving 5 s at 4 fps.

Rendering calculations use a 4× subpixel grid. The generator starts with the original photograph and edits only the liquid in the dispenser, the receiving liquid, and the falling stream. Analytic masks describe the tapered glass interior, projected liquid ellipse, and nozzle-to-surface stream. It downsamples the computed difference and adds that difference to the exact source pixels, so the unchanged scene is not resampled.

The reservoir's existing photographed liquid interface is lowered by a bounded vertical remap. The tap and lever occlude this operation. The receiving water body is remapped from its old level to its new level, keeping its lower boundary fixed and carrying the old meniscus upward. The original rib texture, glass outline, fixed glass-wall highlights, and bright neutral front reflections are retained by local blending.

The stream has a shaded orange core, darker sides, a narrow light reflection, and small time-dependent radius variations. Its speed increases under gravity while its cross-sectional area decreases. It ends at a localized depression in the projected surface. Clipped expanding elliptical rings and a few subpixel entrained bubbles suggest impact and circulation. No liquid leaves the glass and no new object enters the scene.

Fluid approximation

The geometry is estimated from one photograph, so physical dimensions and volumes below are modeling assumptions. The scene is not reconstructed in 3D and there is no full fluid simulation.

The conversion is 4.8 image pixels per cm. Approximate cylindrical radii are 3.35 cm for the glass and 7.30 cm for the reservoir. The inferred initial receiving-water surface is y=96.15; the initial water volume is 263.32 ml. A 0.20 s valve-opening ramp begins after the initial sample, followed by gravity-fed discharge.

For initial head h0, initial flow Q0=30.6 ml/s, reservoir cross section A, and integrated opening time tau, the model uses:

h(t) = (sqrt(h0) - Q0 * tau / (2 * A * sqrt(h0)))**2
added_volume = A * (h0 - h(t))
receiver_rise = added_volume / receiver_cross_section
reservoir_drop = added_volume / reservoir_cross_section
Q(t) = Q0 * sqrt(h(t) / h0)
jet_speed(z) = sqrt(nozzle_speed**2 + 2 * g * z)
jet_radius(z) = nozzle_radius * sqrt(nozzle_speed / jet_speed(z))

The jet diameter is approximately 3.31 px at the nozzle. Nozzle speed uses a 0.95 discharge coefficient and the remaining source head. Its area and speed reproduce the instantaneous volume flow.

Juice concentration uses an analytic descending plume with widening dispersion and an exponentially increasing mixed fraction. A cylindrical chord-length weight normalizes the concentration field to the added-juice mass fraction in every generated frame. Concentration controls a Beer–Lambert-style blend toward shaded diluted grapefruit orange. This is an illustrative advection/dispersion approximation, not a numerical Navier–Stokes solution.

Revision and verification

The first numeric inspection identified large localized changes at a pre-existing bright glass reflection as the liquid rose past it. The final renderer explicitly preserves stationary neutral glare and the front rim. The same revision connected nozzle speed and width to Torricelli discharge, giving slightly decreasing flow as the source head falls.

verification.json records per-frame fluid states, adjacent-frame difference statistics, and PNG SHA-256 hashes. Completed checks:

  • Exactly 20 distinct RGB uint8 frames, each 256 × 144.
  • Frame 00 equals the decoded first_frame.png in every channel.
  • pixels.npz contains pixels with shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, encoding 0xRRGGBB.
  • All 20 PNGs match both the generated arrays and the corresponding packed tensor slices.
  • Decoding the standalone player's initial frame and all 19 delta packets reproduces all 20 tensor frames exactly.
  • Glass rise, source fall, and added volume are monotone; the two cross-section volume calculations agree within 1e-9 ml. Concentration agrees with the prescribed fraction within 1e-5.
  • The stream impact remains inside the glass and the receiving surface remains below the rim. Edits remain inside the declared liquid/stream bounds.
  • 33,055 of 36,864 image positions remain exactly unchanged throughout the sequence. The union of edited positions contains 3,809 pixels and has enclosing bounds x=102–190, y=31–134.
  • Adjacent full-frame mean absolute channel changes range from 0.1244 to 0.1956 on the 0–255 scale. The largest individual channel change is 86; no transition has a change above 100.

These checks establish encoding, containment, mass-model consistency, and bounded temporal changes. They do not constitute visual review of the generated scene.
