METHOD.md

Method

This continuation uses the supplied photograph, local Python pixel operations, and an analytic motion model. No external images, models, engines, network resources, or agents were used. The supplied first image was available visually in the task. Generated images were inspected only with code and numerical checks; neither output inspection sheet was opened through an image viewing tool.

Pixels and scene preservation

generate.py loads first_frame.png as RGB and copies it unchanged into frame 00. The original photograph supplies the fixed camera view, tabletop, ramp, support, and pipe throughout the sequence.

For subsequent frames, the original ball and its leftward cast shadow are removed inside a 670-pixel repair mask. The replacement wood texture comes from a local portion of the same photograph, translated by (−34, +18) source pixels. An automatically selected exemplar minimizes error around the repair boundary. A planar RGB correction matches the surrounding illumination, and an inward feather blends the repaired patch. The fitted boundary RGB root-mean-square error is 6.39 levels on a 0–255 scale. This is local texture copying and illumination fitting; no generative inpainting model is used.

The moving ball is extracted with a radial, softly blurred matte fitted to its measured outline. Its RGB values are decontaminated against the reconstructed tabletop, premultiplied by the matte, and resampled with bilinear interpolation. This preserves its photographed yellow-green color and shading. A subtle spherical seam and felt variation rotate with signed rolling distance. These add at most a few RGB levels; they do not replace the source appearance with a flat drawn circle. The ball becomes slightly smaller farther up the scene and larger as it approaches the pipe.

A broad soft cast shadow follows the ball toward the lower left, with a smaller dark contact shadow underneath. On the incline, the contact point is shifted rightward by 0.20 radii and downward by 0.90 radii to approximate the projected slope normal. The incline shadows are clipped to the cardboard polygon. The source pipe pixels act as a foreground occluder when the ball returns. Only ball, shadow, and background-repair regions change; the camera and setup do not move.

Each new frame averages five local composites across a 20 ms shutter interval. This adds restrained motion blur at the same constant 4 fps sampling rate.

The ball is already outside the pipe in the supplied first image, so the continuation begins at its existing position rather than introducing another ball. On the wood, its center follows a straight projected track from (102.55, 72.65) toward (167.00, 48.50). On the ramp, the projected travel direction is (0.6800, −0.7332).
