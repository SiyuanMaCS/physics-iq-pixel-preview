METHOD.md

Method

This is a local photographic pixel continuation of first_frame.png, using Python, NumPy, SciPy, and Pillow. No external models, scene references, rendering engine, network access, or subagents were used. The attached initial image was available visually; generated images were inspected only with numeric code. No visual review of generated outputs is claimed.

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code and records the measured scene and all 20 planned samples. The camera, table, background, black pipe, 30 lb kettlebell, and duck retain their original pixels. Only the ball, its moving shadow, and the table pixels originally obscured by them are modified.

The original ball and its shadow are removed using a small texture-guided Poisson solve. Adjacent wood pixels 18 rows below supply the grain gradients; the original pixels on the repair boundary determine color and lighting. The grey ball's actual photographed colors are separated with a soft elliptical silhouette. Its source cast shadow is recovered as RGB transmittance relative to the repaired table, restricted to the original shadow region. Bilinear, premultiplied-alpha resampling moves the ball and shadow. No new seam pattern, objects, text, camera movement, or lighting effects are added. The ball's photographic shading remains fixed; rotation of individual felt fibers is not explicitly synthesized at this resolution.

The ball rolls straight right at 220.8 projected pixels/s, contacts the left kettlebell surface at 0.25 s, and reverses with restitution 0.40. A constant 58 projected pixels/s² of rolling resistance brings it to rest at about 1.773 s. These are image-space parameters, not measured world speeds. The kettlebell's approximately 240:1 mass ratio to a standard tennis ball supports keeping its displacement below pixel resolution. Its source pixels occlude the contact sprite. The duck stays fixed and untouched. The collision is sampled at frame 01; its very short elastic deformation is not resolved at 4 fps. Frame 00 uses the initial image's RGB pixels exactly, without repair or compositing. Each of 20 samples is held for 0.25 s, for 5 s total playback.

Run python generate.py from this workspace to regenerate all artifacts. pixels.npz stores pixels, a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor with packed 0xRRGGBB colors. frames/00.png through 19.png are 256 × 144 RGB PNGs. inspection.png is a contact sheet. transition_check.png contains important motion frames and numeric transition captions, plus difference maps. These files are generated for the user's review and were not opened visually. index.html is a standalone embedded-pixel player running at 4 fps.

Validation in verification.json checks every PNG against every packed tensor slice, exact first-frame equality, exact pixels outside the ball-motion region, unchanged kettlebell/duck pixels, straight-line motion, contact position, and ball centers measured independently from the generated RGB colors, monotonic slowing rebound, a completely stationary final hold, and complete decoding of the HTML player's frame packets against the tensor. SHA-256 file hashes and per-transition changed-pixel counts are recorded. These checks do not constitute a perceptual review of the rendered continuation.
