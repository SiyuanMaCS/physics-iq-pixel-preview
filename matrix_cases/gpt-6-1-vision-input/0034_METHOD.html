Input observations and planned continuation

METHOD.md

Method and delivered artifacts

The continuation is an authored 2.5D pixel animation implemented in local Python. Its only scene sources are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The source photograph supplies the visible environment, painted block faces, and fine wood texture. NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV image operations, and SciPy filtering perform the numerical projection, texture sampling, rasterization, and saving. No network, external assets, trained models, external rendering/physics engines, or subagents were used.

Image construction

OBSERVATION.md was written before implementation. Six cuboids are calibrated to measured source-image lower corners, projected widths, depths, and heights. Their front, left, and top face textures are sampled from the original photograph by homography. The previously hidden narrow right face is reconstructed from the same block's painted front texture and receives orientation-dependent shading as it becomes visible. The broad front face retains its source coloration.

Each moved block is removed from its original silhouette and shadow region. OpenCV local inpainting reconstructs the revealed table, with a small amount of high-frequency grain copied numerically from an unobstructed source tabletop patch. Only hidden sections of the black platform are filled with a measured elliptical cylinder and matching dark surface colors. Source pixels outside the active cleanup regions remain fixed. Unstruck faces reuse the original image pixels.

Cuboid surfaces rotate about their lower left edges. Their transformed source textures are rasterized at three times the final resolution and reduced by area averaging. A common camera-depth buffer resolves occlusion between block faces and the arm. Soft cast and contact shadows are computed by projecting cuboid corners onto the tabletop and filtering the resulting masks. The camera, platform body, wall, and illumination do not move.

Motion

The dark wooden arm follows a horizontal circular path projected as an ellipse around (183.5,42.5). Its angular phase is 0.55 + 1.90*t radians, so it rotates clockwise continuously at approximately 109 degrees per second. The tip sweeps into the purple block at approximately 0.75 seconds.

Block motion uses analytic accelerating angular trajectories, 0.45*tau + 2.5*tau^2, constrained by exact separating-axis contact calculations for rigid rectangles in the row/height plane. Each following block activates when the preceding block first reaches its contact angle. Each angle is limited by contact with its neighbor. The yellow block lands on the table and has a tiny decaying rebound; the other blocks settle onto their neighbors. This is physically motivated authored motion, with geometric nonpenetration constraints.

Verification and inspection policy

Generated frames, sheets, and player were checked with local code and numeric measurements only. No generated image was opened with an image Read/View tool, and no visual review of the outputs is claimed. The supplied initial frame was visible in the user message.
