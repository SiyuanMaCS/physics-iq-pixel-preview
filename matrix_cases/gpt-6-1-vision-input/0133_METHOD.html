METHOD.md

Method

This is a deterministic continuation of the provided photograph using local 2D pixel operations. It does not use an image/video model, external renderer, network, references, or subagents. The attached first frame was the only visually inspected image. The generated frames and inspection sheets were checked through local code and numerical comparisons only; no visual review of them is claimed.

Image construction

generate.py reads the supplied RGB photograph. Source-coordinate polygons isolate the crumpled paper and the lower orange/black jaw sections. These polygons are written in the script from measurements of the initial photograph; no mask files are inputs. Locally fitted quadratic wall-color profiles and a little high-frequency texture copied from clean wall pixels repair the areas uncovered by the paper and moving jaws. Repair is confined to the original object region.

Premultiplied source-pixel layers preserve photographed crease detail and tool shading. The jaw tips move apart with smooth spatially varying remaps; the handle and upper mounting through image row 26 are copied exactly from the original in every frame. The paper moves with an affine translation, slight rotation, and a change in projected shape as it settles on the water. Warm contact color from the tool is gradually reduced after release, and lower folds darken modestly as they wet. The rest of the table, wall, bowl boundary, and glass remain fixed.

Motion and water

Frames are sampled at 0.25-second intervals. Paper release begins at 0.30 seconds. Its vertical fall follows the closed-form trajectory for gravity with linear drag, using acceleration 600 pixels/s² and terminal velocity 200 pixels/s. These are image-space parameters chosen for this scene, not calibrated world measurements. The continuous trajectory reaches the contact height at approximately 0.7922 seconds.

At contact, the paper tilts and becomes partly immersed, then follows a small exponentially damped bob with slow drift. It remains floating for the remainder of the five-second playback. This is an analytic approximation of buoyancy, rather than a coupled fluid or deformable-paper simulation.

A smooth elliptical mask confines water changes to the interior of the photographed bowl. Outgoing damped elliptical waves displace the existing water reflections by less than a pixel and add subtle normal-dependent light/dark rings. A soft nearby shadow, low-opacity vertically flipped reflection, brief small meniscus highlights, and lower-fold water tint connect the paper to the surface. There is no large splash. The glass edge and camera do not move.
