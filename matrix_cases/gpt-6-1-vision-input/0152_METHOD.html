METHOD.md

Method and validation

Completed a 20-frame RGB continuation at 256 × 144, with a constant 4 fps playback interval and a 5-second total display duration. Frame 00 uses the exact RGB pixels of first_frame.png. The ball rolls right from its supplied position, contacts the fabric box at frame 08 (2.00 s), rebounds slightly, and is stationary from frame 16 onward. The camera and original tube and box pixels remain fixed.

Actual generation method

generate.py performs deterministic local Python pixel synthesis using NumPy, Pillow, SciPy sampling/filtering, and OpenCV's Navier–Stokes image inpainting. It uses only first_frame.png as appearance input and the described event in prompt.txt. No network access, outside references, AI models, external scene/physics engine, or subagents are used.

  1. Reconstruct the original ball and shadow footprint with a feathered geometric mask. The fill combines 85% interpolation between nearby wood scanlines, retaining horizontal grain and a small adjacent texture residual, with 15% local OpenCV inpainting. Pixels beyond this local footprint retain the source background.
  2. Extract brown color and the visible pale seam from the original ball. Remove an approximate fixed light field to form a front-hemisphere surface texture, and replace contaminated rim colors with a source-calibrated brown. Mirror the observed hemisphere to supply the unseen back. Rotate these surface samples on an analytic sphere around the projected rolling axis, while applying fixed directional lighting. The unseen surface is an approximation derived from the single image.
  3. Move the ball using one-dimensional rolling motion with initial speed 49.9457 px/s and resistance 2.4 px/s². Collision uses the measured slanted boundary x = 209 + 0.53(y − 49), with the ball's ellipse support radius. The uncompressed contact center is x = 204.4914. At the impact instant, 4.5% width compression slightly increases height and adjusts the center to preserve tangency. Rebound restitution is 0.17, followed by braking of 4 px/s²; rest begins at 3.9187 s at x = 197.1287. The box stays stationary, consistent with a small ball hitting a heavier box.
  4. Draw a diffuse right/down cast shadow and a short contact shadow calibrated to the source's footprint. Both move with the ball and stop at the opaque box boundary. The ball gets a small amount of additional shading near the box.
  5. Composite at four spatial samples per native pixel axis. Average three temporal samples across a fixed 1/60-second exposure. Restore original box pixels along the boundary to preserve its antialiased edge. Native static background pixels are replicated during supersampling to prevent resampling drift.

The motion is rendered procedurally; this is a continuation inferred from a single frame, not a reconstruction of an observed later video. The supplied initial frame was available visually in the user message. Later generated outputs were inspected exclusively through local code and numerical measurements. Neither inspection.png nor transition_check.png was opened with an image Read/View tool, and no later visual review is claimed.

Validation results

All 20 PNGs were decoded and compared pixel-for-pixel with the uint32 tensor; every comparison passed. Frame 00 is exactly equal to the initial RGB image. All packed values fit in 24 RGB bits. The standalone HTML's embedded delta stream was decoded locally and reproduced every tensor frame exactly, with 20 frames and a 250 ms playback interval. Both contact-sheet PNGs and all frame PNGs decode successfully.

The analytic ball/box gap is nonnegative at every sampled frame and reaches zero at frame 08 (floating point residual approximately 4 × 10⁻¹⁴ px).
