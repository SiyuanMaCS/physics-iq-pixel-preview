This continuation uses local Python pixel operations, NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. It uses only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. It does not use a generative image/video model, a rendering engine, external references, network access, or subagents.

The original image is copied exactly into frame 00. The remaining frames use a stationary source background. Narrow masks remove the original blocks, shadows, and wooden arm where needed; OpenCV's local Telea inpainting fills exposed tabletop. A small previously hidden portion of the black cylinder is filled by sampling its observed opposite edge. Stationary blocks retain their photographed pixels until their individual fall begins.

The six blocks are projected cuboids. Their front and top faces are rectified from small quadrilaterals in the original image and warped onto the changing projected faces. Texture for a newly exposed long side comes from that same block's front face, with brightness varying as the side turns upward. Front, side, and top faces are painted in depth order. Shadows use projected ground footprints and Gaussian softening. Four-times spatial supersampling smooths the moving edges; the camera and source background stay fixed.

The wooden arm follows a clockwise ellipse in the tabletop projection, around approximately (202, 51), at a constant angular speed of 0.74 radians per second. Its tan shaft, dark connector, fixed hub, and soft shadow are drawn locally. The tip reaches purple first. Purple begins tilting at 0.30 seconds; successive blocks begin 0.23 seconds apart, moving from purple through blue, green, pink, orange, and yellow.

Each block rotates left about its lower table edge. An exponential angular ramp approximates the growing tilt of a damped gravitational instability. The first obstruction to each candidate angle is found by separating-axis tests against all blocks to its left and binary refinement. This maintains nonintersecting cross-sections and leaves the fallen blocks supported by their neighbors. The yellow block reaches the table, and the others settle at roughly 61–66 degrees from upright. The blocks are stationary from approximately 2.25 seconds onward, while the arm continues rotating.

This is an analytic, contact-constrained pixel animation, not a full rigid-body dynamics simulation. Contact timing and damping are chosen explicitly. Lower edges remain fixed, and newly exposed texture and hidden tabletop are approximations derived locally from the source.
