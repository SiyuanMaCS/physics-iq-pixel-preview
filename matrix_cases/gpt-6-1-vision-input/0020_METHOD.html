Pixel construction

Frame 00 is copied exactly from the original 256 × 144 RGB photograph. For later frames, a fixed background plate removes the original ball and its nearby diffuse shadow in a 1,016-pixel elliptical region. OpenCV Navier–Stokes inpainting supplies the reconstruction, blended with same-image horizontal wood texture and scanline color matching. The original image remains pixel-exact everywhere outside that repair and the moving ball/shadow footprint.

The moving tennis ball retains its photographed appearance. A radial matte fit to its green-minus-blue chroma measures center (86.3424, 51.5780), radius 7.7317 pixels, and edge softness 1.1808 pixels. Foreground recovery subtracts the reconstructed wood from partially covered edge pixels. A smoothed source foreground supplies fixed illumination, while the remaining small felt and seam variations rotate on a mathematical sphere. Unobserved rear texture is approximated by mirrored continuation of the available front texture. Thus the ball's texture comes from the supplied image rather than a new model or reference. Sphere sampling at four times resolution provides smooth subpixel motion, and the matte preserves the original photograph's soft edges.

A sum of three Gaussian attenuation fields provides a broad diffuse shadow, a smaller support shadow, and a tight contact shadow. These fields follow the ball and the local surface grade. The ramp, blue support, pipe, wood, and camera are otherwise fixed.
