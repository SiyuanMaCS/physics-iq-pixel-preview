Result and artifacts

The continuation contains 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, displayed at 4 fps for 5 seconds. The orange tennis ball continues from its supplied position outside the black pipe, rolls straight across the wooden tabletop, and reaches the right edge in the final sample. Camera, room, pipe, brown ball, and smaller red ball are fixed. The final ball contact remains on the tabletop; no fall or additional action is introduced.

  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and the 20-frame event plan written before generation.
  • generate.py: deterministic local generator and numerical verification.
  • pixels.npz: pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, each entry packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: lossless RGB frames.
  • inspection.png: contact sheet of selected samples.
  • transition_check.png: adjacent-frame pairs and amplified absolute RGB differences.

  • validation.json: numerical results, trajectory, and per-frame ball measurements.

Actual pixel construction

Only first_frame.png and prompt.txt supplied scene information. Generation uses local Python with NumPy, Pillow and SciPy, plus the supplied output helper. There is no network, external reference, pretrained model, external engine, or delegated agent. The only visually supplied image was the initial user attachment.

The original photograph supplies the fixed scene. A small analytic mask around the original moving ball and its shadow identifies the repair footprint. Wood pixels are interpolated from nearby clear columns in coordinates aligned with the local wood grain. Small real texture residuals from an adjacent clear wood patch are transferred to avoid a flat fill. A feather blends this repair into the existing table. Unrelated pixels remain the original source pixels.

A bounded least-squares fit estimates the source ball's soft elliptical silhouette and a simple diffuse radiance field. Its fitted center is (37.5441,44.7130), radii (6.9659,6.6739) pixels, and soft edge parameter 0.7285 pixels. This is a mathematical fit to source RGB samples. The photographed ball's foreground color is recovered by inverse alpha compositing against the reconstructed wood and estimated shadow. Low-opacity edge samples use the analytic limb color to limit amplified background contamination. This retains the source ball's actual warm color and directional shading.

For later frames, the recovered foreground is locally resampled and composited at four samples per pixel in each dimension. A normal-based sphere parameterization adds very weak rotating fuzz and curved seam detail. The modulation is divided by its initial value, keeping the extracted source colors as the baseline. The shading itself stays aligned to the fixed lighting. The result uses a photographed sprite with mathematical surface modulation, rather than a full scene renderer.

The world-space travel follows distance = speed × time × (1 − 0.5 × 0.010 × time), a small constant rolling resistance. A pinhole scale 1/(1 − 0.0021 × distance) projects that motion with a vanishing point at (-450,-21). Ball radius and tabletop contact scale together. Rotation is distance divided by the mean source radius. This produces a straight projected path with gradually larger apparent size toward the nearer right edge. The center progresses from approximately (37.54,44.71) to (225.00,69.98).

The shadow is an elongated soft Gaussian cast shadow plus a smaller contact term. Its position and scale follow the rolling ball. A tabletop reception mask clips it at the measured right edge, preventing a tabletop shadow from being pasted onto the floor. The ball silhouette may overhang the edge while its final contact remains supported.

Frame 00 is explicitly replaced with the original decoded RGB array after rendering; it is therefore pixel-exact, independently of approximation in the fitted sprite or wood reconstruction. The analytical reconstruction at the original position differs from the source ROI by a mean absolute 3.023 channel values out of 255; this measures the source-fitting approximation, not a discrepancy in saved frame 00.

Revisions and numerical inspection

Initial checks found a cast-shadow tail beyond the right tabletop boundary; it was clipped to the table. A broad stationary-object verification rectangle also included part of the moving lane. Its bound was narrowed to cover the stationary objects, which remain exact. A connected-component check was then added to measure the ball in every saved frame; a Python name collision in that added check was corrected. All final assertions pass.

No generated image, contact sheet, transition sheet, or player was opened through an image Read/View tool. Inspection consisted solely of local code and numerical checks. The saved inspection images are review artifacts, not evidence of a visual review.

Final verification results:

  • All 20 PNGs match their independently decoded uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor entries exactly.
  • Frame 00 exactly matches the source RGB pixels.
  • Embedded player data independently decodes to the same tensor for every frame.
  • Protected pipe, stationary-ball, sofa and right-floor regions remain pixel-exact.
  • All changes occur within the old ball repair footprint or the current moving ball/shadow neighborhood.
  • The projected contact path is straight: cross-product line residual below 6.4e-12.
  • Every horizontal center step is positive; step sizes range from 7.401 to 13.086 pixels because of perspective.
  • The measured warm-colored connected component follows the planned center with maximum error 0.767 pixels; its area increases consistently with the projected sphere size.
  • The final contact lies 0.102 pixels inside the measured right tabletop edge.

The geometry, source matte, repair surface and weak surface details are inferred approximations from a single photograph. The checks establish artifact consistency, locality and continuous motion; they do not constitute a visual assessment of later frames.
