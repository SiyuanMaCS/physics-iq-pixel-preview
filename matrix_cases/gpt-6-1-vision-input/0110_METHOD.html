METHOD.md

Method and verification

This sequence was generated from first_frame.png with local Python, NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV pixel operations. The only scene inputs were that image and prompt.txt. No external images, networks, AI models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

The input frame is preserved exactly as frame 00. For subsequent frames, a narrow measured mask removes the original shaft, charred head, and tiny flame using local Telea inpainting. The original wooden and charred pixels become an antialiased sprite, sampled with inverse affine mapping. A cubic easing curve drives a 25-degree downward rotation about the glass opening near (110,43), plus 12 pixels of axial advance. This makes the match descend through the open lip. An analytic continuation keeps the shaft connected to its off-frame holder where needed.

The small flame uses a softly edged warm-colored analytic shape with a brighter core and a localized glow. It follows the burning section while remaining vertically oriented. The flame contracts at frame 04 and is absent from frame 05 onward. A subpixel damped elliptical ripple is restricted to the original projected water surface. The submerged shaft receives a small lateral refraction shift, reduced contrast, and a tint sampled from the water background. Existing bright glass-edge reflections are composited over the object. The last six frames hold the extinguished match still.

These are source-pixel compositing and analytic optical approximations, not a full fluid simulation or a newly filmed demonstration. The camera, table, wall, glass, and base remain fixed. Changes are restricted to the original match removal region, its moving footprint, and the small water disturbance.
