METHOD.md

Method and verification

This continuation was generated with local Python pixel operations from first_frame.png and the supplied text only. No external images, trained models, physics/rendering engines, network calls or subagents were used. The attached initial frame was available visually. Generated frames and diagnostic sheets were checked through code and numeric measurements; they were not opened with an image Read/View tool. The generated contact sheets are provided for the user's review, not evidence of a visual review by the agent.

Image construction

The input's orange silhouette was measured numerically. A least-squares ellipse fit to its side boundaries estimated center (144.407, 24.838), horizontal radius 27.733 pixels and vertical radius 29.589 pixels. The upper cap is outside the image, and the lower cap is partly hidden by the initial crate rim.

The moving ball uses the actual input's RGB surface texture, including the dark panel seams and photographic lighting. Inverse sphere-coordinate remapping supplies a small yaw and tilt, with subpixel antialiasing at the silhouette. Narrow unseen polar regions use nearest valid source colors and a darker orange cap extension. The ball's illumination is reduced inside the crate. A short, slight vertical compression follows sampled impacts.

The original ball's footprint is removed from a static copy of the input. Rowwise gradients between visible wall samples reconstruct the gray backdrop, with low-amplitude deterministic source-scale noise. A short hidden portion of the crate's top rim is repaired by copying adjacent original rim pixels with the measured screen slope. Other wall, table and crate pixels come directly from the input.

The crate's projected top edge is around y=49–54. Its upper handles and lower diamond lattice are source-derived foreground occluders. A brightness-based aperture matte identifies visible holes and preserves photographed bar edges, structural ribs and rim pixels. The ball appears behind these crate faces and through their selected openings. This is a conservative aperture approximation: dark holes that could not be distinguished reliably from dark bars remain occluded. A restrained local shadow follows the ball along the rim. The crate and table have no simulated displacement.

Motion

Motion is a piecewise analytic free fall with gravity of 230 pixels/s², released from rest. The ball's internal bottom contact is placed at center y=77.0 pixels. The first impact occurs at 0.6735 seconds, with an incoming speed of approximately 154.9 pixels/s. Restitution starts at 0.57 and decreases by 0.08 after each impact. The first rebound rises to center y≈60.1. Subsequent bounces lose height and the ball rests at approximately 1.9717 seconds; its brief shape relaxation is complete by frame 09.

The image uses only the projected physics of this single ball. The depth, internal bottom height, material response and hidden polar colors are estimates from the input, rather than recovered 3D measurements. A small lateral drift of 0.72 pixels/s and small texture rotation stop when the ball settles.

Twenty frames sample time t=0, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds and play at a constant 4 fps for 5 seconds. Frame 00 is copied verbatim from the input, bypassing the reconstruction.
