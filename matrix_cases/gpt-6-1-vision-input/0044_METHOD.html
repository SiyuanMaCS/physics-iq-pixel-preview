cut-paper · 生成方法

This continuation was generated with local Python pixel operations using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. It is a procedural deformation of the photographed objects, with an explicitly prescribed tear and damped motion. It does not use a video generation model, an external reference, a physics engine, or a recovered 3D scene.

generate.py identifies the green paper and the two blue-and-black tools by source-pixel color and connected components. It retains the holes inside the tool loops. A narrow color-line matte extracts soft object boundaries and removes tabletop color from partially covered edge pixels. The source paper texture, its dark folded edge, and the detailed tool pixels supply the foreground colors.

Hidden tabletop pixels are reconstructed by local OpenCV inpainting. High-frequency grain comes from unobstructed pixels in the lower part of the same input image, sampled along a diagonal grain direction and blended into the repaired region. Old shadow footprints are corrected gradually during the first six frames. Soft offset shadows derived from the moving foreground masks then follow the paper and tools. The background remains fixed beyond these local repairs and moving shadows.

The grips translate horizontally in opposite directions, reaching nine pixels of travel each. Before rupture, a common deformation stretches the projected sheet horizontally and reduces the vertical twist slightly while maintaining a continuous neck. From frame 07, an irregular subpixel crack advances from the lower edge of the neck toward its upper ligament. The sheet remains connected through frame 10. Frame 11 breaks that ligament and produces two paper pieces. Their free edges recoil by different amounts and rotate about their respective grip points. Analytic exponential damping gives a small settling flutter. The prescribed deformation changes projected area as the paper bends; it is an approximation to paper motion rather than a constitutive fracture simulation.

Inverse mappings preserve the photographed paper texture on each piece. Boundaries and the jagged tear are sampled at three times the output resolution and reduced by area averaging. A faint lighter green line along the newly exposed cut represents paper fibers. The tools retain their original shape as they translate, and each paper grip is fixed to the corresponding moving tool grip. No loose third piece or other object is added.

The animation contains 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144. Playback advances every 250 milliseconds, giving 4 fps and a five-second sequence. Frame 00 is copied directly from the input without processing; sample times run from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds. The player and supplied inspection-sheet layout label the ends of the displayed quarter-second intervals.

Artifacts

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: the 20 RGB frames.
  • pixels.npz: the pixels uint32 array, shape (144, 256, 20), with axes (y, x, frame) and values packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: six important frame pairs with amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • index.html: a standalone player with play, pause, replay, seek, and loop controls; pixel data is embedded in the file.
  • OBSERVATION.md: initial measurements and the event plan, written before generation code.
  • generate.py: the reproducible generation and validation program.
  • validation.json: numerical results and per-frame geometry measurements.
  • .tmp/geometry_*.npz: numeric intermediate masks and shadow/background arrays.

Checks performed

Every saved PNG was decoded locally and checked for RGB mode, dimensions, and exact equality to its corresponding uint32 tensor slice. Frame 00 was checked for exact pixel equality to first_frame.png. The player’s embedded first frame and delta packets were independently decoded and compared with all 20 packed frames, and its 250 ms playback interval was checked.

Both the deformation masks and the rendered RGB green pixels were checked for connected components. The rendered sheet is one substantial green component through frame 10 and exactly two from frame 11 onward. Analytic grip attachment error is below 1e-5 pixels. The final representative torn edges have 40.07 pixels of horizontal separation and 41.30 pixels of total separation. The top eight rows, bottom 24 rows, and distant lower side regions remain exactly equal to the input in every frame. Per-transition mean absolute RGB changes peak at about 2.23 intensity levels during recoil and decline to about 0.15 at the end.
