balls-collide · 生成方法

This is a locally generated photographic pixel animation, not a new video capture or an external generative-model output. The only visual source is first_frame.png. The attached initial frame was visually inspected. All subsequent review used Python and numeric checks; inspection.png and transition_check.png were generated but were not opened with an image Read/View tool.

Pixel construction

The static background is the input photograph. Only the two original ball/shadow footprints are repaired. OpenCV Telea inpainting estimates their hidden tabletop lighting, and high-frequency wood grain is copied from unoccupied parts of the same image, translated along the visible grain direction. The repair is feathered within these small regions; pixels outside the animation support are unchanged.

The balls use RGB sprites extracted from the captured spheres, with a smooth radial alpha profile and approximate edge decontamination against the repaired wood. Premultiplied-alpha interpolation provides fractional-pixel translation. The captured directional shading stays fixed under the fixed light. A very small analytic sphere-coordinate texture modulation rotates about the rolling axis; this supplies subtle surface motion without inventing a conspicuous tennis seam. Soft elliptical cast and contact shadows are fitted numerically to the original local shadow RGB and translated with each sphere. A warm shadow tint approximates the captured lighting. Three temporal samples approximate a 1/60-second exposure.

Motion

Initial blue and yellow centers: (120.2,71.2) and (185.8,40.5) pixels. Their equal incoming projected speed is 22.251296 px/s. The centers move on their common straight line and contact at t=1.25 s (frame 05), at separation 16.8 px. Effective projected radii are 8.3 and 8.5 px. Equal effective masses and restitution 0.92 reverse both velocities symmetrically; outgoing speed is 20.471192 px/s. Momentum is conserved and collision kinetic energy is reduced by the restitution squared. Friction and perspective size changes are neglected over this brief, short travel. The balls stay on the tabletop.

Artifacts and verification

20 RGB PNG frames are sampled at 0.00,0.25,...,4.75 s and displayed at 4 fps for 5 seconds. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the original input. pixels.npz contains a uint32 tensor named pixels of shape (144,256,20), packed as 0xRRGGBB. index.html is the self-contained player built with the provided artifact helper. inspection.png is a contact sheet. transition_check.png shows the first transition and both transitions surrounding contact, along with RGB difference maps.

generate.py checks every PNG against the packed tensor, independently decodes all HTML delta packets, checks frame 00 equality, confirms constant equal speeds on each side of collision, confirms the radius-sum contact and absence of overlap, checks color-component centers, and checks exact static scenery outside all moving/repaired footprints. Detailed numeric results are saved in .tmp/numeric_checks.json. Run python generate.py to regenerate and recheck.

Limitations: hidden wood is reconstructed locally rather than recovered; shadows are analytic approximations; ball surfaces are captured sprites with subtle synthetic texture motion rather than full recovered three-dimensional materials.
