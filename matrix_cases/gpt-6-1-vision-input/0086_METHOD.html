METHOD.md

Method

The animation uses the supplied photograph as its fixed pixel background. It was generated locally with Python, NumPy, Pillow and SciPy. There are no external images, pretrained models, external engines, network requests, or subagents.

generate.py isolates the duck using a restricted yellow-color connected component, fills holes so the eyes and beak stay inside the silhouette, and includes the shaded lower body. The floor boundary is an analytic polygon measured from the first image. Liquid masks and highlights are evaluated at four samples per pixel in each direction and composited over repeated source pixels. The samples are then averaged, preserving every unmodified source pixel exactly.

The incoming red stream follows the already visible sloping thread, from the tap toward the crown. Small changes in width and highlight phase suggest continuous motion. A connected flattened impact cap and a translucent coating follow the duck's original shading. Two main curved rivulets follow the head and body sides; narrow central runoff, wet reflections and short runnels complete the path into the tray. Eye and beak masks attenuate the coating so recognizable facial details remain.

The pool grows with an analytic accumulated-flow parameter. Its elliptical footprint is foreshortened, its perimeter has fixed small lobes, and its shallow edge is blended with the tray surface. Its color combines red transmission with subdued source texture and environmental reflection. Expanding elliptical ripples originate near the runoff contacts. Small droplets have deterministic birth times, initial velocities and gravity-based screen-space trajectories. Their shape stretches with speed, and they disappear at the tray surface.

This is a two-dimensional photographic pixel-compositing approximation. It does not solve three-dimensional fluid dynamics or calculate a measured liquid volume. The pool stays shallow and the duck remains in place. Lighting, basket, dispenser, tray rim and camera registration are preserved.

Timing and artifacts

The output is 20 RGB PNG frames at 256 × 144 and four frames per second, giving five seconds of playback. Samples run from t=0.00 to t=4.75 seconds. Frame 00 is exactly the original decoded RGB image.
