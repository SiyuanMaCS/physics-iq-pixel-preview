METHOD.md

Method

The sequence was generated entirely in local Python from first_frame.png and prompt.txt, using NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV pixel operations. No external model, reference, engine, network resource, or subagent was used. The supplied initial image was observed through the user attachment. All subsequent inspection used local code and numeric checks; generated images were not opened with image Read/View tools.

The ball is already outside the black tube in the initial image. The continuation therefore begins at that exact visible position, rolls it rightward into the olive fabric box, and gives it a short, dissipative rebound before it stops. The camera, tube, lighting context, and box body remain fixed.

Pixel construction

  1. Preserve frame 00 by copying the original RGB pixels exactly.
  2. Remove the original ball and its shadow in a compact local mask using OpenCV's Navier–Stokes image inpainting. Restore small wood texture inside the reconstructed region using a high-frequency component sampled from another clean area of the same first frame. Feather that texture near the mask boundary. All other background pixels retain their original values.
  3. Fit a soft, elliptical cast-shadow attenuation field to source pixels outside the ball silhouette. The fitted shadow is translated with the ball and applied to the table's existing RGB values. It terminates at the measured box edge.
  4. Infer a soft foreground coverage matte using the source ball's measured ellipse, brown colour, and source/background differences. Preserve an opaque inner body and recover foreground RGB by unpremultiplying the source photograph. Undefined surrounding colours are extended from the nearest valid object pixel for interpolation.
  5. Translate the photographed foreground with subpixel interpolation. Rotate a small source-derived texture component using analytic spherical coordinates; retain the source's dominant stationary shading. At the impact sample the ball compresses horizontally by 5.5%, with a 1.925% vertical expansion anchored at its bottom. The box stays fixed.
  6. Round and clip RGB results to uint8. The generation uses source photography and mathematical pixel compositing rather than a reconstructed 3D scene.

Motion

Frame times are t=n/4 for n=0…19; each sample is displayed for 0.25 seconds, producing 5 seconds at 4 fps. The initial center is (121.25, 62.35). The box edge is fitted from source pixels as x=0.87271557y+151.67996568. The ellipse's tangent to that edge determines the contact position, with a small fuzzy-edge allowance: x=195.60194 at t=2 seconds.

Initial horizontal velocity is 43.17597 pixels per second, with constant rolling deceleration of 6 pixels per second squared. After collision, restitution is 0.43 and the leftward rebound decelerates at 6.6 pixels per second squared. Motion stops at t≈4.03116 seconds with center x=181.98740. Frames 17–19 are identical stationary frames. These are analytic image-plane motion parameters chosen for the demonstration; they are not recovered physical measurements of mass, friction, or world scale.

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png contain the 20 RGB frames at 256×144.

Verification checks exact equality of frame 00 with the source, all PNGs with the uint32 tensor, tensor shape and type, the total frame count, monotonic approach and rebound positions, the contact sample, stationary final frames, unchanged tube and box interior, and restriction of all changes to the ball/shadow corridor. PNG modes and dimensions, both saved diagnostic-image dimensions, and the presence of every required artifact are checked numerically.

These checks establish artifact consistency and the planned image-plane event. They do not constitute a visual review of the generated images. Reconstruction of the covered wood, shadow fitting, the foreground matte, and contact deformation are approximate local pixel synthesis.
