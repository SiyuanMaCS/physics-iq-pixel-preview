Pixel construction

Source face quadrilaterals are rectified into painted-wood textures and warped into each new pose. Narrow cuboid surfaces are drawn with source-derived paint colours, small deterministic texture variations, soft bevel shading, and analytic face visibility. The blue surface previously concealed by the tool is filled from its own visible paint. The red support keeps its photographed front and original table shadow; only its newly uncovered top and upper front are reconstructed.

The moving silhouettes are removed from a fixed source background. Exposed wall pixels use a smooth polynomial fit to the unobstructed source wall plus small source-derived noise. Table repairs combine neighbouring colour estimates with wood-grain residuals copied from an unobstructed strip of the same source frame. The narrow interrupted horizon uses neighbouring source pixels. The rest of the source background remains fixed.

The two orange/black jaw sprites come from colour-based source segmentation. They open by small opposing translations and withdraw upward, clearing the image by 1.25 seconds. They are composited separately from the blue block. Placement frames gradually replace source object pixels with the texture reconstruction, preventing a sudden wholesale change in appearance.

All object faces are rasterized at four times the output resolution, then reduced with area filtering. Rapid-motion frames average three closely spaced physical poses spanning 12 milliseconds, giving modest motion blur. Source perspective taper is eased toward the simulated contact-face geometry as the narrow bearing face rotates into view; this avoids visible sinking at the tabletop. Soft projected shadows vary with object height and location.

Motion and contacts

generate.py contains the complete custom planar rigid-body integrator. It represents three finite rectangles on a table, with scaled dimensions red 18 × 40, yellow 60 × 12, and blue 12 × 60. The initially loaded centres are x = 0, 9, and 34 relative to the red support. The blue block's load extends beyond the support footprint.

The release occurs at 0.75 seconds. Simulation uses 500 steps per second, gravity of 210 image-scale units per second squared, SAT separation tests, clipped face-contact manifolds, and 14 sequential impulse iterations per step. Contact friction is 0.48, with modest restitution and air damping. The unforced collision calculation keeps the red support upright. Yellow rotation leads the fall. The blue piece initially drops nearly upright, contacts the table, and then topples right. All poses are sampled on the fixed 4 fps timeline. Confirmed resting poses are held for the final frames to suppress subpixel solver jitter.
