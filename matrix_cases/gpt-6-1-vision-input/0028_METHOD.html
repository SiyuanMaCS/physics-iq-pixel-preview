The clip is a local Python continuation of the supplied photograph. It uses NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV pixel operations, and SciPy's distance transform. No external images, learned models, rendering engines, network resources, or subagents were used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generate.py, with measurements from the attached initial image and a twenty-frame event plan. Numeric pixel sampling then refined the sphere centers and antialiased silhouette sizes. The final motion samples, including the measured collision time, are in motion.json.

Pixel construction

The background is the original photograph. Small elliptical regions containing the original balls and their shadows are filled using local OpenCV inpainting. High-frequency wood grain from a nearby part of the same tabletop is added to the fill. The pipe, furniture, camera framing, table edges, and all pixels outside the swept ball corridor remain fixed.

Each ball is extracted from the source photograph with a soft elliptical matte. Foreground colors are recovered against the reconstructed tabletop and contact shadow. A nearest-valid-pixel fill repairs the small rims hidden where the brown and orange balls touch. Broad photographic shading stays fixed in screen orientation. Fine photographic texture rotates using analytic sphere normals as the balls roll; unavailable rear texture is mirrored from the available hemisphere. This is photographic pixel compositing with approximate spherical texture motion, not a reconstructed three-dimensional scene.

The original soft cast and contact shadows are extracted as negative RGB residuals, with the sphere interior inpainted. Shadows move beneath the spheres. The two resting target balls retain their original pixels until the collision. Afterward all spheres are composited in depth order. A very small size adjustment follows their movement toward the back of the tabletop.

Motion

The grey ball is already outside the pipe in frame 00, so the continuation begins at its visible position. A one-dimensional, equal-mass contact model runs at 2,400 steps per second and is sampled at 4 fps. Unilateral spring/damper forces act between adjacent balls, including both contacts together while the brown/orange pair is touching. This transfers the grey ball's impulse through the brown ball to the orange ball. Dry rolling resistance reduces speed without reversing motion at rest.

The grey ball's initial image speed is 21.2 pixels/second; rolling resistance is 2.1 pixels/second squared. The image trajectory follows the pipe-to-brown direction, with a vertical slope of approximately −0.1023. The collision occurs at approximately 1.465 seconds, between frame 05 and frame 06. The grey ball slows to rest near the collision site. The brown ball rolls a shorter distance and reaches rest; the orange ball travels furthest and retains a small forward speed in the final frame. There is no camera movement, cut, added object, or bounce off the tabletop.
