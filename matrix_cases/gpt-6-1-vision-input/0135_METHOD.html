OBSERVATION.md

Scene observations and event plan

The input is a 256 × 144 RGB image. The view is fixed, with a gray wall above a wooden tabletop. A clear glass bowl is centered near (126, 103); its upper rim spans approximately x=73–179 and y=69–128. The visible water surface is an oblique ellipse centered around (126, 104). The orange and black grabber enters from the top near x=146, with its fork around (134, 24). Its left jaw curves to approximately (111, 46), and its right jaw ends near (142, 47). A pale crumpled paper wad, roughly 24 × 36 pixels, is held between the jaws at center (129, 46), with its lowest folds around y=63. The light comes predominantly from the upper left. The wall has smooth illumination and the glass carries bright, thin curved highlights.

Only the grabber jaws, released paper, its local shadow/reflection, and the water response will change. Use the input pixels as the source of the material texture, preserve the table, bowl, wall and shaft, and reconstruct only the small wall area uncovered by the tool/paper. The paper falls under gravity after release, lands inside the bowl, briefly depresses/bobs in the water, and remains afloat as the ripples fade. Its apparent shape foreshortens on landing; it does not vanish or sink immediately. The tool remains suspended with open jaws.

Planned method and review

Local Python only: hand-described geometric masks refined using input pixel values, source-pixel cutouts, alpha compositing and affine warps, smooth local wall reconstruction, a gravity trajectory and a damped floating response, and small analytic water ripples. No model, engine, network or external imagery. Later artifacts will be checked with array statistics, frame differences, object coordinates, boundary checks, file decoding and tensor/PNG equality. No later image will be opened with an image viewing tool.

METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is a deterministic, local Python pixel animation from first_frame.png. It uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV for array processing, drawing masks, image resampling, local inpainting, and file encoding. It uses no network, reference images, external AI models, simulation engines, rendering engines, or subagents.

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code. The initial frame was supplied visually by the user. No generated frame, contact sheet, or transition sheet was opened through an image Read/View tool or visually reviewed. All output inspection was performed by local code and numeric checks.

Static scene and moving objects

The source image supplies all background and material pixels. Geometric masks describe the left jaw, right jaw, and paper. A small mask covers only the initial tool/paper footprint against the wall. OpenCV inpainting initializes this wall region, then 1,600 iterations of a discrete Laplace solve interpolate the surrounding wall illumination. A weak high-frequency residual copied from nearby unobstructed wall pixels restores grain. The table and glass bowl are retained from the input.

The upper shaft stays fixed. Jaw sprites rotate around the top fork with a smooth opening actuator curve, reaching +19 and −23 degrees by 0.42 seconds. Source color and contrast suppress neutral wall/paper pixels within the jaw outlines. This refinement was added after numerical source-matte checks found approximately 39 neutral pixels in the left geometric outline and 14 in the right. The refined neutral areas are approximately 1.4 and 0.9 pixels respectively. The texture itself remains from the input.

The paper uses its input folds, shading, and silhouette. A small local inpaint repairs source pixels contaminated by the adjacent orange jaw. Premultiplied cubic affine resampling moves the sprites without creating background-colored fringes. These masks and all coordinates are manually specified from this case's initial frame; no masks were imported.

Motion and water

Release occurs at 0.18 seconds. The airborne vertical displacement is 0.5 × 328 × (t − 0.18)^2 pixels, with a small horizontal drift and rotation. Contact is modeled at 0.68 seconds, so frame 02 shows the paper falling and frame 03 shows water contact. At the nominal contact time, the uncompressed source paper's lower edge reaches approximately y=105.

After contact, affine foreshortening and brief compression shorten the apparent paper height to about 77% of its initial height. Its floating center settles near (130, 95); damped sinusoidal bobbing and rocking decay over roughly a second, and horizontal drift slows exponentially. The paper remains visible through frame 19, with an alpha area near 558 pixels. Its lower few pixels darken and become slightly translucent to represent the wetted, submerged edge.

An analytic ellipse confines water effects to the bowl interior. Three decaying wave bands refract existing water pixels by less than a pixel and add faint paired light/dark arcs. A soft contact shadow and weak, vertically compressed reflection remain beneath the paper. Four small analytic droplets appear only at the first contact frame. There is no fluid solver: these are restrained local pixel warps and shading functions. Paper shape changes, buoyancy, wetting, and water ripples are approximations based on the single view.

Files and reproducibility

Run python generate.py from this workspace. It writes:

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: pixels, a uint32 array with shape (144, 256, 20), using 0xRRGGBB values.
  • inspection.png: eight sampled frames in a labeled contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: the input plus 19 amplified adjacent-frame difference maps.
  • index.html: a self-contained player with 250 ms frame intervals, play/pause, replay, seek, and looping.
  • .tmp/motion.json and .tmp/verification.json: motion values and numerical validation results.

The sequence is 5 seconds at 4 fps. Frame 00 is displayed at time zero, and frame 19 starts at 4.75 seconds.

Verification

The generator decodes every saved PNG and checks exact equality against the packed uint32 tensor. It also decodes the embedded HTML player and applies every delta packet, checking every reconstructed frame against the same tensor. All 20 frames pass. Frame 00 is pixel-identical to first_frame.png. The upper 15 image rows remain identical throughout. Every changed pixel is within the restricted tool/paper/water setup region; no changes occur elsewhere.

The airborne paper alpha area remains near 711 pixels. Floating frames retain 558–576 pixels, and the final paper bounds are approximately x=116–143 and y=80–108. Mean absolute RGB differences between adjacent frames decline from 1.35 at contact to 0.020 at the last transition (on a 0–255 channel scale), consistent with the intended settling motion. These checks establish file integrity, change locality, paper persistence, and the intended time sequence; they do not constitute visual review.
