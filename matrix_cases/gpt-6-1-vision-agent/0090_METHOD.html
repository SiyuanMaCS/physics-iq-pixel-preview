METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence is a local Python pixel animation built exclusively from first_frame.png and the action in prompt.txt. It does not use a generative model, external images, network services, a 3D renderer, or another agent.

The camera, glass, blue spigot, dispenser, woven stand, tray, table, and background retain the supplied photograph. Frame 00 is copied directly from the RGB input. The input glass is already full, so the continued pour is represented by a short red jet, disturbed liquid at the rim, thin rivulets and a wet film down the glass, and accumulating overflow on the tray.

generate.py evaluates fluid masks and shading on a four-times supersampled pixel grid. Alpha compositing preserves the original image beneath the transparent wet film. The jet and rivulets use continuous-time analytic paths and changing narrow highlights; the descending front of each rivulet follows a gravity-like quadratic travel law. Surface ripples use radial traveling sine waves with spatial attenuation. These are visual approximations, not a numerical solution of the Navier–Stokes equations.

The shallow puddle uses projected horizontal-plane geometry. Its squared radius grows linearly after overflow reaches the foot of the glass, approximating constant inflow into a shallow pool. A smoothly irregular boundary, darker thin edges, local tray texture, soft directional lighting, and a warped and blurred reflection sampled from the original photograph give its surface depth and gloss. The glass silhouette occludes the rear of the pool. The pool remains inside the baking tray. No additional objects are introduced.

The supersampled composites are reduced by averaging to RGB uint8 at 256 × 144. The photographic input is replicated on the supersampled grid so averaging returns unchanged pixels exactly outside the animated fluid. The run is deterministic. artifact_io.py serializes the 20 frames into a uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor of shape (144, 256, 20), writes the PNGs, builds the inspection sheet, and embeds the same pixel sequence in a standalone HTML player. The player runs at four frames per second, giving five seconds of playback. Frame sample times are 0.00–4.75 seconds; the player labels each displayed frame by the end of its 0.25-second display interval.
