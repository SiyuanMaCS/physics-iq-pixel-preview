OBSERVATION.md

Scene observations and measurements

Input is a 256 × 144 RGB image. The camera is fixed, looking down obliquely at a wooden tabletop. The back table edge runs approximately from (0,53) through (80,28) toward the right; the upper background is a pale blue-gray wall. The black circular platform occupies approximately x=191–255, y=42–85. Its top is an ellipse centered near (230,55); the rotating wooden arm is attached at approximately (211,57). A small green mounting piece sits over the hub. The arm extends left to (151,65), about 61 image pixels long, and is about 1.3 pixels wide.

There are seven dominoes in two separated, collinear groups. Four distant dominoes stand at approximate base centers (67,63), (78,66), (89,68), and (104,71), alternating black, white, black, white. Three nearer dominoes have base centers near (135,79), (147,82), and (158,84), alternating black, white, black from left to right. Their visible heights are about 20–23 pixels. The rightmost black domino is already slightly inclined toward the right; the arm's tip touches its upper region. The near group progresses leftward and slightly away from the camera. The gap between the near black domino and the distant white domino is approximately 31 pixels along the table, greater than the projected reach of a fallen domino. Thus the fall should stop at the gap.

The original image supplies all static pixels. Only the arm, its attached green mount, the three near dominoes, and their local shadows will change. No camera motion, cuts, text, hands, or new objects are added. Clockwise arm rotation is interpreted on the platform's horizontal plane: from its initial left-facing position the tip moves toward the back of the table before circling toward the right.

METHOD.md

Generation method

This is a local, deterministic pixel animation made with Python, NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. Only first_frame.png and prompt.txt were used as scene inputs. No network, external reference images, pretrained models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Static scene and reconstruction

Frame 00 is an exact RGB copy of the supplied image. The wall, table perimeter, four distant dominoes, and platform are retained from the image. The areas originally covered by the three near dominoes and their shadows are restored using a donor patch translated 31 pixels left and 35 pixels down along the table grain. A polynomial illumination fit to neighboring unobstructed wood adjusts the donor's brightness. Narrow arm and tape regions are locally inpainted. The small platform-rim area affected by the original arm shadow is repaired using the measured sheared elliptical edge, a fitted top-surface color gradient, and nearby side-surface pixels. These are localized pixel edits, not a newly generated background.

Motion and appearance

The dominoes are analytical cuboids projected onto the image with a table direction of (1, 0.28), a width direction near (6.3, -4), and vertical direction (0, -1). Each rotates around its bottom width edge, preserving its attachment to the tabletop. The nearest black domino begins from a 19-degree inclination measured from the image. Its neighbor starts falling at 0.17 seconds; the last domino in the near group starts at 0.43 seconds. Accelerating easing curves approximate gravity, with a small damped settling motion after contact. The final angles are 90, 82.7, and 77.5 degrees, producing an overlapping fallen stack. This is a manually timed, physically motivated rigid-body approximation; it is not a numerical collision simulation.

The tabletop gap exceeds the leading fallen domino's reach, so the distant group stays upright. The stick and its green mounting tape rotate clockwise around (211, 57), at 61 degrees per second. Its horizontal-plane circle projects to an ellipse with a 0.375 vertical scale. The stick may temporarily extend beyond the image's right edge, as a fixed camera would show. Its initial left-facing orientation progresses toward the back, right, and then front of the platform.

Domino surfaces use locally generated color gradients, low-amplitude texture, changing illumination, and faint bevel highlights. Polygonal faces are rendered at four times the output resolution, softened, and downsampled. Contact and cast shadows follow the domino orientation and height. The arm has a narrow shadow and a cylindrical highlight. Feathered masks combine the changed regions with original static pixels.

Outputs and verification

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty RGB images at 256 × 144.
  • index.html: self-contained pixel player, holding each frame for 250 milliseconds, totaling 5 seconds.
  • inspection.png: eight representative frames.
  • transition_check.png: the first eight frames and amplified adjacent-frame differences.
  • generate.py: reproducible generation and verification code.
  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and the twenty-frame event plan written before coding.

Every delivered PNG was decoded and compared exactly against its corresponding packed tensor slice. Frame 00 was independently checked for exact pixel equality with the input. The tensor range, shape, and dtype were checked. The standalone player's reconstructed frame packets were also checked against the tensor.

Visual review and revisions

Image tools were used to inspect an enlarged initial image, near-domino details, the first inspection and transition sheets, the restored background, close-up falling dominoes, and the platform rim. The first pass revealed a donor-patch speck and an inpainted platform-rim irregularity. The donor translation was revised to avoid sampling original domino pixels, and the affected rim was repaired analytically. The revised background, inspection sheet, and transition sheet were viewed again. The final review shows a continuous clockwise arm rotation, three falling dominoes, a stable fallen stack, and four standing dominoes beyond the gap.
