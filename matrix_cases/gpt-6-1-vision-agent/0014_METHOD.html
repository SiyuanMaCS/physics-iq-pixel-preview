ball-in-basket · 生成方法

This is a local photographic pixel animation made with Python, NumPy, Pillow, SciPy interpolation, and OpenCV raster operations. It uses only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. No network, reference imagery, learned model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator and records the measured scene and all twenty planned samples. Run python generate.py in this workspace to regenerate the deliverables and perform the numerical checks.

Photographic layers

The supplied frame is copied exactly for frame 00. For subsequent frames, the ball's former position on the wall is repaired by a cubic color surface fitted to nearby visible wall pixels. A broad, smooth blend removes the original blurred silhouette, and fine texture is copied from a nearby clear part of the same wall. The hidden section of the crate's rear top lip is completed with a narrow dark raster patch. The initial repair left a faint circular trace; visual inspection prompted a wider blend and a correction to the restored rim. A texture sampling offset was also adjusted to avoid copying an edge residual.

The moving ball retains the source photograph's orange surface, curved seams, and highlights. Its originally occluded lower cap is completed by extending photographic colors at corresponding horizontal chord positions, with lower-cap shading and two continued seams. An antialiased silhouette and the small inflation nub form the moving ball layer. Bilinear pixel interpolation supplies translation, slight rotation, and temporary contact deformation.

The crate remains a stationary photographic foreground. Bright handle and upper grille openings are measured from the source luminance. The dark lower grille openings are traced with two measured diagonal rib families, intersected with the original rib brightness and front-panel boundaries. The ball is composited only through those openings, preserving the solid rim, ribs, side panels, and bottom panel. Interior shading reduces the light on the ball as it descends. The tabletop, wall outside the repair region, and frame edges remain the original pixels.

Motion and sampling

The opening appears wider than the ball, so the selected continuation is a drop into the crate, followed by damped rebounds on its internal floor. With downward-positive image coordinates, the initial center is approximately (132.4, 37.7), the resting center is y=82, and gravity is 1600 image pixels/s². The first floor contact occurs at about 0.235 seconds. Restitution is 0.72 for the first rebound and 0.54 for later rebounds. Each airborne segment is a gravity parabola. A brief velocity-dependent flattening represents the inflatable surface at contact. The center drifts about one pixel sideways and the texture rotates by about five degrees while settling. Motion is frozen from frame 06 onward.

The sequence has 20 RGB frames at 256×144, sampled at t=0.00, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds. Displaying each frame for 0.25 seconds gives five seconds at a constant 4 fps. The camera and framing stay fixed throughout. Hidden crate depth, material response, and the completed ball cap are assumptions inferred from one photograph, rather than recovered three-dimensional measurements.

Artifacts and verification

  • generate.py: deterministic pixel generator and checks.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 array with shape (144, 256, 20), packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: RGB PNG sequence.
  • inspection.png: all twenty frames with release-relative timestamps.
  • transition_check.png: exact-source comparison and all nineteen adjacent absolute differences, with difference intensity multiplied by three.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-data player, with constant 250 ms playback intervals and frame seeking.
  • verification.json: machine-readable check results and sampled trajectory.

Every PNG was reloaded and compared exactly with its tensor slice. Frame 00 was separately compared with the supplied image. Every embedded HTML delta packet was decoded and checked against the same tensor. The generator also verifies that the outer scene remains fixed and frames 06–19 are identical.

Visual review was performed using image View tools on the supplied photograph, magnified crate detail, the first inspection and transition sheets, the repaired final view, and the final inspection and transition sheets. The final review checked the wall repair, rim occlusion, orange visibility through the grille, the rebound, and the stable ending. The HTML data and interval were checked in code; no browser playback review is claimed.
