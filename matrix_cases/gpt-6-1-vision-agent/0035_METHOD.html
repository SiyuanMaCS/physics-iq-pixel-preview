block-domino · 生成方法

generate.py generates the sequence using local Python pixel operations. It reads only first_frame.png as visual input. NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV provide array processing, image sampling, polygon operations, and output; no external model, rendering engine, network source, or subagent was used.

Image construction

The camera and frame stay fixed at 256 × 144. The first frame is copied directly from the input and remains byte-exact. For subsequent frames, the wall, visible table outside the reconstruction area, and most of the black platform come from the original photograph.

The hidden table is estimated with grain-following remapping of a clean strip near the bottom of the same photograph, illumination adjustment, and feathering. The small platform area formerly covered by purple is reconstructed with projected ellipse geometry and shading sampled from the unobstructed right side of that same platform. Local inpainting removes the original shaft. These newly revealed surfaces are estimates of occluded image content.

Each block is represented by a projected cuboid whose dimensions and position were measured from the first frame. Original front, side, and top colors/textures are sampled into small texture atlases, then perspective-warped to the rotated faces. Faces are culled using their projected winding. A face whose original side was poorly visible uses a darker version of that block's own front texture. Modest angle-dependent gains approximate the change in lighting as a side faces upward. Stationary blocks use original image cutouts. All moving polygons are rasterized at four times the final resolution and reduced with area sampling.

The wooden shaft is drawn as a narrow shaded line rotating around a fixed spindle. Positive angle advances clockwise in the tabletop projection, from the near-left quadrant toward the left and far side. The endpoint follows an ellipse with an additional downward offset representing a shaft that slopes from the elevated spindle. Blocks occlude the shaft, and a platform ellipse hides its lowered outer section during the far-side portion of the sweep.

Motion and contact

The stick initiates the purple block's leftward motion. Staggered gravity-like tipping curves initiate blue, green, pink, red, and yellow in that order. Each block rotates around its lower left edge with constant dimensions. These are analytic pose curves, rather than a full rigid-body dynamics simulation.

For every sampled pose, a convex-polygon intersection check finds the first contact with the left neighbor, then bisection limits the right block's angle. Thus an incoming block cannot pass through the front polygon of the block it strikes. Final resting angles are derived by the same contact calculation against the settled neighbor. Yellow reaches the table, and the other blocks form a supported overlapping row. A small damped pose adjustment gives the final settling. This uses projected contact geometry, not a reconstructed metric 3D scene.

Soft projected shadows are drawn beneath and to the right of the row and blurred on the local table texture. The settling is finished near frame 16, while the rod continues its clockwise rotation.

Outputs and verification

Run python generate.py from this workspace. It writes:

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images at 256 × 144, displayed at 4 fps for a five-second sequence.
  • inspection.png: an eight-frame contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: all 19 adjacent pairs with amplified absolute pixel differences.
  • index.html: a self-contained player using the same packed pixels, with play, replay, scrub, and loop controls.

The script decodes every tensor slice and checks exact equality with its corresponding PNG. It also asserts exact equality between frame 00 and the RGB input image. The final run passed those checks. Image view tools were used to review generated contact sheets, the start transitions, enlarged final frames, and the reconstructed background. Revisions removed visible color remnants, made the tabletop fill continuous, tightened contact limits, and smoothed the platform edge revealed by the moving purple block.
