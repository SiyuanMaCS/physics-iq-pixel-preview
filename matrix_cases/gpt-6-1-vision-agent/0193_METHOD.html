METHOD.md

Method and verification

This continuation was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations applied to first_frame.png. prompt.txt supplied the action. No network, external reference image, learned image/video model, physics engine, external renderer, or subagent was used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before implementation. It records the measured object and pillow locations and the intended 20-frame event sequence. generate.py contains the complete generation procedure and can be rerun with python generate.py from this workspace.

Pixel construction

The photographic kettlebell was extracted using a local contrast threshold constrained by a hand-traced silhouette. The handle opening remains transparent. Partial edge pixels were unmatted against the reconstructed background. A very small arc completes the cropped uppermost handle when it moves into the frame; the rest of the object, including its marking, uses source pixels. The kettlebell remains upright, retains its original scale, and translates downward.

The original object occlusions were removed with OpenCV local inpainting. The gray wall inside the removed silhouettes was then reconstructed with discrete harmonic interpolation using observed boundary colors. The hidden portions of the pillow crowns use local color interpolation and neighboring photographed fabric. This is a source-pixel approximation of occluded surfaces, rather than newly filmed imagery.

The left pillow uses an inverse displacement map, remapping the original fabric and folds. A horizontal Gaussian distributes the load around x=103. The upper crown moves downward while displacement decays toward the fixed support at the bottom, shortening the fabric beneath the weight. A small lateral displacement models the pillow's bulge, and a soft local darkening reinforces the indentation. The bell and pillow share a damped step response with natural frequency 3 rad/s, damping ratio 0.63, and final displacement 27 pixels. The response slightly overshoots and then settles. At 3 seconds the remaining small motion is stopped for the final hold.

The green sheet uses its source texture and a perspective transform. A fitted green color plane fills the small part originally obscured by the blue holder. The holder retreats upward and leaves the frame during the first half second. Beginning at the same time as the kettlebell's placement, the paper tips forward around its lower edge, passes through an edge-on view, and rests in a foreshortened quadrilateral on the right pillow. Its tip reaches approximately 90 degrees at 1.4 seconds, followed by a tiny damped angular settling motion. The paper has a thin soft shadow and modest orientation-dependent shading. The right pillow has no deliberate load-induced indentation; the left-pillow deformation has a small, smoothly decaying influence near the pillows' shared edge.

The camera, framing, object scale, and lighting setup remain fixed. There are no added hands, people, captions, or props in the generated frames.

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.

Review and exact checks

The initial image, intermediate enlarged views, and the final inspection.png and transition_check.png were actually viewed with the image tool. Two revisions improved the reconstructed background, slowed the initial loading response, and removed the holder's dark imprint from the paper texture. The final visual review shows a deep central indentation under the kettlebell and a flat sheet on the pillow that retains its loft.

Validation passed for all 20 frames: PNG dimensions and RGB channels, uint32 tensor shape and packing, exact PNG-to-tensor equality, and exact equality of frame 00 to the source pixels. Frame 00 also matches the input file byte-for-byte. The HTML player's first-frame and delta payloads were independently decoded and all 20 reconstructed frames exactly matched the tensor. Verification results and numerical motion states are saved under .tmp/.

The procedure is a photographic pixel deformation with an analytic motion model. Pillow stiffness, hidden fabric, and the sheet's projection are inferred from this single view; this is not a measured material simulation or a full reconstruction of the three-dimensional scene.
