METHOD.md

Method

This animation was generated entirely inside the workspace with Python, NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. The scene inputs were the supplied first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The provided artifact_io.py and player_template.html were used for output packaging. No network, external images, masks, AI models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Photograph and motion

The fixed camera view, wooden table, wall illumination, flashlight, beam, and platform front come from the original photograph. The moving object outlines and shadow regions were measured manually from that image. The original mug, handle, and block were removed from a background copy using local inpainting. A smooth thin-plate radial-basis interpolation of visible wall colors estimates the wall behind the initial shadows and objects. The small exposed turntable top is filled with a dark elliptical surface, a fixed soft highlight, and moving contact shadows/reflections.

World X points right and world Y points toward the rear of the platform. At each 0.25-second interval, both object centers and their orientations rotate by six degrees clockwise as seen from above. The platform center remains at approximately (153,111.1) pixels. The mug's initial projected foot is (144.9,111.4); the block's is (163.4,109.7). At frame 19 these are approximately (155.4,108.6) and (152.9,114.7). The initially left mug moves across the rear of its orbit while the initially right block crosses the foreground. The sampled turn is 114 degrees over 4.75 seconds; the final sample displays for 0.25 seconds to complete the five-second sequence.

The mug body is the original photographic cutout, translated with its circular orbit and given a small perspective scale adjustment. A mathematical cylinder supplies per-pixel depth for occlusion. The mug handle is a rounded rectangular tube built from locally calculated vertices and normals. The blue block consists of five visible candidate box faces, with lighting from the fixed lower-right direction and a blue vertical color gradient. A small custom triangle rasterizer implements projection, face shading, and depth comparisons at four times the output resolution. Area downsampling provides soft edges. This allows the foreground block and the handle to be correctly occluded by the mug where their projected surfaces overlap.

Moving wall shadows

The original soft mug and block shadows are represented as RGB optical-density images relative to the estimated lit wall. Their motion and changing scale are calculated from a point-light projection onto a wall plane. The projection is calibrated separately to the observed initial mug and block shadow extents. Least-squares affine transformations of the projected cylinder tangencies and box corners transport the photographed shadow densities. The mug's complete photographed silhouette includes its original handle contribution. The wall/table joint retains the fixed dark strip from the source photograph. Shadows combine in optical density and are clipped to the wall.

This is a single-view pixel reconstruction. The covered wall and table texture are estimates. The mug body retains its photographic highlight. Its complete wall shadow uses a deforming photographic silhouette; the handle's changing wall silhouette is approximated within that transported shape. The visible handle and block have geometric rotation and depth occlusion. The circular, nearly featureless platform has a fixed outline during rotation.

Artifacts and checks

  • generate.py: reproducible local generation and PNG/tensor validation.
  • pixels.npz: pixels, a uint32 array of shape (144,256,20), encoding 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: twenty RGB PNGs, each 256×144. Frame 00 is also copied from the source file, preserving its exact bytes.
  • inspection.png: eight-frame contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: selected full frames and enlarged object crops, including the opening transition.
  • index.html: standalone canvas player with 4 fps playback, frame selection, replay, and looping.
  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and the pre-generation 20-frame event plan.

The contact sheets, opening transition, final frame details, and reconstructed background were viewed with the image tool during revision. Revisions expanded the initial shadow region, removed a separately warped handle shadow that produced detached shapes, constrained dark-wall color extrapolation, preserved the wall/table joint, and adjusted the block and handle appearance.

Final validation decodes every PNG and compares it exactly with the tensor. It also checks that frame 00 is pixel-identical and file-identical to the input, all packed values fit in 24 bits, and the standalone player's initial frame and every decoded delta reconstruct the same 20 arrays. The frame rate is four samples per second with twenty frames.
