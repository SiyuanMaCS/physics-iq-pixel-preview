This sequence uses local Python pixel processing of the supplied photograph. It uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and the provided output-only artifact_io.py helper. No network, external images, AI models, external engines, or subagents were used.

Scene continuation

The source ball is already near the middle of the coffee table, so frame 00 retains the entire source photograph exactly. Subsequent frames continue its existing rightward roll. The black pipe, table, sofa, lamp, camera framing, and room remain stationary. The ball passes out of view through the right frame boundary. No additional action is introduced.

The refined source center is (161.9, 79.9), and the nominal sphere radius is 6.85 pixels, with the source's soft focus preserved. Sample time is t = frame / 4 seconds. Projected travel follows d = 24.2*t - 0.375*t*t pixels. The center is (161.9+d, 79.9-0.045*d). This gives gentle deceleration from 24.2 to 20.64 pixels/second over the samples, a final center around (268.39, 75.11), and smooth clipping at the fixed right image boundary. Material rotation angle is traveled distance divided by radius. trajectory.json stores all twenty sample positions and angles.

Pixel construction

  1. Remove the source ball and its shadow from a small union of elliptical footprints. Reconstruct the wood using a directionally weighted harmonic fill, with horizontal neighbor weights of 7 and vertical weights of 1. The linear solve uses the photograph's pixels at the boundary and leaves every pixel outside the footprint unchanged.
  2. Recover a photographic sphere sprite with a soft elliptical opacity profile. Its RGB appearance comes from the actual source ball, unblended against the estimated tabletop and shadow. Retain this photographed shading in screen coordinates to keep the light direction fixed.
  3. Fit a broad soft cast shadow and a narrow contact shadow to source luminance ratios outside the ball. Translate their multiplicative attenuation with the ball. Retain a compact photometric residual so source softness and color are preserved across the first transition.
  4. Add very weak mathematical felt and curved seam variation in sphere material coordinates. Rotate those coordinates with Rodrigues' formula about a projected rolling axis. Subtract the initial material variation, so it does not alter the initial appearance. This is a photographic sprite with a rotating mathematical material modulation; it is not a full 3D reconstruction or a recovered physical material model.
  5. Sample the translated sprite, opacity, and shadow with bilinear interpolation, composite over the fixed clean plate, and round to RGB uint8. Use the unmodified source array for frame 00.
