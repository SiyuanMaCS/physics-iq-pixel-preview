OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

Inputs: first_frame.png and prompt.txt only. The source is a 256 × 144 RGB view. The camera is fixed. A warm wooden tabletop occupies the lower part of the image; a pale wall is on the left, a vertical mirror spans the central/right background, and a dark doorway is visible at the right edge. Illumination comes from the left.

The real orange tennis ball is centered approximately at (120, 73), with a radius of about 9 pixels. Its gray magnetic attachment and black hanging hardware occupy approximately x=112–129, y=26–65. The orange cord reaches from the top border near x=118 to the hanging hardware. A smaller, darker mirror image of this entire assembly is centered near (188, 56), with a ball radius of about 6 pixels and an orange cord near x=190. This is one ball and its reflection, not two independently moving balls. The table, wall, mirror, doorway, camera, and exposure remain stationary.

The continuation will slowly lengthen the visible orange cords and lower the real hanging assembly and its reflection together. Existing image pixels will preserve the photographed texture and hardware. The mirror image will have a smaller projected displacement, consistent with its smaller apparent size. Motion will stay nearly vertical, with no added hand or apparatus. Both balls remain attached to their magnets; the clip and hardware move with them. The final lowering distance will be selected after inspecting the source at larger display scale, keeping both balls plausibly suspended over the table.

20-frame event plan (4 fps)

Frame 00 is exactly the supplied image. Frames 01–03 begin the slow descent without a sudden jump. Frames 04–08 continue the descent at a nearly constant gentle speed. Frames 09–14 continue lowering, maintaining the same real/reflected geometry. Frames 15–18 ease towards the final suspended height. Frame 19 ends at the lowered height. Nominal displacement fractions by frame: 0.000, 0.013, 0.034, 0.067, 0.108, 0.157, 0.213, 0.274, 0.339, 0.406, 0.474, 0.543, 0.610, 0.676, 0.738, 0.796, 0.848, 0.893, 0.929, 0.957. Final values may be normalized to reach 1.0 at frame 19. The movement is a prescribed slow cord payout rather than free fall.

Save generate.py, 20 RGB PNG frames, packed pixels.npz, a contact sheet, a transition comparison, a standalone 4 fps HTML player, and a method report. METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was produced using local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. The only scene description used was prompt.txt. No network access, external images, external models, external render engines, or subagents were used.

Scene continuation

The real ball, its magnet, small links, and black hanging loop descend as one assembly. The mirror image follows the same motion, scaled to its smaller apparent size. The orange cords extend from fixed upper positions. The camera, framing, exposure, table, wall, mirror, and doorway remain fixed.

There are 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144 pixels, played at 4 fps. Each frame is held for 0.25 seconds, for a total playback duration of 5 seconds. Frame 00 is the supplied image without any pixel changes. The sampled motion times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds.

For frame index t, let u = t / 19. The prescribed cord payout uses e = 3u² − 2u³, with a real-image descent of 19e pixels and a reflected descent of 12.5e pixels. This gives a gentle start and stop. The balls remain attached and suspended; there is no free fall or table collision.

Pixel construction

  1. Measure the two assembly silhouettes directly from the input. Draw local soft mattes with supersampled polygons, ellipses, and transparent holes in the hanging loops. Preserve the original ball texture, seam, magnet, and hardware pixels.
  2. Reconstruct the backgrounds hidden behind those silhouettes. OpenCV Telea inpainting uses neighboring input pixels for the pale reflected wall. For the real assembly, local row interpolation reconstructs the dark wall and its transition to the tabletop, with explicit treatment of the narrow vertical mirror edge.
  3. Form premultiplied image layers and translate them downwards at subpixel precision with bilinear interpolation. The real and reflected layers retain their original sizes and horizontal positions.
  4. Extend the orange cords with measured source pixel profiles and their inferred edge coverage. Original cord pixels above the payout regions stay fixed.
  5. Reconstruct a small shadow area beside the original real magnet. Translate, soften, and diminish its source-derived residual as the assembly lowers, preventing the original shadow from remaining as a stationary fragment.
  6. Keep all other source pixels exactly fixed. Clamp and round to RGB uint8, and save with the supplied output-only artifact_io.py helper.

This is an image-derived 2D continuation. The perspective ratio, slow lowering, and local shadow adjustment are prescribed approximations; no 3D scene reconstruction or physical rendering is claimed.

Visual review and revision

The image view tool was used on the original frame, enlarged source crops, the initial inspection and transition sheets, and enlarged early/final output frames. Enlarged review exposed a residual original magnet shadow and an interruption in the extended cord. Both were corrected. The final enlarged image and regenerated inspection sheets were reviewed with the image view tool.

Verification

The generator verifies the frame count, dimensions, data type, exact frame-00 equality with the input, and exact equality of all PNGs with the decoded packed RGB tensor. It also checks monotonically increasing descent and exact background invariance outside the complete changing assembly, cord, and shadow regions. A separate check decodes the HTML player's initial frame and delta packets and compares all 20 reconstructed frames with pixels.npz.
