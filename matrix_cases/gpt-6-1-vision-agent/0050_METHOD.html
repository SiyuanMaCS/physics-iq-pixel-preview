dominos-with-space · 生成方法

This sequence was made entirely with local Python pixel operations, using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy were used. No external images, network, AI models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

generate.py preserves the supplied photograph as frame 00. For subsequent frames it constructs a local clean plate by masking the three moving dominoes, their old shadows, the wooden arm and its green taped joint. OpenCV Telea inpainting fills those areas. A high-frequency component from nearby tabletop pixels restores some wood grain in the filled regions; neighboring pixels from the same black platter fill the old taped-joint area. The wall, cable, table edges, and four standing dominoes retain their original pixels. Pixels outside the bounded motion region are copied exactly from the original photograph.

The moving dominoes are rectangular prisms measured from the input. A simple fixed orthographic projection maps depth slightly left and upward, and maps height upward. Each piece rotates leftward about its original bottom edge. The hinge angles use monotone piecewise cubic interpolation of specified contact and settling times. The first piece starts with the lean visible in the photograph; the white piece reacts after the first contact; the last black piece follows. Their final angles are 75, 79 and 90 degrees, respectively, to approximate overlapping support. This is a kinematic reconstruction informed by geometry and contact spacing, rather than a general rigid-body simulation. The final piece is too short to bridge the measured gap to the second row.

Visible prism faces are projected, culled, sorted, and composited with perspective texture warps. Front-face texture is sampled from the original dominoes, excluding sampled pixels contaminated by the arm or surrounding background. Other faces use measured colors, fixed local surface variation and normal-dependent lighting. Cast shadows and contact shadows are drawn from the projected geometry and softened with Gaussian filters. The stick follows a clockwise elliptical sweep at a constant 52 degrees per second, with a rotating green joint. Its layered warm colors provide a shaded wooden dowel. Rendering uses fourfold spatial resolution and area downsampling for antialiasing.

Artifacts and verification

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty RGB frames at 256 x 144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20), containing packed 0xRRGGBB values.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-data player, displaying the twenty frames at 4 fps, with seek and replay controls.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames for overview inspection.
  • transition_check.png: selected adjacent frames and amplified absolute RGB differences.
  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and twenty-frame plan written before generation code.
  • generate.py: complete reproducible generation and verification code.

Frame samples are t=0.00, 0.25, ..., 4.75 seconds, each displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving a five-second sequence. All twenty decoded PNGs were checked pixel-for-pixel against the packed tensor. Frame 00 was checked to be exactly the input image. The four standing dominoes and the entire image region left of x=132 were verified unchanged in all frames. The embedded player data was independently decoded and compared with all twenty PNGs.

The initial image, overview contact sheets, transition sheet and enlarged fall details were viewed with the image tool. Review prompted removal of background/arm contamination from sampled black-face textures and regeneration of the outputs. The final overview and fall details were visually reviewed: three dominoes settle on the near side of the gap, four stay standing, and the arm continues its clockwise sweep with a fixed camera.

Limitations: hidden surfaces and pixels exposed behind the moving setup are locally reconstructed. Contact timing and shading are approximations at the input's low spatial and temporal resolution.
