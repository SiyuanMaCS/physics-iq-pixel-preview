METHOD.md

Method and validation

This sequence was generated locally with Python, NumPy, Pillow, SciPy and OpenCV pixel operations. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external references, trained AI models, rendering engines, other cases, or subagents were used. OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code.

Image construction

The original photograph supplies the stationary scene. A feathered elliptical region surrounding the initial ball, diffuse shadows, and green defocus halo is reconstructed using clean tabletop pixels from the same rows on either side. A rowwise illumination gradient and a small amount of copied wood detail preserve the table's appearance. An initial inpainting version left a visible halo; it was replaced with this rowwise reconstruction after visual inspection.

The moving ball is a transparent cutout recovered from the original photograph with a soft elliptical coverage mask. Its photographic colors and diffuse shading are retained. A very faint analytic spherical seam and surface variation rotate with signed travel distance. The sphere is composited at fractional positions with 4× supersampling and three exposure samples spanning 1/90 second. Its apparent size changes by less than 2% on the ramp. This is photograph-based 2D pixel compositing with spherical surface detail, not a full 3D reconstruction or measured dynamics simulation.

Two broad Gaussian cast shadows approximate the paired shadows visible in the source. A smaller contact shadow attaches the ball to its receiving surface. As the ball climbs, the shadows shear along the projected ramp slope and are clipped to the cardboard polygon. The pipe, cardboard, support, camera, tabletop outside the reconstruction area, and original setup shadows remain fixed.

Motion

The source already shows the ball beyond the pipe, so the animation continues that roll without repeating its emergence. The ball moves toward the low ramp edge, climbs, slows, reverses under gravity, and returns to the tabletop. All motion is sampled from a continuous analytic trajectory at 0.25-second intervals.

The table approach speed is 82 image pixels/s. The ramp entry is at 0.773 s, near x=150. The initial ramp speed is 76 pixels/s and downslope acceleration is 55 pixels/s²; the projected rise is approximately 0.378 image pixels per horizontal pixel. The ball turns at 2.154 s near (202.5,32.0), leaves the ramp at 3.536 s, and continues left at approximately 74 pixels/s with mild rolling resistance. The brief velocity losses at the low edge approximate the cardboard lip. Values are chosen for a plausible image-space demonstration; the source gives no physical dimensions or camera calibration.
