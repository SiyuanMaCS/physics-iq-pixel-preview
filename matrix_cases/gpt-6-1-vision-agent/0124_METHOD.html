Extract and transform the original mug for the fall, then construct shaded polygonal/curved ceramic fragment sprites in the original yellow palette. METHOD.md

Method and delivered artifacts

This is a local Python pixel animation derived from the supplied photograph. The scene sources are first_frame.png and the description in prompt.txt. OBSERVATION.md was written before implementation and records the measured setup and twenty-frame event plan.

Image construction

The original photograph supplies the fixed screen, floor, brick, rail, and grabber. A sparse discrete Laplace solve interpolates the screen inside the small region previously covered by the mug and its diffuse cast shadow. Original boundary pixels constrain the solve; a narrow blend and low-amplitude texture soften the join. Pixels elsewhere in the fixed background are preserved. Frame 00 is copied directly from first_frame.png, preserving both the pixel values and the PNG file bytes.

The falling mug is extracted from its original pixels. A manually bounded silhouette and blue-channel difference against the restored screen form its alpha matte. Its recovered colors are transformed and composited lower in frame 01, retaining the source photograph's soft appearance and yellow/brown shading. A broad, blurred screen shadow follows the mug.

The mug's lower edge reaches the top of the brick at approximately 0.3285 seconds under the chosen image-space initial velocity and gravity. Frame 02 shows the ceramic pieces separating after contact. Thirteen hand-shaped ceramic fragments represent the body wall, base, handle, rim, and small chips. Their yellow glazed surfaces have curved shading, dark edges, pale exposed fracture boundaries, and soft texture. These are locally drawn sprites at four times output resolution.

Projected ballistic trajectories use 2 ms integration, gravity, dissipative contact rebounds, angular motion, and exponential sliding friction. One small fragment rests on the brick; the others settle on the surrounding plank floor. Height-dependent shadows and five exposure samples soften moving fragments. Ground-plane foreshortening is applied after rotation when pieces settle. Motion has effectively ended by two seconds, followed by a stationary aftermath. Motion and fracture geometry are specified in image coordinates from the single photograph.

Only Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy were used for generation, together with the supplied output-only artifact_io.py and player template. No network resources, external reference images, AI models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Review and validation

The initial photograph was viewed with an image tool, including enlarged views. The first generated inspection and consecutive-frame sheets were also viewed. That review led to replacing the initial illumination fit with harmonic interpolation to remove a visible restoration patch, and to correcting fragment foreshortening. Both revised sheets were viewed with an image tool before completion.

Validation checks all 20 PNGs for RGB dimensions of 256 × 144, and compares every pixel to the uint32 0xRRGGBB tensor. The tensor has shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32. The first frame exactly matches the input. The player contains the same frames, packed as a first frame and lossless delta patches; its reconstructed data were independently compared against the tensor. Playback advances every 250 ms, giving 4 fps and 5 seconds for 20 frames.
