OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

The input is a 256 × 144 RGB photograph. Camera and exposure remain fixed. A honey-brown wooden table occupies the lower half, with its back edge running approximately from (0, 91) through (90, 53) toward the silk-covered right background. A dark wall strip remains at the left. The ivory/champagne silk hangs from the top across most of the background and reaches the table near y = 91 at the right. Its existing highlights and folds supply the material appearance.

The stationary ceramic teapot has a round cobalt-blue body, approximately x = 104–166, y = 45–107; an olive-green lid around (138, 36); a red handle at x = 94–119, y = 35–79; a red spout extending to (191, 51) from x = 157, y = 101; and a green foot at x = 116–160, y = 105–120. The table shadow is soft and extends slightly to the right. The silk must conceal all of these features by the end while suggesting their shape beneath it. No hands or other objects are added.

Method planned: local Python pixel synthesis, photographic silk sampling and deformation, an antialiased advancing cloth boundary, analytic surface/fold shading over the stationary pot, and soft contact shadows on the table. This is a constructed image animation rather than a measured physical simulation. Preserve the first image exactly. Use temporaries only inside .tmp.

METHOD.md

Method

This animation was made entirely with local Python pixel operations. The only scene sources were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy were used for array operations, image output, bilinear image sampling, and Gaussian filtering. No network, external images, masks, AI models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

The original photograph supplies the stationary teapot, wood, wall, and silk appearance. A small displacement of the photographed upper silk begins the action. A rounded, center-sagging cloth boundary then advances downward from the top, covering the lid, handle, body, spout, and foot in that order. The teapot remains at its original position; progressive opaque occlusion produces its disappearance under the fabric.

The foreground silk color and photographic sheen are sampled from the clean right-hand silk region of the input (source x ≥ 196). Source coordinates are deformed using the cloth surface and temporal movement. An analytic height field combines a rounded body, lid, handle, spout support, and several radiating and hanging folds. Height gradients supply directional diffuse shading and a narrow satin highlight. A small rolled-edge highlight and darker interior hem emphasize the moving edge. A blurred mask supplies a soft shadow as the cloth reaches the table. These are image-space approximations, not a full mechanical cloth simulation or a recovered 3D scene.

Foreground boundaries are rasterized at three times the output resolution and averaged to 256 × 144 for antialiasing. Unaffected background pixels are preserved by nearest replication of the original image before averaging. Frame 00 bypasses rendering and copies the source RGB pixels exactly. Early movement is subtle; the main descent happens in frames 04–12. The hem and folds relax through frame 16, and frames 16–19 hold the final covered state. Twenty frames displayed at 4 fps give five seconds of playback.

Artifacts and reproduction

Run python generate.py from this workspace. It writes:

  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32; each value is packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: an eight-frame overview.
  • transition_check.png: all 19 adjacent-frame pairs and amplified absolute RGB changes.
  • index.html: standalone local canvas player with all frames embedded, playback, replay, seek, and loop controls at 4 fps.

OBSERVATION.md was written before coding and records scene measurements and the 20-frame plan. artifact_io.py and player_template.html are the supplied output helpers. verification.json records the final artifact checks.

Inspection and verification

One revision followed the initial render. It reduced the conical impression of the folds, added a more rounded body surface and stronger spout support, narrowed the left drape, preserved unaffected background pixels, and smoothed the temporal movement of the photographed texture.

Image tools were used to view the original image, enlarged source and silk sample, both rendered inspection sheets, both enlarged final frames, and all 19 final transitions split into three viewing crops of transition_check.png. The final sequence visually shows progressive coverage, full concealment by frame 12, and a resting drape at the end.

The generator decodes the saved uint32 tensor and verifies all 20 PNGs against it with exact array equality. It separately verifies frame 00 against the input. The final check also reconstructs the HTML player's embedded first frame and all delta packets and compares each with the tensor. This verifies its image payload; the player was not reviewed in a browser.
