OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and frame plan

The sole visual input is first_frame.png, a 256 × 144 RGB photograph. Coordinates below refer to that image, with the origin at the top left. The camera is locked. The background is a grey wall with broad lighting gradients; the wooden tabletop rises toward its rear edge near (179, 68). The clear bowl spans approximately x = 78–191 and y = 71–143. Its upper lip is an ellipse centred near (134, 103), with its far edge at y = 73; the visible water surface lies inside the bowl, approximately x = 94–178, y = 92–129. The paper will meet the water near (134, 107), safely inside the bowl.

The black grabber handle enters at x = 106–119 along the top. Its orange-lined jaws extend through x = 110–158, y = 21–56. The left jaw curves downward and the right jaw arcs to the right. A textured grey-white crumpled piece of paper hangs between them, approximately x = 123–148 and y = 31–65, with centre around (135, 48). The scene includes soft shadows, reflected bright patches on the glass, and wood grain visible through the water. These features must remain registered throughout the shot.

Motion and compositing decisions

Use only local Python pixel operations. Extract the photographed paper and grabber with manually measured antialiased silhouettes. Reconstruct only the wall occluded by the original paper and moving jaw tips using a smooth boundary-conditioned fill. Keep the handle anchored and spread the lower jaws slightly. Move and rotate the paper under analytic gravity, then show buoyant bobbing, modest wetting, and a slowly damped float. Preserve its photographed creases. Small elliptical ripples displace and lightly shade the original water pixels; a soft contact shadow and subtle submerged lower edge tie the paper to the surface. No added scene objects or camera movement.

Twenty frames at 4 fps represent a five-second clip. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the input, without regeneration. Sampling times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds. Opening begins after the first sample, release occurs at approximately 0.375 s, and first contact follows about 0.20 s later. The approximately 2000 pixel/s² projected acceleration represents a short real-world drop, rather than slow motion.

METHOD.md

Method

This is a deterministic, local Python pixel-compositing continuation of first_frame.png. The photograph and prompt.txt are the only scene inputs. No external images, models, engines, network services, or subagents were used. NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy provide array operations, masks, resampling, and a sparse numerical solve. The supplied artifact_io.py saves the final outputs and embeds them in the supplied standalone player template.

Pixel construction

  1. Measured the photographed grabber, hanging paper, bowl, and water surface and wrote the twenty-frame plan in OBSERVATION.md before implementing the generator.
  2. Drew manual silhouettes for the paper and grabber at four times the output resolution. Gaussian feathering matches the source's soft photographic edges. Estimated foreground colours by removing the contribution of the grey wall; very faint boundary pixels use nearby interior colours.
  3. Constructed a clean grey wall only in a dilated union of the original silhouettes. A discrete Laplace solve interpolates the original surrounding RGB boundary values. The source wall, wooden table, bowl, and highlights remain registered. The upper 22 image rows are copied from the photograph in every frame to anchor the handle exactly.
  4. Opened the lower jaws with a smoothly increasing horizontal deformation while keeping the upper arch anchored. The paper remains held in frame 01, releases at 0.375 seconds, and falls with a projected acceleration of 2000 pixels/s². Frame 02 shows the intermediate fall with a small rotation and approximately 1.3 pixels of vertical Gaussian motion blur. First water contact occurs at approximately 0.585 seconds, between frames 02 and 03.
  5. Rotated and gently flattened the photographed paper into a floating orientation. Its centre settles near (133, 104), with a damped vertical oscillation and less than three pixels of sideways drift. The original folds supply all the paper's visible texture. A gradual brightness adjustment simulates wetting. A soft shadow, partially transmitted lower folds, and a faint meniscus provide contact with the water.
  6. Made three small elliptical ripple packets centred near (136, 109). Analytic radial functions displace existing water pixels and add paired light/dark slope shading. Their amplitudes decay exponentially, and an elliptical mask restricts them to the interior water area. These are a visual approximation of surface waves, not a fluid simulation.
  7. Downsampled the changed pixels to 256 × 144. Preserved the original outside the edited area. Within erased silhouettes, explicitly removed the source's high-frequency resampling residual; retaining it in the first pass produced faint traces of the original paper creases. Frame 00 bypasses all construction and exactly copies the input RGB array.

The final motion remains a single fixed-camera shot with the photographed setup.
