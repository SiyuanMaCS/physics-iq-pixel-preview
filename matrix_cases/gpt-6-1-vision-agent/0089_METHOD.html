This continuation uses the supplied photograph as its background, with local Python, NumPy and Pillow pixel operations. It is a procedural photographic composite, not an external video model or a fluid-engine render. Only first_frame.png and prompt.txt supplied scene information.

The source glass already looks nearly full. The animation therefore depicts a gentle top-up: a narrow red jet travels from the blue tap into the nearby glass, the surface rises approximately 1.25 image pixels, and contained ripples and tiny surface bubbles follow the impact. Flow eases off near the end and the surface settles. The glass, dispenser, woven stand, tray, table and camera stay fixed.

generate.py evaluates the effect at six times the output resolution. The stream uses a soft analytic cross section with red shading and a small specular highlight. Its width follows the square root of a smooth flow-rate envelope and decreases slightly along the fall. The surface rise is proportional to the numerical integral of that envelope, an approximate volume-to-height relationship chosen for the visible headspace rather than a calibrated volumetric simulation. Elliptical Gaussian fields produce the impact depression, meniscus, expanding damped ripples and short-lived bubbles. A subpixel bilinear displacement of the existing liquid texture creates a small current below the impact while preserving the glass's main reflections.

Only the high-resolution color difference is downsampled with a Lanczos filter and added to the original RGB photograph. This prevents a photograph resize roundtrip from changing untouched pixels. The effect is restricted to the tap-to-rim gap, liquid surface and glass interior. All pixels outside that region stay exactly equal to the supplied photograph. Frame 00 bypasses all processing and is exactly equal to first_frame.png.

Artifacts and timing

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty RGB images, each 256 × 144 pixels.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32; each value is 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: representative full-frame contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: the source frame and all nineteen consecutive transitions, with amplified differences and changed-pixel counts.
  • index.html: standalone player containing the complete frames as packed base64 data and lossless delta packets, with a 250 ms playback interval.
  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and event plan written before generation code.
  • verification.json: machine-readable verification results.

Frame times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds in steps of 0.25 seconds. Displaying each frame for 0.25 seconds produces the required five-second clip. The player's and inspection sheet's displayed times denote the end of each frame's display interval.

Review and verification

Image view tools were used to inspect the supplied image, an enlarged rim crop, the generated contact sheet, the transition sheet and enlarged generated rim comparisons. The visual review confirmed that the jet is aligned with the tap and glass, the surface effects remain contained, and the scene geometry remains fixed. The short falling distance and initially full appearance make the resulting motion deliberately small.

The compositing was revised after the first review to downsample only pixel differences, preserving untouched glass pixels exactly. The final contact sheet and transition sheet were reviewed again after regeneration.

Running python generate.py generates all outputs and checks the following with assertions:

  • Twenty RGB frames at the required dimensions and four-frame-per-second timing.
  • Exact equality of frame 00 and the supplied image.
  • Exact equality of every saved PNG to its packed uint32 tensor slice.
  • Exact equality of the fixed background across the full sequence.
  • Exact reconstruction of all twenty frames from the HTML player's embedded delta packets.

The numerical generation is deterministic. Temporary enlarged crops used for visual review are stored in .tmp/.
