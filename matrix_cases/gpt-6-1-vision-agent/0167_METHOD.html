METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations using NumPy, SciPy, OpenCV, and Pillow. It uses first_frame.png and prompt.txt as the scene inputs. No network, external reference, learned model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used.

The ball's visible image is inverse-projected onto an analytic sphere centered at image coordinate (129.7, 57.7), with projected radii 19.3 and 19.5 pixels. The camera elevation is estimated as 23 degrees from the turntable ellipse. The sphere turns around the vertical world axis, clockwise as viewed from above, at 20 degrees per second. Frame i samples angle 5i degrees, so frame 19 is at 95 degrees. Sampling is uniformly spaced at 4 fps. Each of the 20 frames is displayed for a quarter second, giving five seconds of playback.

The facial ink and the visible curved seam are source-image texture, moved by inverse spherical coordinate mapping and bilinear sampling. The original felt pixels also supply the visible material texture. A robust polynomial fit to unmarked green pixels estimates a fixed diffuse illumination field. Dividing source color by that field before rotation, then applying illumination at the destination, reduces the appearance of lighting rotating with the material.

The unseen hemisphere cannot be recovered from one image. It is synthesized as yellow-green felt using the median normalized source color and deterministic fine/coarse noise defined in spherical coordinates. A pale rear seam is a procedural continuation fitted to eight points on the visible original seam. A smooth blend joins the observed surface to this completed surface near the original visibility boundary. This is image-derived pixel animation with analytic spherical geometry, rather than a simulation of the full scene.

A color-derived filled silhouette and a soft edge matte constrain the edit to the ball. Difference compositing against the zero-angle render preserves the original ball's edge coverage. The original wall, table, circular black platform, cable, and contact shadow remain fixed. The unmarked rotationally symmetric platform has no visible rotation cue. The method approximates a rigid ball turning in place; it does not model deforming felt fibers or moving contact reflections.

Frame 00 is explicitly copied from the supplied RGB image, without any resampling or recompression changes to decoded pixels. artifact_io.py writes 20 RGB PNGs and pixels.npz. The tensor has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and every value is encoded as 0xRRGGBB. The output helper also creates the inspection sheet and the standalone HTML player. generate.py additionally creates transition_check.png and verification.json.

Visual review was performed with the image View tool on inspection.png and transition_check.png, as well as a magnified crop sheet of frames 00, 05, 10, 15, and 19. The reviewed sequence shows the smile and seam moving left and wrapping around the sphere, with stable framing, ball position, and platform. The initial transition was also inspected at enlarged scale. No further revision was needed after this review.

Verification checks performed by generate.py:

  • Exactly 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144.
  • First PNG and first tensor slice exactly match the source image's pixels.
  • Every PNG decodes identically to its uint32 packed tensor slice.
  • Every pixel outside the ball edit mask remains unchanged across all frames.
  • Uniform five-degree pose steps and per-transition pixel-change statistics.

An additional verification reconstructs all frames from the HTML player's first-frame payload and delta packets and compares them to pixels.npz. All checks passed. Reproduce the artifacts by running python generate.py from this workspace with the installed local Python libraries.
