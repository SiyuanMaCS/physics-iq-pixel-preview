METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was generated locally with Python, NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV pixel transforms, and SciPy interpolation, optimization, and sparse linear algebra. Its only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external assets, external AI models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

The animation contains 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144. Playback holds each frame for 0.25 seconds, giving 4 fps and a five-second sequence. The source pose is at time zero, and the final sampled pose is at 4.75 seconds. frames/00.png is a byte-for-byte copy of the supplied PNG.

Pixel construction

The fixed camera view is retained. Manually measured, antialiased polygons isolate the source mug, blue block, and their wall shadows. A smooth elliptical spotlight model is fitted to unobscured wall pixels. A global thin-plate interpolation supplies the wall's color residual, and a discrete Poisson fill matches reconstructed wall colors to the surrounding measured pixels. Foreground pixels are excluded from the fill's boundary conditions. Local pixel inpainting reconstructs the small tabletop area behind the objects. The dark platform surface receives a separate texture fill to clear the old colored reflections.

The object centers follow a constant clockwise rotation, as seen from above, about the calibrated platform center (116.5, 137.3). World depth projects vertically with a factor of 0.5. The rotation is 6 degrees per frame, or 24 degrees per second, reaching 114 degrees at frame 19. The upright objects are sorted by depth before compositing.

The mug uses the supplied cylindrical body texture with an affine translation and small depth-dependent scale change. Its narrow ceramic glint shifts slightly with position relative to the fixed light. No separate handle was invented, since the source does not resolve a handle clearly. The blue block uses a source-calibrated geometric proxy: its footprint and vertical faces rotate together, and each visible face is rasterized with a homography. Initial face textures are sampled directly from the source; unseen faces reuse those textures with normal-dependent shading.

Wall shadows combine measured multiplicative attenuation with complete, softly edged silhouette proxies that fill regions originally hidden behind the objects. Foreground colors are removed from the shadow textures. Relative point-light projection from a light in front and to the left changes shadow position and scale with the orbit. This is a calibrated projection approximation, rather than a recovered exact camera and light solution. Short blurred yellow and blue reflections follow the object bases on the dark platform, with small contact shadows.

Frame zero is preserved exactly. For following frames, a source-minus-proxy calibration residual decays with exp(-frame/1.15) to soften the transition from the source photograph to the reconstructed view. The background then remains fixed; the changing geometry, shading, shadows, and reflections supply the motion.

Review and verification

Image tools were used to review the original image, enlarged source objects, reconstructed background, inspection sheets, and adjacent-frame transition sheets. Revisions removed interpolation discontinuities, stationary foreground remnants, incorrect face lighting, and foreground colors entering the wall shadows. The final inspection and transition sheets were visually reviewed.

generate.py verifies that all 20 exported PNGs are RGB arrays with the intended dimensions and that every pixel agrees with the saved tensor. pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20). Each element is packed as 0xRRGGBB, with no alpha channel. The original first-frame pixels are checked for exact equality. The standalone HTML player is also checked by independently decoding its first frame and every delta packet and comparing the reconstructed sequence with the tensor.

The hidden background, block back faces, exact camera calibration, and light geometry cannot be uniquely determined from one photograph. They are reconstructed approximations. The motion is explicitly calculated and constant in angular speed; it is not a video generated by a learned model or a full scene simulation.
