fill-glass-red-drink · 生成方法

This is a deterministic local pixel animation of first_frame.png. The only scene inputs were that image and prompt.txt; GENERATION_RULES.md supplied the output requirements. No network, other scene references, external models, rendering engines, or subagents were used. OBSERVATION.md was written before animation coding and records scene measurements and the 20-frame plan.

generate.py uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy's bilinear image sampler. Each output begins with the original photographic pixels. Analytic spatial coverage masks confine changes to the liquid in the glass, the falling stream, and a small band around the dispenser's liquid boundary. The wall, tabletop, basket, glass silhouette, and other scenery retain the original pixels outside those areas.

The initial image already shows pouring. The continuation therefore keeps the tap open throughout. Over frames 00–19, the receiving surface center rises from y=103 to y=82.5. It remains below the glass mouth. The dispenser's visible front meniscus falls by 4.51 pixels, using a cross-sectional area approximation of 0.22 relative to the receiving glass.

The original receiving surface texture is translated upward while retaining its elliptical aspect ratio. A separate vertical texture remap stretches the lower red liquid body, anchored at the glass bottom. Taper-aware horizontal coordinates and protected edge highlights retain the glass outline. Small analytic waves and paired light/dark elliptical ripples perturb the receiving surface. The impact texture follows the measured stream center as the liquid level rises.

The falling stream mainly retains the photographed stream. Its analytic center is x=124.8−0.72√(y−59). Small downward-moving brightness patterns are applied only within the stream and above the new receiving surface. This approximates flowing liquid without a full fluid simulation. The dispenser's newly exposed transparent band uses nearby ribbed-glass pixels from the same image. A geometric tap mask and chroma-based edge protection preserve the foreground tap; donor coordinates that intersect the tap use an empty glass band above it to prevent duplicated tap features.

This method preserves the source photograph's lighting and detail but uses image warps and approximate liquid geometry rather than reconstructing the scene in 3D. The reservoir approximation updates the visible front boundary, not every internal refraction or rear-surface reflection.

Artifacts

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 tensor with shape (144, 256, 20) and values packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: standalone player containing all 20 frames, with playback at 250 ms per frame (4 fps, five seconds), replay, seek, and loop controls.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames from across the continuation.
  • transition_check.png: consecutive frames at the beginning, middle, and end, each with a separate thumbnail of the adjacent absolute RGB difference amplified by four.
  • generate.py, OBSERVATION.md, and this method record.

Checks and review

The generator checks the frame count, dimensions, tensor shape and dtype, exact initial RGB equality, and equality of every saved PNG with the decoded tensor. Frame 00 also copies the source PNG verbatim, preserving byte equality. Pixels outside the defined fluid neighborhoods remain identical across all 20 frames. Independent validation reconstructs the player packets and compares all frames with the tensor.

Image View tools were used to inspect the source, enlarged source, generated contact sheets, transition sheet, and enlarged final frame. Visual review led to refining the tap's occlusion mask and excluding the tap from transparent-glass texture donors. Final review checked the continuous fill, contained impact texture, retained headspace, and fixed scene composition. Validation details are recorded in .tmp/verification.json.

To regenerate all pixel artifacts and run the built-in checks, run python generate.py in this directory.
