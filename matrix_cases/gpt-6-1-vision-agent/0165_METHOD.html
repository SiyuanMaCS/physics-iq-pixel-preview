The broad glass bowl occupies x=73–199, y=21–96. Its upper elliptical rim is centered around (136,26); the red liquid surface is around y=70–75 and the curved lower bowl edge around y=94. These features and the wood grain must stay stationary.

A narrower transparent jar occupies approximately x=112–179. Its upper part is clipped by the top of the image. Its lower curved edge is at y=68–75, already near the liquid surface. The match bundle/support stands near x=126–150, y=10–73: dark heads near x=137–144, y=10–16, blue band near y=23–35, and a pink-red rectangular support below. The source has only faint warm coloration at the match tips; do not change frame 00 to enhance it. In following frames, small flames can make the specified lit state visible. No person, tool, label, or extra object should enter the shot.

Use a short, smooth downward jar translation of approximately 9 pixels, enough to immerse the open mouth in the liquid. The jar remains vertical and surrounding glass bowl rim occludes it. The stationary match bundle stays behind the moving transparent glass. Small flames flicker, shrink after enclosure, and disappear. The red liquid inside the jar subsequently rises by approximately 8 pixels, with gentle damped surface ripples. This is a restrained continuation of a realistic match-and-inverted-glass demonstration; the source does not support a larger camera movement or a large splash.

Planned method

Use only local Python/Pillow/NumPy/SciPy pixel operations. Estimate the view behind the glass from neighboring source pixels, derive a glass appearance residual, and translate that residual with subpixel resampling while retaining the stationary bowl and match support. Add antialiased, softly shaded flame shapes, weak glow, and a transparent red inner-liquid region with an elliptical meniscus. Use deterministic smooth temporal controls. Export through the supplied output-only artifact_io helper. Review generated contact sheets with an image tool and verify every PNG against the packed uint32 tensor.

METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is a deterministic local pixel animation written in Python using NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. Its only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, other scene references, external models, rendering engines, or subagents were used. OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code.

The original RGB image supplies the stationary scene. Neighboring horizontal strips provide an approximate background estimate inside the narrow jar. Subtracting that estimate produces a glass appearance residual. The residual over the match support and foreground bowl rim is interpolated from adjacent glass regions to avoid moving those stationary objects. A cubic smoothstep moves this residual down by nine pixels over 1.75 seconds; bilinear coordinate sampling handles fractional pixel positions. The residual difference is composited onto the original image, with protection masks for the stationary support, bowl rim, and foreground red liquid. Texture above the image boundary is extended from the first available row. This is an image-space approximation of glass transmission and motion, not a reconstruction of physical geometry or a fluid simulation.

Three small flames are drawn as polygons and cores at four times the output resolution and reduced with Lanczos filtering. Smooth deterministic oscillations control their tips and lengths. A weak Gaussian glow accompanies the warm flame colors. The flames diminish after enclosure and disappear by 2.75 seconds. A faint blurred smoke curve appears briefly afterward.

The inner liquid rise is an eight-pixel smoothstep over 1.95–4.70 seconds. Analytic elliptical contours define the meniscus, transparent red body tint, and subtle reflected edge highlights. Fading the body and glass below the bowl's foreground liquid keeps the submerged portion visually occluded. Small elliptical wavelets during descent decay rapidly. The wood table, wall outside the jar, bowl shape, and match support remain fixed.
