This sequence was generated with local Python pixel operations using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. generate.py is the reproducible renderer; artifact_io.py and player_template.html are the supplied output helpers. No external images, learned models, network services, subagents, or external rendering engines were used.

Photograph editing

The original frame supplies the fixed camera view, table, background, cradle base, chrome support, grabber surface and metal-ball surface. Hand-measured local contours and RGB colour thresholds isolate the blue and black grabber. The two jaws are separated at a sloping contour, rotated slightly about their common pivot, and translated out through the lower-left edge. Ball silhouettes and reflections are masked locally. Thin suspension-thread repair initially uses local inpainting and horizontal interpolation, with the support posts protected.

Wood hidden by the grabber is inferred from a quadratic fit to exposed table colours, with deterministic, low-amplitude, elongated grain. The hidden base surface uses a fit to exposed base pixels. A discrete Laplace equation solves a colour residual across the combined repaired area, joining these inferred surfaces to the photographed boundary. The rear-left support is continued using pixels from its exposed portion. The foreground support is composited in front of the moving parts. Hidden surfaces are inferred rather than recovered from another image.

Motion and pixels

Five sphere centres are placed on the measured sloping rest row. A damped small-angle pendulum approximation has a full cycle of 1.16 seconds and an initial horizontal displacement of about 21 pixels. The angular signal is asin(21.2/80) * exp(-0.036*t) * cos(2*pi*t/1.16). During one sign of this signal the left two balls move; during the other sign the right two move. The other three stay in the resting row. This gives an ideal instantaneous two-ball transfer at each passage through equilibrium; elastic deformation and the brief internal collision sequence are below the sampled resolution.

Projected horizontal displacement is 80*sin(angle). Vertical rise is 76.5*(1-cos(angle)), combined with the measured swing-plane slope of -0.296. Each moving sphere retains paired threads attached to fixed points on the photographed crossbars. Sphere RGB values are resampled from an unobstructed photographed chrome ball, masked by an antialiased circular silhouette and given a small analytic specular adjustment. Blurred dark reflections and narrow cool glints follow the projected sphere positions on the black base.

Rendering uses four-times spatial supersampling, Pillow compositing, NumPy arithmetic, OpenCV sampling, and SciPy numerical operations. Three subframe samples over an 18-millisecond shutter interval give restrained motion blur. Frames are downsampled to 256 × 144 RGB. Frame 00 is inserted directly from the unmodified source pixel array, bypassing rendering.
