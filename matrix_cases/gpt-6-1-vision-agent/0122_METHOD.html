METHOD.md

Method

Inputs and timing

The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. GENERATION_RULES.md supplied the output constraints. OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator and records the measured scene and the 20-frame plan.

The output contains 20 RGB frames, each 256 × 144, displayed at 4 fps for five seconds. Frame t samples angle 6t degrees at time t/4 seconds. The clockwise turn therefore spans 0–114 degrees at 24 degrees per second. Frame 00 is copied from the input without any pixel changes; its PNG file is also byte-identical to the input.

Pixel construction

generate.py uses local Python with NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV image operations. The geometry and rasterization are written directly in Python. No network requests, external images, AI models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

The static source photograph supplies the wall, tabletop, mirror, furniture, black bases, and power cord. Small regions covered by the original teapot and red attachments are recovered using OpenCV's local Navier–Stokes image inpainting. A source-column fit preserves the sharp mirror/furniture boundary where the original handle covered it. All output changes are constrained to the two teapot footprints. The lower black platforms and the rest of the scene are restored from the input exactly.

Each round body uses a manually fitted, stationary silhouette and an approximate ellipsoid normal map. Source pixels from the foreground body and the reflected body are inverse-projected into a shared longitude/latitude color atlas. Red attachment pixels and silhouette boundaries are excluded from this material map. The material rotates while the axisymmetric body outline stays fixed. A simple stationary diffuse-light factor removes and reapplies approximate shading during reprojection.

The photograph does not show the entire surface. Unobserved areas use blue ceramic color with small flower-and-leaf decals sampled from unobstructed motifs in the input photograph. These are placed on the same rotating material atlas. A confidence blend connects them to observed source texture. Seeded, low-amplitude color variation is deterministic. This constructs a plausible unseen face; it does not recover its exact original ornament.

The red handle is a spline-shaped loop with a flattened tubular cross-section. The spout is a curved, tapered tube with a dark mouth. Both are locally triangulated, rotated around the vertical axis, projected into the image, and composited using a depth buffer. Normal-based diffuse shading and narrow, bounded specular highlights give them a red ceramic appearance. The attachment elevation and placement are fitted to the two photographed silhouettes. This is an approximate 2.5D reconstruction rather than a calibrated camera/geometry solution.

The real handle's projected azimuth advances with the rotation. The reflected handle's projected azimuth decreases by the same angle. Reflected material longitude is also reversed, with a fitted 115-degree offset between the two observed views. This maintains synchronized opposite apparent rotation. The source lid, knob, and pedestal are retained as fixed axisymmetric photographic patches; the few red attachment pixels overlapping the lid are removed from those patches.

Rendering uses four-times supersampling and Lanczos reduction. Output is quantized to uint8 RGB. artifact_io.py saves the packed tensor, PNG sequence, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player. transition_check.png is a chronological contact sheet of all 20 frames.

Revisions and visual inspection

Image View tools were used on the input enlargement, early contact sheets, close-ups, and the final inspection.png and transition_check.png. The initial unseen-surface fill stretched color into bands; it was replaced with source-derived floral decals and a confidence blend. Attachment placement, handle thickness, specular normalization, and recovery of the mirror boundary were then refined. The final visual review checked fixed framing, upright rotation, changing attachment occlusion, synchronized mirror motion, and the transitions across the entire sequence.

The single photograph provides limited geometry and hidden-surface information. Floral details and projection on newly revealed sides are approximations, and the first generated frame transitions from the exact photograph into that reconstruction.

Verification

  • Exactly 20 PNGs, named frames/00.png through frames/19.png, each RGB and 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz contains key pixels, dtype uint32, shape (144, 256, 20).
  • Each tensor value uses 0xRRGGBB packing, with the time axis last.
  • Every PNG was decoded and compared exactly with the corresponding unpacked tensor slice.
  • frames/00.png matches first_frame.png both in pixels and in file bytes.
  • The embedded HTML first-frame and 19 delta packets were decoded and compared exactly with all tensor slices.
  • The player has 20 frames and a 250 ms playback interval.
  • Every consecutive frame changes, while pixels outside the two constrained teapot footprints are identical throughout.
  • Final inspection and transition contact sheets were visually reviewed using image tools.
