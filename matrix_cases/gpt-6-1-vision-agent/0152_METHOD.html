METHOD.md

Generation method and validation

This is a deterministic local Python pixel continuation of first_frame.png. It uses NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy, with artifact_io.py solely for output packaging. It uses no external image, model, engine, network resource, or subagent. The prompt supplies the action and the caption. The scene measurements and initial 20-frame plan were written to OBSERVATION.md before generate.py was created.

Image construction

The camera and background are inherited from the supplied image. The initial ball and its soft shadow obscure a small patch of wood. That patch is reconstructed by interpolating clean tabletop colors from the same image row on either side, then transferring low-amplitude wood grain from a nearby clean strip. Smooth ellipse masks blend this reconstruction into the original image. A first version used a fitted color field and donor pixels; visual inspection exposed a pale patch and a remaining brown halo. The final version uses the broader mask and row interpolation, which removes those artifacts while continuing the horizontal grain.

The moving ball is an analytically projected sphere with a radius of approximately 9.55 pixels, increasing by about 0.14 pixels along the slightly descending projected path. Its visible material comes from the original ball: source pixels are sampled on the initial visible hemisphere and approximately separated from fixed illumination and edge blending. The initially hidden hemisphere is filled with deterministic brown felt variations and a continuous curved pale seam. Thus, the hidden surface is a locally constructed approximation, not a recovered photograph. Inverse Rodrigues rotation maps the sphere normals into the ball's material space as it rolls. The illumination remains anchored above and to the left while the texture rotates.

The foreground sphere is rendered at four times the output resolution, with premultiplied soft edges and filtering to match the source focus. Five samples across a 0.02-second exposure provide slight motion blur. A displaced elliptical soft cast shadow and a narrower contact shadow follow the ball. At impact the ball compresses by a maximum of 3.5% horizontally for a short instant, and a small contact shadow appears on the fabric. The box remains stationary.

Frame 00 is copied directly from the original RGB image. All later frames are composited over the reconstructed background. No new scene object is added.

Timing and physics

Twenty RGB frames at 256 × 144 are sampled at 4 fps, at times 0.00 through 4.75 seconds. Each frame is held for 0.25 seconds, giving a five-second sequence. The ball is already out of the tube in the supplied initial frame, so the continuation starts from that state.

For the initial roll, with time in seconds and coordinates in pixels:

  • x(t) = 108.8 + 56t − 3.2t², for 0 ≤ t ≤ 2.
  • y(t) = 61.4 + 0.036(x(t) − 108.8).
  • Contact occurs at t = 2, center (208.0, 64.9712). The ball's leading edge reaches the slanting fabric silhouette near x=217.3.
  • The rebound starts at −18 pixels/second, with 9 pixels/second² of rolling deceleration. For τ = t − 2, x = 208 − 18τ + 4.5τ² until τ = 2.
  • The ball reaches rest at (190.0, 64.3232) at 4 seconds and remains there through the final hold.

The sphere's rotation angle is its signed displacement divided by the initial apparent radius. Rebound therefore reverses its rolling rotation. These are projected image-space physical approximations; a single source frame does not provide a calibrated three-dimensional scene.
