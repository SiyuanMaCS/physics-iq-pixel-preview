METHOD.md

Method

This is a local, image-based animation made from first_frame.png. It uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV for pixel operations. No network access, reference images, learned models, external engines, or subagents were used.

Motion and scene interpretation

The wrench turns clockwise around the platform's vertical center axis, retaining its initial radial placement. The platform, magnet, purple strip, cable, tabletop, and camera remain fixed. Its original jaw overhang is retained as it travels around the rim. This is an analytic continuation of the pictured setup, rather than a simulation of magnetic forces.

The horizontal plane is projected using a vertical compression of 0.505. Rotation is centered at image coordinates (154.8, 61.1). For each frame, the transformation is P R(theta) P^-1, plus the translation that keeps that center fixed, where P = diag(1, 0.505) and the positive angle produces clockwise image motion. Applying this to the original foreground rotates both the wrench's location and its orientation with consistent perspective.

The 20 frames are sampled at t = frame / 4 seconds, with theta = frame * 18 degrees. This is a constant 72 degrees per second. Frame 00 is at 0.00 s, and frame 19 is at 4.75 s. Each frame is displayed for 0.25 s, yielding five seconds of playback. The next sample at 5.00 s would return to the initial pose. Frame 00 bypasses all processing and is copied exactly from the input.

Pixel construction

  1. Trace a local outline around the source wrench. Build an antialiased support mask, retaining the transparent opening between the jaw prongs.
  2. Reconstruct only the small area originally covered by the wrench and its contact shadow. Local inpainting estimates the wooden background. A quadratic color fit from intact upper-face pixels estimates the platform top, and a local linear fit estimates the front side. Supersampled elliptical coverage preserves the boundary between them. Tiny deterministic noise avoids a completely flat repaired area. Original pixels outside this repair region are preserved.
  3. Estimate foreground alpha from the silver wrench's brightness above the reconstructed background. Unpremultiply its colors against that background to reduce dark and wood-colored fringes when the wrench moves.
  4. Warp foreground color and alpha at four times the output resolution. Average three closely spaced angular samples spanning 0.64 degrees for a short-exposure blur, then downsample. Attach a small blurred contact shadow, offset in the fixed image lighting direction, to the moving footprint.
  5. Composite the resulting foreground onto the reconstructed source image. No captions or diagnostic marks enter any frame.

This method approximates the hidden tabletop/platform pixels and contact shadow. It reuses the source metal texture rather than computing changing three-dimensional reflections. There is no inferred magnetic-field model or full 3D reconstruction.

Review and revision

The source image, a magnified source crop, the generated inspection montage, the transition difference sheet, foreground alpha, reconstructed background, and enlarged selected frames were viewed using an image tool. The first review exposed a small residual source shadow at the front rim. The removal region was expanded and all artifacts were regenerated. The final montage, repaired background, and transition sheet were visually checked after this revision.
