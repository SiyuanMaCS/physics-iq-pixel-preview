OBSERVATION.md

Scene observations and event plan

The input is an RGB image, 256 by 144 pixels. The camera is fixed, slightly above the tabletop. A gray wall fills the background. The wooden table's far edge runs from about (21,80) to (206,61); its near edge fills the bottom of the frame. Illumination comes from the front/left and produces two bright reflections on the balloon.

The glossy black balloon has silhouette bounds approximately x=78..151, y=27..103, center (115,65), and a small tied end near (125,101). It rests on the table, with a soft contact shadow below and to its right. The black circular platform has an elliptical top around (200,75), radius approximately 24 by 8 pixels, and a front wall reaching y=92. A cable exits to the right and must remain fixed. A small pinkish fixing tab near (193,75) attaches the pale match/wooden arm. The arm extends toward the lower left, with a dark pink head near (129,98), just outside the balloon silhouette. The tiny ignition in the input is subdued; subsequent frames will make the small warm flame legible without enlarging it into a torch.

Motion model: clockwise rotation in the plane of the platform, projected to an ellipse in image coordinates. Pivot (193,75), initial tip about (129,98), tabletop depth foreshortening 0.43. Advance about 2 degrees per frame (8 degrees/second), with a fixed pivot and length. Clockwise motion carries the tip left and slightly upward into the balloon's lower-right side. After brief heating the balloon ruptures rapidly, because an ordinary air-filled latex balloon cannot sustain contact with a flame. At 4 fps rupture occurs between adjacent exposures; residual latex pieces recoil, fall, and settle. No camera motion, people, tools, or unrelated objects are introduced.

METHOD.md

Method and verification

This is a deterministic local Python pixel animation derived from first_frame.png. It uses NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy; no network, outside imagery, external models, rendering engines, or subagents were used. Run python generate.py from this workspace to regenerate the artifacts.

Image construction

Frame 00 is the source photograph, copied byte for byte to frames/00.png. Frames 01–04 retain the photographed balloon's silhouette, highlights, and surface. The original pale arm is removed using a narrow local OpenCV inpaint, and its moving replacement is drawn at fourfold pixel resolution then reduced with Lanczos filtering. Its pivot stays at (193,75). A constant 2-degree advance per exposure rotates the match clockwise on the horizontal plane; a 0.43 vertical projection factor accounts for the camera's view of the tabletop. The pale shaft, charred head, cast shadow, warm glow, and small vertically rising flame are composited locally. Flame shape varies deterministically between exposures. The photographed fixing tab is retained.

The balloon bursts between frames 04 and 05, after several exposures of flame contact. An air-filled latex balloon is assumed. The rupture itself is faster than the 250 ms sampling interval, so the next exposure shows recoiling latex rather than an extended shrinking balloon. Torn membranes are irregular antialiased polygons shaded with dark procedural folds and restrained highlights. Their positions, rotations, and flattening are prescribed sample trajectories: raised recoil at frame 05, descent at 06, contact with the table at 07, then settling through 09. These are authored pixel motions inspired by recoil and falling, not a full dynamics or fracture simulation. Three small latex remnants and the tied neck settle near the original balloon base.

After rupture, the occluded background is reconstructed only within a region surrounding the old balloon and its contact shadow. Visible gray wall pixels determine a smooth cubic lighting fit. Unobstructed source tabletop pixels to the left are copied and warped relative to the measured back edge to supply wood grain. An explicit sparse Laplace solve adds a harmonic color correction whose boundary conditions come from visible source pixels. This removes the old balloon and shadow while blending the reconstructed wall and table into their surroundings. The hidden texture is an approximation; it cannot be recovered exactly from a single photograph. The camera and all other source pixels stay fixed outside the reconstruction and match effect regions.
