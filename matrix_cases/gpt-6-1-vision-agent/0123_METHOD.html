OBSERVATION.md

Scene observation and event plan

The input is a 256 × 144 RGB photograph. The camera is fixed, looking down slightly at a wooden table. A blue, decorated ceramic teapot stands on a black circular turntable near (100, 112). Its oval body occupies approximately x=78–118, y=62–100, with a yellow-green lid at y=55–64 and a small dark knob near (97, 53). An orange-red attachment is seen nearly end-on over the right half of the body. The mirror occupies the upper-right area, with its left boundary near x=132 and lower edge slanting from (133, 91) to (214, 115). It reflects the teapot around (193, 65), its spout extending left to x=170 and its handle to the right to x=217. The reflected pedestal is near (192, 87). The table, black power cable, wall, mirror edge, and two pedestal locations stay fixed.

The blue body has yellow, pale green, red, and white floral decoration. Its appearance must remain rounded, opaque, shaded, and ceramic as it turns. The reflected view is smaller and sees another side of the same object. Both views must evolve synchronously under a common rotation, with reversed apparent handedness in the mirror. No new objects, text, camera motion, cuts, or changes to illumination are planned.

Planned pixel method

Use only the supplied photograph, local Python image operations, and a small analytic 3D projection of rounded ceramic surfaces. Estimate the body texture from the two visible views, locally fill unseen surface regions using colors and decorations from the input, and project the rotating body with attached spout and handle. Fill only the background previously occluded by the moving teapot; preserve all other pixels. Render the direct and reflected projections with linked angles. Supersample for clean edges. METHOD.md

Generation method

The sequence is a deterministic local Python reconstruction from first_frame.png. No network, external models, reference images, simulation engines, or subagents were used. Run python generate.py in this workspace to reproduce the artifacts.

The pot body is an analytic ellipsoid with a fixed upright axis. The program inverse-projects colors from the larger direct view and the smaller mirror view into one latitude/longitude texture atlas. It locally inpaints the red handle that overlaps the directly visible body, and excludes background colors from atlas sampling using a blue-ceramic neighborhood mask. Unseen areas copy photographed ornament from other longitudes at the same latitude, followed by a small amount of seam smoothing. Unseen decoration is an approximation; the source image does not establish a complete surface texture.

The handle is a curved tube, and the spout is a tapered tube along a cubic curve, with a rim and dark recessed opening. These attachments use analytic normals, red ceramic shading, highlights, and sparse gold flecks. A local CPU triangle rasterizer supplies depth occlusion against the ray-intersected body. Both projections share one texture atlas, one attachment geometry, and the same rotation angle. The direct projection and the reflected projection use estimated view directions separated by about 102 degrees, and the reflected projection reverses horizontal handedness. These directions approximate the photograph rather than a calibrated mirror/camera arrangement.

The rotation proceeds clockwise as viewed from above, at 48 degrees per second: 12 degrees between adjacent frames, ending at 228 degrees in frame 19. Neither object center moves. The lid, small knob, and flared foot retain their photographed pixels because their shapes are approximately symmetric about the rotation axis at this image resolution. Their tiny surface markings consequently remain fixed. The black platforms, mirror frame, wall, wooden table, and cable stay stationary. Only the originally occluded ceramic regions are filled into the background, using local OpenCV inpainting. The renderer preserves untouched source background pixels.

Rendering uses 3× spatial supersampling and area reduction to 256 × 144 RGB. Frame 00 is copied directly from the input. A body-interior residual correction fades out over the first two generated frames to reduce the transition from the photograph to the approximate reconstruction; it does not affect later frames or silhouette regions. There is no camera motion, interpolation between video clips, or added scene content.

Artifacts and validation

  • generate.py: all synthesis, inspection-sheet generation, and verification code.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, played at 4 fps for a 5-second sequence.
  • pixels.npz: pixels, a uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20), with each value packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: standalone player supplied by the local output helper, containing the complete pixel data.
  • inspection.png: eight full-frame samples.
  • transition_check.png: enlarged direct and reflected views from the opening frames and representative later turns.
  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and the event plan written before coding.

The script verifies the tensor shape and dtype, exact equality of frame 00 with the source RGB pixels, and equality of every PNG with its corresponding packed tensor slice. The two inspection images were opened with an image-view tool. Visual review prompted a revision to prevent stretched color bands on the unobserved body surface and a revision to the spout centerline and height.

This is a photograph-based pixel reconstruction with synthesized unseen surfaces, not a recovered physical model of the original teapot.
