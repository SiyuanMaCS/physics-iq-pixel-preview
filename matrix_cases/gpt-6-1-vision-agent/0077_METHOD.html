light-on-mug · 生成方法

This sequence was made with local Python pixel operations using NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. The only input image is first_frame.png. No external imagery, AI model, network request, physics engine, or external rendering engine was used.

The source image supplies the wall, stationary spotlight, wooden tabletop, wire, turntable, and cylindrical cup body. Because the cup is approximately rotationally symmetric, its body silhouette, elliptical rim, and world-fixed glossy highlights can remain photographed as it rotates. The handle is the primary visible orientation cue.

Two small hand-measured regions isolate the original mug handle and its wall shadow. A discrete harmonic RGB fill reconstructs the background in those regions while preserving the photographed cup and cup-body shadow. These region masks are generated locally in code; they are not input assets.

The replacement handle is a rounded C-shaped spline swept with a circular tube cross-section. The geometry rotates at a constant 72 degrees per second about the vertical cup axis. A small software triangle rasterizer implemented in generate.py projects it with depth-dependent scale, tests it against an approximate cylindrical cup for occlusion, and evaluates yellow ceramic shading under a stationary low frontal light. A modest color correction sampled from the original yellow handle retains its photographed hue. Rendered coverage and premultiplied color are softened together at four times the target resolution.

The same handle geometry is projected onto the wall with an approximate point-light mapping calibrated to the original large shadow. Moving closer to the lamp increases the projection; moving rearward decreases it. A soft attenuation silhouette combines with the preserved dark body shadow. The lighting calibration and cylinder occlusion are approximations intended to reproduce this small image, rather than a complete recovery of physical camera and lamp parameters.

A very faint analytic brushed-surface pattern rotates on the top of the black platform, retaining the fixed photographed reflections. When the handle crosses the front, a small local attenuation adds its shadow to the cup face. Native-resolution delta compositing preserves all source pixels outside the reconstructed and moving regions.

The twenty poses are sampled at 0.00, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds with 18 degrees of clockwise rotation between poses, from 0 through 342 degrees. The player displays each for 250 ms, giving 4 fps and a five-second sequence. Frame 00 is copied directly from the original RGB image, without reconstruction or resampling.

Artifacts and validation

  • generate.py: deterministic generation and validation script; run with python generate.py from this workspace.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty RGB PNG images, each 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 array with shape (144, 256, 20); each element contains 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: all twenty frames in chronological order, with sample time, angle, and mean absolute consecutive RGB change.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-data player with playback, seeking, and looping at 4 fps.
  • OBSERVATION.md: scene measurements and the event plan written before generation.

The script decodes every packed tensor slice and checks exact equality against its corresponding PNG. It also checks the tensor shape and dtype and verifies exact RGB equality between frame 00 and first_frame.png. Image tools were used to review the source, enlarged details, the first full transition sheet, and a comparison of initial, opposite-side, and front-facing handle poses. Both final regenerated contact sheets were also visually reviewed. A final independent artifact check confirmed twenty RGB PNGs at the required dimensions and exact preservation of source pixels in the fixed outer scene.
