OBSERVATION.md

Observations and event plan

The input is a 256 × 144 RGB image. The camera looks obliquely down on a transparent, textured glass cutting board on a light brown wooden table. The board's upper corner is near (111, 9), its left edge runs from (11, 64) toward (67, 143), and its right side continues out of frame. The table and board are stationary.

The orange tangerine half occupies approximately x=92–136, y=59–101. Its rind is bright orange, with a pale pith border and visible juicy segments. Its face is seen obliquely, with a center near (113, 80). The metal knife covers a diagonal band from its tip near (73, 110) to its shaft exiting the upper border near x=170–204. Its lower edge crosses the fruit near (111, 82). The blade has a blue-gray metallic face and repeated scalloped indentations. No hand is visible. The blade should move rigidly, retain these details, and remain connected to its offscreen handle.

The intended event is one continuous downward slice through the tangerine half, slight separation of the cut portions, then withdrawal of the knife. The right portion moves only a few pixels along the board and settles; no extra objects, camera motion, captions, or scene changes are introduced. The first frame is preserved byte for byte. Areas revealed by moving foreground objects will be reconstructed locally from the input's own board/table texture. Hidden fruit pixels will be reconstructed with local rind/segment texture and geometric shading, using no external inputs.

Planned implementation

Use local Python/NumPy/Pillow/SciPy pixel compositing: manually measured antialiased object masks; source-texture translation and local texture filling for newly exposed static background; rigid blade translation; fruit texture completion and a small affine movement of the cut section; soft contact shadows. Preserve the original static pixels wherever no foreground object or shadow changes. Export RGB PNGs, the packed uint32 tensor, an HTML player, and diagnostic sheets. Inspect generated sheets with an image tool and revise if needed. Verify every PNG exactly against its tensor slice.

METHOD.md

Method

This is an input-only, deterministic Python pixel animation. It uses first_frame.png for all photographic texture and prompt.txt for the action. NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV provide local raster operations. No network, external images, trained models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation program and contains the measured setup and the 20-frame event plan.

Construction

The board, table, viewpoint, and lighting stay fixed. Manually measured polygons isolate the knife and the tangerine. The polygons are rasterized at four times the output size and downsampled to make soft subpixel edges.

The initially hidden background is filled using OpenCV's traditional Telea image inpainting. Nearby texture from the same input supplies glass and wood grain. Samples near the glass rim are excluded to prevent copying the rim onto the table. The part of the glass rim initially covered by the knife is reconstructed from a sampled profile of its visible continuation.

The visible orange rind and flesh retain the original photograph. Pixels initially hidden by the blade are completed with an oblique orange flesh field, radial pith lines, deterministic small-scale noise, and patches of the original exposed segments. This is a local raster reconstruction, not an inferred three-dimensional model. The reconstruction mask includes the antialiased blue spine pixels so they do not remain on the fruit after withdrawal.

The knife moves by rigid subpixel translation. Its original scallops and steel texture move with it. The blade is extended above the original image boundary with sampled steel, keeping its connection offscreen during the downstroke. Small fruit-colored pixels caught in the source knife matte are replaced with steel sampled farther along the blade. The dark reflection around the original contact region fades during withdrawal.

The tangerine is divided along a diagonal cutting plane. The back portion stays in place. The front portion separates by 6.3 pixels horizontally and 2.8 pixels vertically and rotates 3.4 degrees around its lower contact point. A thin shaded edge and soft local shadows give the separated pieces thickness and contact with the board. Smooth cubic progress functions produce one downward cut, a brief low position, and upward withdrawal. The knife has left the image by frame 17, and the final three frames hold the resting pieces.

This approach preserves source detail at the required small resolution. Newly exposed fruit and background are synthesized estimates; the motion is prescribed, rather than the result of a rigid-body or material fracture simulation.

Deliverables and timing

  • generate.py: reproducible generator; run with python generate.py from this workspace.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: a pixels array with shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, values packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-data player using 250 ms frame intervals, for 4 fps and a 5-second cycle.
  • inspection.png: eight representative frames.
  • transition_check.png: consecutive early/cutting/withdrawal views and amplified adjacent differences.
  • verification.json: generator checks and frame motion parameters.

Frame t begins at t / 4 seconds and is held for 0.25 seconds. The contact sheet and player display the end time of the held frame. Frame 00 copies the exact original PNG bytes and pixels. The final held frame ends at 5.00 seconds.

Review and verification

Revisions removed a blue residual strip on the background, a blue spine remnant on the fruit, and warm-colored pixels moving with the blade. The final inspection and transition sheets were visually reviewed.

The generator verifies the tensor shape and dtype, the RGB mode of all 20 PNGs, exact first-frame pixel equality, and exact equality of every PNG's packed colors to its tensor slice. An independent final check also verifies byte-for-byte equality of the first PNG and reconstructs every frame from the HTML player's embedded first-frame and delta packets, comparing all 20 against the saved tensor. All checks passed.
