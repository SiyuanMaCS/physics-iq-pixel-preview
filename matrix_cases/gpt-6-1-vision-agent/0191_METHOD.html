OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The input is 256 × 144 RGB. The camera is fixed, looking slightly down at a wooden tabletop. The rear table edge is near y=81, with the nearer table filling the lower part of the frame; the rest is a softly textured gray wall. Light comes mainly from the upper left.

The dark kettlebell body is approximately x=112–172, y=20–80, centered near (142, 51), with its blue 30 LB marking around (153, 57). Its iron handle occupies x=101–176 and is cropped by the upper frame edge. The kettlebell and its marking must move as one rigid object, without changes in scale. The source image already contains the only objects in the scene.

The white foam cup is lying on its side beneath the kettlebell. Its visible extent is about x=124–165, y=78–103. The broad open rim is at the left, with a rim seam near x=131; the smaller closed end faces right. It rests on the tabletop at y≈102, and the bell touches its upper side near y≈79. The heavy load can buckle this thin foam shell. The continuation uses a short load-induced collapse, not a long fall. The cup remains as a flattened, creased white shell beneath the bell.

I will preserve the source image outside the local object and shadow region. Newly exposed wall will be reconstructed from neighboring wall pixels. The cropped portions of the handle revealed by the downward translation will be extended locally with curved, shaded iron tubes consistent with the existing sides. There will be no hand, additional objects, camera motion, zoom, or cuts.

METHOD.md

Method

This is a deterministic local Python image animation built from first_frame.png. The continuation shows the 30 lb kettlebell crushing the sideways styrofoam cup, dropping approximately 20 pixels, making a small recovery, and resting on the flattened foam. The camera, framing, table edge, and image dimensions stay fixed.

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV for pixel operations. It does not use an external renderer, learned model, network service, additional scene images, or a physical simulation engine.

Pixel construction

  • The kettlebell silhouette is extracted with a local dark-pixel threshold and connected-component contour, keeping the opening in its cropped handle. The original surface texture and 30 LB lettering are translated together as a rigid image layer. A soft subpixel mask and premultiplied resampling handle the edges.
  • The wall exposed by the falling kettlebell is reconstructed with a discrete harmonic inpaint, solved with SciPy's sparse linear solver. The removal mask expands past the original antialiased edge. This revision removed an outline of the original bell that was visible in the first render.
  • A small region of table behind the original cup is reconstructed by copying nearby grain from lower in the same source image. The source scene stays unchanged outside the small object and contact-shadow region.
  • The upper cropped handle sides are continued from their existing pixels, with a curved coordinate shift and local source-texture sampling. The top arch remains outside the frame; the model does not invent an uncropped handle or change its scale.
  • The cup uses a hand-drawn soft silhouette and inverse coordinate warp. The bottom remains at its original table contact; the upper wall lowers, the shell spreads slightly, and spatially varying folds and low-amplitude crease shading appear during compression. Its visible rim becomes an irregular flattened lip under the load.
  • A narrow soft contact shadow supports the crushed cup and weighted body. All layers are rendered at the original 256 × 144 resolution.

Timing

The prescribed sequence has 20 frames at 4 fps, for a five-second display. Frame 00 is copied directly from the source. The subsequent bell offsets in pixels are 1.15, 6.20, 15.10, 19.80, 19.25, and 19.55. Frames 06–19 share the final resting state. Thus the deformation occupies the first second, followed by a small settling movement and a stationary hold.

This motion is a parameterized, physically motivated approximation of a thin foam shell buckling under a heavy load. Material stiffness and force are not estimated from the image, and no claim of quantitative stress simulation is made.

Outputs and validation

artifact_io.save writes pixels.npz, the 20 RGB PNGs in frames/, inspection.png, and the standalone index.html player. generate.py also writes transition_check.png, showing consecutive early frames and their amplified absolute pixel differences.

The packed tensor is named pixels, has dtype uint32 and shape (144, 256, 20), and encodes each pixel as 0xRRGGBB. All 20 PNGs are read back and checked for exact equality against the corresponding tensor frame. Frame 00 is separately checked for exact RGB equality with first_frame.png.

Actual visual reviews used the image View tool on the initial frame, an enlarged initial image, the inspection and transition sheets, and an enlarged final frame. After the wall reconstruction revision, the updated inspection sheet, transition sheet, and final frame were reviewed again. The final checks also decode the standalone player's base64 first frame and delta packets and compare every reconstructed frame against the tensor.
