METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is a local pixel-based continuation of first_frame.png, guided by prompt.txt. It uses Python, NumPy, Pillow and SciPy image sampling. No external images, video, network access, trained model, external rendering engine or subagent was used. The supplied artifact_io.py is used solely to package the results and produce the standalone HTML player.

The source image already contains a faint transparent water stream, so the continuation starts with the pour underway. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the decoded RGB pixel level. For the other nineteen frames, the liquid surface is raised by 9 pixels over 4.75 seconds, at constant sample intervals of 0.25 seconds. The original liquid pixels are resampled vertically with the glass bottom fixed, retaining the photographed liquid shading and textured meniscus. Soft analytic masks follow the elliptical liquid surface, the tapered glass sides, and rounded base. Bright reflections on the stationary glass are selectively restored at their original coordinates.

The liquid-height change uses a nearly constant cup cross-section approximation. Pigment concentration is approximated as the initial liquid depth divided by the current liquid depth. This controls a mild shift toward lighter orange; small pale plumes beneath the impact suggest mixing. The large dispenser, tap, wicker stand, background and tabletop remain fixed. A dispenser-level drop is not explicitly synthesized; the visible action is concentrated on the narrow flow and receiving glass.

The jet is an analytic narrow cylinder with slight acceleration-dependent narrowing and a small time-dependent centerline perturbation. Source pixels are sampled across it to suggest refraction, then faint bright and dark edges and downward-moving glints are composited. Low-amplitude elliptical ripples, a small impact dimple and a few subpixel bubble glints are confined to the liquid surface. There is no overflow or external splash.

These operations are computed at four times the output resolution. Only the difference between the modified high-resolution image and the sampled source is averaged down and added to the exact original pixels. This avoids introducing background smoothing. Every pixel outside the small tap-and-glass region remains exactly equal to the original throughout. This is an image-space reconstruction with approximate fluid cues, not a full three-dimensional fluid simulation.

Review and correction

The original image and an enlarged setup crop were inspected with the image-view tool. The first generated inspection.png and transition_check.png were also viewed. That review confirmed the rising surface and revealed a small first-transition softening caused by averaging the sampled original. The generator was revised to downsample only its pixel changes, preserving the original texture. Final inspection and transition images were viewed after regeneration.
