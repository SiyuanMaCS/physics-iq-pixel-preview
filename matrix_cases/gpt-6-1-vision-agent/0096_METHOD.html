METHOD.md

Method

This continuation was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations using first_frame.png and the scene description in prompt.txt. It uses NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy for image arrays, antialiased polygons, and local filtering. No network, external references, learned models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

The block is treated as a thin rectangular solid rotating with the black platform about the platform's vertical axis. A two-dimensional affine map approximates the camera's projection of the horizontal plane: depth is foreshortened by 0.535, with a 7.5-degree image-plane tilt. The platform center is (143.25, 62.1) pixels. The initial top corners are manually measured from the source. Their corresponding bottom corners lie 3.4 pixels lower in the image. Rotating those contact-plane coordinates and adding the fixed projected thickness produces the top and side faces in each frame. Positive angles move clockwise around the visible ellipse. The rotation is prescribed at 72 degrees per second, sampled every 0.25 seconds. The geometry and speed are approximations inferred from the photograph; magnetic force is not numerically simulated.

The starting block and its small contact shadow hide a portion of the platform and wood table. That small region is reconstructed once. A nearby wood patch twenty image pixels higher supplies the missing wood texture. A quadratic fit to unobstructed original platform pixels supplies the missing matte black top, with a small amount of local source texture. An estimated elliptical boundary separates the two surfaces. A soft local mask blends this reconstruction into the unchanged source. The first revision accidentally copied part of the source block into the reconstruction; shifting the wood sampling farther upward removed that visible residue.

For subsequent frames, the projected block is drawn with polygon masks at four times the output resolution and downsampled with Lanczos filtering. The top uses the measured pale lavender-white source color and faint texture. The visible vertical faces have direction-dependent shading based on their projected normals. A softly filtered contact shadow follows the block and is restricted to the platform top. The upright magnet's source silhouette is composited in front wherever the overhanging block passes behind it. The table, wall, purple strip, platform housing, and cable retain the original fixed view. Only the moving object and the small reconstruction area change.

Frame 00 is the original photograph: its output PNG is copied byte for byte from first_frame.png. Frames 01–19 are locally synthesized. The clip contains 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, displayed at 4 fps for five seconds. Sample times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds, with the final sample displayed for the last quarter-second.

Artifacts and reproduction

Run python generate.py in this workspace to reproduce the outputs. The script generates:

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png.
  • pixels.npz, containing the pixels uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20), with each value packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png, a labeled contact sheet containing all 20 frames.
  • transition_check.png, enlarged frame pairs and amplified difference images for the initial, middle, and final transitions.
  • index.html, a self-contained player with embedded frame data and a 250 ms display interval.

OBSERVATION.md records the measurements and 20-frame plan written before implementation. Temporary reconstruction previews, measured poses, and verification results are stored under .tmp/.

The final full contact sheet was reviewed after the reconstruction correction. The sequence shows a consistent clockwise orbit and changing block orientation, with perspective foreshortening and a fixed background.
