A clear glass bowl occupies approximately x=69–185, y=71–143. Its upper rim is an ellipse centred near (127, 103), with horizontal radius about 57 pixels and vertical radius about 31 pixels. The visible water surface is inside the bowl, centred approximately at (127, 111), with horizontal radius about 43 pixels and vertical radius about 15 pixels. Glass highlights, the table and the wall should remain stationary.

The grabber enters at x=128 from the top edge. Its black stem and orange-lined curved fingers surround a vertically elongated crumpled white/grey paper piece at approximately x=117–143, y=28–66. The paper falls under gravity to the water, makes a small splash and ripples, and remains buoyant as trapped air and the dry folds initially support it. Intended pixel method

Use only local Python image operations on the supplied photograph. Extract the paper and moving fingers using manually measured contours, reconstruct their exposed background from nearby wall pixels, and composite transformed source textures with antialiased edges. Animate a short ballistic fall followed by damped buoyant bobbing. Add restrained procedural water displacement, reflections, and splash/ripple shading inside the water surface. Preserve the fixed photographic background and original glass geometry. Save the full frame sequence and validate every saved PNG against its packed RGB tensor.

METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was made entirely with local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. The text in prompt.txt supplied the action. No external images, models, network resources, simulation engines, or subagents were used. The implementation uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV for image arrays, masks, interpolation, filtering, and inpainting.

Photographic layers

The paper and grabber have manually measured, antialiased polygon contours. Their textures come directly from the input photograph. A small dilated removal region is reconstructed with OpenCV Telea inpainting and local Gaussian smoothing to expose the wall behind the released paper and opened fingers. Boundary pixels are unmatted against this recovered wall and transformed as premultiplied colour, which reduces grey fringes when the paper moves over the brown water. The original wall, wood, glass bowl, and lighting remain fixed outside the localized moving-object and water regions.

The grabber fingers open using a continuous inverse pixel warp, reaching approximately seven pixels of outward tip displacement on each side. Displacement tapers to zero at the upper joint; the black stem remains suspended. The original paper texture undergoes affine translation, rotation, and a small damped compression. It is not replaced by a newly drawn paper object.

Motion and water

Sample timestamps are i / 4 seconds for frames 00 through 19. Frame 00 bypasses all compositing and is an exact copy of the input RGB pixels. Opening begins at 0.08 seconds, with release at 0.40 seconds. The airborne paper follows y = 47 + 155(t - 0.40)^2, with a small lateral drift and rotation. A restrained directional exposure smear is added to the fastest airborne sample.

Contact is modelled at 0.925 seconds. A damped spring response matches the incoming ballistic position and velocity, then approaches a floating centre near y=99. The paper dips, rises, and settles. Its rotation approaches 58 degrees as it rests on its side; scale changes remain modest. Trapped-air buoyancy is the chosen physical interpretation during this five-second demonstration. Lower folds gradually darken and acquire a warm tint where they meet the water. A faint mirrored paper reflection and a soft contact shadow connect it to the surface.

Water motion is an approximate image-space effect. Three expanding elliptical wave packets displace the original water texture and shade its surface; their amplitudes decay over time. A low translucent contact crown and five small ballistic droplets appear briefly around the impact, using colours consistent with the photographed bowl. These effects are restricted to the existing water region. They do not constitute a full three-dimensional fluid simulation or a measured paper absorption model.

The input, enlarged grabber detail, complete initial sequence, adjacent-frame difference sheet, impact details, and revised contact views were actually viewed with the image tool.
