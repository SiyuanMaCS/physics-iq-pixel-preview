The orange cord extends from the top edge near x = 109 to its attachment near (112, 35). METHOD.md

Method and delivered artifacts

The animation is a local source-pixel continuation of first_frame.png, guided by prompt.txt. It uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy for pixel arithmetic, image output, interpolation, and a sparse linear solve. It uses no external images, models, rendering engines, network access, or subagents.

Actual pixel construction

  1. The initial scene and twenty-frame lowering plan were recorded in OBSERVATION.md before writing the generator. The real ball center is approximately (114.4, 81.4); its smaller, partly hidden reflection is at the right mirror edge.
  2. Small manually measured search regions isolate the source cord, knot, metal connector, magnet, and ball. The vacated wall is reconstructed by solving the discrete Laplace equation within those regions, using surrounding source RGB pixels as boundary values. This reconstructed background is substituted only where the foreground matte has support.
  3. A local foreground matte is estimated from the difference between source pixels and the reconstructed wall. Opaque interior and dark metal pixels supply nearby foreground-color anchors. Per-channel alpha bounds keep the source recoverable. Premultiplied foreground RGB retains the original ball shading, seam, magnet texture, and connector. The loop's opening remains transparent. Bright background differences are excluded from the matte to avoid light halos.
  4. The rigid assembly moves downward by the exact displacements listed in OBSERVATION.md. Bilinear subpixel resampling provides gradual movement. The upper orange cord is stretched vertically between its fixed top and moving knot, while the lower assembly retains its shape and horizontal position. There is no swinging or detachment.
  5. The existing reflected foreground is separated from the stationary mirror-edge antialiasing using a locally estimated sigmoid edge profile. Its source pixels move down by 0.83 times the real assembly displacement, then are composited through the stationary edge again. The reflection remains smaller and partly occluded. No unseen full reflection is invented.
  6. A modest Gaussian shading multiplier darkens the existing wood pixels below the lowered ball, with final maximum darkening of 6.5%. It is an approximate diffuse shadow adjustment. The ball remains suspended through the last frame.
  7. Frame 00 is copied directly from the input without processing. The camera, wall, tabletop, and mirror stay fixed. Frames 01–19 are composited from the static source background and the moving source-derived foreground.

This is a two-dimensional image continuation with approximate foreground matting, mirror occlusion, and shadow shading. It does not simulate a three-dimensional scene or compute ray-traced reflections.

Revision and visual inspection

The first render used local Telea inpainting and a broader color-difference matte. Actual image-tool review of the inspection sheet, transition sheet, reconstructed background, and enlarged final frame revealed a faint light halo around the lowered ball. The final revision replaced that fill with harmonic interpolation, excluded bright background differences, and restricted foreground-color anchors to opaque interiors and dark metal. The revised inspection sheet, enlarged last frame, and transition sheet were visually reviewed with the image tool. The motion is smooth, the connector stays attached, the cord lengthens, and the clipped reflection descends alongside the ball.
