liquid-on-duck · 生成方法

The sequence is a deterministic, photograph-based pixel composite implemented in generate.py with local Python, NumPy and Pillow. Its only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. artifact_io.py and player_template.html supply output serialization and the standalone player. All temporary inspection images were stored in .tmp/.

The scene measurements and frame-by-frame event plan were written in OBSERVATION.md before the generation code. The original photograph supplies the background, duck, tray, basket, dispenser, lighting and texture. Frame 00 is copied directly from the input, including the PNG file bytes.

Liquid effects are evaluated at four times the output resolution. The original narrow pour receives a red translucent overlay with small time-dependent changes in width and centerline. A crown contact patch and two hand-measured gravity-directed surface trajectories add red liquid films to the photographed duck. Their advancing fronts, varying widths, transparency and narrow highlights create runoff while retaining the visible yellow surface. The photographed eye and beak are protected by the yellow-surface mask. A few subpixel ballistic droplets appear briefly around the contact and runoff points.

After runoff reaches the tray, a shallow pool grows in oblique tray coordinates. Its horizontal and vertical radii increase with the square root of accumulated inflow. Low-frequency angular terms make the boundary slightly irregular. The duck silhouette occludes the pool. Original tray pixels contribute to its translucent dark-red color and texture; soft yellow and ambient reflections, capillary edge shading, weak expanding rings, and small surface-wave distortions vary over time. These are analytic approximations designed for this camera view.

Only the difference between the composite and the source is downsampled and added to the original pixels. This keeps unaffected photographic pixels exact. The dispenser, basket, table, tray rims, camera viewpoint and duck position remain stationary throughout.

Outputs and validation

Run python generate.py from this workspace to reproduce all frame artifacts. The output contains 20 RGB images at 256 × 144. Each frame is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving 4 fps and a 5-second sequence. Frame timestamps are 0.00–4.75 seconds.

pixels.npz contains the key pixels: a uint32 tensor of shape (144, 256, 20), with each value packed as 0xRRGGBB. frames/00.png through frames/19.png are checked pixel-for-pixel against the corresponding tensor slices. Frame 00 is additionally checked against the source. verification.json records these assertions and adjacent-frame differences. index.html embeds the first frame and all 19 frame deltas for local playback, seeking and looping.

inspection.png samples eight frames across the sequence. transition_check.png compares early adjacent frames, middle adjacent frames and the last two frames, with magnified duck crops. Both sheets and a magnified final frame were viewed using the image tool. The first inspection led to a revision that softened the puddle boundary, added reflected-light shading and preserved all unaffected source pixels. The revised inspection sheets were also visually reviewed.
