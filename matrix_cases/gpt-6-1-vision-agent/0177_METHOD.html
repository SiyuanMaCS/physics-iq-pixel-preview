METHOD.md

Method

The sequence is synthesized locally in Python from first_frame.png, using NumPy, SciPy, Pillow, and OpenCV pixel operations. No external assets, AI models, network access, other scenes, or rendering engines are used.

This is a source-image-based 2.5D reconstruction. Smooth measured silhouettes define the blue body, green lid and foot, and checker-painted knob. A surface of revolution is estimated from the main-body silhouette and joined to an inferred neck. Analytic camera-ray intersections give curved depth for the body, lid, and foot; an ellipsoid supplies knob depth. The fixed camera looks downward by 14 degrees. Inverse rotation and projection map these surfaces into source-image materials while their silhouette remains seated above the turntable.

The red spout uses a closed curved mesh reconstructed from its original outline, with an inferred concealed half. A local NumPy triangle rasterizer rotates and projects it, interpolates source texture coordinates, and depth-tests it against the pot. A small inferred red handle loop is rendered behind the body. Concealed spout and handle materials use nearby source red pixels. Colored antialiased ceramic pixels around the original outline are included when removing the old silhouette from the background.

Previously hidden body pixels are filled using a smooth cobalt-blue color field and copies of source floral ornaments. Original visibility is checked before transferring paint onto a newly exposed surface; the inferred rear decoration reuses a small source flower and its leaves. Material borders exclude adjacent room pixels. The low-frequency blue illumination field and separated original body highlights stay under the fixed light, while paint detail rotates. Hidden wall, tabletop, and platform-top pixels are filled by local horizontal interpolation. Existing background pixels are copied without resampling. Unseen geometry and decoration are approximations, rather than observations recovered from the input.

The motion is uniformly clockwise as viewed from above, at 10 degrees per second. Frame t uses 2.5t degrees; the nearer spout moves from the right toward the center and left. The teapot stays centered and in contact with the platform. The camera, platform housing, cord, and room stay fixed. There are 20 frames at 4 fps: samples from 0 to 4.75 seconds, occupying a five-second playback interval. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the input.

The renderer uses four-times spatial sampling and area downsampling to RGB 256 × 144. generate.py writes all outputs using artifact_io.save, and makes the transition sheet. pixels.npz contains pixels, a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor packed as 0xRRGGBB. The generator checks every saved PNG against its tensor slice, verifies image mode and dimensions, and checks frame 00 against the original. The standalone HTML player contains the first image and lossless per-frame pixel deltas, with a 250 ms playback timer.

The source, initial reconstruction, hidden body texture, reconstructed background, successive contact sheets, transition sheets, and enlarged end views were visually inspected using the image viewing tool. Revisions removed background contamination on the turning body, avoided transferring front-view paint to the unobserved back, kept diffuse lighting fixed, and excluded source contour pixels that produced streaks. transition_check.png shows frames 00/01, 09/10, and 18/19, alongside amplified absolute differences and temporal overlays.

Final validation passed: exactly 20 RGB PNGs at 256 × 144, exact first-frame equality, and exact agreement between every PNG and the uint32 tensor. Independently decoding the HTML first frame and all 19 delta packets also reproduced the tensor exactly. Pixels outside the teapot region, including the distant room, cord, and platform sides, were checked against the source and remain exact in every frame. The final contact sheet, paired transitions, and enlarged final view were reviewed with the image tool after the last rendering revision.
