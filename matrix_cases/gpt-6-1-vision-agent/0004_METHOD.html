ball-behind-rotating-paper · 生成方法

This sequence was made with local Python pixel operations using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external reference images, external AI models, subagents, or rendering/physics engines were used.

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. The original RGB image is copied directly into frame 00. For the continuation, the originally hidden background is reconstructed locally: masked wall rows interpolate illumination from visible pixels on either side, and visible wood from the left tabletop supplies grain for the concealed table area. OpenCV inpainting and soft boundary blending join these areas to the original photograph. The concealed portion of the turntable's black top is drawn as an ellipse; its visible cylinder and power cable come from the source image.

The cardstock is represented by two faces of a folded tent. The front face texture is rectified from the input using a homography. A height-dependent projection matches its original corners and rotates the horizontal width/depth vectors clockwise by five degrees per frame, for 95 degrees over the continuation. Perspective warps place the source texture on the moving faces; the rear face is shaded more darkly. Thin edge strokes and soft footprint shadows complete the card. This is an approximate projected geometric model, rather than an inferred exact camera calibration.

The orange tennis ball is an analytic shaded disk with a spherical diffuse-light field, deterministic fine felt noise, and a curved muted ivory seam. It is initially fully hidden by the cardstock, then rises above the fold, travels right, and descends to its resting location. The observed left grabber jaw and handle are isolated from the source and translated with the ball. A matching black and blue right jaw is drawn with cubic curves. The jaws open after placement and the empty tool withdraws upward. The ball position remains constant after contact. Far-to-near compositing places the ball and tool behind the rotating card; they become visible where the paper silhouette allows. A soft contact shadow appears under the ball as it nears the table.

Moving layers are sampled at four times the output resolution and reduced with Lanczos filtering. Pixels beyond the areas affected by the removed or moving objects and their shadows are restored from the fixed source. There is no camera motion. Hidden surfaces and the trajectory are locally constructed approximations; the sequence is not a recording of a physically simulated experiment.

Artifacts and verification

  • OBSERVATION.md records measurements and the event plan, and was written before generator coding.
  • generate.py deterministically regenerates all outputs.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png are 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144, played at 4 fps for 5 seconds.
  • pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20). Each value is 0xRRGGBB.
  • index.html is a standalone embedded-data player with play, replay, seek, and loop controls.
  • inspection.png shows eight selected frames. transition_check.png shows all 20 frames with adjacent-frame mean absolute RGB differences.

Image View tools were used to inspect the input, an enlarged input, the first rendered inspection and transition sheets, and reconstructed background. This review exposed an erroneous source sampling region that copied a pale cardstock remnant into the hidden tabletop. The wood sampling region was corrected and the outputs regenerated. The revised inspection sheet, reconstructed background, and final transition sheet were visually reviewed with Image View tools.

Validation checks require an exact RGB match between frame 00 and first_frame.png, the required frame count and dimensions, and equality of every PNG's packed RGB values to the corresponding tensor slice. A separate final validation also decodes the embedded player data and compares all 20 frames to the tensor. Detailed validation results are saved in .tmp/verification.json and .tmp/final_validation.json.
