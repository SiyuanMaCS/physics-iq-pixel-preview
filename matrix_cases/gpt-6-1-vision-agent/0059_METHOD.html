duck-falls-in-box · 生成方法

This is a deterministic continuation made with local Python pixel operations. The only visual input is first_frame.png; the intended action comes from prompt.txt. No external imagery, models, engines, references, or other cases were used. NumPy handles the pixels, Pillow reads and writes images and draws inspection labels, and SciPy interpolates the extracted pixel arrays. artifact_io.py packages the output tensor, PNGs, and standalone player; it generates no scene content.

The original image supplies the rubber duck, both orange and black grabber jaws, box, table, and wall. Quadratic fits to wall pixels on either side estimate the background behind the duck and jaws. Feathered local patches remove their original positions. Duck transparency is inferred from its blue-channel contrast with the wall; jaw transparency is inferred from dark-channel contrast. Premultiplied color and alpha arrays preserve their source texture, shading, and soft edges. These are local arrays computed from the supplied image, not imported masks.

The grabber tips shear outward by up to 3.4 pixels per side while their upper ends stay anchored. Opening begins at 0.18 seconds and finishes at 0.45 seconds. Release occurs at 0.40 seconds. The duck follows y = 24.5 + 0.5 × 960 × (t − 0.40)² after release, with x = 127.5 + 0.6 × (t − 0.40) and a small roll of 12 degrees per elapsed second. Projected gravity is an assumed pixel-space scale because physical dimensions cannot be recovered from this image. Seven sub-samples over a 1/120-second exposure provide short motion blur. A modest lighting gain, capped at 10%, follows the source wall's broad brightness gradient.

At frame 03 the duck reaches the opening. A feathered visibility boundary between y=84.1 and y=85.3 models the front lip covering the duck as it falls inside. The duck renders over the back rim and behind the front lip and panel. It is fully hidden by frame 04. Its unseen impact is not rendered. The grabber remains open and the setup stays still through frame 19. The box, tabletop, and all pixels from y=86 downward are unchanged throughout.

Frame 00 is copied directly from the source, without resampling or re-encoding changes to its RGB values. The sequence has 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144. Samples run from t=0 through t=4.75 seconds; each is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving a five-second playback at 4 fps. The player uses delta-encoded lossless RGB data and supports play, pause, replay, scrubbing, and looping.

Verification passed for dimensions, dtype, 24-bit packing, exact source equality of frame 00, and every PNG's pixel-for-pixel equality with its tensor slice. The player data was independently decoded and checked against all twenty tensor slices. The static lower scene and identical settled frames 04–19 were also checked.

I used the image View tool to inspect the supplied first frame, an enlarged first frame and rim crop, the generated inspection sheet, and the generated transition sheet. The review confirmed the duck's original appearance is retained, the jaws visibly open, the duck falls along the opening's centerline, and its lower body is covered by the front lip before it disappears. The final inspection layout includes all early release samples so this brief event can be reviewed at 4 fps.

Limitations: this uses source-pixel compositing with an analytic trajectory, rather than a recovered three-dimensional scene. Object interiors, unseen surfaces, and the hidden collision are not synthesized. The single image does not establish a metric gravity scale or exposure time.
