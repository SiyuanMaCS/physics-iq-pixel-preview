light-on-mug · 生成方法

Inputs and timing

The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The rules in GENERATION_RULES.md were read, and OBSERVATION.md was written before implementation. The output contains 20 RGB images at 256 × 144, displayed at a constant 4 fps for five seconds. Frame 00 is copied byte-for-byte at the pixel level from the supplied image. The camera is fixed. The handle rotates clockwise as seen from above at 60 degrees per second, with 15 degrees between consecutive frames and a final orientation of 285 degrees at t=4.75 s.

Actual pixel construction

This is a photographic composite with an analytic handle and projected shadow, not a full reconstruction of the room. Local Python, NumPy, SciPy, and Pillow perform all generation. No network, external reference image, learned model, simulation engine, external rendering engine, or subagent was used.

The circular mug body, dark opening, tabletop, spotlight illumination, black platform, cord, and fixture are retained from the photograph. An unmarked, circular platform and circular mug body have the same appearance during axial rotation under stationary illumination. Their fixed highlights are therefore preserved while the handle reveals the motion. The platform is not given an invented moving marker or texture.

The small initial handle is removed where it lies outside the body using a nearby continuation of the photographed dark background. A narrow attachment on the body is restored from the same body's pixels eight rows below. The old wall-shadow loop is removed only to the left of the original cylindrical shadow. The exposed gray wall is estimated using a robust fourth-degree polynomial fit to visible wall pixels, supplemented by faint, fixed texture copied from the input. The photographed cylindrical shadow and the low shadow from the platform remain in place.

The moving handle is a rounded rectangular loop with a circular tube cross section. Its radial and vertical curve is sampled with 144 points and its tube with 20 samples. A custom triangle rasterizer written in NumPy projects this surface at four times the image resolution. Depth tests against the photographed mug's silhouette and an analytic cylindrical front surface hide the handle when it passes behind the body. The ceramic colors use surface normals, a fixed light, yellow diffuse shading, a white specular component, and stronger illumination near the base. Gaussian filtering and area averaging produce soft antialiased edges.

The handle's wall shadow comes from rays extending from one fixed point light through the handle vertices to a fixed wall plane. This produces changing foreshortening, position, and magnification as the handle moves nearer to and farther from the light. Rasterizing the projected surface and blurring its coverage gives a soft shadow. An existing opaque shadow and a new overlapping opaque shadow are combined by maximum light blockage, so their overlap does not artificially darken twice. The photographed mug occludes wall shadows wherever it lies in front of them.

To ease the transition from the exact input, the measured initial handle's photographic residual is warped with the rotating handle plane and smoothly reduced over the early frames. A small correction to the analytic wall-shadow shape is also warped through the light projection. Near edge-on views, the analytic tube supplies finite thickness. The initial broad white glare is lightly preserved over the handle when it crosses the mug's front. These are local pixel and geometric approximations; camera calibration, material values, background reconstruction, and the light position were inferred from the single image.

Inspection and revisions

Image View tools were used on the supplied image, enlarged setup, generated contact sheets, transition comparisons, and a close-up sheet. The first review identified artificial extra darkening where handle and body shadows overlapped, minor resampling of stationary pixels, and a clipped rightmost handle edge. Those were corrected using maximum blockage, native-resolution photographic compositing, and a wider allowed motion region. A subsequent close-up identified a stationary attachment remnant on the original left side; the exposed body patch was restored before the final generation. The final contact sheet and transition sheet were visually reviewed after these changes.

Saved artifacts and verification

  • generate.py: reproducible local generation, artifact export, and assertions.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty 256 × 144 RGB frames.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames at native image size.
  • transition_check.png: six adjacent-frame pairs and amplified absolute pixel differences.
  • index.html: standalone 4 fps player with embedded pixel data, seeking, replay, and looping controls.
  • OBSERVATION.md: initial measurements and the twenty-frame plan.
  • METHOD.md: this report.

generate.py asserts that frame 00 exactly matches the input and that every saved PNG exactly matches its corresponding packed tensor slice. A separate check also decodes every frame from the standalone player's embedded first-frame and delta packets and compares them with the tensor. Verification details and temporary inspection images are saved inside workspace .tmp/. The final pose continues rotation; looping playback resets to the initial pose after five seconds.
