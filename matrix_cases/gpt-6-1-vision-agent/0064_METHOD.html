Its bright red liquid has a curved surface centered near y=32 and a lower edge near y=61. The black tap occupies x=143–166, y=34–59. A narrow orange-red stream already descends from the nozzle near (161,58) into the glass near (164,101). No tap-opening action is needed.

The clear receiving glass is approximately x=140–182, y=68–138, with an elliptical rim centered around (161,72), a subtly tapered body, and a rounded thick base. Basket weave remains visible through the empty upper portion of the glass. Existing glass reflections, rim, and outlines should be retained. The table and plain walls are stationary.

Physical continuation: maintain uninterrupted gravity-driven flow for all 20 frames. Raise the receiving surface smoothly by about 21 pixels over 4.75 seconds, leaving headroom below the rim. Remove the corresponding volume from the wider dispenser (about a 5-pixel surface drop); preserve the initial curved liquid surface and ribbed texture. Model a nearly vertical stream with small variations in thickness, a moving impact dimple, and faint ripples/bubbles confined to the surface. METHOD.md

Method and validation

This is a local, deterministic pixel continuation of first_frame.png, made with Python, NumPy, and Pillow. It uses no external images, network resources, AI models, rendering engines, or subagents. prompt.txt supplies the action. artifact_io.py and player_template.html are the provided output helpers.

The source already contains an active pour. The generated action continues it for 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, played at 4 fps for five seconds. Frame 00 is an unmodified copy of the input. The samples occur at t = 0, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds, and each frame is displayed for 0.25 seconds. The camera, table, basket, dispenser outline, tap, glass rim, and glass base stay fixed.

The receiver's photographed red liquid and curved surface are resampled upward using bilinear pixel sampling. The surface rises 21 pixels, while the warp fades toward zero displacement at the fixed base. A small horizontal adjustment follows the glass's taper. Smooth analytic masks blend the moving surface with the existing translucent walls and keep the rim and thin reflections visible. Newly filled pixels cover the original stream's lower portion, so a separate background inpainting step is unnecessary.

The dispenser's liquid boundary descends approximately 5.12 pixels. A source-image warp reuses its observed curved meniscus and vertical rib texture; a manually drawn, supersampled protection mask preserves the opaque tap and handle. The level transfer uses an approximate cross-sectional area ratio of 0.244. A slightly decreasing flow rate represents the falling reservoir head. This is a geometric volume approximation from the single image, not a calibrated fluid simulation.

The free stream retains the source's narrow gravity-directed shape. Small advected luminance changes provide flow texture. Low-amplitude analytic ripples, an impact dimple, and subpixel bubble highlights move on the rising surface. The original surface texture supplies most of the visible detail. No overflow or large splashes occur, and pouring is still active in the final frame.
