ball-in-sand · 生成方法

The animation was generated with local Python pixel operations. first_frame.png was the sole visual source, and prompt.txt supplied the action. No networks, external references, generative models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Image construction

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV for numerical image operations. A cubic surface fitted to the neighboring grey-wall pixels reconstructs the small wall area exposed when the original ball and grabber tips move. The source ball is extracted with a soft elliptical mask. The tool mask uses its measured image region and source color contrast. Edge colors are separated from the original wall before compositing. Grabber pixels are resampled with an outward displacement that increases toward the tips, keeping the upper arch fixed. The source ball texture, shading, and seam are translated with the sphere.

The ball is held at its original position until 0.366 s. An analytic vertical acceleration of 4000 pixels/s² brings it to initial sand contact at about 0.494 s. This corresponds approximately to terrestrial gravity using the measured image size of an ordinary tennis ball. A heavily damped exponential penetration then moves the center from y = 68.5 to y = 83.0, with just 0.4 pixel of horizontal displacement. Five samples across a 1/60 s exposure add mild motion blur during the brief impact. Frame 00 is copied directly from the supplied RGB image.

The sand response is a local displacement of the original granular texture: the upper center compresses, the sides spread slightly, and the front moves down slightly. Soft darkening supplies the depression and contact shadows. A textured front rim is composited over the ball's lower hemisphere, making the ball partly embedded. There is a small green reflected-light adjustment on its lower side. After the small creep ends, the composition is held steady for the remainder of the clip.

This is a source-image compositing method with an analytic motion model and a visually constructed sand response. It is not a volumetric or individual-grain physical simulation. The fall and initial compression happen between the coarse 4 fps samples, so frame 02 shows the onset of penetration and frame 03 shows the ball almost settled.

Artifacts

  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and the 20-frame plan, written before the generator.
  • generate.py: complete reproducible generation and serialization code.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 shape (144, 256, 20), values packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • inspection.png: eight representative frames.
  • transition_check.png: all 20 frames with adjacent-frame pixel-change counts and zero-based timestamps.
  • index.html: standalone player with embedded pixel data, play/pause, replay, seek, loop, and a 250 ms frame interval. The last frame is displayed through the end of the five-second sequence.

The supplied inspection/player template labels time at the end of each frame interval (0.25–5.00 s); the event plan and transition sheet label the beginning of each interval (0.00–4.75 s).

Review and checks

I used actual image-view tools to review the original image, an enlarged source crop, the generated inspection sheet, and the complete transition sheet. I also viewed enlarged generated details. This revealed a stray strip of ball-edge pixels in the opened grabber; the tool extraction mask was corrected and the regenerated transition sheet and enlarged open grabber were visually reviewed. The final sequence shows one ball dropping into the green pile, a shallow local deformation, and a stable final pose.

The generator asserts all image dimensions and the packed tensor layout, then decodes each tensor slice and verifies exact equality with its saved PNG. It also asserts that frame 00 exactly equals first_frame.png. A separate final verification decodes the standalone player's first frame and all delta packets and checks all 20 reconstructed frames against the tensor. The distant wall/table pixels stay exactly equal to the source, and the settled frames remain identical. Player data and timing were checked programmatically; browser playback was not separately visually reviewed.
