METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was generated entirely inside the workspace using local Python pixel operations on first_frame.png. The only scene description used was prompt.txt. No network, reference images, learned generative models, external rendering or physics engines, or subagents were used.

Photograph reconstruction

generate.py traces antialiased masks around the original woven basket and its black magnet. It isolates the cord and metal links using their contrast with the adjacent gray wall, preserving the openings through the links. The photographed object pixels are unmatted against the restored background and translated vertically. The basket keeps the source weave, silhouette, dimensions, and orientation throughout the lift.

Newly uncovered background is reconstructed from this same photograph. A quadratic fit to unobstructed wall samples supplies its low frequency illumination, with fine residual texture taken from a clean wall strip. The table edge is estimated at y = 95.65 − 0.0713x over the covered region. A warped patch of unobstructed wood to the right supplies tabletop grain. A screened Poisson solve transfers these wall/table gradients into the entire covered region and the original contact-shadow region, matching the surrounding original pixels. This is local gradient-domain inpainting, implemented with NumPy and SciPy; OpenCV is used only for image sampling and translation.

Ball and motion

The orange tennis ball is a mathematically shaded, stationary sphere centered at (132, 116.5), with radius 11.8 pixels. It is drawn at six times the output resolution, using diffuse lighting from the upper left, subtle deterministic felt noise, and a pale seam projected from a closed three-dimensional spherical curve. It is downsampled with antialiasing and composited with a soft table shadow and a narrow contact shadow. The basket is composited in front of the ball, so the ball emerges progressively from under its lower rim.

The upward displacements, in pixels, are:

0, 0, 0, 1, 3, 7, 13, 21, 30, 40, 51, 63, 75, 85, 94, 100, 104, 107, 108, 108.

These produce a brief opening hold, accelerating lift, and decelerating stop. The final basket bottom is at approximately y=21, near the top of the frame. Its attachment and links move with it and pass out through the top edge. The camera and ball remain stationary. The original concentrated basket shadow fades as the cover rises; a softer lift-dependent shadow and the ball's contact shadow remain.

The inferred ball, covered background, and shadow evolution are reconstructed pixels. Their true hidden appearances cannot be recovered from a single input photograph. No physical simulator was used.

Artifacts and validation

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty 256 × 144 RGB images at 4 fps, spanning five seconds.
  • pixels.npz: the pixels array has shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and encoding 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight representative frames.
  • transition_check.png: twelve transition samples and four amplified consecutive-frame difference images.
  • index.html: standalone, locally playable animation with playback, replay, scrubbing, and looping controls.
  • verification.json: machine-readable output validation.

Frame 00 is pixel-identical to the supplied image, and its PNG file is copied byte-for-byte from the input. Every saved PNG is decoded and compared to its corresponding packed tensor plane. All twenty comparisons pass. The HTML's initial-frame and delta packets are independently decoded and compared to the same tensor.

Visual review and revisions

Image tools were used to review the supplied image, enlarged views, the generated inspection sheet, the transition sheet, and an enlarged final frame. A first background repair was revised because it sampled a portion of the basket into the wall texture. A subsequent repair was revised to jointly solve the wall and tabletop, removing discontinuities along the table edge. Fine wall residuals are sampled before tiling to avoid tile seams. The reviewed final sequence shows a single vertical basket lift, progressive reveal of the orange tennis ball, and a stationary final ball on the table.
