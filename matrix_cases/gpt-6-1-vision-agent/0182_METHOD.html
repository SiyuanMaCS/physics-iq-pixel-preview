Observation and event plan

Measured object silhouettes, in input pixel coordinates:

  • Red support: approximately x = 123–144, y = 92–134. Its broad red front has corners near (125, 96), (144, 94), (143, 131), (126, 134). It stands vertically on the table.
  • Yellow beam: approximately x = 112–174, y = 73–95. Top corners are near (112, 76), (171, 73), (174, 81), (114, 85); the front extends down to y = 95 at the left and y = 91 at the right. Its right end overhangs the red support.

The load shifts the combined centre of mass beyond the right edge of the red support. The beam initially rotates around that edge; the blue block loses stable support, then both fall rightward, collide with the table, and settle. The red support remains standing. There is no camera movement or additional object.

METHOD.md

Method

This is a local Python pixel synthesis from first_frame.png, guided by prompt.txt. It uses NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV pixel operations, and SciPy's distance transform. It does not use an external model, rendering engine, network resource, or additional scene input.

Image construction

The starting image supplies the camera view, wood texture, background, block face textures, and grabber pixels. Color predicates and small coordinate bounds separate the blue, yellow, red, and orange/black objects. The wall hidden by the grabber is reconstructed by horizontal interpolation between unobstructed wall pixels. The exposed table is reconstructed with local inpainting and intact neighbouring wood patches, joined with a distance feather. These operations produce an approximate background, rather than a recovered photograph of the hidden surface.

Block geometry is represented by planar rectangles extruded into short cuboids. The analytic projection is u = x, v = 142 - z - 0.067*x, with a constant depth offset for each block. Original front face textures are mapped to the changing projected quadrilaterals. Visible side faces use source textures or locally chosen material colors. Previously occluded blue pixels are filled from adjacent cyan pixels; the hidden top uses a dark blue material color. Yellow top shading is reconstructed to avoid retaining the original blue contact shadow after separation. Soft Gaussian shadows change with block height and tabletop contact. The red support stays fixed; its newly exposed top is reconstructed in red.

The grabber's two jaws are copied from the source and independently translated outward and upward. Their opening/retraction is prescribed. Frames 01–02 preserve the original resting blocks and table shadow, while filling the newly revealed upper blue surface. Frame 00 is the input image copied exactly, without rendering, resampling, or recompression changes to its decoded RGB pixels.

Motion

The falling blocks use a custom two-dimensional rigid-body contact simulation implemented in this file. Yellow dimensions are 60 × 10 image-space units; blue dimensions are 11 × 60. Their relative masses are 1 and 1.05. The red support and tabletop are static collision bodies. Gravity is 320 image-space units/s². The timestep is 1/600 s, with 150 steps between 4 fps samples after release at 0.50 s. Oriented rectangle contacts are found by the separating-axis test. A sequential impulse solver applies normal forces, friction (coefficient 0.55), small collision restitution, and a penetration correction. This is an approximate planar mechanics model, not a reconstruction of measured physical dimensions or a full three-dimensional simulation.

After release, the beam turns clockwise off the support. By about 1.25 s both blocks have contacted the tabletop on their ends. They continue leaning and overturning, with the yellow beam settling partly across the blue block by about 3.00 s. After the settled 3.25 s sample, the simulation sleeps and the final arrangement holds for the remaining frames. The camera, background, and red support stay fixed.
