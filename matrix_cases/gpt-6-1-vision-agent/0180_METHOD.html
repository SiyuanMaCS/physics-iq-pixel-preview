METHOD.md

Method

This is a local Python pixel reconstruction using the supplied first_frame.png and prompt.txt only. No network, external references, trained model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy implement the image operations and analytic rendering. generate.py reproduces the artifacts.

The first frame is copied exactly from the reference. In subsequent frames, two small elliptical regions containing the initial balls and their shadows are repaired with nearby wood from the same reference. The copied texture is shifted along the projected wood grain. A sparse discrete Laplace solve extends boundary color differences into each region, joining the transplanted texture to the surrounding table. The repaired background stays fixed in every subsequent frame. The second revision widened the grey ball's repair region to include its diffuse left-side shadow and corrected the donor's vertical offset to align the board boundary.

The moving balls are analytically shaded spheres with brown and bluish grey colors estimated from the reference. Curved pale seams and faint surface texture rotate around a transverse axis according to traveled distance divided by radius. Illumination uses the current camera-facing sphere normals and a fixed upper-left light. Surface markings rotate independently of illumination. Ball size increases modestly toward the foreground. Elongated soft shadows and darker contact shadows move with the balls.

The grey center follows (53.5 + 145 t, 74.8 + 26.1 t) in image pixels. The brown center initially follows (132 - 120 t, 44.5 - 20.8 t). Their separated paths cross horizontally without impact. After 0.83 seconds, the brown ball drops along an approximate image-space ballistic arc and is occluded by the rear-left tabletop edge and the left pipe. The grey ball exits the right border by about 1.5 seconds. No new balls enter. These are chosen image-space trajectories consistent with the setup, rather than a calibrated reconstruction of three-dimensional distances or forces.

Rendering uses four-times spatial supersampling, five exposure samples spanning 0.01 seconds, and a slight optical blur applied to ball layers. Background pixels outside the original repair areas and moving layers are restored to their exact reference RGB values. Frame 00 is always the original image, including its original shadows and texture.

The sequence has 20 frames at 256 × 144, sampled at 4 fps from t = 0 through 4.75 seconds. Holding each frame for 0.25 seconds gives a five-second shot. pixels.npz contains pixels, a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor packed as 0xRRGGBB. frames/00.png through frames/19.png are RGB PNGs. index.html is a standalone player with all frame data embedded. inspection.png shows selected frames; transition_check.png shows the first eight frames and amplified differences from their predecessors.

Verification and visual review

The generator verifies every PNG against the unpacked tensor, checks dimensions and dtype, asserts frame 00 equals the source, checks exact preservation of untouched background pixels, and checks the final hold is stable. Results are saved in verification.json.

The image View tool was used to review the supplied frame, the initial inspection sheet and transition sheet, and enlarged views of the repaired tabletop and first moving frame. Those views prompted the grain-alignment revision and a modest adjustment to grey ball color and seam contrast. Final output review and verification are recorded after regeneration.

Final review used the image View tool on the regenerated clean table and transition sheet. The wood repair follows the board boundary, both balls maintain separate trajectories, and the shot holds on the empty table. Final checks passed for all 20 PNGs and all 20 frames decoded from the standalone HTML player. All required artifacts are present.
