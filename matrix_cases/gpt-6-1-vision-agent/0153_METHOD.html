METHOD.md

Method

This animation was generated entirely with local Python pixel operations using first_frame.png and prompt.txt as the scene inputs. It uses NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. No network, external images, AI models, rendering engines, or subagents were used. artifact_io.py and player_template.html are the supplied output and playback helpers.

Background and ball

The source photograph supplies the fixed camera view, wooden table, black tube, olive fabric box, and background. The initial ball is centered at (102.1, 50.0), with an estimated radius of 8.65 pixels. Its original image and soft shadow are removed from subsequent frames inside an ellipse centered at (107, 53), with radii (24, 19). The replacement grain comes from the same source rows, shifted 48 pixels from the right. A local discrete Poisson solve matches the patch's illumination to the unmodified boundary. The surrounding photograph is retained.

The moving ball is rasterized as an analytic sphere. The visible front hemisphere samples the photographed ball RGB, with approximate correction for stationary lighting and edge coverage. The unseen hemisphere uses a matching brown color, deterministic fine mottling, and a curved gray seam. Rodrigues rotation maps the sphere texture according to signed rolling distance divided by ball radius; rotation reverses with the rebound. Fixed sphere lighting prevents the illumination from rotating with the texture. This is a small photograph-based reconstruction, not a recovered full 3D scene or a measured ball material.

The cast shadow is extracted as channel-dependent attenuation from the source photograph relative to the reconstructed tabletop. A soft analytic shadow fills the region originally hidden by the ball. The photographed shadow footprint is translated with the ball using bilinear sampling. Four-times spatial supersampling, a soft optical boundary, and less than one pixel of horizontal exposure blur produce fractional-pixel motion without sharp cutout edges.
