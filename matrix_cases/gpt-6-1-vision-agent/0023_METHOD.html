METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was created entirely with local Python pixel operations using NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network access, external references, generative model, rendering engine, or subagents were used. artifact_io.py and player_template.html were used as the supplied output and playback helpers.

The first frame is copied exactly. Since it already shows the single ball near the table's middle, subsequent frames continue from that position. The original room, sofa, lamp, pipe, table outline, and camera framing are retained from the supplied photograph.

The background immediately beneath the original ball and its shadow is reconstructed with a feathered local mask. Low-frequency tabletop colors are interpolated between nearby unoccluded pixels, and genuine high-frequency wood detail is copied from an adjacent tabletop region. This reconstructed patch remains fixed for frames 01–19.

The ball is an antialiased analytic sphere with elliptical image projection, approximately 15 × 16 pixels. A weighted least-squares fit of low-order sphere-normal terms to the original ball pixels estimates its stationary blue-grey illumination. The remaining source surface detail is sampled as a material residual and rotated with a camera-projected rolling axis. The unobserved rear material uses a mirrored continuation of the same residual. This is a source-derived approximation of the unseen surface; it is not a recovered 3D tennis-ball model.

The center follows x(t) = 149 + v0*t - t*t, with v0 ≈ 25.5921 pixels/second and t = frame / 4. This represents 2 pixels/second² of gradual rolling deceleration. Its projected height follows y(t) = 116.5 + 0.018*(x(t)-149). Rolling angle is distance divided by a 7.7-pixel effective radius. The final center is (248, 118.282), near the right end of the visible table. The ball remains supported; the sequence adds no bounce or fall. Each of the 20 frames is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving a five-second sequence.

Three analytic Gaussian terms make a broad penumbra, a cast shadow, and a dark contact shadow. They follow the ball on the table. Moving layers are evaluated at four times the output resolution and box-averaged, while the underlying stationary pixels are repeated exactly rather than interpolated. This keeps the room and wood detail steady.

Review and validation

The supplied frame was visually reviewed with the image tool, including an enlargement. The generated inspection.png, transition_check.png, and reconstructed tabletop were also reviewed with the image tool. After the first review, compositing was revised to avoid resampling stationary wood grain. The final contact sheet and early-transition sheet were visually reviewed again.

generate.py checks the exact first frame, RGB PNG/tensor equality for every frame, the (144, 256, 20) tensor shape and uint32 dtype, and unchanged background pixels above the table and around the pipe. The tensor stores each color as 0xRRGGBB in the pixels key. A separate validation also decodes the standalone HTML player's first frame and delta packets and checks them against the tensor. All checks passed.
