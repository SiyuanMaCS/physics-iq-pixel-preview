METHOD.md

Method and verification

This continuation was made with local Python pixel operations using NumPy, Pillow, and OpenCV. The only scene source was first_frame.png; the action description came from prompt.txt. No external images, models, network resources, simulation engines, or rendering engines were used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before implementation and records the scene measurements and all 20 planned samples. generate.py is the complete reproducible generator. It imports the supplied output helper artifact_io.py to write the tensor, PNG files, contact sheet, and standalone HTML player.

Pixel construction

The original photograph provides the stationary scene and photographic sphere and grabber textures. Hand-measured polygons and ellipses identify the tool, balls, strings, reflections, and chrome supports. OpenCV local inpainting removes small old details. For the larger area hidden by the grabber, clean wood pixels in the same photograph provide a robust quadratic lighting fit and locally copied high-frequency grain. Clean dark pixels from the base provide a smooth color estimate in the missing reflection regions. The original front bevel, silhouette, and chrome structure are retained.

The extracted blue and black tool opens its jaws slightly and withdraws left during the first second. The five metal sphere layers retain the photographic highlights. They are composited at four times the output resolution with antialiased masks, then reduced to the required resolution. Each sphere has two fine suspension threads and a dim, blurred reflection on the glossy base. Foreground supports occlude the moving objects.

Motion

The release time is 0.08 seconds after the initial sample. An idealized equal-mass Newton’s cradle model transfers motion between the two left balls and the two right balls. The center sphere stays at equilibrium. Signed displacement is

d(t) = -16.5 * exp(-0.075 * tau) * cos(2*pi*tau/1.06), where tau = max(0, t-0.08).

The left pair uses the negative part of this displacement and the right pair uses the positive part. Small perspective corrections match the measured initial left positions. Vertical displacement follows a circular pendulum arc of 75 projected pixels, with a 0.18 slope for the photographed row. This is an inferred, idealized trajectory, rather than a measurement of the original real apparatus. The photograph does not determine hidden geometry or exact timing. The moving source textures keep their photographed lighting; a full changing environment reflection is not simulated.

The 20 samples are at t = frame_index / 4, from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds. Each frame is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving a five-second clip at a constant 4 fps. Frame 00 is the original RGB pixel array with no edits.

Image viewing tools were used to inspect the original photograph, enlarged ball details, generated background plates, the frame contact sheet, an enlarged continuation frame, and the adjacent-frame transition sheet. Revisions removed blue tool remnants, repaired the base outline, and reduced the suspension thread visibility. inspection.png shows eight representative frames. transition_check.png shows every frame with its adjacent absolute pixel difference amplified four times; the difference under frame 00 is zero.
