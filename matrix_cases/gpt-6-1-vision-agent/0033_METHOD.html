+    """Poisson blend a same-board donor into a small ball/shadow footprint."""
+    mask = ((X-cx)/rx)**2 + ((Y-cy)/ry)**2 < 1
+    donor = np.stack([map_coordinates(image[:,:,k], [Y+donor_dy, X+donor_dx],
+                                      order=1, mode='nearest') for k in range(3)], -1)
+    yy, xx = np.nonzero(mask)
+    ids = np.full((H,W), -1, int)
+    ids[yy,xx] = np.arange(len(xx))
+    matrix = lil_matrix((len(xx),len(xx)), dtype=float)
+    rhs = np.zeros((len(xx),3))
+    for i,(y,x) in enumerate(zip(yy,xx)):
+        matrix[i,i] = 4
+        for dy,dx in ((0,1),(0,-1),(1,0),(-1,0)):

Both rest on the table and cast soft shadows toward the lower right. Their center-to-center direction is about (0.993, 0.117), consistent with motion from the opposing pipes. Use equal incoming speeds of about 23.5 image pixels/second along the center line. Contact occurs around 1.25 seconds, with ball centers near (105.5, 53.4) and (120.0, 55.1). METHOD.md

Method

This is a local, deterministic pixel continuation of first_frame.png. The only scene inputs are the supplied first image and prompt.txt. It uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy; no network requests, external imagery, AI models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used. OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code, with measurements and an explicit 20-frame event plan.

Static scene reconstruction

The input image is kept at its original 256 × 144 resolution. Two small elliptical footprints cover the original balls and their shadows. Wood pixels sampled elsewhere on the same tabletop provide gradient guidance for a sparse Poisson solve inside each footprint. The boundary pixels anchor the repair to the original photograph. All remaining background pixels are copied from the source.

The first repair had a visible angular wood feature from its donor region. After reviewing the background with an image-view tool, the blue ball's donor was moved to a clean wood region to the right. The revised background was visually reviewed again.

Balls and shadows

The measured centers are blue (76.16, 50.03) and yellow (149.07, 58.25). The soft source silhouettes are recovered using their blue and yellow-green chromatic differences from the reconstructed tabletop. The source ball colors are separated from the underlying shadowed wood, with nearest-surface extension for transparent margins.

Each original cast shadow is approximated with a fitted rotated elliptical Gaussian plus a smaller contact Gaussian. The fit uses visible wood pixels around that ball. These fitted shadows move with their respective balls and darken the fixed wood multiplicatively.

The recovered foreground is split into smoothly varying illumination and a small surface-detail residual. Lighting and the silhouette remain fixed relative to the camera. Surface normals are rotated mathematically about the axis transverse to the travel direction to move the detail as the ball rolls. Visible source texture is extended to cover newly exposed parts of the mathematical sphere; this is an approximation, rather than an observation of the hidden side of either ball. The first texture version produced a bright yellow spot, so the detail residual was limited to ±6 RGB levels for blue and ±2.5 for yellow. The corrected version was visually reviewed.

Motion and timing

There are 20 frames at 4 fps. Frame n samples n/4 seconds and is displayed for 0.25 seconds, producing a five-second sequence. Frame 00 copies the original RGB pixels exactly. The initial image already places both balls outside their pipes; the continuation proceeds from that state without inserting an earlier emergence event.

Both balls travel on their initial center line at 23.418762 image pixels per second. Their incoming speeds are exactly equal. Contact is at 1.25 seconds, frame 05, with effective radii 7.075 and 7.75 pixels. An equal-mass collision reverses both velocities with restitution 0.9. The rebound speeds are equal and decrease under 0.45 image pixels/second² rolling deceleration. Ball spin follows signed displacement divided by radius, and reverses with travel direction. The balls stay on the tabletop and continue outward through the final frame.

No camera movement, cuts, added objects, text overlays on scene frames, or airborne motion are introduced. The physical model operates in projected image coordinates; it does not recover an exact three-dimensional calibration from the single photograph.

  • pixels.npz contains the key pixels, with shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and each value encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png are the 20 RGB images.
  • inspection.png shows eight selected frames with zero-based sample times.
  • transition_check.png shows all 19 adjacent pairs, alongside absolute RGB differences amplified by three.
  • index.html is a standalone embedded-data player with playback, scrubbing, replay, and looping.
  • .tmp/trajectory.json, .tmp/shadow_fits.json, and .tmp/verification.json record the computed motion, source-shadow fits, and checks.

Verification and visual review

All 20 PNGs were loaded and independently packed to uint32 RGB, then compared exactly with every tensor slice. Frame 00 was compared exactly with the supplied first-frame RGB pixels. The standalone HTML player's first frame and all differential packets were decoded and checked against the same tensor. All checks passed.

The minimum mathematical surface gap is zero to floating-point precision; the balls do not pass through one another. The top/background band, front table edge, and both pipe regions were compared with the first image in every frame, with maximum RGB error zero.

Image-view tools were actually used to inspect the original frame, enlarged ball crop, repaired background, revised inspection sheet, enlarged approach/contact/rebound samples, and the complete transition-check sheet. The final review showed a fixed scene and a coherent equal-speed approach, contact, and outward rebound. The ball edges and spin are image-derived approximations at the supplied low resolution.
