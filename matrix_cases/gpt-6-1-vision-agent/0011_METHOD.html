+def sample(field, x, y):
+    coords = [y, x]
+    if field.ndim == 2:
+        return map_coordinates(field, coords, order=3, mode='constant', cval=0,
+                               prefilter=True)
+    return np.stack([sample(field[..., c], x, y) for c in range(field.shape[-1])], -1)

OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The only scene references are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The source is a 256 × 144 RGB image. Coordinates below use the top-left pixel as the origin.

Measured scene

  • The camera is fixed. A pale, horizontally grained wooden tabletop fills most of the image, approximately y = 29–124. Its rear corners are near (30, 28) and (217, 32); its right silhouette reaches the image boundary near y = 76.
  • A mustard sofa occupies the upper background. The table fascia and a little floor are visible along the bottom. These remain exactly stationary.
  • A black pipe enters from the left and has its open end near (11, 47). The orange tennis ball is already outside it, centered near (54.7, 51.6), with a radius of about 7 pixels. The prompt's brown/orange ball is this same object.
  • The orange ball has a warm illuminated upper-left surface and a darker lower edge. Its soft shadow extends rightward across roughly x = 54–76, y = 49–60. Both ball and shadow must move; their initial footprint must be restored to matching wood grain.
  • A separate smaller red ball is near (110, 36), beside a short dark/brown cylindrical object near (116, 32). Neither is on the rolling path.

Motion and appearance

Use a straight projected path continuing from the pipe toward the right side: ball center (54.7 + 11.1*n, 51.6 + 1.4*n) for frame n. This is constant velocity, sampled every 0.25 seconds. The slight downward slope follows the table perspective. The ball rolls on the tabletop, with its original shading and a moving soft contact/cast shadow. A very subtle rotating surface texture can indicate rolling, without adding a conspicuous pattern absent from the reference. By the final sample the ball has passed out of the right boundary. No unseen subsequent landing is invented.

METHOD.md

Method and delivered artifacts

Actual generation method

This is a deterministic photographic pixel composite made with local Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. It uses only first_frame.png for scene imagery and prompt.txt for the requested action. No network, external imagery, AI model, external rendering/physics engine, or subagent was used. OBSERVATION.md was written before generation code, with the measurements and all twenty events.

  1. Read the original 256 × 144 RGB photograph. Frame 00 is copied directly, including copying the original PNG file bytes to frames/00.png.
  2. Restore the wood under the initial orange ball and its shadow. For rows 41–63, interpolate the unobstructed left/right wood colors and borrow a small amount of high-frequency grain from an unobstructed region of the same photograph, 46 pixels to the right. Two feathered elliptical masks limit restoration to the ball/shadow footprint. That restored background is fixed for frames 01–19.
  3. Extract the source-minus-restored-background residual. An error-function sphere matte approximates the original soft silhouette (horizontal radius 7.65 pixels, vertical radius 6.95 pixels). Translate with cubic pixel interpolation. Preserve the ball's photographed shading; outside the sphere matte, scale the original shadow residual by the destination/source wood ratio so it darkens the destination tabletop naturally.
  4. Advance the center by exactly (11.1, 1.4) pixels per frame, starting at (54.7, 51.6). This produces a straight path and constant projected speed, approximately 44.4 horizontal pixels per second. The path passes below the stationary red ball, traverses the visible table, clips at the right image boundary in frame 18, and is outside it in frame 19. The first frame already shows the ball clear of the black pipe; the continuation does not restart its emergence.
  5. Add at most 0.28 intensity units of rotating material variation, using analytical sphere normals and an angle equal to traveled pixel distance divided by a 7.3-pixel rolling radius. This is intentionally subtle because the source ball has no clearly visible seam. Illumination stays fixed.

The motion is prescribed in image coordinates rather than recovered from a calibrated 3D camera. The hidden tabletop is an estimate from its surrounding pixels. The clip ends when the ball leaves the frame; it does not depict an unobserved landing. No camera movement, cuts, new objects, or changes to the pipe, red ball, sofa, or table structure are introduced.

Timing and output

There are twenty RGB frames at 256 × 144, played at 4 fps. Sample times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds; holding every frame for 0.25 seconds gives a five-second clip. index.html is a standalone player with embedded lossless frame deltas, play/pause, replay, scrubbing, and looping. It has no network dependencies. artifact_io.py and player_template.html are provided output/packaging helpers; they do not generate the scene pixels.

Review and verification

The actual image view tool was used to inspect the supplied image, an enlarged source-ball crop, the initial generated contact/transition sheets, and the revised final inspection.png and transition_check.png. One revision replaced the rectangular restoration window with feathered ball/shadow masks to reduce unnecessary moving grain residuals. Static sheet review was performed; a browser playback review was not performed.
