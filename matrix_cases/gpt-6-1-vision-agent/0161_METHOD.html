METHOD.md

Method and verification

This is a local source-pixel animation, generated with Python, NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV image operations, and SciPy array filtering. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external references, AI models, scene engines, or subagents were used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator, then updated with measurements from magnified image-tool inspection and the final 20 sampled ball positions. Run python generate.py from this workspace to reproduce the outputs. The script uses artifact_io.py only to package the generated RGB frames and create the standalone player.

Pixel construction

The wall, tabletop, chrome frame, base, and three inner balls come from the photograph. The two moving end balls use a supersampled cutout of the photographed right sphere, with a circular matte and a small suspension eye. The initially displaced left sphere is partly hidden, so its full appearance is reconstructed using that source sphere texture. Tiny brightness adjustments approximate changing metallic reflections.

The grabber is extracted using a traced outline and a blue/neutral pixel-color matte. Its jaws are already open in the initial photograph. After release, the photographed tool moves smoothly left and slightly upward and exits by 0.75 seconds. The revealed tabletop is repaired using a local fitted RGB illumination plane and real wood-grain residuals sampled from an unobstructed lower strip of the same image. Old ball silhouettes and fine wires are removed with local Telea inpainting. Old end-ball reflections are replaced by feathered nearby unlit base pixels. These repairs are approximations to the unseen background.

The chrome supports are overlaid from the source after the balls and wires, preserving their foreground occlusion. The top frame bars are kept fixed. The three middle spheres are retained in their original positions. Both end spheres have two thin, antialiased suspension lines. A soft, low-contrast analytic reflection follows each moving end sphere within the base's top face.

Motion

A damped pendulum is integrated with a 0.0005-second step and a nominal full alternating period of 1.05 seconds. Its initial angle matches the approximately 23.2-pixel left displacement; release occurs after 0.07 seconds. Negative angular phases move the left end ball, and positive phases move the right end ball. Zero crossings represent contact and transfer of momentum to the opposite end while the three inner spheres stay near contact. Damping gradually reduces the swing amplitude.

Projected pendulum radii are approximately 94 pixels horizontally and 80–81 pixels vertically. These form an elliptical image projection chosen to match the source geometry. This is a simplified image-space Newton's cradle, rather than a calibrated three-dimensional reconstruction. The balls follow continuous arcs, but the required 4 fps samples may fall between collisions. Each rendered frame averages three samples spanning 0.012 seconds to provide slight exposure blur.

  • frames/00.png–19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, each value packed as 0xRRGGBB.

  • transition_check.png: all 19 adjacent frame pairs and amplified absolute differences.

Twenty frames displayed for 0.25 seconds each produce five seconds of playback. Physical sample times run from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds. The overview/player labels use the end of each display interval.

Checks and visual review

All 20 PNGs were decoded and checked against the packed tensor. Frame 00 is pixel-identical to the source and is also copied directly from the original PNG, preserving its file bytes. Dimensions, RGB mode, tensor shape, dtype, and 24-bit value range were checked. The embedded HTML's initial pixels and all 19 delta packets were independently decoded and compared with the tensor.

Image tools were actually used to inspect the source, magnified source crops, generated overview sheets, the complete 20-frame sheet, repaired background, magnified generated frames, and crops covering all 19 transitions. Revisions removed a residual dark tool edge, improved the revealed tabletop texture, reduced reflection smearing, and preserved the top bars. The final render keeps the camera fixed and depicts continued alternating end-ball motion at the end of the five-second clip.
