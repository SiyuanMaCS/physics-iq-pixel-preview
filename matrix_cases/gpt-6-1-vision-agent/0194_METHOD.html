METHOD.md

Method

This continuation was generated locally in Python from first_frame.png and prompt.txt, using NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. It uses source-photo pixel extraction, harmonic background reconstruction, inverse fabric warping, perspective projection, alpha compositing, and explicitly chosen motion curves. It does not use a generative image/video model, external references, a rendering engine, or a full physical simulation.

Image construction

The kettlebell silhouette was extracted by a local luminance threshold and contour fill. The hole through its handle remains transparent; the small source support tip inside that opening is cleared after withdrawal. Its photographed body, highlights, and faint weight marking are retained. The paper was extracted by its green color. Its four measured corners define a perspective transform; the few green pixels initially hidden by the clip are interpolated from the same photographed sheet so the released paper has a complete upper edge.

A union of the object and support masks is enlarged by three pixels. The occluded background is reconstructed by solving the discrete Laplace equation inside that region, separately for each RGB channel, with the surrounding source pixels as boundary conditions. This supplies continuous wall gradients behind the moved objects and a softly reconstructed cushion crown where necessary. All background reconstruction is limited to portions covered by the initial props.

The left cushion is remapped with a depression centered at x=96. Its horizontal contact profile is exp(-((x-96)/29)^4), and its displacement decreases to zero at the cushion's lower edge around y=131. Source columns are inverted to generate a continuous destination image, so the crest, photographed folds, and dark seam all move together. A small lateral displacement models lower-fabric spreading. The wall above the descending crown is included in the continuous map to reveal the cleared space. Local soft darkening supplies the contact shadow. The right cushion has no appreciable displacement from the light paper.

The bell's downward displacement in pixels over the 20 frames is: 0, 3, 10, 22, 34, 43, 47, 45.3, 43.4, 43.8, 44.5, 44.2, 44, 44.05, 44, 44, 44, 44, 44, 44. This is a controlled placement curve with a small damped settling motion, chosen to illustrate the heavy/light comparison. It is not a quantitatively calibrated 30-lb pillow compression model.

The paper begins tipping at the same time. Its initial corners (171,23), (200,24), (199,64), (169,62) move to (167,64), (198,70), (204,86), (171,80). Placement progress is 0, .055, .20, .43, .72, .94, 1 over frames 00–06, and then remains fixed. Premultiplied RGB and alpha are projected together to keep edges clean. A slight lighting adjustment and a light, narrow contact shadow make the sheet read as resting on the right cushion.

The visible dark and blue supports retain their source-image tip textures, with their upper cross sections extended while connected during lowering. Both release after frame 04, retract through frame 05, and have left the image by frame 06. No additional hands or props are drawn. The camera, lighting direction, frame dimensions, and wooden support remain fixed.

Review and revision

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code, with measured scene geometry and a plan for every frame. Image tools were used to inspect the source, the first generated inspection and transition sheets, and the revised outputs. The initial flat polynomial wall reconstruction produced visible patches; it was replaced by harmonic interpolation. The clip-occluded notch in the paper was reconstructed from local sheet pixels. The final inspection sheet, transition sheet, and enlarged settled frame were visually reviewed.
