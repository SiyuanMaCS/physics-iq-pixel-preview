OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The input is a 256 × 144 RGB image. The camera is fixed, looking slightly down at a brown wooden tabletop against a gray wall. The initial visual estimate of the table/wall boundary was approximately (0, 88) through (49, 66), then behind the basket and down to (255, 106). Light comes from above and left. The wall has a soft darker gradient toward the right; the wood has fine diagonal grain.

The inverted woven basket occupies approximately x = 65–137, y = 53–123. Its upper elliptical surface has a central dark magnet at approximately (100, 61); the basket body flares slightly toward its rounded bottom. The bottom edge rests on the table. A steel attachment and linked connectors extend upward from the magnet, along x ≈ 100, and the orange cord enters at the upper image edge. The ball described in the prompt is initially completely occluded by the basket. It will be reconstructed as an orange tennis ball centered near (102, 108), radius approximately 11 pixels, resting at y ≈ 120. Its stationary contact location fits inside the basket footprint.

The basket and attached magnet/links will move as one assembly. The visible cord remains taut above it. The initial image will be copied exactly for frame 00. The basket will pause briefly, then rise smoothly by about 88 pixels, putting it at the top of the frame and revealing the ball. No camera movement, extra objects, hands, or additional actions are introduced. The revealed wall/table will be reconstructed using only local image pixels, with a fitted wall gradient and wood texture sampled from unobstructed table regions. The ball will use mathematical sphere shading, subtle felt texture, curved pale seams, and a contact shadow. Basket shadow strength decreases as it rises.

METHOD.md

Method

This sequence was generated with deterministic local Python pixel operations using NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. The only scene sources were first_frame.png and the action described in prompt.txt. No network, external images, AI models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used. The existing artifact_io.py and player_template.html provide output serialization and playback only.

Scene construction

The supplied image provides the visible basket, magnet, metal attachment links, cord, wall, and tabletop. A manually measured Bézier silhouette extracts the basket. Contrast against the gray wall extracts the thin metal links and cord while retaining their holes. Fractional edge coverage and premultiplied RGB compositing reduce background fringes. The original basket texture and metal appearance are copied from the supplied pixels, rather than synthesized.

The previously occluded background is a local reconstruction. A robust fourth-degree color surface continues the wall illumination beneath the basket and the narrow attachment is locally inpainted. The tabletop boundary is measured from the visible image. A robust second-degree surface estimates unshadowed wood illumination. Grain from an unobstructed portion of the same tabletop is remapped into the occluded footprint. Existing grain outside the footprint is retained, with low-frequency correction to reduce the original basket contact shadow. A refinement after visual inspection corrected the reconstructed table edge and removed a faint connector remnant.

The orange tennis ball is initially hidden, so its visible pixels are synthesized. It is a stationary sphere of approximately 11.3 pixels radius, centered at (101.0, 109.8). Analytic sphere normals determine diffuse lighting from above and left, warm floor reflection, and a small broad highlight. Fixed random felt texture adds fine surface variation. A continuous sinuous curve on the sphere produces a pale tennis seam; distances to that three-dimensional curve determine seam coverage and foreshortening. The ball and its antialiased edge are rendered at four times the output resolution and averaged down. Soft floor shadows include a fixed ball contact shadow and a basket shadow that weakens as the basket rises.

Motion and timing

The camera and ball remain fixed. The basket, magnet, and attachment move together by vertical translation. Their lift values for frames 00–19 are:

0, 0, 1, 3, 7, 12, 18, 25, 33, 41, 49, 57, 64, 71, 77, 82, 86, 88, 88, 88 pixels.

The brief initial hold is followed by acceleration, steady upward travel, deceleration, and a final hold with the basket at the top of the frame. Occlusion compositing reveals the ball progressively under the moving basket. The attachment naturally exits through the fixed upper image boundary. Twenty RGB frames at 256 × 144 play at a constant 4 fps for five seconds; frame sample times are frame index / 4.

This is a pixel-based animated reconstruction with prescribed motion and analytic shading. Hidden geometry and background are estimated; a full three-dimensional physical simulation is not performed.
