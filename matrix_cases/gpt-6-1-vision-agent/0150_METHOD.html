OBSERVATION.md

Scene observations

  • The supplied image is a 256 × 144 RGB view of a light wooden tabletop. The camera is fixed, and the table fills most of the frame.
  • A black tube enters from the left edge. Its circular opening is approximately centered at (20, 41), with an outer radius of 11 pixels. The tube remains fixed.
  • A small white, angular lampshade occupies approximately x=110–166, y=0–37. It remains fixed and above the ball's projected path.
  • The brown tennis ball is already outside the tube in the initial frame. Its center is approximately (104, 47), with radius 8–9 pixels. The ball is dark brown with a soft upper highlight and a faint pale seam.
  • A soft ball shadow lies mainly below and slightly to the right of the ball. The tabletop has low contrast, nearly horizontal grain and uneven illumination.
  • Continue the existing ball's rightward motion without a second ball or a return to the tube. Use a straight projected table path, y=47.7+0.0625(x−104), retaining a roughly constant apparent radius. The ball rolls with a small amount of friction and exits the right edge at the end.

Twenty-frame event plan

Frames are sampled every 0.25 seconds. Frame 00 is copied exactly from the source. Ball positions use x=104+37.2t−0.24t², y=47.7+0.0625(x−104). Rotation follows traveled distance divided by the ball radius. The camera, tube, lampshade, lighting, and table stay stationary.

METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was made entirely with deterministic local Python pixel operations. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network access, external images, AI models, other cases, external renderers, or subagents were used.

The initial image already shows the brown tennis ball outside the black tube. The continuation therefore moves that same ball farther right. The tube and lampshade stay fixed, and no new objects enter the shot.

The code reconstructs the tabletop hidden by the initial ball and its blurred shadow using clean same-row samples to either side. It interpolates those samples, adds faint nearby photographed wood texture, and blends the result through a broad cosine feather. Reconstruction is restricted to the tabletop below row 37. This estimates previously hidden pixels; it does not recover observed video content.

The moving ball uses brown body shading sampled from the photographed ball. OpenCV's local, non-neural inpainting removes the original pale seam from that small source surface. A soft elliptical silhouette then separates the body color from its original background. This photographed body shading remains in screen coordinates. The visible rotation is supplied by a procedural pale seam and a small felt variation on analytic sphere normals, rotated using an inverse Rodrigues transform. It is a source-based image composite with an analytic spherical surface pattern, rather than a full 3D reconstruction.

For frame number n, t=n/4 seconds. The ball center is:

  • x = 104 + 37.2t - 0.24t²
  • y = 47.7 + 0.0625(x - 104)

The apparent radii are 8.7 and 9.0 pixels. The angular displacement is traveled distance divided by 8.85 pixels. This provides steady rightward rolling with slight frictional slowing, without bouncing. The ball clips naturally at the right border and is entirely outside the view by the final frame.

Two soft analytic Gaussian shadows travel with the ball: a broader shadow to its right and a tighter contact shadow below it. Each moving patch is computed at four times the output resolution, softly filtered, and reduced to 256 × 144 pixels. Scene pixels outside the small reconstruction and moving patches retain the source values. The tube and lampshade retain their original pixels.

Review and revisions

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator and includes measurements and the complete 20-frame event plan. Image view tools were used to inspect the initial image, enlarged source ball, generated contact sheets, consecutive early frames, and reconstructed background. The first tabletop inpaint spread a brown halo into the vacated region. It was replaced with same-row texture interpolation. Subsequent feathering and restricting reconstruction below the lampshade removed visible reconstruction edges and a bright donor artifact. The final inspection.png and transition_check.png were visually reviewed.

Output and verification

Run python generate.py from this directory. It writes:

  • pixels.npz: pixels has shape (144, 256, 20) and dtype uint32. Each value is 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images, each 256 × 144.
  • inspection.png: eight representative full frames.
  • transition_check.png: frames 00–07 with enlarged path crops.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-frame player at 4 fps, with playback, replay, and seeking.
  • .tmp/verification.json: format and pixel equality results.

The sequence is 20 frames at 4 fps, occupying 5 seconds of playback. Frame 00 is copied byte for byte from first_frame.png; its RGB pixels also equal the first tensor slice. Every saved PNG is verified against both the generated RGB array and the unpacked uint32 tensor. An additional check reconstructs all frames from the player's embedded first frame and delta packets, and compares them with the tensor. The final frame contains the fixed reconstructed tabletop after the ball exits.
