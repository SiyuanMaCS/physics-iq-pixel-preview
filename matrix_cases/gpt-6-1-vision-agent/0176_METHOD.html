METHOD.md

Method

This is a local Python continuation of one photograph, using NumPy, SciPy and Pillow. It uses no external assets, learned models, network calls, or external rendering or physics engines. Run python generate.py from this workspace to regenerate the deliverables.

The frame plan is recorded in OBSERVATION.md, which was written before implementation. The camera, wall, wood table and black platform remain fixed. The teapot rotates clockwise as viewed from above, at a constant 10 degrees per second. Frame i samples t=i/4 seconds and yaw=2.5*i degrees. Twenty frames displayed at 4 fps give a five-second sequence; the final sample is at 4.75 seconds.

The vessel is represented by photographed layers and analytical surfaces. Manually measured silhouettes and bounded color mattes separate the red handle and spout. The blue body, green lid and pedestal foot use row-dependent radii and angular texture coordinates. A cylindrical/spherical inverse warp moves the floral decoration around the vessel. Source-row iteration accounts for the slightly elevated camera and prevents sampling the wall at the curved silhouette. The lid knob uses a small spherical warp. Ceramic glints extracted from the blue body are held at their original lighting positions, while a modest analytical illumination ratio adjusts the rotated material.

The left handle is projected as a rigid attachment that moves behind the body. The red spout centerline rotates toward the camera with an elevation-dependent vertical displacement; its nearly circular tube retains its thickness. Photographic colors are unmatted at attachment edges. The background uncovered by these parts is reconstructed from visible wall samples and a nearby patch of the same tabletop. Layer ordering supplies the change in overlap. Fourfold supersampling smooths subpixel motion. The surfaces have approximate circular symmetry, so their outline stays fixed while the painted texture rotates. A zero-angle residual preserves the photograph's stationary details, excluding the original attachment silhouettes so they do not leave fixed outlines. Frame 00 is copied directly from the input, rather than rendered.

This is an approximate 2.5D reconstruction from a single view. The back-side floral pattern is inferred by extending the visible hemisphere and blending toward the opposite visible flank across an angular seam. Hidden blue ceramic behind the initial spout is inferred from the opposite visible flank. Ceramic edge colors are extended outward to prevent wall antialiasing from entering the surface texture. The attachment projections approximate their three-dimensional shapes. These inferred surfaces are not additional observations.
