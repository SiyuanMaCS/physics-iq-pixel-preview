This is a deterministic local Python pixel animation using only first_frame.png and prompt.txt as scene inputs. It does not use a generative model, external images, references, network access, a rendering engine, or subagents. artifact_io.py and the supplied HTML template are output helpers.

OBSERVATION.md was written before coding and records the measured scene and initial twenty-frame plan. generate.py preserves the fixed photograph behind the action. OpenCV's local Navier–Stokes inpainting reconstructs the small regions of wood initially covered by the balls and shadows. The source ball pixels provide the colors, shading, and white seams. Local seam inpainting separates the observed seam texture from approximate fixed diffuse shading. Supersampled orthographic sphere coordinates rotate the seam texture according to displacement divided by radius. The unobserved hemisphere is filled by mirroring the observed surface; this is an approximation, and the small source image limits surface detail. Antialiased compositing and analytic diffuse/contact shadows keep the moving balls attached to the tabletop. The stationary brown/orange pair is retained from the source until impact.

The trajectory is a one-dimensional equal-mass collision calculation with 0.00025-second fixed steps, restitution 0.79, initial gray speed 44 projected pixels/second, and rolling resistance 7.3 projected pixels/second squared. Touching contacts propagate within a time step. A small vertical image displacement follows the table's projected direction; there is no bounce. These parameters are plausible animation assumptions rather than quantities recovered from the photograph.

The gray ball contacts brown at approximately 0.799 s. Brown moves about 1.08 px, gray settles near x=136.43, and orange rolls from x=164.25 to x=228.06 while slowing. At the final sample the orange ball is still moving slowly, at about 1.73 projected pixels/second. All balls remain visible and supported by the tabletop. Twenty frames are sampled at t=0, 0.25, …, 4.75 s; displaying each for 0.25 s gives the requested five-second sequence.

The first revision allowed the orange ball to travel beyond the tabletop and restored the stationary-pair patch over the approaching gray ball. The final revision increases rolling resistance and restores the stationary patch before painting the gray ball. The final inspection.png and transition_check.png were viewed with the image tool. The inspection covered the starting-image handoff, consecutive frames around impact, ball visibility, deceleration, shadows, and the final tabletop position. The diagnostics are supporting images rather than extra animation frames.

The required artifacts are:

  • generate.py: reproducible generator; run python generate.py in this workspace.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: twenty 256 × 144 RGB images. Frame 00 is a file-identical copy of the original PNG.
  • pixels.npz: pixels, a uint32 tensor of shape (144, 256, 20), encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight selected frames in a contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: frames 00–07, enlarged action crops, and amplified consecutive-frame differences.
  • index.html: standalone playback with embedded initial pixels and delta packets, at four frames per second.
  • OBSERVATION.md: pre-coding measurements and event plan.
  • METHOD.md: this explanation.
  • verification.json: machine-readable output checks and sampled physics states.

Verification checks every PNG against its tensor slice, all twenty decoded HTML player frames against the same tensor, RGB mode and size, tensor shape and dtype, unused high bits, exact initial PNG bytes, ball separation, horizontal bounds, and fixed background pixels above y=39 and below y=72. All checks passed. The HTML player data was checked programmatically; browser playback was not inspected. Temporary crop and background images are confined to .tmp/.
