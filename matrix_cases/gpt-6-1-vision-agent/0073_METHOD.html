light-on-block · 生成方法

This continuation was made entirely with local Python pixel operations, using NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external references, learned models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Geometry and motion

An upright rectangular cuboid is represented by eight 3D vertices with a width of 7.3, depth of 1.85, and height of 22.65 in calibrated image-scale units. Its contact center projects to approximately (154.25, 109.3). A fixed orthographic projection includes the camera's downward view of the tabletop. The same cuboid rotates clockwise, seen from above, by 12 degrees per frame interval, equivalent to 48 degrees per second. The final sample is at 4.75 seconds and 228 degrees. Each of the 20 samples is displayed for 250 milliseconds, producing five seconds of playback.

The circular turntable is unmarked and nearly rotationally symmetric. Its visible boundary and fixed-light highlights are retained from the source photograph. The block's changing faces communicate its rotation with the platter. No camera movement, translation, tipping, lighting change, or additional objects are introduced.

Pixel reconstruction and rendering

The original upright wall shadow was removed only within its small local region. A quadratic horizontal fit to adjacent visible wall pixels reconstructs the smooth spotlight illumination, with a small amount of detrended texture reused from neighboring wall pixels. The original horizontal platform shadow remains fixed. The source block and its blue reflection were removed by short row interpolation from neighboring wall, tabletop, and platter pixels.

Visible cuboid faces are selected with rotated surface normals. Their blue texture is sampled from the original front face, with Lambert-style shading from a fixed light to the right and toward the camera, ambient illumination, and a subtle edge darkening. The top face has a darker cap color. All polygons are rasterized at six times the output resolution and reduced with Lanczos filtering.

The wall shadow comes from the convex hull of the same eight rotating cuboid vertices after a calibrated affine mapping to the rear wall. This mapping approximates local point-light projection; its strong depth-to-horizontal component represents the light's position to the right. It is an analytic approximation, not a full perspective ray tracer. Shadow edges receive a 0.43-pixel Gaussian penumbra, and the umbra retains dim ambient illumination. The vertical shadow joins the photograph's existing platform shadow.

A small footprint contact shadow and a separate mirrored, blue-tinted cuboid layer provide the contact and glossy reflection. The reflected layer is softened and fades slightly across its approximately seven-pixel height. It ends at the upper platter rim and does not extend onto the cylindrical front.

Frame 00 is copied directly from the input RGB image, without reconstruction or rendering. Frames 01–19 composite the rotating geometry, changing wall shadow, and reflection over the reconstructed local background. Everything outside these small regions retains the original image pixels.

Review and revision

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation implementation and includes the full 20-frame plan. Image tools were actually used to view the source, enlarged source details, generated inspection.png, transition_check.png, and an enlarged block comparison. A close-up pixel check distinguished the reflected dark blue strip at y=110–116 from the physical front face ending near y=109. The model was revised to use the correct physical height and a separate reflection confined to the platter top. Both required image sheets were viewed again after this revision.
