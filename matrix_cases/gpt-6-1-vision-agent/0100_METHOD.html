METHOD.md

Generation method

This is a deterministic continuation made with local Python pixel operations. The only scene sources are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No external images, AI models, rendering engines, network requests, or subagents were used. OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code, with measurements and a twenty-frame motion plan.

generate.py uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV for image arrays, local fitting, matting, and affine resampling. The supplied artifact_io.py writes the packed tensor, frame PNGs, inspection sheet, and standalone HTML player.

The original wrench is isolated by brightness within its measured region. Connected-component selection excludes the disconnected tabletop at the bottom left. Antialiased coverage is estimated relative to the recovered platform color and nearby opaque metal pixels. The metal appearance is taken from the input image, including its open jaw, shaft, rounded end, and highlights.

Only the small region occupied by the original wrench and its contact shadow is recovered. Visible pixels on the platform are fitted with a local quadratic color function. A tilted elliptical front boundary blends that top-surface estimate into the darker cylindrical side. Very subtle grain is borrowed from an unobstructed nearby part of the same image. The rest of the input is retained.

The wrench turns clockwise by 131.5 degrees in the projected tabletop plane. A fixed linear projection, with vertical foreshortening 0.54 and image tilt 0.16, maps the plane rotation into image coordinates. The center moves smoothly by five pixels right and six pixels up during the initial turn, which keeps the wrench supported on the disk. The planned angular sequence accelerates, turns through the steep orientation, and decelerates toward a nearly horizontal alignment with the magnet. This is prescribed image motion rather than a numerical electromagnetic simulation.

The recovered original shadow is warped separately underneath the wrench. Its offset is corrected so the light direction stays fixed while the wrench turns. Foreground and coverage layers are interpolated at six times the output resolution and area-downsampled to 256 by 144. This avoids the serrated appearance that arose from rotating the initial low-resolution extraction without smooth interpolation. Frame 00 bypasses all reconstruction and is copied exactly from the input RGB image.

The sequence contains 20 RGB frames, each displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving four frames per second and a five-second playback. Their sample times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds. pixels.npz contains the key pixels, a uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20). Each element stores 0xRRGGBB. frames/00.png through frames/19.png contain the same values as RGB PNGs. index.html embeds the first frame and lossless delta packets for the remaining frames.

Review and verification

The image View tool was used to review the supplied frame, a magnified wrench crop, generated inspection sheets, the recovered platform, magnified output details, and the final transition sheet. Review found a disconnected tabletop component in the first extraction; that component was excluded. Resampling was smoothed, and the shadow offset was corrected to keep the illumination direction fixed. The final sheets show the wrench remaining visible and supported, a clockwise turn that settles, and a stationary camera, magnet, tape, cable, and tabletop.

generate.py verifies the tensor dimensions and dtype, exact equality of frame 00 to the input, and equality of each PNG to the unpacked tensor. An additional check reconstructs all twenty frames from the embedded HTML delta packets and compares them to the PNGs. Numerical results are saved in .tmp/verification.json. Visual review was performed on the image sheets; a browser playback test was not performed.
