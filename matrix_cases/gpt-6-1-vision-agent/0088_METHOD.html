METHOD.md

Method

This is a local photographic pixel continuation. The only visual source is first_frame.png; prompt.txt supplies the action. generate.py uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, and the supplied output-only artifact_io.py. It uses no network, external reference, learned model, external scene engine, or subagent.

The first frame is copied exactly. Later frames begin with the original photograph enlarged six times for smooth local compositing. Analytic masks describe the short jet below the blue outlet, the elliptical red surface inside the glass rim, two attached wet rivulets on the glass, and a shallow pool on the baking tray. The camera and physical objects remain in place.

The original glass is already full to its lip, and a short red connection is visible beneath the tap. The continuation therefore keeps pouring, creates impact ripples, and lets excess liquid run over the rim. The wetting fronts accelerate downward using a quadratic gravity-inspired travel law, then remain attached to the glass. Pool area grows approximately linearly with accumulated inflow, so its radius grows with the square root of time. The elliptical pool projection follows the tray plane. A glass silhouette occludes the pool behind the glass, and surface normals, red absorption colors, meniscus shading, and subdued moving highlights give the liquid its wet appearance. Surface and contact waves use deterministic continuous phase functions.

This is a hand-built approximation of the visible fluid motion, not a full three-dimensional fluid simulation. Original glass reflections and image texture are retained. Masks are supersampled and reduced locally; pixels away from the liquid motion are restored directly from the source to avoid resampling changes to the static photograph.

The result has 20 RGB frames at 256 x 144. Samples are 0.25 seconds apart, from t=0 to t=4.75 seconds, and each is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving a 5-second playback at 4 fps. The supplied helper saves frames/00.png through 19.png, pixels.npz, an inspection sheet, and a standalone index.html player. The tensor key is pixels, its shape is (144, 256, 20), and its dtype is uint32; each value is packed as 0xRRGGBB. transition_check.png includes early, middle, and late consecutive samples with enlarged glass crops.
