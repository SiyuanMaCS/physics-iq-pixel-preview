METHOD.md

Method and verification

The output is a five-second continuation of the supplied photograph: 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, played at four frames per second. Sampling times are 0.00 through 4.75 seconds; each frame is held for 0.25 seconds. Frame 00 is the exact decoded RGB input.

Actual pixel method

generate.py uses Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. It uses the supplied photograph as the stationary background and measures the four straight ramp top edges and the lower catcher in source-image coordinates. The original marble is already at the purple ramp, so the continuation begins there. The upstream teal and curved green ramps stay as photographed.

For frames 01–19, the original marble and its blurred halo are removed by interpolating nearby photograph pixels. Neighboring samples are aligned by their distance above the sloping purple ramp, preserving its blurred upper boundary. The repair changes 61 source pixels. Everything outside this repair and the current moving marble's small local footprint remains exactly equal to the source photograph.

The moving marble is drawn as an analytic, softly shaded yellow sphere with a two-pixel radius. Sixfold supersampling, translucent alpha compositing, broad diffuse illumination, a small yellow highlight, and a Gaussian-softened silhouette match the low-contrast source. A faint offset shadow reproduces the source marble's soft local halo. Five subframe samples over a 1/120-second exposure provide slight motion blur. This is local photograph compositing with a two-dimensional physics approximation, not a generated or captured video or a full three-dimensional glass simulation. No external images, masks, models, engines, network resources, or agents are used.

Motion

The analytic model integrates at 0.0005-second steps with screen-space gravity of 1000 pixels/s². Each ramp is a measured straight segment. Sphere centers are offset from its surface by the radius along the normal. Contact acceleration is the component of gravity down the ramp multiplied by 5/7, the solid-sphere rolling factor. At a lip, the marble retains its velocity and falls ballistically. Landing projects velocity onto the next ramp's tangent and applies dissipation; it briefly slows or travels uphill before gravity reverses it. The catcher uses a small normal bounce, tangential damping, and its inner left wall to bring the ball to rest.

Final events: purple departure at 1.258 s; yellow landing at 1.427 s and departure at 2.081 s; blue landing at 2.221 s and departure at 3.034 s; green landing at 3.205 s and departure at 4.229 s; catcher landing at 4.429 s; rest at 4.559 s. At the requested 4 fps, the short airborne intervals between the first ramps occur between sampled frames. Frame 17 catches the final departure, frame 18 follows the small catcher bounce, and frame 19 shows rest. Detailed positions are saved in .tmp/trajectory.json, and the refined table is included in OBSERVATION.md.

Frame 00 was compared exactly against the source RGB image. Every encoded player frame was reconstructed from the initial frame and delta packets and compared exactly with the tensor.
