OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The input is a 256 × 144 RGB photograph. The camera is fixed and looks obliquely down at a pale wood tabletop. The table grain runs approximately from lower left to upper right. A dark cylindrical pipe enters at the left edge, with its mouth near (20, 112), directed toward the upper right. The fluorescent yellow-green tennis ball is already out of the pipe: its center is approximately (102.5, 72.5), its visible radius approximately 9–10 pixels, and its blurred shadow extends toward the lower left. The cardboard ramp occupies the upper-right corner; its lower edge runs from about (145, 35) to (180, 67). A blue support is visible near (231, 24). The ramp rises toward the upper-right edge. The background, lighting, pipe, cardboard, and blue support must remain fixed.

The continuation starts with the supplied photograph, without resetting the ball to the pipe. The ball rolls in a straight world-space line from the pipe toward the middle of the ramp toe, around (164, 52) at the contact point. Its image-space path bends upward slightly as it climbs the inclined surface. It slows under gravity, briefly reaches its maximum height, and reverses along the same line. No additional object, hand, camera motion, or cut is introduced. A softly shaded textured ball and a contact/cast shadow move together. The ball stays in contact with the surface and becomes slightly smaller as it recedes.

METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was generated entirely through local Python pixel operations using NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. The only scene inputs were first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network access, external scene references, external AI models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

OBSERVATION.md was written before generate.py, with measurements from the supplied photograph and an event plan for all 20 frames. The supplied image shows the tennis ball already outside the pipe, so the sequence continues that position rather than putting it back inside the pipe.

The photographed scene is the fixed background plate. A small union of elliptical regions around the initial ball and its cast shadow is repaired with nearby tabletop pixels, sampled 40 pixels downward. OpenCV's Poisson seamless cloning integrates the donor texture with the surrounding photographed wood. Pixels outside that repair region are restored exactly from the source image. An early repair had a faint green remnant and a shadow copied from the donor; visual inspection led to enlarging the repair region and moving the donor to unobstructed wood. The final repair is used identically in frames 01–19.

The moving ball is an analytic sphere rendered with fourfold spatial supersampling. Surface normals provide diffuse shading under a fixed upper-right light. Deterministic noise supplies a subtle felt texture, and a faint wavy equatorial seam rotates with the ball. Both are attached to rotating sphere coordinates rather than to the background. The sphere has a small perspective size reduction as it recedes. A five-sample average over an 18 ms exposure and a Gaussian filter reproduce soft photographic edges. These are generated sphere pixels, rather than a copied foreground cutout or a learned video model.

The sphere is composited over the fixed plate with premultiplied alpha. A pair of Gaussian shadow profiles supplies a soft lower-left cast shadow and a tighter contact shadow. The shadow projection blends from the tabletop to the cardboard near the ramp toe. Lighting and static scene geometry remain fixed.

Motion uses a piecewise kinematic approximation inferred from the photograph. The tabletop segment has constant velocity. The ramp segment has constant opposing acceleration, slows to a turning point, and reverses along the same world-space line. Its projected direction differs because the ramp slopes upward. The ball reaches the ramp toe at 1.40 s, turns at 2.72 s, and returns across the toe at 4.04 s. Its center moves from (102.5, 72.5) in the initial frame to approximately (190.0, 20.0) at the turning point, ending at (132.8, 58.0). These are visually estimated kinematics; the single source image does not determine calibrated physical dimensions or camera intrinsics.

Exactly 20 RGB images are sampled at 0.25 s intervals, from t = 0.00 through 4.75 s. Displaying each sample for 0.25 s gives a 5-second sequence at 4 fps. Frame 00 bypasses all generation and uses the original RGB image exactly.

Artifacts and verification

  • generate.py: reproducible local generator and PNG/tensor assertions.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 256 × 144 RGB frames.
  • pixels.npz: a pixels uint32 tensor of shape (144, 256, 20); each entry is packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight representative frames for overall sequence review.
  • transition_check.png: six adjacent-frame pairs, with enlarged crops covering the initial transition, ramp entry, turning point, and return to the table.

The image viewing tool was used to review the source, intermediate clean plates, contact sheets, and the final transition image. The initial repair was revised based on that review. The final review found the removed-ball area clear and the ramp-entry and reversal transitions continuous.

Programmatic checks read every saved PNG and recover its RGB values independently from the corresponding packed tensor slice. All 20 comparisons pass. The tensor shape and uint32 dtype are checked, all values fit in 24 bits, and the first saved frame matches the input pixel for pixel. The standalone player's embedded first frame and all 19 decoded delta frames are also compared with the tensor. See verification.txt for the final result.
