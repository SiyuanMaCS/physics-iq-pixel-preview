ball-behind-rotating-paper · 生成方法

This is a locally constructed pixel animation using the supplied photograph, Python, NumPy, Pillow, and SciPy. It uses no external images, networks, AI models, scene engines, or subagents. It is a source-image composite with analytic drawing and prescribed object motion, rather than a video-model output or a full physical simulation.

Scene construction

The original photograph supplies the stationary wall, wooden tabletop, turntable body, cable, and white paper texture. The narrow shaft already visible above the cardstock is interpreted as the grabber. Its held orange ball begins hidden behind the card; the tool lifts it into view before lowering it behind the rotating card.

The initial cardstock and grabber footprints are removed into an estimated clean background. A fourth-degree color polynomial fitted to visible wall pixels supplies hidden wall shading. Visible wood pixels from the right of the photograph are warped to supply the hidden tabletop grain, with a second-degree color fit correcting spatial brightness. A local discrete Poisson solve matches the estimated region to the photograph along its boundary. The small hidden top surface of the existing black turntable is reconstructed with a shaded ellipse. The original turntable body and cable remain photographic.

The cardstock follows a rigid, projected clockwise turn about the turntable's vertical axis, interpreted as viewed from above. Its angle increases uniformly from 0 to 82 degrees. A homography pulls the measured original cardstock texture onto each projected quadrilateral; slight angle-dependent shading and a thin edge represent its thickness. The card stays attached at the turntable and progressively reveals the ball.

The grabber is drawn as a metallic shaft, dark housing with blue insets, and two curved jaws. Prescribed positions raise its held ball, lower it behind the card, pause at table height, open the jaws, and withdraw upwards. The ball has a fixed 10.3-pixel radius and a final center near (149,111). An analytic sphere normal provides directional illumination, with deterministic low-amplitude felt texture and curved pale tennis seams. Its position remains fixed after release. Soft shadow fields represent approach to the table and final contact.

Objects are composited behind the cardstock, with the photographic turntable lip restored where appropriate. Working layers use 4× spatial supersampling and are downsampled with Lanczos filtering. Pixels outside the union of moving regions are restored exactly from the source. Frame 00 bypasses all drawing and is copied directly from first_frame.png.

Review and revision

OBSERVATION.md was written before implementation, with measured scene features and the twenty-frame event plan. Closer visual inspection identified the source shaft as the existing grabber, and that interpretation was recorded before coding.

Image View tools were used on the supplied frame, enlarged source, first-pass inspection sheet and final-frame enlargement, revised inspection sheet and final-frame enlargement, and the complete transition sheet. The revision replaced background boundary seams with Poisson blending, removed a source-card edge remnant from the turntable restoration, and shifted the landing point right so the ball rests beside the platform on the table. The transition review checked lift, descent, occlusion, release, withdrawal, and continuing card rotation.

Artifacts and checks

  • generate.py: reproducible local generator. Run python generate.py from this directory.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: twenty 256 × 144 RGB frames, each held for 0.25 seconds, totaling five seconds at 4 fps. Sample times run from 0.00 through 4.75 seconds.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, with axes (y,x,frame) and values packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: eight representative frames.
  • transition_check.png: all nineteen adjacent frame pairs with their absolute RGB differences multiplied by four for inspection.
  • index.html: standalone embedded animation player with playback, looping, and seeking; no external assets are required.

The generator asserts dimensions, dtype, source-exact frame 00, and exact equality between every PNG's packed RGB values and its tensor slice. An independent final check also decodes the HTML player's initial frame and nineteen delta packets and compares every reconstructed frame to the tensor.

The hidden background and obscured turntable top are estimates. Motion is specified directly, lighting is analytic, and ball contact is represented by occlusion and shadows; no measured dynamics or physical solver is claimed.
