METHOD.md

Actual generation method

This is a locally synthesized continuation of the supplied photograph, made with Python pixel operations and a simplified two-dimensional numerical marble simulation. It is not a recorded experiment or a learned video model output. The only scene inputs used were first_frame.png and prompt.txt; GENERATION_RULES.md supplied the delivery constraints. No network, external reference, external model, external engine, or subagent was used.

Source image and background

OBSERVATION.md was written before generation code, documenting the measured setup and an initial twenty-frame event plan. The supplied 256 × 144 RGB photograph supplies the static camera view, whiteboard, four straight ramps, unused upper curved piece, purple catcher, black frame, transparent mounts, and reflections. Frame 00 is an exact copy of its RGB pixels.

For frames 01–19, the original marble is removed in a small feathered ellipse around (174.4, 26.5). The replacement is copied from a clean region twelve pixels to the right, shifted vertically by −1.3 pixels to follow the ramp edge. The source board tone is matched above the ramp, and the copied patch includes the unobstructed dotted ramp edge. This fixes the upward dark smear produced by an earlier inpainting attempt. The remainder of the background stays static. The reconstruction is an estimate of the occluded pixels, not additional scene evidence.

Motion

The marble has an estimated image radius of 2.7 pixels. The four contact edges are measured straight lines from the source image. Motion is integrated at 4000 steps per second, with an effective gravity of 1550 pixels/s². On a ramp with slope m, horizontal rolling acceleration is (5/7) * g * m / (1 + m*m). The factor 5/7 approximates a solid sphere rolling without slipping. The center is offset from the contact edge by the marble radius along the surface normal.

A ramp exit starts a ballistic flight with the outgoing horizontal and vertical velocities. Landing is detected when the sphere crosses the next contact edge from above. The new horizontal velocity comes from tangent projection of incoming velocity, multiplied by 0.46 to model dissipative capture by the channel. The sphere can briefly travel uphill before rolling in the opposite direction. Contacts have no normal bounce on the straight ramps. These are chosen image-space approximations; the photograph does not determine true dimensions or material friction.

The U-shaped receiver uses its measured sloping floor and a right wall. The final landing keeps 10% of vertical speed for a small rebound and 20% of horizontal speed, followed by exponential damping. The final sequence leaves the first blue ramp at 0.978 s, lands on yellow at 1.114 s, leaves yellow at 2.130 s, lands on middle blue at 2.268 s, leaves blue at 3.166 s, lands on green at 3.296 s, leaves green at 4.309 s, and reaches the catcher at 4.490 s. It remains in the catcher in frames 18 and 19.

Pixels and sampling

The moving marble is rendered analytically in a local patch at sixfold spatial sampling. A spherical normal field controls diffuse gold shading, a darkened translucent rim, a small upper-left highlight, and a faint lower caustic. Transmission blends the local image into its golden color; this is an appearance approximation rather than optical ray tracing. A faint offset Gaussian shadow grounds the sphere against the board. Gaussian softness matches the small, softly focused original marble.

Only the marble and shadow differences are downsampled and added to the background, so ramp pixels outside their influence do not acquire resampling blur. Each generated frame averages three positions over a 0.01-second exposure. There are twenty RGB frames sampled at t = 0, 0.25, …, 4.75 seconds. Playback holds each frame for 0.25 seconds at 4 fps, giving five seconds total. The source frame is explicitly substituted at index zero.

Artifacts and verification

Run python generate.py from this workspace to regenerate the outputs. The script uses local NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, SciPy, and the supplied output-only artifact_io.py helper. It writes:

  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png, all 256 × 144 RGB.
  • pixels.npz, containing key pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, with values 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png, an eight-frame contact sheet with sample times.
  • transition_check.png, all twenty frames paired with highlighted changes from the preceding frame.
  • index.html, a self-contained player with embedded pixel data, playback, looping, and a seek control.
  • .tmp/trajectory.json, constants, contact events, frame states, and verification results.

The script asserts frame 00 pixel equality with the supplied photograph, checks the tensor's shape, dtype and packed RGB range, and decodes every tensor slice to confirm exact equality with its corresponding PNG. The standalone player's initial frame and nineteen delta packets were also decoded locally and checked against all twenty tensor slices.
