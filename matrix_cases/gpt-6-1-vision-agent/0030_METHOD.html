ball-train · 生成方法

Created 20 RGB frames at 256 × 144, sampled at 4 fps. They represent five seconds of playback, with sample times from 0.00 through 4.75 seconds. Frame 00 is an exact pixel copy of first_frame.png.

Inputs and implementation

The scene inputs were only first_frame.png and prompt.txt. The rules were read from GENERATION_RULES.md, and measurements and the event plan were written to OBSERVATION.md before implementation. generate.py uses local Python, NumPy, Pillow and SciPy pixel operations. The supplied artifact_io.py and player_template.html only serialize outputs and build the player. No external image, model, renderer, physics engine, network source, or subagent was used.

The fixed room, table edges, sofa and pipe remain photographed pixels. Small regions occupied by the original balls and shadows are filled by a sparse Poisson solve. Its boundary colors come from the photograph; its interior gradients come from nearby clear tabletop texture, with a coordinate offset that follows the wood-grain direction. The donor range is compressed horizontally to avoid the original balls, their long shadows and the table edge. These masks are constructed analytically in the script from measured ellipses.

The moving ball surfaces sample the original photographed balls. A spherical-normal warp rotates the observed surface as each ball travels; the rotation angle is travel distance divided by radius. The unobserved back repeats a mirrored version of the photographed front. Analytic diffuse illumination compensates the sampled lighting and keeps the light direction fixed in the room. Edges are composited at fourfold resolution and reduced to the native pixel grid. This is an approximate pixel-based surface continuation, not a recovered complete three-dimensional scene.

Soft elliptical cast shadows and small contact shadows move with each ball. Their pixels are clipped to the tabletop. During the two approach frames, the stationary brown and orange pair retain their source pixels and original shadows.

Motion

Equal-mass balls move along the observed line, projected in image space along direction (1, 0.117). The gray ball starts at 34 image pixels per second. A fixed 0.0005-second integration step applies rolling resistance of 6 image pixels per second squared. Contact impulses use restitution 0.93, with small overlap corrections. This approximates the visible rolling demonstration; image coordinates are not calibrated world units.

The gray ball reaches the brown ball at 0.665 seconds. The brown ball contacts the orange ball at 0.6755 seconds. Most motion passes to the orange ball, while the other two soon settle. Final centers are gray (107.323, 53.883), brown (121.588, 54.945), and orange (198.887, 64.016). The orange ball remains on the table and slows through the final frame. Detailed computed positions and velocities are in .tmp/motion_report.json.

Artifacts and verification

  • generate.py: reproducible pixel-generation script.
  • pixels.npz: pixels array of shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: the 20 RGB frames.
  • inspection.png: eight-frame contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: frames 00–03, enlarged ball crops, and amplified pixel differences from the initial frame.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-data player at 4 fps.
  • OBSERVATION.md and METHOD.md: pre-implementation observations and the actual method.

Image View tools were used on the input, enlarged source crops, the reconstructed table, the contact sheet and the transition check. Review revealed a residual shadow in the first background reconstruction. The mask and donor coordinates were revised; the final table reconstruction, transition check and contact sheet were then viewed again.

Automated verification passed for exact frame-00 equality, 20 RGB PNGs at the required dimensions, every PNG's equality to its packed tensor slice, and unchanged static room pixels. The standalone player's first frame and all 19 delta packets were independently decoded and compared with the tensor; all 20 match. The verification also checked the exact required frame filenames and all required artifact files.
