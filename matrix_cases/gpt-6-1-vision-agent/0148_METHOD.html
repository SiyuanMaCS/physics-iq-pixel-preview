METHOD.md

Generation method

This is a deterministic local Python pixel reconstruction and animation. The only scene inputs are first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, other cases, external imagery, AI models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used. NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and the provided output-only artifact_io.py helper were used.

The input photograph is copied exactly into frame 00. For later frames, a small feathered region around the original ball and shadow is reconstructed from nearby wood grain on the same board. The donor is offset by 39 pixels horizontally and −4.8 pixels vertically. A low-order color correction, estimated from the surrounding visible wood, matches its illumination. The rest of the photographed background is retained.

The moving ball is rasterized as an analytic sphere at four times the output resolution. Its brown color and soft silhouette are informed by the input. A mathematical closed tennis seam, defined by sinusoidal latitude on a unit sphere, is oriented using measured pale seam locations in the input. It rotates with the sphere. A small deterministic surface texture is also attached to the sphere. The unseen surface and seam are mathematical approximations, since the input contains only one view of the ball. Illumination comes from analytic sphere normals, a fixed light direction, and ambient shading. Premultiplied Gaussian soft focus and supersampling match the softness of the input. Five subframe samples over a 1/60-second shutter add mild motion blur.

The ball follows x(t) = 93.1 + 46t − 1.4t² and y(t) = 60.3 − 0.075[x(t) − 93.1]. This is a straight projected roll with modest deceleration. The initial radius is 8.3 pixels and decreases slightly with perspective. Rotation angle integrates traveled distance divided by radius; its axis is the cross product of the inferred table normal and travel direction. A diffuse shadow and a compact contact shadow move with the ball. The diffuse shadow fades to zero smoothly at its boundary. The ball remains in contact with the table and exits through the right frame boundary. Frames 18 and 19 show the same stationary empty setup.

Artifacts and reproduction

Run python generate.py from this workspace to recreate the outputs. pixels.npz stores the array named pixels, shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32. Each value is packed as 0xRRGGBB. The 20 RGB PNGs are frames/00.png through frames/19.png. index.html is a standalone player containing the first frame and lossless frame deltas, played at 4 fps. inspection.png shows eight selected frames; transition_check.png shows consecutive early frames and the right-edge exit. The 20 samples are spaced 0.25 seconds apart, starting at t = 0; each is displayed for 0.25 seconds, giving a 5-second sequence.

Review and verification

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code and records measurements and the 20-frame plan. The image-view tool was used on the input, an enlarged input, a ball crop, the initial contact sheets and reconstructed table, a close comparison of generated balls, and both final contact sheets. After review, the sphere radius was adjusted from the initial 8.0-pixel estimate to 8.3 pixels, the seam was strengthened to resemble the input's worn pale seam, and soft focus was increased. The fixed-region check was narrowed to actual static landmarks because the rolling ball approaches the upper rows of the tabletop on the right.

The generator reads back all 20 PNGs and verifies exact equality with the decoded tensor. It also checks the tensor dimensions, dtype, 24-bit range, exact frame-00 pixels, unchanged tube and lampshade regions, and equal final empty frames. Verification details are written to .tmp/verification.json. The standalone player's embedded frames were separately decoded and checked against the same tensor. All required artifact files were checked for existence.
