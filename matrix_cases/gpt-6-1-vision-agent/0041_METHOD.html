The glass starts near (27, 10), (229, 9), with its lower border at about y=142. The board and wood have fine vertical grain and scratches. Lighting is soft and comes primarily from above/right.

The tangerine half occupies approximately x=99–154, y=35–86. Its orange peel outlines two lobes, with a depression at the top around (125, 42), pale central pith, and visible radial flesh segments. The cut face is oriented toward the camera. The left lobe reaches (99, 60); the right reaches (154, 53). The lower contact area is around (124, 85). A narrow, nearly edge-on steel knife runs from approximately (133, 0) through (126, 58) to a pointed tip at (120, 110). The knife partly occludes the central pith. There are no hands or other objects in frame.

The continuation uses the existing knife axis as the cut line. The knife presses down, briefly reaches the board, and lifts away. The two cut pieces initially remain in contact, then tip and slide gently to either side, retaining their flesh and rind texture. Both remain on the board. The background, camera, illumination, and framing stay stationary. No new objects enter.

METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was generated with deterministic local Python pixel operations from first_frame.png only. prompt.txt supplied the action. No network, additional scene inputs, external models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

generate.py measures and masks the photographed fruit and thin blade, then divides the fruit mask along the existing knife axis. The two cut-face layers retain the original peel, pith, and flesh textures. Pixels occluded by the original blade are repaired by interpolation between nearby pixels on the same row. Four-times-resolution masks and inverse affine sampling provide antialiased motion at the final 256 × 144 resolution.

The knife translates down its original axis during the first two seconds, pauses near the glass, and withdraws upward. Its cropped upper continuation uses the photographed blade's first-row colors and measured width. The fruit pieces progressively tip outward around their lower rind, slide a few pixels apart, and settle. Smooth analytic trajectories give small changes between successive frames at a constant 4 fps. The camera and board remain fixed. The final three frames hold the settled result.

Newly exposed glass is reconstructed locally. A discrete Laplace solve interpolates board color from the surrounding unoccluded source pixels, and high-frequency grain comes from a clear patch of the same board sixty pixels to the right. Soft analytic contact shadows and faint orange reflections follow each fruit piece. This is a photographic layer animation with approximate surface shading, rather than a three-dimensional reconstruction or a numerical material-fracture simulation.

Frame 00 is copied directly from the original RGB array, bypassing rendering. artifact_io.py saves twenty RGB PNGs and packs the pixels into a (144, 256, 20) uint32 tensor, with each value encoded as 0xRRGGBB. It also creates the inspection sheet and a standalone HTML player containing the complete sequence. generate.py creates transition_check.png, showing every consecutive frame and its mean absolute RGB change.

Review and verification

The initial image was viewed with an image tool, including an enlarged measurement view. The first generated inspection and transition sheets were also viewed with the image tool. That review revealed orange color contamination in the reconstructed board. The fill was revised to use unblurred, unoccluded board boundary colors and a finer source texture residual. An enlarged detail review also prompted widening the reconstruction margin to remove residual fruit-edge color. The final inspection and transition sheets were then visually reviewed.

The generation script checks every PNG against the decoded uint32 tensor, confirms the tensor shape and type, checks frame 00 against first_frame.png pixel for pixel, and verifies that the final held frames are identical. An independent check also decodes the HTML player's initial frame and all nineteen delta packets and confirms that each displayed frame equals the tensor. These checks pass. Playback is configured at 250 milliseconds per frame for twenty frames, totaling five seconds.
