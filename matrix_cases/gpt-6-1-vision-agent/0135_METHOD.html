The water is clear, with brown table color visible beneath it and bright reflections on the glass.

A black shaft enters from the top at x≈147 and joins orange and black curved jaws around x=110–150, y=20–49. The bowl, table and room retain the original pixels outside the localized action.

METHOD.md

Method and validation

The delivered continuation uses only the supplied first_frame.png and scene description. It is a deterministic local Python pixel composition, not output from an AI video model or a 3D engine. The implementation is in generate.py; running python generate.py rebuilds the complete deliverable.

Pixel construction

The photograph is retained as the fixed scene. Manually measured antialiased polygons isolate the paper, the two orange/black jaws, and a stationary collar. Their color textures come directly from the source image. A local quadratic least-squares fit to neighboring gray wall pixels restores the wall that is revealed as these objects move. A feathered boundary joins this small restoration to the source; dark shaft pixels and colored object pixels are excluded from the fit.

Each jaw texture is rotated around its attachment to the fixed collar. Opening increases gradually and reaches its final angle at 0.50 seconds. The left and right jaw rotations are +21 and −25 degrees in image coordinates. The black shaft remains fixed. The source image is used directly for frame 00.

The paper is released at 0.42 seconds. Its vertical displacement follows a quadratic free-fall path for 0.42 seconds, from a center of (129,46.6) to approximately (129.9,95.4). The frames at 0.50 and 0.75 seconds show the intervening fall. Four closely spaced samples approximate a short camera exposure during free fall. After contact, an exponentially damped sinusoid provides a small bob; slow drift and a limited rotation settle the paper near (132,95). Small changes in scale approximate the crumpled paper settling on the water. Its lower folds darken gradually and become slightly translucent where they meet the water.

The water disturbance uses expanding, damped elliptical wave envelopes. They displace the existing water texture by a fraction of a pixel and add subdued highlight/shadow variation. The effect is restricted to an interior water ellipse and preserves the glass rim. A soft contact shadow, a faint blurred reflection, and a thin meniscus join the floating paper to the surface. No extra objects, hands, camera changes, or dramatic splashes are introduced.

This method preserves the original paper texture; the wetting, deformation and fluid response are image-space approximations. They are not a volumetric paper or fluid simulation.

Inspection and revision

The source photograph and enlarged coordinate crop were viewed using the image tool. The first inspection.png and transition_check.png were also viewed. Review found a faint residual outline in the restored wall and a release timing that left the paper too long beneath fully open jaws. The restoration was revised to exclude dark shaft samples, fit a quadratic wall surface, and expand the removal area. Release was moved to 0.42 seconds, with slower initial jaw opening. The revised restored background, early transition sheet, and complete final inspection sheet were viewed with the image tool.

  • pixels.npz: key pixels, dtype uint32, shape (144,256,20), with each value encoded as 0xRRGGBB.

A separate readback reconstructs the embedded HTML frame deltas and verifies that all twenty reconstructed frames match pixels.npz.
