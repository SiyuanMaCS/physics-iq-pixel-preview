This sequence was generated entirely from first_frame.png using local Python pixel operations. prompt.txt supplied the intended action. No network access, other scene references, external models, rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Source layers and recovered background

The initial RGB photograph supplies all tool details, paper color and shading, wood grain, and wall texture. Color thresholds inside manually measured regions identify the green sheet and the blue/black tools. The metallic left shaft receives an additional small polygon selection. Small bright tool fasteners are retained; the two large handle openings remain transparent. Local OpenCV inpainting and nearest interior colors estimate edge coverage and foreground colors.

Behind the original objects, the tabletop is reconstructed with a quadratic illumination field fitted to unobstructed wood pixels, plus grain residuals sampled from the clear bottom strip of the same image. Where only old shadows were visible, fine local luminance detail is retained while the estimated wood color replaces the shadow and green reflected light. Feathering blends the recovered regions into the untouched photograph. The wall hidden by the original left shaft is locally inpainted. These operations synthesize previously occluded pixels from this one photograph.

Motion and tear

The paper is split into two material domains along an explicitly defined irregular path from approximately (134,40) to (121,66). Each domain has a small triangular mesh built with SciPy Delaunay triangulation. Mesh triangles are rasterized in Python and sampled from the source texture with OpenCV remapping. Shared seam vertices coincide below the advancing tear tip, so the lower sheet remains connected until the last bridge gives way. Above the tip, the two seam boundaries move apart under the opposing pull.

The displacement is constrained to each gripping corner and reduces toward the unbroken center. A height-dependent bend makes the arched sheet become taut, then lets the free torn ends droop and flutter after separation. Narrow pale green fiber highlights reveal the irregular torn boundaries. The photographic blue/black tools undergo rigid translations and very small rotations about their gripping tips. They move outward by roughly 24 and 25 pixels, respectively, and continue holding their paper pieces.

The tear begins at frame 05, advances through frames 06–11, and completes at frame 12. Frames 12–14 include separation and recoil. A damped, prescribed flex settles over the remaining frames. Cast/contact shadows are recalculated from the moving layer silhouettes using small offsets and Gaussian blur. The sequence is a two-dimensional photographic warp with analytic deformation and tearing, rather than a measured three-dimensional simulation.

Rendering uses a 4× pixel grid for coverage and triangle rasterization, followed by area downsampling to 256 × 144 RGB. Frame 00 bypasses every rendering operation and copies the initial image exactly. The camera and frame stay fixed throughout.

Saved artifacts and validation

  • OBSERVATION.md: measurements and the twenty-frame plan, written before generate.py.
  • generate.py: the complete deterministic local generator, including output validation.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: twenty 256 × 144 RGB frames.
  • pixels.npz: pixels, shape (144,256,20), dtype uint32, encoding 0xRRGGBB.
  • inspection.png: an eight-frame contact sheet.
  • transition_check.png: every frame paired with a 3× absolute pixel difference from its predecessor.
  • index.html: a standalone embedded-pixel player, with 250 ms frame intervals, replay, seek and looping controls. Twenty frames at 4 fps give a five-second playback.
  • verification.json: read-back checks for the images, tensor and embedded player data.

The generator checks all twenty saved PNGs against the packed tensor, validates the tensor shape and type, and checks that frame 00 equals the supplied RGB image exactly. A separate read-back check also decodes the HTML player's initial frame and nineteen delta packets and compares every reconstructed frame against the tensor.

Image tools were used to review the supplied image, source layers, recovered background, contact sheets, and transitions. The background cleanup was revised after inspection to remove residual shadows and green tint at the original object positions. The final image review checks progressive tearing, corner attachment, outward tool motion, exposed wood in the gap, and settling of both held pieces.

To reproduce all pixel artifacts, run python generate.py from this workspace. The local dependencies are NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and OpenCV.
