cut-paper · 生成方法

This sequence was generated locally with Python, NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. The visual inputs were exclusively first_frame.png and the action described in prompt.txt. The method uses source-image pixel extraction, mathematical two-dimensional warps, local background reconstruction, and alpha compositing. No network, external assets, learned models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used.

Scene and motion

OBSERVATION.md was written before the generation code. Channel differences isolate the green sheet and the blue/black grabbers inside manually measured source-image polygons. These mattes preserve the photographed surface texture, illumination, handle openings, and gripping tips.

The left and right gripping points begin near (64, 41) and (201, 43). A smooth outward pull reaches approximately 12 pixels per tool. The paper's projected width increases about its narrow center while its vertical extent contracts slightly, representing the tightening of its folded shape. An irregular, prescribed cut crosses the center around x=133, y=43–56. The opening grows from the upper edge downward between frames 07 and 11. The upper split opens while the lower connection remains narrow. After that connection breaks, the two pieces and their grabbers recoil another 7 pixels outward. A small exponentially decaying flexing motion lets the flaps settle by the final frame.

Each paper half is inverse-warped independently. Complementary mattes are summed before compositing so the intact sheet has no artificial partition seam. A very narrow lighter green rim follows the newly exposed torn edges. The paper and tools retain the source photograph's colors; mild shading changes accompany the curl tightening. The camera and tabletop coordinates remain fixed throughout.

Tabletop and shadows

Covered tabletop regions are estimated using a quadratic color field fitted to visible clean wood and high-frequency grain copied from the source's lower tabletop strip. Local Navier–Stokes pixel inpainting fills residuals inside the original foreground footprint. The observed floor residual is divided into fixed small-scale grain and a smooth illumination component. The grain remains stationary, while the illumination component moves smoothly with the pulling setup. This revision reduces the background change at the first transition and retains the original tabletop's measured appearance.

Soft shadows are formed by projecting the paper mattes toward the table and applying Gaussian blurs for diffuse illumination. Tool contact shadows are shorter and sharper. These analytic shadows are combined with the transported observed shading residual. Fourfold spatial supersampling and Lanczos reduction produce the final 256 × 144 RGB images. Frame 00 bypasses generation entirely and uses the original RGB pixel array.

The fold geometry, tearing front, and recoil are analytically prescribed visual approximations. The code does not solve three-dimensional sheet mechanics or derive fracture propagation from material properties.

Artifacts and verification

  • generate.py: deterministic generator, artifact export, and verification.
  • pixels.npz: pixels tensor with shape (144, 256, 20), dtype uint32, and values encoded as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png: 20 RGB images at 256 × 144.
  • inspection.png: eight-frame overview with sample timestamps.
  • transition_check.png: detailed coverage of the initial transition, tearing, recoil, and final state.
  • index.html: standalone player containing all frame data, with 250 ms frame intervals.
  • OBSERVATION.md: initial measurements and the 20-frame event plan.
  • METHOD.md: this account of the actual generation method.

The sample timestamps run from 0.00 to 4.75 seconds. Holding each of the 20 frames for 0.25 seconds gives a 5-second sequence at 4 fps. Verification asserts that frame 00 equals the input RGB array, every PNG equals its corresponding packed tensor slice, and every reconstructed HTML player frame equals the same tensor. The verification report is saved in .tmp/verification.json.

Visual review and revision

Image-view tools were used on the supplied frame, an enlarged copy, the initial inspection and transition sheets, the revised transition sheet, and the revised final frame. The initial render had an excessive change in tabletop tone at frame 01. The floor residual preservation described above reduced mean absolute RGB change for that transition from 4.13 to 1.03 intensity units per channel. Visual inspection of the revised sequence confirmed a continuous initial pull, an irregular center tear, a clear final gap, and both torn pieces retained in their grabbers. The final inspection sheet was also reviewed after updating its timestamp labels.
