OBSERVATION.md

Observation and event plan

The supplied RGB image is 256 × 144 pixels. The camera looks down at a dark navy mug on a warm gray-brown wooden tabletop. The fixed mug has an approximately elliptical outer rim, spanning x=99–165 and y=39–92, with center near (132,65). The liquid is very dark red-brown, bounded approximately by x=105–158, y=53–87; its projected horizontal surface has center near (132,70). The body ends near y=115. Its handle extends to x=176 and y=122. The mug, its highlights, tabletop grain, lighting, and soft cast shadow must remain stationary.

The grabber's two blue jaws with dark outer edges enter from the top: the left jaw spans roughly x=105–122, y=0–19; the right jaw x=133–151, y=0–19. A white ceramic domino is almost edge-on, about 7–8 pixels wide and 48 pixels long, from approximately (126,17) to (130,64). Its face is pale white with blue-gray side shading; no pips are visible. The tool holds its upper end, and the lower end is a few projected pixels above the liquid. Downward motion will put the visible domino in front of the rear rim, then progressively hide it below the liquid surface.

Use only the supplied image and local analytic pixel operations. Build a clean static plate for the small regions initially occluded by the tool and domino using nearby tabletop texture, local image interpolation/inpainting, and neighboring rear-rim color. Extract the original domino and jaws as antialiased image layers. Translate/open the jaws, translate the domino downward under gravity, and hide its submerged section. Add a brief dark liquid splash, a few small droplets, and perspective-compressed damped ripples constrained to the existing liquid. Do not add objects, camera motion, text, or scene cuts. Frame 00 stays byte-for-byte equal in decoded RGB pixels to the input.

METHOD.md

Method

Generated locally from first_frame.png with Python, NumPy, Pillow, OpenCV, and SciPy. No network, external reference image, generative model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. prompt.txt controls the action. OBSERVATION.md was written before the generator, including measurements and a plan for all twenty frames.

The continuation is image compositing with analytic motion and liquid effects. The stationary photograph supplies the tabletop, mug, handle, light, and shadows. Small occluded tabletop areas are reconstructed with OpenCV Telea inpainting. The hidden rear-rim and liquid strip is reconstructed from two intact neighboring columns, aligning their samples to an elliptical rim and feathering the repair into the surrounding photograph. The domino and two jaws are extracted as image layers with coverage inferred from their color contrast against this plate. Premultiplied affine translations retain their original shading and softened edges.

The jaws separate at frame 01, rise by frame 02, and leave the frame at frame 03. The rigid ceramic edge descends under a quadratic gravity approximation: a 19-pixel displacement by 0.50 seconds. A fixed liquid-surface cutoff progressively hides the submerged part; the domino is completely submerged by 0.75 seconds. The mug remains fixed.

The impact splash at 0.50, 0.75, and 1.00 seconds uses locally drawn irregular liquid sheets and a few droplets, supersampled at four times the output resolution. Distance-transform gradients shade their brown surfaces, with small warm reflected highlights. These splash silhouettes are hand-authored approximations. Subsequent liquid movement uses an analytic radial wave packet, a reflected wave, and a damped Bessel sloshing mode in a perspective-compressed surface plane. Height gradients drive small image warps, soft reflection highlights, and shadows. The waves decay to a nearly still dark surface. This is a constructed 2D continuation, not a fluid solver or a recovered 3D scene.

Artifacts and timing

  • generate.py: reproducible generator; run python generate.py in this workspace.
  • pixels.npz: key pixels, uint32 tensor with shape (144, 256, 20), packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png–frames/19.png: twenty 256 × 144 RGB PNGs.
  • inspection.png: eight sampled frames labeled with zero-based sampling times.
  • transition_check.png: fifteen consecutive frames including release, entry, splash, and settling.
  • index.html: standalone embedded-pixel player with 250 ms playback intervals, seek, replay, and looping.

Frame sampling is t = frame_index / 4, from 0.00 through 4.75 seconds. Holding each of the twenty frames for 0.25 seconds gives a five-second clip. Frame 00 is taken directly from the original decoded RGB image without processing.

Review and verification

The supplied image, enlarged scene crops, generated inspection and transition sheets, and enlarged generated splash/settling frames were actually viewed with the image tool. Review led to revisions to the occluded rear-rim reconstruction and increased visibility of the damped liquid ripples.

The generator verifies every exported PNG against the decoded uint32 tensor, checks RGB dimensions and tensor shape/dtype, checks that frame 00 exactly equals the supplied RGB pixels, and checks the unchanged fixed background, mug body, and handle. A separate artifact audit also decodes the HTML player's embedded initial frame and all nineteen delta packets and verifies them against the exported tensor. All checks passed on the final generation.
