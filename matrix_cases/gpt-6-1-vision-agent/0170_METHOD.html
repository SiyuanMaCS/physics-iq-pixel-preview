METHOD.md

Generation method

This sequence was generated from first_frame.png and prompt.txt using local Python pixel operations. OBSERVATION.md records the scene measurements and frame plan written before coding. No network, external reference, external AI model, scene engine, or subagent was used.

Image construction

The basket, black magnet, orange cord, hook and links are source-image cutouts. Their silhouettes were traced with local antialiased polygon masks. The original basket weave and lighting were retained. All suspension parts translate upward together with subpixel bilinear resampling; the upper frame boundary clips them naturally. The camera stays fixed. Lift amounts are listed explicitly in generate.py, beginning with a short hold, easing into the lift, and slowing to a final lift of 105 pixels. The basket's lower edge finishes around y=27, leaving part of the basket visible at the top of the frame.

The hidden wall is reconstructed with interpolation between nearby observed wall colors. A narrow interpolation patch removes the original suspension from the stationary background. The concealed table is reconstructed by shearing and resampling source wood pixels from the right side, matching row illumination to neighboring source table pixels, and blending the patch edges. Weak, deterministically seeded texture variation prevents a completely flat repair. These are estimates of areas that the input image does not show.

The orange tennis ball is drawn at four times the output resolution and downsampled. Its center stays at (132,117), its radius is about 12 pixels, and its contact point is at y=129. Sphere normals provide soft directional lighting; filtered seeded noise provides fine felt texture. Two bowed pale seam arcs are projected over the front of the ball. A small soft contact shadow anchors it to the table. The basket occludes the ball until it lifts sufficiently to reveal it.

The basket shadow uses a height-dependent Gaussian attenuation field: it spreads and weakens as the basket rises. A fading residual of the observed surroundings preserves the initial visible table and its contact shadow at the start. The original object's entire repaired footprint is excluded from that residual to prevent stationary chain or basket remnants. The original lower basket rim is preserved; no additional object, hand, camera motion or ball motion is introduced.

Frames 00 and 01 are direct copies of the input RGB pixels. Frames 02–19 use the constructed background, stationary ball, evolving shadow and translated basket cutout. The process is deterministic.
