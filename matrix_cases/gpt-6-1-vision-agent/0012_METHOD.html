This sequence was generated with deterministic local Python pixel operations, using the supplied first frame as the only image source. No external images, models, rendering engines, network access, or subagents were used. Run python generate.py from this directory to reproduce the outputs. The script uses NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, OpenCV pixel interpolation, and the supplied output helper.

Image construction

The initial image was visually inspected, and scene measurements and the frame plan were written to OBSERVATION.md before generation code was written. The orange ball is already outside the cropped black pipe in frame 00, so the continuation starts with its existing position rather than repeating the emergence.

The photographic background is retained. A local removal region covers the original orange ball and its shadow. A clean donor patch is sampled 40 pixels to the right and 5 pixels down, following the approximate wood-grain direction. An explicit sparse Poisson solve preserves those donor gradients while matching the untouched image on the removal boundary. This is a local inferred repair of the hidden wood, rather than recovered ground-truth pixels.

The moving ball uses a cutout recovered from the observed orange ball, with a blurred elliptical alpha boundary and foreground colors separated from the repaired background. Its original photographic shading stays in the room's fixed lighting direction. A very small procedural felt-color perturbation is evaluated on sphere normals rotated according to travel distance divided by radius. The main appearance is thus a photographic sprite with subtle mathematical rolling texture, not a fully reconstructed 3D scene.

Ball centers follow a straight projected path from (37.35, 45.15) to (222.2, 84.39). The projected tabletop contact slopes 0.215 pixels down per pixel right. The nominal radius increases from 6.7 to 7.2 pixels to suggest the modest perspective change. Positions advance smoothly at the fixed sample rate. A soft elongated shadow and narrow contact shadow move with the ball; their direction remains constant, and the cast shadow is clipped at the tabletop edge. The final frame brings the ball to the right edge of the table without adding a fall or collision.

Compositing runs at four times the output dimensions. Only the overlay difference is reduced to output size, so the photographic background is not globally resampled. The red ball, brown ball, pipe, sofa, table outline, and floor remain stationary.

Revision and visual review

The first generated inspection sheet exposed an incomplete removal of the source ball. The clone operation was replaced with the explicit Poisson solve, and compositing was revised to preserve static source pixels exactly. The corrected background, corrected eight-frame inspection sheet, and corrected transition sheet were viewed with the image tool. The final 20-frame contact sheet was also reviewed. These checks show one orange ball crossing the table with no residual ball at the original position.

Artifact format and checks

  • pixels.npz contains pixels, a uint32 array of shape (144, 256, 20), packed as 0xRRGGBB.
  • frames/00.png through frames/19.png are 256 × 144 RGB PNGs. Frame 00 is copied from the input PNG and also exactly matches its decoded pixels.
  • All 20 PNGs are unpacked and checked against their corresponding packed tensor slices. The check passes exactly.
  • The stationary region containing the red and brown balls is checked for exact equality throughout all 20 frames.
  • index.html is a standalone player containing the same pixel sequence and displaying it at 4 fps: 20 frames occupy 5 seconds. Its decoded first frame and all delta packets are checked against the tensor.
  • inspection.png shows eight selected frames. transition_check.png shows frames before and after four transitions, plus their absolute differences at 3× contrast.
  • .tmp/verification.json records the automated format and pixel checks. .tmp/all_frames.png is the supplementary full-sequence contact sheet.

The tabletop trajectory, hidden background, tiny perspective change, and shadow evolution are estimates inferred from a single image. The sequence is a local pixel-based continuation, not measured footage or a physical simulation recovered from the source.
