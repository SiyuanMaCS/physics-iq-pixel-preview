OBSERVATION.md

Observations and frame plan

The input is an RGB image, 256 × 144. A wooden tabletop fills the lower part of the frame and a plain gray wall fills the upper part. The black cylindrical powered platform spans approximately x=104–179, y=68–117. Its cable runs toward the upper right. The tennis ball touches its top at approximately (140,81). The yellow-green ball occupies x≈122–157, y≈47–82, with center approximately (139.4,64.6) and projected radii approximately (17.6,17.3). Its two black eyes are on the upper left surface, the curved mouth below them, and the pale seam curves down the visible front-right surface. The upper/front-right side is brighter; the contact shadow is below the ball. No other scene element moves.

The action is interpreted as the ball spinning in place about the vertical turntable axis, clockwise when viewed from above. At this camera angle, the front surface travels toward image-left. Use constant angular speed of 12 degrees/second: 3 degrees per frame at 4 fps. The visible leftward face motion and seam curvature must come from a sphere rotation rather than a flat translation. Maintain the original ball position, contour, illumination, contact, and all background pixels. The unseen surface will need a local procedural yellow-green felt continuation; it is not available from the input.

METHOD.md

Method and delivered artifacts

This is a local Python pixel animation made solely from first_frame.png and prompt.txt. No network, external image, learned model, external rendering engine, or subagent was used. NumPy performs the sphere math and color calculations, SciPy interpolates the photographed pixels, and Pillow writes the images. artifact_io.py packs and exports the resulting frames and the standalone player.

Motion

The sphere remains centered at (139.15,64.25), with projected radii (17.70,17.60) pixels. Camera elevation is approximated as 20 degrees from the platform's perspective. The ball turns about a world-vertical axis at a constant 12 degrees per second, clockwise viewed from above. Its front surface consequently moves toward image-left. The 20 samples are taken at t=0,0.25,...,4.75 seconds; frame 19 is displayed for the last quarter-second of the five-second animation. Rotation covers 57 degrees between the first and last samples. The face becomes foreshortened and turns out of view while the pale seam follows the surface. The ball neither translates nor bounces.

Pixel construction

For each pixel within the ball ellipse, a visible sphere normal is computed. Rodrigues rotation maps that normal back into the initial sphere orientation. The normal projects to a source-image coordinate, where cubic interpolation samples the photographed yellow felt, eyes, mouth, and seam. A robust polynomial color fit over clean felt approximates stationary lighting; dividing by the source illumination and multiplying by destination illumination keeps the light direction fixed as the texture turns.

Newly revealed areas use deterministic low-amplitude trigonometric grain attached to 3D surface coordinates. The pale seam's unseen continuation is approximated by a small circle fitted to eight points on the photographed seam. These hidden pixels are inferred, not recovered from another view. The horizon blend is broader on the original right side to exclude photographed silhouette/background mixtures, and narrower on the left to retain ink. A smooth elliptical rim blend preserves the photographed soft edge and contact. All pixels outside that ellipse are copied exactly from the source. Frame 00 is directly copied from the input without rendering.

The rendering uses cubic pixel interpolation at output resolution; it does not use a 3D engine or supersampling. The observation document originally proposed supersampling, but cubic interpolation proved sufficient at this resolution.

Review and revision

Image tools were used to view the input, an enlarged input-ball crop, generated inspection.png and transition_check.png, and enlarged generated ball crops. An early version dragged a dark antialiased source boundary onto the newly visible surface. The horizon blending was revised to remove that band. Enlarged crops of the revised frames were then visually reviewed. The final contact sheets were reviewed after the revision. The scene and ball center stay fixed, and the markings move consistently toward the left limb.

Verification

generate.py verifies every decoded PNG against the saved uint32 tensor and the in-memory RGB frame. The tensor key is pixels, shape is (144,256,20), and values use 0xRRGGBB. It checks that frame 00 equals the decoded input, all packed values fit in 24 bits, and every pixel outside the ball ellipse remains exactly fixed. verification.json records these results and consecutive-frame changes. The standalone player's first frame and all decoded delta packets were independently compared to the tensor. All checks passed.
