+    ball_region = ((X - CX) / 9.5) ** 2 + ((Y - CY) / 9.5) ** 2 < 1

+    region = ball_region | shadow_region

+    ids[region] = np.arange(len(coords))
+    donor = SOURCE[np.minimum(Y + 18, H - 1), X]
+    matrix = lil_matrix((len(coords), len(coords)), dtype=float)
+    rhs = np.zeros((len(coords), 3))
+    for i, (y, x) in enumerate(coords):
+        matrix[i, i] = 4
+        for dy, dx in ((0, 1), (0, -1), (1, 0), (-1, 0)):
+            yy, xx = y + dy, x + dx
+            rhs[i] += donor[y, x] - donor[yy, xx]
+            if region[yy, xx]:

+            else:
+                rhs[i] += SOURCE[yy, xx]

METHOD.md

Method and completed checks

This sequence was generated with local Python image operations using only first_frame.png and the scenario in prompt.txt. No network resources, external reference images, learned models, external rendering engines, or subagents were used. generate.py regenerates the deliverables using NumPy, Pillow, SciPy, and the supplied output helper artifact_io.py.

The camera, table, sofa, pipe, kettlebell, and duck come from the photograph. Frame 00 is the exact input RGB array. For subsequent frames, the original ball and its shadow are removed with a local Poisson blend. The donor wood gradients are copied from the same photograph, 18 pixels below the removed region. This estimates the tabletop hidden behind the ball; it is not a recovered observation of that hidden surface.

The ball's photographed colors are extracted with a soft elliptical alpha mask. Boundary colors are decontaminated against the estimated table and extended to avoid a wood-colored halo. Its low-frequency photographic shading stays fixed in screen space. Its measured fine texture is remapped over an analytic sphere and rotated according to rolling distance. The unobserved hemisphere uses mirrored source texture. The outline and compositing are antialiased. During travel, five subpixel samples approximate an effective 1/120-second exposure. The sampled impact frame has slight horizontal compression and vertical expansion. Soft Gaussian cast and contact shadows move with the ball and are occluded by the stationary kettlebell.

The motion is an image-space kinematic approximation, not a full rigid-body simulation. The ball moves from (61, 52) to (115.4, 56.624) in 0.5 seconds, touches the left side of the 30 lb kettlebell, and rebounds with a velocity ratio of 0.42. Constant rolling deceleration of 18 image pixels/second² brings it to rest at approximately (57.397, 51.694) at 3.039 seconds. The heavy kettlebell stays planted under the small ball's impulse, and the duck is untouched. The final seven frames hold the stopped state. Frame times are 0.00–4.75 seconds; displaying each for 0.25 seconds gives a five-second clip.

Verification and visual review

The generation script verifies the exact input match for frame 00 and checks all twenty PNGs against both the in-memory RGB arrays and the packed tensor. It also independently decodes the HTML player's first-frame and delta packets and compares every reconstructed pixel to the tensor. The duck, pipe, and kettlebell foreground pixels are verified unchanged, and frames 13–19 are verified identical.

I used image View tools to inspect the enlarged input, ball crop, generated overview, transition sheet, and repaired tabletop. The review confirmed the ball's contact and rebound, a fixed view, and a stable final rest. A boundary check identified a few kettlebell edge pixels affected by the ball's shadow; the foreground occlusion mask was expanded and the outputs regenerated. The final overview and transition sheet were visually inspected again after that revision. At this resolution and frame rate, rolling texture and compression are subtle, and the rapid contact is represented by one sampled frame.
